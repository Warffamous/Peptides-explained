# Review: TL;DR and side chapters (27 October 2026)

What you asked for: a TL;DR at the top of the pages that need one ("only the very essential information that people
are most likely to be looking for", readable in one minute), chapters on the side that stay with you as you scroll,
and both working on phones. The old "In short" box is gone.

## What changed

- **TL;DR box** at the top of 52 pages: all 21 compounds, the safety, law and guide pages, the basics, evidence and
  history pages, the reform page and the home page. 3–6 questions readers actually ask, each with a plain answer.
  - On compound pages the box holds the four answer tiles (approved? tested in people? sport? biggest risk), then the
    red "Stop and get help" strip, then the questions. The strip still sits on the first two phone screens.
- **No TL;DR, only chapters:** the section overviews (basics, evidence, history, law, safety, compounds), the about
  pages, the timeline and the corrections log. The glossary, sources, news and search pages keep their one intro line
  under the title.
- **Chapters:**
  - Wide screens (from 1200 px): a column on the left that stays in view and marks the chapter you are reading, with
    the reading time.
  - Phones and tablets: the chapter list follows the top of the page; once you scroll past it, a slim bar at the top
    shows the current chapter, and tapping it opens the list.
  - Without JavaScript the list still works (no bar, no marking).

## How the TL;DRs were checked

Every line was written only from facts already on that page, then checked four times:

1. against the page's facts;
2. as a worried reader would act on it;
3. across pages, so the same fact reads the same everywhere;
4. against the open points from those checks.

The full record is in `research/tldr/verify-summary.md`; all TL;DRs side by side are in `research/tldr/all-tldrs.md`.

Decisions taken along the way (yours to overrule):

- **Safety first:**
  - The infection lines say to call your local emergency number when there is fever with a fast heartbeat or fast
    breathing.
  - TB-500 mentions the hidden-tumour concern.
  - Semaglutide says why fake pens holding insulin are dangerous.
  - Tirzepatide carries the label's contraceptive-pill warning.
- **The July 2026 FDA advisory vote** is always "only advice", and "reports say" wherever the page itself only has the
  reported outcome (no minutes yet). When the FDA posts minutes, the hedge can come off on all those pages at once.
- **Selling:** where a page says selling is illegal in all four countries, the TL;DR says a flat "No", never
  "not as a medicine" (which could read as "legal as a research chemical").
- **Crisis page:** "Will the doctor report me?" leads with "their job is to treat you".

## Optional follow-ups (not part of this change)

These came up in the checks. They concern page bodies or data, not the TL;DR:

- **Tested-in-people tile wording:**
  - AOD-9604 and ipamorelin say "No large trials published yet" although development stopped.
  - Epitalon says "Small trials in patients" although two of its three studies were in healthy shift workers.
- **SS-31:** the page never says what online "SS-31" is marketed for, so the TL;DR cannot answer that.
- **GHK-Cu:** the page has no single sentence on whether US pharmacies may make the injectable form.
- **KPV and "What's changing":** in places the body states the July 2026 vote as fact, and elsewhere "as reported".
  The body should say the same in both places.
- **Doctors and the police:** "If something goes wrong" says this was not checked, while "Talking to your doctor" has
  sourced answers for all four countries. The first page could reuse that research.
