<Section id="what-it-is">

<Lead question="What is Semax?">A lab-made chain of seven amino acids. It is a medicine in Russia.</Lead>

<Compare title="Semax is a medicine in Russia. The US, the UK, Canada and Australia have not approved it." detail="What is known about the substance sold under this name, checked on 22 and 23 October 2026.">

| | Semax |
|---|---|
| Made in a lab? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Seven amino acids: a piece of the hormone ACTH plus Pro-Gly-Pro.<Cite id="pubchem-semax,fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" /> |
| A registered medicine anywhere? | <Verdict is="yes" /> In Russia, as nose drops.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing,deigin-2022-russian-peptides" /> |
| Approved in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia? | <Verdict is="no" /> None of the four has approved it.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing,mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-d-2026-10-23" /> |
| Well tested for safety? | <Verdict is="no" /> The FDA found too little data to describe its safety.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" /> |

</Compare>

In the US, Semax is sold as nose sprays and injections by clinics and online shops, for memory, anxiety, stroke and more.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" /> A Russian registration says nothing about products sold elsewhere under the name.

<InDetail title="The molecule, and what a Russian registration means">

Semax is <Seq>Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro</Seq>, formula C37H51N9O10S, mass <Q>813.9 g/mol</Q>.<Cite id="pubchem-semax" /> Its first four amino acids match part of ACTH, a pituitary hormone. The ending Pro-Gly-Pro is thought to protect it from enzymes. It lacks ACTH's hormone effects.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" /> It was made at the Institute of Molecular Genetics of the Russian Academy of Sciences.<Cite id="deigin-2022-russian-peptides" />

A registration in Russia lets the product be sold there. It is not an assessment by a regulator in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia, and it does not make Semax legal to supply as a medicine in any of them.<Cite id="usc-21-355,uk-hmr-2012-reg-46,ca-food-and-drug-regulations,au-tg-act-s19b-19d-offences" />

</InDetail>

<Section id="mechanism">

<Lead question="How does it work?">Not well understood. In rats it raised a growth factor for nerve cells.</Lead>

<Mechanism />

<InDetail title="What each step rests on">

Labelled Semax bound to rat brain membranes, and one dose in the nose raised BDNF in the rat brain. Both findings come from the developers' institute.<Cite id="dolotov-2006-semax-binding,dolotov-2006-semax-bdnf" /> The FDA summarises the rat stroke work and the clotting effects. It notes that most studies used a single dose and that the mechanisms are poorly understood.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" />

</InDetail>

<Section id="evidence">

<Lead question="Does it work?">Unknown. Animal studies are many. Studies in people are small and hard to check.</Lead>

<KeyNumber title="The largest stroke study was small and not clearly randomised" value="110" label="people after a stroke, with or without Semax" who="Gusev 2018: rehabilitation patients in Moscow, Russian-language paper; allocation and blinding not stated" source="gusev-2018-semax-stroke" level="human-limited" />

<KeyNumber title="In a migraine study, most headaches did not stop" value="4 of 12" label="people with migraine whose headache stopped after one dose; the other 8 said the pain eased" who="Koroleva 1996: one dose in the nose, no comparison group (FDA review)" source="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing,koroleva-1996-semax-analgesia" level="human-limited" />

The FDA concluded that there is not enough evidence that Semax works for stroke, migraine or facial nerve pain.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" />

In sport its status is unclear everywhere. It is not named on the list, and because it is registered in Russia, the catch-all ban (S0) may not apply. Ask your anti-doping organisation.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en,wada-2027-list-en,wada-2027-list-explanatory-note,fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" />

<InDetail title="Status in each country, and in sport">

<RegulatoryTable />

The FDA had put Semax in category 2, for substances that may present significant safety risks; the nominations were later withdrawn.<Cite id="fda-category-2-safety-risks" />

In sport, S0 bans substances that no government health authority has approved. Semax is registered in Russia, so S0 may not cover it. WADA warns that peptides that are not named may still be banned under another section.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en,wada-2027-list-explanatory-note" /> The sport row in the table is not a ruling. Only an anti-doping organisation can give one.

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="The UK, Canada and Australia">

- **United Kingdom.** The MHRA's database of licensed medicines has no Semax product.<Cite id="mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23" /> The Food Standards Agency records that the MHRA treats Semax as a medicine, not a food, so it may be sold or supplied in the UK only with a marketing authorisation, and none exists.<Cite id="uk-fsa-2018-importing-food-supplements,mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23" /> It is not a controlled drug under the Misuse of Drugs Act.<Cite id="uk-mda-1971-sch-2" />
- **Canada.** No Semax product has a Drug Identification Number (DIN), the number every authorised drug carries.<Cite id="ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-extract-search-merge-2026-10-24" /> Health Canada says injectable peptides are generally regulated as prescription drugs. In 2025 it named Semax among unauthorised injectable peptides seized from and sold by an online seller.<Cite id="ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides,ca-hc-recalls-search-status-d-2026-10-23" />
- **Australia.** Semax is not in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG), so it is not approved. The Poisons Standard does not name it, so it has no schedule.<Cite id="au-artg-search-status-d-2026-10-23,au-poisons-standard-2026-10" /> A product that is not in the ARTG still may not be imported or supplied unless an exemption applies.<Cite id="au-tg-act-s19b-19d-offences,au-tga-2026-04-peptide-responsibilities" /> Since June 2026 the regulator, the TGA, has made unapproved peptides a priority for its checks.<Cite id="au-tga-2026-06-peptide-compliance-priority" />

More: the law in [the US](/en/legal/us/), [the UK](/en/legal/uk/), [Canada](/en/legal/ca/) and [Australia](/en/legal/au/).

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="The July 2026 US vote, point by point">

- On 23 July 2026 the FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) voted 8–6 with one abstention to recommend BPC-157 for the 503A bulk drug substances list (count as reported from the public meeting).<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-07-meeting,fda-pcac-2026-questions" />
- The committee considered seven peptides, including Semax, which the FDA reviewed for poor blood flow to the brain, migraine and facial nerve pain.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-07-meeting,fr-2026-07361-pcac-notice" /> As reported from the meeting, it recommended six: BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Semax and epitalon; emideltide (DSIP) was not recommended. The FDA asked for separate votes on the free base and the acetate salt of each.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-questions" /> The FDA had posted no minutes by 23 October 2026, so the counts and outcome above are as reported from the public meeting.
- The vote is advisory and non-binding, and it went against FDA staff's own recommendation not to list Semax.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-briefing-intro" />
- Legal compounding remains prohibited until the FDA formally adopts the recommendation and completes rulemaking.<Cite id="fda-503a-bulks-page" />
- None of the six it recommended is an approved medicine in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia. Semax is registered as a medicine in Russia, as the FDA's own briefing notes. A 503A listing would allow pharmacy compounding on prescription in the US. It is not a marketing authorisation, says nothing about efficacy, and changes nothing outside the US.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-presentations-day1,mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23,uk-mhra-substance-index-status-c-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-c-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-d-2026-10-23" />

<Section id="risks">

<Lead question="What are the risks?">Little tested in people. In animals it thinned the blood and boosted a stimulant.</Lead>

<Compare title="Several harms are possible. None has been studied properly in people." detail="What the FDA review and the animal studies suggest, as of 22 October 2026.">

| Possible harm | Shown in people? |
|---|---|
| Bleeding, especially with blood thinners | <Verdict is="unknown" /> In rats, Semax made blood clot less.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" /> |
| Stronger effects of stimulants such as amphetamine | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Seen in rats and mice.<Cite id="eremin-2004-semax-amphetamine" /> |
| An immune reaction to the peptide or its impurities | <Verdict is="unknown" /> The FDA cannot rule it out, by injection or nose spray.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" /> |
| Eye pain and burning | <Verdict is="unknown" /> One person reported it to the FDA after nose drops bought online. Nobody knows if Semax caused it.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" /> |
| A product that is not what the label says | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Two unidentified seized products turned out to hold Semax.<Cite id="vanhee-2020-nootropic-peptides" /> |

</Compare>

<InDetail title="What the FDA found, and why it matters">

The FDA's adverse-event database held one report up to December 2025. A woman had eye pain and burning after Semax nose drops bought online. She went to hospital, and the pain had not gone a year later. That does not prove Semax was the cause.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" /> The published studies in people used nose drops only and mostly did not report side effects. There are no safety data for injections.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" />

Two animal findings matter for people who combine substances. In rats, Semax made blood clot less, so the FDA raises the risk of bleeding with blood thinners.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" /> In rats and mice it raised the dopamine response to amphetamine. The FDA calls this "concerning", because drugs of abuse raise dopamine in the same brain area.<Cite id="eremin-2004-semax-amphetamine,fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" />

</InDetail>

</Section>

<Section id="harm-reduction">

<Lead question="How can someone lower the risk?">Check the product first, bring a doctor in, and know when to stop.</Lead>

<ProductCheck extra="Check which form the label names. Semax is sold as a free base or an acetate salt. The FDA treats them as different substances." extraSources="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" />

### When to stop and get help

<Callout type="stop" title="Stop and get help if" id="stop-now">
- you get hives, swelling of the face or throat, or difficulty breathing (an allergic reaction);
- you bleed or bruise more than usual, especially if you take a blood thinner;
- your eyes hurt or burn after using nose drops;
- an injection site becomes hot, red, swollen or painful, or you get a fever (infection).

If it is severe, call your local emergency number.
</Callout>

<Cite id="nhs-anaphylaxis,nhs-cellulitis,fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" />

<WarningSigns only="general,injection,allergic" />

<InDetail title="Interactions, checks, and who should not use it">

These interactions are plausible from the animal studies; none has been tested in people. A doctor can help you weigh them (see [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/)):

- **Blood thinners and antiplatelet medicines**, because Semax made rat blood clot less.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" />
- **Stimulants, including ADHD medicines and amphetamine**, because Semax strengthened amphetamine's effect on dopamine in rats and mice.<Cite id="eremin-2004-semax-amphetamine" />

People with a bleeding disorder or who are pregnant have most reason for caution: there are no data in pregnancy.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" /> If you compete, ask your anti-doping organisation first. See [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/) and [sterile technique](/en/safety/sterile-technique/).

</InDetail>
