<Section id="what-it-is">

<Lead question="What is hexarelin?">A lab-made chain of six amino acids that makes the body release growth hormone.</Lead>

<Compare title="It was tested in people long ago, but never approved." detail="What is known about the substance sold under this name, checked on 23 October 2026.">

| | Hexarelin (examorelin) |
|---|---|
| Made in a lab? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Six amino acids, two of them in a lab-changed form.<Cite id="pubchem-hexarelin" /> |
| Releases growth hormone in people? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Shown in healthy volunteers.<Cite id="ghigo-1994-hexarelin-routes" /> |
| Approved as a medicine? | <Verdict is="no" /> Not in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia.<Cite id="openfda-drugsfda-search-stubs-b-2026-10-23,mhra-products-search-hexarelin,ca-dpd-search-status-b-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-b-2026-10-23" /> |
| Long-term safety studied? | <Verdict is="no" /> The longest studies we found lasted up to 8 months, in 12 or fewer people.<Cite id="laron-1995-hexarelin-children,rahim-1998-hexarelin-long-term,rahim-1998-hexarelin-desensitisation" /> |

</Compare>

It belongs to the growth-hormone-releasing peptides (GHRPs), like GHRP-2 and GHRP-6; its official name is examorelin.<Cite id="pubchem-hexarelin,wada-2026-list-en" /> No trial is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov.<Cite id="pubmed-hexarelin-count-2026-10-23,ctgov-search-status-b-2026-10-23" />

<InDetail title="The molecule">

Hexarelin is <Seq>His-D-2-Me-Trp-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys-NH2</Seq>. The second building block is a mirror-image tryptophan with an extra methyl group, the fifth is mirror-image phenylalanine, and the chain ends in an amide. Formula C47H58N12O6, mass <Q>887.0 g/mol</Q>.<Cite id="pubchem-hexarelin" /> A review describes it as chemically more stable than ghrelin.<Cite id="mao-2014-hexarelin-cardiovascular-review" />

</InDetail>

</Section>

<Section id="mechanism">

<Lead question="How does it work?">It acts on the hunger-hormone receptor, so more growth hormone is released. It also binds a heart protein.</Lead>

<Mechanism />

<InDetail title="What each step rests on">

Hexarelin switches on the same receptor as ghrelin, the body's hunger hormone.<Cite id="bodart-2002-hexarelin-cd36,mao-2014-hexarelin-cardiovascular-review" /> The growth hormone effect is measured in people.<Cite id="ghigo-1994-hexarelin-routes" /> Like ghrelin, it also raises the stress hormone cortisol for a short time.<Cite id="takaya-2000-ghrelin-humans,bisi-1999-hexarelin-heart,rahim-1999-hexarelin-adrenal" /> Unlike ghrelin, one dose did not raise blood sugar in healthy volunteers.<Cite id="broglio-2001-ghrelin-glucose" /> The CD36 heart finding comes from rat and mouse hearts outside the body.<Cite id="bodart-2002-hexarelin-cd36" />

</InDetail>

</Section>

<Section id="evidence">

<Lead question="Does it work?">It does release growth hormone. No controlled trial has shown a lasting benefit.</Lead>

<KeyNumber title="With weeks of use, the effect fell by almost half" value="19.1 → 10.5" label="growth hormone released after a test dose: at the start, then after 16 weeks" who="Rahim 1998: 12 healthy elderly people, injections twice a day, no placebo group" source="rahim-1998-hexarelin-long-term,rahim-1998-hexarelin-desensitisation" level="human-limited" />

<KeyNumber title="Children grew faster, but there was no comparison group" value="5.3 → 8.3 cm" label="growth per year, before and during treatment" who="Laron 1995: 8 short children, nasal spray for up to 8 months" source="laron-1995-hexarelin-children" level="human-limited" />

Most human studies lasted hours, weeks or a few months and had no placebo group. The heart findings in people are short-term changes after a single dose.<Cite id="bisi-1999-hexarelin-heart,imazio-2002-hexarelin-cardiomyopathy" />

<InDetail title="Every study in one table">

<StudyTable />

About 40% of all hexarelin papers come from one group in Turin and its partners, and groups in Milan and Montreal add about another 20%.<Cite id="pubmed-hexarelin-count-2026-10-23" /> Animal studies, for example in rats after a heart attack, report better heart function, but no controlled trial tested this in people.<Cite id="tivesten-2000-hexarelin-rat-mi,mao-2014-hexarelin-cardiovascular-review" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="The UK, Canada and Australia">

### United Kingdom

The MHRA, the UK medicines regulator, lists no product with hexarelin or its official name examorelin (searched 23 October 2026), and no MHRA alert or news item names it.<Cite id="mhra-products-search-hexarelin,uk-govuk-search-status-b-2026-10-23" /> Selling or supplying an unauthorised medicinal product is prohibited.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-46" /> It is not a controlled drug under the Misuse of Drugs Act.<Cite id="uk-mda-1971-sch-2" />

### Canada

Health Canada's Drug Product Database and its database of approvals since 1994 (notices of compliance) hold no hexarelin product.<Cite id="ca-dpd-search-status-b-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-extract-search-status-b-2026-10-23,ca-noc-search-status-b-2026-10-23" /> Health Canada has not authorised it, and its safety and effectiveness have not been established. Hexarelin is not on the Prescription Drug List, but Health Canada says injectable peptides are regulated as prescription drugs, and in August 2025 it listed hexarelin among unauthorised injectable peptides seized from an online seller.<Cite id="ca-pdl-human,ca-hc-recalls-database" /> A manufacturer may not sell a drug in Canada without a drug identification number (DIN).<Cite id="ca-food-and-drug-regulations" />

### Australia

No hexarelin product is on the ARTG.<Cite id="au-artg-search-status-b-2026-10-23" /> Since 1 June 2015 the Poisons Standard, Australia's national list of scheduled medicines, has named hexarelin as prescription only (Schedule 4) and on the list of medicines that are illegal to possess without authority (Appendix D).<Cite id="au-poisons-standard-2026-10,au-poisons-standard-2015-06,au-poisons-standard-2015-02" /> Possession rules are set by each state and territory: in New South Wales hexarelin is on the state's list of restricted medicines, and in Victoria possessing any Schedule 4 medicine without authority is an offence. The other states and territories were not checked.<Cite id="au-nsw-health-s4d,au-vic-dpcs-act-1981" /> Hexarelin is not named among the substances that need an import permit; whether a broader entry could still apply is not settled. Someone importing it for themselves must meet the conditions of the Personal Importation Scheme, which for a prescription-only medicine include a prescription.<Cite id="au-customs-prohibited-imports-regs,au-odc-growth-hormones-import-permit,au-tga-personal-importation-scheme" />

Nothing the FDA decides changes the law in the UK, Canada or Australia.
</Callout>

<Section id="risks">

<Lead question="What are the risks?">Long-term effects are unknown. It raises hormones, and the heart effects need more study.</Lead>

<Compare title="Some effects are shown in people; the bigger questions were never studied." detail="What small human studies and animal work show, as of 23 October 2026.">

| Possible harm | Shown in people? |
|---|---|
| Short rises in cortisol and other hormones | <Verdict is="yes" /> After single doses.<Cite id="bisi-1999-hexarelin-heart,rahim-1999-hexarelin-adrenal" /> |
| Narrowing of the heart's arteries | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Seen in rat hearts outside the body.<Cite id="bodart-2002-hexarelin-cd36" /> |
| Effects of too much growth hormone, such as fluid build-up or high blood sugar | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Listed for growth hormone medicines; not studied for long hexarelin use.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-cjc1295-briefing" /> |
| Infection or impurities from the product | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Not studied; a known risk of any unsterile injection.<Cite id="nhs-cellulitis" /> |

</Compare>

<InDetail title="Why these risks are theorised">

Hexarelin raises growth hormone, and in children IGF-1 rose with it.<Cite id="laron-1995-hexarelin-children" /> For approved growth hormone medicines, the FDA lists risks including fluid retention, high blood sugar and diabetes, raised pressure in the skull, and a higher risk of tumours.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-cjc1295-briefing" /> In the 16-week study, IGF-1 did not change, so whether these risks apply is unknown.<Cite id="rahim-1998-hexarelin-long-term" /> CD36 has been linked both to protecting the heart in animals and to narrowing the heart's arteries in rat hearts, so its heart effects could cut both ways.<Cite id="mao-2014-hexarelin-cardiovascular-review,bodart-2002-hexarelin-cd36" /> See [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/).

</InDetail>

</Section>

<ProductCheck />

### When to stop and get help

<Callout type="stop" title="Stop and get help if" id="stop-now">
- you get chest pain, or a racing or uneven heartbeat that does not settle;
- the injection site becomes hot, red, swollen or painful, or you get a fever (possible infection);
- you get hives, swelling of the face or throat, or difficulty breathing (an allergic reaction).

If it is severe, call your local emergency number.
</Callout>

<Cite id="nhs-chest-pain,nhs-heart-palpitations,nhs-cellulitis,nhs-anaphylaxis,bodart-2002-hexarelin-cd36" />

<WarningSigns only="general,injection,allergic,cardiovascular" />

<InDetail title="Checks that make sense, and who should be careful">

A doctor can help you decide which checks fit you (see [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/)):

- **Blood sugar**, especially with diabetes, because too much growth hormone can raise it.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-cjc1295-briefing" />
- **Your heart**, if you have heart disease, because of the heart effects seen in studies.<Cite id="bodart-2002-hexarelin-cd36,imazio-2002-hexarelin-cardiomyopathy" />
- **Cancer history**, because growth hormone medicines carry a warning about tumours.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-cjc1295-briefing" />
- **Anti-doping rules**, if you compete: it is banned at all times.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en" />

For injecting, see [sterile technique](/en/safety/sterile-technique/).

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Legal routes, and reporting side effects">

### The legal routes, country by country

No hexarelin product is approved in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia, so there is nothing a doctor can [prescribe](/en/legal-routes/#prescription) as an approved medicine, and [off-label use](/en/legal-routes/#off-label) applies only to medicines that are approved.<Cite id="openfda-drugsfda-search-stubs-b-2026-10-23,fda-off-label-use" /> [US compounding](/en/legal-routes/#us-compounding) has its own rules on which raw ingredients a pharmacy may use.<Cite id="usc-21-353a" /> Our page on [treatment routes](/en/legal-routes/) explains the other legal routes in each country and their limits: [UK "specials"](/en/legal-routes/#uk-specials), [Canada's Special Access Program](/en/legal-routes/#canada-special-access), [Australia's Special Access Scheme](/en/legal-routes/#australia-sas), [importing for yourself](/en/legal-routes/#personal-import) and [clinical trials](/en/legal-routes/#trials). Each has strict conditions, and none turns an unapproved product into an approved one. No clinical trial of hexarelin is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov or in the Australian and New Zealand registry.<Cite id="ctgov-search-status-b-2026-10-23,au-anzctr-search-status-b-2026-10-23" />

### Report side effects

Patients can report a side effect themselves, not only doctors. In the US, report to [FDA MedWatch](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medwatch-fda-safety-information-and-adverse-event-reporting-program); in the UK, to the [Yellow Card scheme](https://yellowcard.mhra.gov.uk/); in Canada, to Health Canada's [Canada Vigilance Program](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medeffect-canada/adverse-reaction-reporting.html); in Australia, to the [TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/safety/report-problem/report-adverse-event-or-safety-problem).<Cite id="fda-medwatch,mhra-yellow-card,ca-hc-medeffect-reporting,ca-hc-canada-vigilance,au-tga-report-adverse-event" /> For Ireland and New Zealand, see [our notes page](/en/legal/ireland-and-new-zealand/).
