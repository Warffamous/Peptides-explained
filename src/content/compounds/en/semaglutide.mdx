<Section id="what-it-is">

<AuthorisedProducts />

Semaglutide is a prescription medicine. In the EU it is authorised as Ozempic (weekly injection for type 2 diabetes, since 8 February 2018), Rybelsus (daily tablet for type 2 diabetes, since 3 April 2020) and Wegovy (weight management: weekly injection since 6 January 2022, daily tablet since 14 July 2026).<Cite id="ema-epar-ozempic,ema-epar-rybelsus,ema-epar-wegovy,ema-wegovy-procedural-steps" /> The marketing authorisation holder is Novo Nordisk A/S.

<Callout type="note" title="Why this page looks different">
Most compounds on this site are experimental. Semaglutide is an authorised medicine with large trials behind it. It is here because its name is also used for vials sold online as research chemicals, and because it is often lumped together with experimental peptides, which misleads in both directions (see below).
</Callout>

### What the molecule is

Semaglutide is a chain of 31 amino acids. Twenty-nine are the same as in human GLP-1 (7–37), the active form of a hormone the gut releases after a meal; the EU label calls this 94% sequence homology.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> The chain starts <Seq>His-Aib-Glu-Gly-Thr-Phe</Seq>.

<SequenceView />

Three changes make the hormone last a week. Positions use the GLP-1 (7–37) numbering of the literature and the US label, in which the first histidine is position 7.<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic,lau-2015-semaglutide-discovery,pubchem-semaglutide" />

- **Position 8:** alanine is replaced by Aib (2-aminoisobutyric acid), which stabilises the peptide against the enzyme DPP-4.
- **Position 34:** lysine is replaced by arginine, so only one lysine is left for the side chain.
- **Position 26:** that lysine carries two short hydrophilic spacers, a glutamic-acid linker and an 18-carbon fatty diacid. This part binds to albumin in the blood.

The formula is C187H291N45O59 and the molecular mass <Q>4113.58 g/mol</Q>; the US label states that the peptide backbone is produced by yeast fermentation.<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic" />

### Where it came from

In 1987 three groups showed that a gut fragment of proglucagon releases insulin: in the pig pancreas, the rat pancreas and <Claim level="human-limited">seven human volunteers, in whom an infusion raised insulin and lowered glucose and glucagon</Claim>.<Cite id="holst-1987-glp1,mojsov-1987-glp1,kreymann-1987-glp1" /> In 1993 <Claim level="human-limited">a GLP-1 infusion normalised fasting glucose in 10 people with type 2 diabetes, and insulin fell back once glucose was normal</Claim>.<Cite id="nauck-1993-glp1-type2" /> But the natural hormone lasts about 1–2 minutes, because DPP-4 cuts it and the kidneys clear it.<Cite id="muller-2019-glp1-review" /> In a study of 8 people with type 2 diabetes and 8 healthy volunteers, most of the GLP-1 measured in blood 30 minutes after an injection under the skin was already the cut, largely inactive form.<Cite id="deacon-1995-glp1-degradation,muller-2019-glp1-review" /> An earlier analogue, liraglutide, used a fatty-acid side chain and was given daily; semaglutide was designed for stronger albumin binding and full stability against breakdown.<Cite id="lau-2015-semaglutide-discovery" />

### Injection and tablet

Peptides are digested in the gut, so most are injected. Semaglutide tablets contain an absorption enhancer, SNAC (salcaprozate sodium); <Claim level="human-limited">in dogs and people, absorption took place in the stomach, next to the dissolving tablet</Claim>.<Cite id="buckley-2018-oral-semaglutide" /> Only about 1–2% of the dose is absorbed, varying widely between people, so tablet strengths are much higher than injection strengths; the EU label states that the effect of switching between tablets and injections cannot easily be predicted.<Cite id="ema-epar-rybelsus" />

### Why "semaglutide" is not one thing

The evidence on this page belongs to manufactured products: a known substance in a pen or tablet that delivers a fixed amount, with a label updated as new risks are found. A vial sold online as "research semaglutide" shares the name, but none of the guarantees that let that evidence apply: known content, a fixed dose, sterility and an updated label.<Cite id="fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" />

Lumping authorised GLP-1 medicines together with grey-market peptides misleads in two directions:

- **It flatters the experimental peptides.** "Ozempic works and it is a peptide, so the other peptides probably work too." Semaglutide's evidence comes from its own trials in tens of thousands of people, not from being a peptide; most peptides sold alongside it have no comparable trial.
- **It undersells the medicine and oversells the vial.** "It is all grey-market peptide stuff" can put people off a medicine that, in specific groups, lowered the combined risk of heart attack, stroke or cardiovascular death, and of serious kidney events, in large trials, while inviting them to treat an online vial as if it carried that safety record. It does not.

<Section id="mechanism">

Semaglutide switches on the GLP-1 receptor and keeps doing so for days. Most effects, wanted or unwanted, follow from where that receptor is found.

<Mechanism />

### What each step rests on

**Staying in the blood.** The EU label gives a half-life of about one week and more than 99% albumin binding, measured in people.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> The chain is mechanical: albumin is too big for the kidneys to filter, so only the small free fraction is cleared, and Aib at position 8 protects against DPP-4. Semaglutide therefore stays in the circulation for about five to seven weeks after the last dose; side effects do not end the day you stop.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy,ema-epar-rybelsus" />

**Receptor and cAMP.** Semaglutide is a direct agonist at the GLP-1 receptor, with an affinity measured in cell systems.<Cite id="chembl-semaglutide,lau-2015-semaglutide-discovery" /> The activated receptor switches on adenylate cyclase, so cAMP rises inside the cell; in beta cells, cAMP acts through PKA and Epac2 to increase insulin release.<Cite id="muller-2019-glp1-review" /> <Claim level="in-vitro">This chain is established in cell lines and animal islets</Claim>, not traced step by step in human pancreas.

**Why the insulin effect depends on glucose.** cAMP amplifies an insulin release that glucose has already triggered; it does not start one. <Claim level="human-controlled">In a 12-week, manufacturer-funded, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 75 people with type 2 diabetes, semaglutide increased insulin secretion and lowered glucagon after meals</Claim>.<Cite id="kapitza-2017-beta-cell" /> The EU label adds that during hypoglycaemia semaglutide reduces insulin secretion and does not impair glucagon release.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> That is why its own risk of low blood sugar is low, and why much of that protection is lost when it is combined with medicines that raise insulin regardless of glucose.

**Appetite.** <Claim level="animal">In rodent experiments by the manufacturer, semaglutide reached the brainstem and hypothalamus through regions where the blood–brain barrier is open, and activated areas thought to be involved in ending a meal</Claim>.<Cite id="gabery-2020-semaglutide-brain" /> <Claim level="human-controlled">In a manufacturer-funded, double-blind, randomised trial in 72 adults with obesity, 20 weeks of semaglutide cut energy intake at a free-choice lunch by 35% compared with placebo, with less hunger and fewer cravings</Claim>.<Cite id="friedrichsen-2021-appetite" /> The eating effect is measured in people; the brain sites are inferred from animals.

**The stomach.** GLP-1 slows stomach emptying.<Cite id="muller-2019-glp1-review" /> The EU label describes a minor delay early after a meal, and no clinically relevant effect at the <Q>2.4 mg</Q> strength, probably because of tolerance; the appetite trial found no clear delay at week 20.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy,friedrichsen-2021-appetite" />

### What the mechanism does not explain

The heart and kidney benefits are real in the populations studied but only partly explained: the EU label calls the cardiovascular benefit likely multifactorial, in part driven by weight loss and by effects on blood pressure, lipids, glucose and inflammation, and states that "the exact mechanism of cardiovascular risk reduction has not been established".<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

</Section>

<Section id="evidence">

<StudyTable />

Large trials do not make every claim about semaglutide true. Below is what each key study tested, in whom, and what it could not show. (The 1987 physiology study in the table explains the mechanism; it is not evidence that the medicine works.<Cite id="kreymann-1987-glp1" />)

### Type 2 diabetes and the heart

<Claim level="established">Semaglutide lowers HbA1c in type 2 diabetes</Claim> (in PIONEER 1, a double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial in 703 adults, at all three tablet strengths over 26 weeks).<Cite id="aroda-2019-pioneer-1" /> But lower glucose does not by itself mean fewer heart attacks. SUSTAIN-6 randomised 3,297 people with type 2 diabetes at high cardiovascular risk to weekly semaglutide (<Q>0.5 mg</Q> or <Q>1.0 mg</Q>) or placebo for 104 weeks: cardiovascular death, non-fatal heart attack or non-fatal stroke occurred in 6.6% versus 8.9% (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.58–0.95).<Cite id="marso-2016-sustain-6" /> It was designed to rule out harm, so the favourable result was encouraging rather than conclusive. PIONEER 6 did the same check for the tablet in 3,183 people over a median 15.9 months (3.8% versus 4.8%; HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.57–1.11): no large excess risk, but no proof of benefit.<Cite id="husain-2019-pioneer-6" />

SOUL then tested benefit directly in 9,650 people with type 2 diabetes and atherosclerotic disease, chronic kidney disease or both, over a mean 47.5 months: <Claim level="human-controlled">major cardiovascular events occurred in 12.0% with oral semaglutide and 13.8% with placebo (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.77–0.96)</Claim>; the confirmatory secondary outcomes, including a kidney composite, did not differ significantly.<Cite id="mcguire-2025-soul" />

### Kidneys

FLOW randomised 3,533 people with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease to weekly semaglutide or placebo (median follow-up 3.4 years). <Claim level="human-controlled">Kidney failure, a fall in kidney function of at least half, or kidney or cardiovascular death was 24% less frequent with semaglutide (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.66–0.88)</Claim>.<Cite id="perkovic-2024-flow" /> It stopped early at a prespecified interim analysis. Trials stopped early for benefit tend to overstate the effect, although large overestimates are mainly seen in trials with fewer than 500 events; FLOW had 741.<Cite id="bassler-2010-truncated-trials,perkovic-2024-flow" />

### Weight management, and what happens after stopping

STEP 1 randomised 1,961 adults with obesity, or overweight with a weight-related condition, and no diabetes, to weekly semaglutide (<Q>2.4 mg</Q>) or placebo for 68 weeks, with lifestyle support. <Claim level="established">Mean weight fell by 14.9% versus 2.4%</Claim>; half of the semaglutide group lost 15% or more, while about one in seven lost less than 5%.<Cite id="wilding-2021-step-1" />

<Claim level="human-controlled">Much of the effect is lost when treatment stops</Claim>. In STEP 4, a double-blind phase 3 trial, 803 people who had reached the target dose during a 20-week run-in on semaglutide (average loss 10.6%) were randomised to continue (a further 7.9% lost over 48 weeks) or switch to placebo (6.9% regained).<Cite id="rubino-2021-step-4" /> In the STEP 1 extension, of 327 people followed for a year after stopping, those who had taken semaglutide regained about two-thirds of the weight they had lost (a net loss of 5.6% remained), and most measures of blood pressure, lipids and glucose moved back towards baseline.<Cite id="wilding-2022-step-1-extension" />

The daily tablet for weight management was tested in OASIS 4, a manufacturer-funded, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 307 adults without diabetes: mean weight change at 64 weeks was −13.6% versus −2.2%, with gastrointestinal adverse events in 74.0% versus 42.2%.<Cite id="wharton-2025-oasis-4" /> The EMA cites the same trial, as phase 3, for its tablet recommendation.<Cite id="ema-2026-wegovy-tablets" />

### Heart outcomes without diabetes

SELECT randomised 17,604 adults with cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity, but no diabetes, for a mean 39.8 months. <Claim level="human-controlled">Cardiovascular death, non-fatal heart attack or non-fatal stroke occurred in 6.5% with semaglutide and 8.0% with placebo (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.72–0.90)</Claim>; 16.6% stopped because of adverse events, against 8.2% on placebo.<Cite id="lincoff-2023-select" /> It does not show that semaglutide prevents a first heart attack in people without heart or vessel disease.

### Newer uses, and a negative result

In the US, Wegovy has accelerated approval (2025) for the liver disease MASH with moderate to advanced scarring but no cirrhosis; continued approval depends on confirmatory trial results.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy" /> ESSENCE, a manufacturer-funded, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial, reported a planned interim analysis of its first 800 participants at 72 weeks: on biopsy, steatohepatitis resolved without worsening fibrosis in 62.9% versus 34.3%, and fibrosis improved without worsening steatohepatitis in 36.8% versus 22.4%. The trial continues for clinical liver outcomes.<Cite id="sanyal-2025-essence" /> In people who already had MASH cirrhosis, a smaller phase 2 trial (71 adults, 48 weeks) found no significant improvement in fibrosis or steatohepatitis.<Cite id="loomba-2023-semaglutide-cirrhosis" /> The indication is not in the EU label.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

Health-record studies had suggested less dementia on GLP-1 medicines, but in the double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 evoke and evoke+ trials (3,808 people with early Alzheimer's disease) <Claim level="human-controlled">oral semaglutide did not slow clinical decline over 104 weeks</Claim>.<Cite id="cummings-2026-evoke" />

### Who paid for the evidence

Every pivotal trial here was funded by the manufacturer, with many company authors. That is normal for medicines and does not make the results false, but the questions asked were mostly the manufacturer's. A crude PubMed count on 20 October 2026 found 118 randomised-trial records on semaglutide (of 311) with no Novo Nordisk-affiliated author; some of these may still be company-funded. The main recent safety questions, NAION, thyroid cancer and suicidal thoughts, were tested by independent academic groups and regulators.<Cite id="hathaway-2024-naion,bezin-2023-thyroid-cancer,pasternak-2024-thyroid-cancer,ema-prac-2024-04-suicidal-thoughts" />

<Section id="unknowns">

- **Use beyond a few years.** The longest outcome trials averaged about four years; in June 2024 Zorginstituut Nederland wrote that, in the weight-management evidence it assessed, there were no data on use beyond two years.<Cite id="mcguire-2025-soul,zin-2024-wegovy-pharmacotherapeutic-report" />
- **How to stop.** Weight returns after stopping, and no trial has tested a way of tapering off while keeping the benefit.<Cite id="zin-2024-wegovy-pharmacotherapeutic-report,wilding-2022-step-1-extension" />
- **NAION.** The size of the risk outside type 2 diabetes, who is most at risk, and whether it depends on dose or speed of weight loss.<Cite id="ema-prac-2025-06-naion" />
- **Thyroid cancer.** Registry studies disagree and follow-up is short; the rodent finding has neither been shown to apply to people nor fully ruled out.<Cite id="bezin-2023-thyroid-cancer,pasternak-2024-thyroid-cancer" />
- **Muscle and function.** How much lean mass lost is muscle, and whether it matters for strength and falls in older people.<Cite id="neeland-2024-lean-mass" />
- **Pregnancy.** Animal studies showed harm to the embryo; human data are limited.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **The <Q>7.2 mg</Q> Wegovy pen.** More altered skin sensation than lower strengths; experience is still short.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **Who benefits most.** Zorginstituut could not determine which patients with obesity gain the most health.<Cite id="zin-2024-wegovy-not-reimbursed" />

### European Union

All three are authorised through the centralised procedure, valid in every member state; Wegovy tablets and a higher-strength pen were added in July 2026.<Cite id="ema-epar-ozempic,ema-epar-rybelsus,ema-epar-wegovy,ema-wegovy-procedural-steps" /> The EMA safety committee (PRAC) concluded that the evidence does not support a causal link with suicidal thoughts (April 2024);<Cite id="ema-prac-2024-04-suicidal-thoughts" /> that labels should warn about residual stomach content before anaesthesia, although no causal link with aspiration was established (July 2024);<Cite id="ema-prac-2024-07-aspiration" /> and that NAION is a very rare side effect (June 2025).<Cite id="ema-prac-2025-06-naion" />

### Netherlands

The EU authorisations apply directly; semaglutide is prescription-only. Reimbursement is uneven:<Cite id="fk-semaglutide,fk-regeling-zorgverzekering-bijlage-2" />

- **Ozempic and Rybelsus** are reimbursed for type 2 diabetes only, under the conditions of Bijlage 2 of the Regeling zorgverzekering (for example a BMI of at least 30 when other medicines are not enough, or a very high cardiovascular risk).
- **Wegovy** is not reimbursed. In July 2024 Zorginstituut Nederland called it effective for weight loss but advised against reimbursement, citing unclear health gain, long-term effect and way of stopping, and costs of up to €1.3 billion a year.<Cite id="zin-2024-wegovy-not-reimbursed" /> A reassessment, first for people with a BMI of 30 or more with related disease or a BMI of 35 or more, had its first scientific discussion on 5 October 2026; consultation is expected in early November 2026.<Cite id="zin-wegovy-reassessment,zin-2025-obesity-staged-approach" />

This gap has real costs, and the reasons for it are also real. An authorised weight-management medicine has existed since 2022, with outcome-trial evidence in people with heart disease since 2023,<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy,lincoff-2023-select" /> but people who cannot pay do not get it, while the CBG notes that these medicines are increasingly used as slimming aids without a prescription.<Cite id="cbg-glp1-position" /> Whether non-reimbursement pushes people towards online products has not been measured. Equally, the Zorginstituut's concerns about long-term evidence, stopping, and a budget impact that could displace other care are legitimate.

In March 2023 the CBG warned of a long Ozempic shortage and against slimming use without a doctor; it later noted that it was unclear whether off-label use had caused the shortage, which ended on 2 December 2024.<Cite id="cbg-ozempic-shortage" /> In June 2024, reporting EMA recommendations, it wrote that slimming use by people without obesity had made shortages much worse, and that GLP-1 medicines sold online may be falsified.<Cite id="cbg-2024-glp1-shortage-recommendations" />

### Germany

Authorised through the EU procedure.<Cite id="ema-epar-ozempic" /> The BfArM's shortage advisory board lifted its Ozempic recommendation on 18 March 2026.<Cite id="bfarm-glp1-shortage-archive" /> Falsified German-labelled Ozempic pens found in 2023 contained insulin, not semaglutide.<Cite id="bfarm-ozempic-falsification" />

### Belgium

Authorised through the EU procedure; prescription-only.<Cite id="ema-epar-ozempic" /> Additional Belgian prescribing or reimbursement rules are not covered, because we could not verify them on the regulator's website on 20 October 2026.

### United Kingdom

The MHRA lists Ozempic, Rybelsus and Wegovy as licensed, prescription-only medicines; GLP-1 products supplied as powder in vials are not authorised.<Cite id="mhra-glp1-what-you-need-to-know" /> In 2023 it reported seizing 369 potentially fake Ozempic pens, and hospital admissions suggesting that some fakes contained insulin.<Cite id="mhra-2023-fake-weight-loss-pens" />

### United States

The FDA approved Ozempic on 5 December 2017, Rybelsus on 20 September 2019, Wegovy injection on 4 June 2021 and Wegovy tablets on 22 December 2025, later adding cardiovascular risk reduction for Wegovy (March 2024) and a kidney indication for Ozempic (January 2025).<Cite id="fda-drugsatfda-ozempic,fda-drugsatfda-rybelsus,fda-drugsatfda-wegovy,fda-drugsatfda-wegovy-tablets" />

The US and EU labels differ. US labels carry a boxed warning on rodent thyroid C-cell tumours and contraindicate use with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2; EU labels list only hypersensitivity.<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic,ema-epar-wegovy" /> The US Wegovy label dropped its suicidal-behaviour warning in early 2026, and the US Ozempic and Wegovy labels we checked (revised May and June 2026) do not list NAION.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy,fda-label-ozempic" />

From 2022 semaglutide injections were on the FDA shortage list, which let pharmacies compound copies. The FDA declared the shortage resolved on 21 February 2025; the grace period ended for pharmacies after a court ruling on 24 April 2025 and for outsourcing facilities on 22 May 2025.<Cite id="fda-glp1-compounding-policy" /> In April 2026 the FDA proposed not to put semaglutide on the list of bulk substances that outsourcing facilities may compound from; the comment period was extended to 30 July 2026, and no final decision had been published by 20 October 2026.<Cite id="fda-2026-503b-glp1-exclusion-proposal,fr-2026-12937-503b-comment-extension" /> By 31 May 2026 the FDA had 990 adverse-event reports for compounded semaglutide, likely an undercount.<Cite id="fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" />

### Sport (WADA)

Semaglutide is not on the 2026 Prohibited List, and the S0 class for non-approved substances does not apply to an approved medicine.<Cite id="wada-prohibited-list" /> It has been on the WADA Monitoring Program since 2024: laboratories look for it to detect misuse patterns, but it is not banned.<Cite id="wada-2026-monitoring-program" />

<Section id="risks">

Unless stated otherwise, risks and frequencies come from the EU product information and the trials named.

### Stomach and gut

In the Wegovy weight trials (STEP 1–4, 68 weeks), nausea occurred in 43.9% with semaglutide versus 16.1% with placebo, diarrhoea in 29.7% versus 15.9%, vomiting in 24.5% versus 6.3% and constipation in 24.2% versus 11.1%. Most episodes were mild to moderate and came while the dose was being raised; 4.3% stopped treatment permanently because of them. Dehydration from vomiting or diarrhoea has rarely worsened kidney function, and intestinal obstruction has been reported after authorisation.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

### Gallbladder and pancreas

Gallstones were reported in 1.6% versus 1.1%, and gallbladder inflammation in 0.6% versus 0.3%.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> <Claim level="human-controlled">A meta-analysis of 76 randomised trials of GLP-1 medicines (103,371 participants) found more gallbladder and bile-duct disease (relative risk 1.37), more so in weight-loss trials, at higher doses and with longer use</Claim>.<Cite id="he-2022-gallbladder" /> Adjudicated acute pancreatitis occurred in 0.2% versus less than 0.1% in the weight trials, and 0.2% versus 0.3% in SELECT; semaglutide should be stopped if pancreatitis is suspected and not restarted if it is confirmed.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

### Eyes: diabetic retinopathy

In SUSTAIN-6, diabetic retinopathy complications (bleeding in the eye, blindness, or the need for laser or eye injections) occurred in 3.0% versus 1.8% (HR 1.76), mainly in insulin-treated people with existing retinopathy.<Cite id="marso-2016-sustain-6,ema-epar-wegovy" /> <Claim level="human-limited">A manufacturer-funded post hoc analysis of SUSTAIN-6 suggested that most of the excess may be due to the size and speed of the fall in HbA1c in the first 16 weeks</Claim>, an "early worsening" also seen with insulin.<Cite id="vilsboll-2018-retinopathy" /> The label notes that other mechanisms cannot be excluded.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

### Eyes: NAION

The PRAC classes NAION as very rare (up to 1 in 10,000 people): <Claim level="human-limited">large epidemiological studies in type 2 diabetes suggest roughly a doubling of risk, about one extra case per 10,000 person-years</Claim>.<Cite id="ema-prac-2025-06-naion" /> An earlier retrospective single-centre study of 1,689 matched patients found much higher hazard ratios (4.28 and 7.64), with few events and wide confidence intervals, in patients referred to a specialist eye service.<Cite id="hathaway-2024-naion" /> Neither the PRAC announcement nor the EU label proposes a mechanism.<Cite id="ema-prac-2025-06-naion,ema-epar-wegovy" />

### Low blood sugar

Semaglutide's own risk of hypoglycaemia is low because its insulin effect depends on glucose, but it is not zero: in STEP 2, a weight trial in people with type 2 diabetes, clinically significant low blood sugar occurred in 6.2% with semaglutide versus 2.5% with placebo, with and without a sulfonylurea. Insulin and sulfonylureas act regardless of glucose, so in combination blood sugar can fall too low; the label says this risk can be lowered by reducing the dose of those medicines.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

### Thyroid C-cell tumours in rodents, and why they may not carry over

In two-year studies, semaglutide caused thyroid C-cell tumours in rats and mice at clinically relevant exposures.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy,fda-label-ozempic" /> <Claim level="animal">Rodent C-cells carry GLP-1 receptors, and GLP-1 agonists stimulated calcitonin release and, over time, C-cell hyperplasia in rats and, less, in mice</Claim>. <Claim level="animal">Human and monkey C-cells had low receptor levels, the agonists did not switch on adenylate cyclase or calcitonin release in primates, and 20 months of liraglutide caused no C-cell hyperplasia in monkeys</Claim>.<Cite id="knudsen-2010-thyroid-c-cells" /> The EU label rates human relevance as low but not excluded.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

Human data are mixed: a French case-control study linked one to three years of GLP-1 medicines with more thyroid cancer, including medullary cancer, while a larger Scandinavian cohort found no clear increase over a mean 3.9 years but could not rule out a modest one. Both analysed GLP-1 medicines as a class rather than semaglutide alone; in the Scandinavian study, semaglutide made up about a third of use.<Cite id="bezin-2023-thyroid-cancer,pasternak-2024-thyroid-cancer" /> <Claim level="hypothesis">More thyroid check-ups in people on these medicines would find more small cancers and could inflate an observed risk</Claim>. The US contraindication covers a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma, or MEN 2, where the baseline risk is already high.<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic" />

### Anaesthesia, muscle and weight regain

Slower stomach emptying means food may remain in the stomach during general anaesthesia or deep sedation, and could be breathed into the lungs; cases have been reported, so tell the doctor about semaglutide before a procedure.<Cite id="ema-prac-2024-07-aspiration,ema-epar-wegovy" />

<Claim level="human-limited">In trials of GLP-1-based medicines, lean mass has made up from about 15% to 40–60% of the weight lost</Claim>. Lean mass also includes organs, bone and water, and MRI studies suggest the muscle change may fit the weight lost, but older or frail people may lose function; the source is a narrative review and the question is open.<Cite id="neeland-2024-lean-mass" />

In the STEP 1 extension, about two-thirds of the lost weight returned within a year of stopping, and most gains in blood pressure, lipids and glucose faded; the STEP 1 investigators see this as confirming that obesity is chronic.<Cite id="wilding-2022-step-1-extension,rubino-2021-step-4" />

### Mood, pregnancy and other effects

The PRAC concluded that the available evidence does not support a causal link with suicidal thoughts; if your mood changes on any medicine, tell your doctor.<Cite id="ema-prac-2024-04-suicidal-thoughts" />

In rats semaglutide harmed embryos, partly through a yolk-sac mechanism thought not to apply to humans; rabbits and monkeys had more pregnancy loss with marked maternal weight loss. The label says not to use it in pregnancy or while breastfeeding, and to stop at least two months before a planned pregnancy.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> Weight loss can improve fertility and so raise the chance of an unplanned pregnancy.<Cite id="fk-semaglutide" />

The label also lists a rise in heart rate (on average about 3 beats per minute), hair loss (2.5% versus 1.0%), headache, dizziness, tiredness, rare allergic reactions including anaphylaxis and angioedema, and, mainly at <Q>7.2 mg</Q>, altered skin sensation (21.6% versus 0.3% in STEP UP).<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

<Section id="harm-reduction">

For an authorised medicine, the legal route has the fewest unknowns. This section gives no doses: your prescriber decides the dose.

### The legal route in the Netherlands

Semaglutide needs a prescription from a doctor, usually your GP (huisarts) or a specialist, and comes from a pharmacy. Ozempic or Rybelsus may be reimbursed for diabetes under conditions; Wegovy is authorised for weight management but, as of 20 October 2026, basic health insurance does not pay for it.<Cite id="fk-semaglutide,zin-wegovy-reassessment" /> Dutch prescribing advice adds obesity medicines to a combined lifestyle programme (GLI), not instead of it.<Cite id="fk-semaglutide" /> A prescription brings a check that the medicine suits you, interaction checks and follow-up by a doctor and pharmacist;<Cite id="cbg-glp1-position" /> see [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/).

### Why an online "research" vial is a different product

The FDA has warned companies selling semaglutide falsely labelled "for research purposes" or "not for human consumption" to consumers, and calls such products of unknown quality. It also warns that some compounded products use salt forms (semaglutide sodium or acetate), which are different active ingredients from the one in the authorised medicines.<Cite id="fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" /> The MHRA states that powder-in-vial GLP-1 products are not authorised.<Cite id="mhra-glp1-what-you-need-to-know" /> The CBG's summary: you do not know what is in it, how to use it, or what it will do to your health.<Cite id="cbg-glp1-position" /> See [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/).

Vials also bring dosing errors that pens prevent. The FDA has reports of people taking five to 20 times the intended amount from multi-dose vials of compounded semaglutide, often from confusing milligrams, millilitres and syringe "units". Some needed hospital care; with a one-week half-life, overdose may need prolonged observation and treatment.<Cite id="fda-2024-compounded-semaglutide-dosing-errors" /> For multi-dose vials of compounded GLP-1 medicines, the FDA advises a new sterile needle and syringe every time, discarding a vial 28 days after first use because contamination risk rises after that, and not using a product that arrived warm.<Cite id="fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" /> See [sterile technique](/en/safety/sterile-technique/).

### Fake pens

Falsified Ozempic pens have been found in the EU, the UK and elsewhere since 2023; some contained insulin, which can cause dangerously low blood sugar.<Cite id="ema-2023-falsified-ozempic,bfarm-ozempic-falsification,mhra-2023-fake-weight-loss-pens,who-2024-falsified-semaglutide" />

- Get pens and tablets only from a pharmacy, on a prescription.
- Compare your pen with the picture and description in the package leaflet.
- Do not use a pen you suspect is fake, for example one that looks different from the leaflet; contact your pharmacist.

### Who should not use it, and interactions

- **Pregnancy, trying to conceive, breastfeeding:** animal harm to the embryo; stop at least two months before a planned pregnancy.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma, or MEN 2:** contraindicated in the US because of the rodent finding; not a formal EU contraindication, so discuss it.<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic,ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **Previous pancreatitis:** use with caution, because GLP-1 medicines are linked to acute pancreatitis. **Severe gastroparesis:** not recommended, because semaglutide slows the stomach further. **Uncontrolled or unstable diabetic retinopathy:** not recommended, because there is no experience in this group and a fast fall in glucose can temporarily worsen retinopathy. **Type 1 diabetes:** not studied; semaglutide never replaces insulin.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **Insulin or sulfonylureas (such as gliclazide):** higher risk of low blood sugar. **Other GLP-1 medicines:** do not combine.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **Tablets that need fast or precise absorption:** slower stomach emptying can change absorption; with semaglutide tablets levothyroxine exposure rose by 33%.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy,ema-epar-rybelsus" />
- **Warfarin or other coumarins, such as acenocoumarol:** cases of lower INR have been reported with acenocoumarol; the label advises frequent INR checks when starting.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

### Checks that make sense, and why

- **Eyes**, if you have diabetic retinopathy: a fast fall in glucose can temporarily worsen it, and the label asks for close monitoring.<Cite id="vilsboll-2018-retinopathy,ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **Blood glucose**, if you also use insulin or a sulfonylurea; **INR** on coumarins; **thyroid tests** on levothyroxine (see above).
- **Kidney function** after prolonged vomiting or diarrhoea, because dehydration can harm the kidneys.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

### When to stop and get help

<Callout type="stop" title="Get medical help now">
- **Severe, constant pain in the upper belly**, often into the back, with or without vomiting: possible pancreatitis. Stop semaglutide and get urgent care.
- **Sudden loss of vision or rapidly worsening eyesight, often in one eye:** possible NAION. Contact a doctor without delay.
- **Shaking, sweating, confusion or drowsiness after injecting a product from outside a pharmacy:** possibly insulin in a fake. Take something sugary if you can swallow safely; call 112 if symptoms are severe or the person becomes drowsy or confused.
- **Swelling of the face, lips or throat, or difficulty breathing:** possible allergic reaction. Call 112.
- **Vomiting so much you cannot keep fluids down, or passing very little urine:** risk of dehydration and kidney injury. Get same-day care.
</Callout>
