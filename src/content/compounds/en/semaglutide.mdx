<Section id="what-it-is">

<AuthorisedProducts />

Semaglutide is a prescription medicine. In the EU it is authorised as Ozempic (a weekly injection for type 2 diabetes, since 8 February 2018), Rybelsus (a daily tablet for type 2 diabetes, since 3 April 2020) and Wegovy (for weight management: a weekly injection since 6 January 2022 and a daily tablet since 14 July 2026).<Cite id="ema-epar-ozempic,ema-epar-rybelsus,ema-epar-wegovy,ema-wegovy-procedural-steps" /> The marketing authorisation holder for all of them is Novo Nordisk A/S.

<Callout type="note" title="Why this page looks different">
Most compounds on this site are experimental: no marketing authorisation, and little or no controlled human data. Semaglutide is the opposite. It is one of the most thoroughly tested medicines of the past decade. It is on this site because the name "semaglutide" is also used for powders and vials sold online as research chemicals, and because it is often lumped together with experimental peptides. That lumping misleads in both directions, as explained below.
</Callout>

### What the molecule is

Semaglutide is a chain of 31 amino acids. Twenty-nine of them are the same as in human GLP-1 (7–37), the active form of a hormone the gut releases after a meal; the EU label describes this as 94% sequence homology.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> The sequence starts <Seq>His-Aib-Glu-Gly-Thr-Phe</Seq>.

<SequenceView />

Three changes turn the hormone into a once-weekly medicine. The positions below use the GLP-1 (7–37) numbering of the literature and the US label, where the first histidine is position 7.<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic,lau-2015-semaglutide-discovery,pubchem-semaglutide" />

- **Position 8:** alanine is replaced by Aib (2-aminoisobutyric acid). This stabilises the peptide against the enzyme DPP-4, which otherwise cuts off the first two amino acids.
- **Position 34:** lysine is replaced by arginine. This leaves only one lysine in the chain, so the fatty-acid side chain can be attached at exactly one place.
- **Position 26:** the remaining lysine carries a side chain: two short hydrophilic spacers, a glutamic-acid linker and an 18-carbon fatty diacid. This part binds to albumin in the blood.

The molecular formula is C187H291N45O59 and the molecular mass is <Q>4113.58 g/mol</Q>. According to the US label, the peptide backbone is produced by yeast fermentation.<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic" />

### Where it came from

In 1987 three groups independently showed that a fragment of proglucagon made in the gut releases insulin: in the isolated pig pancreas, in the perfused rat pancreas and in seven human volunteers.<Cite id="holst-1987-glp1,mojsov-1987-glp1,kreymann-1987-glp1" /> In 1993 an intravenous GLP-1 infusion brought fasting glucose to normal within four hours in 10 people with poorly controlled type 2 diabetes.<Cite id="nauck-1993-glp1-type2" />

The natural hormone was useless as a medicine, because it does not last. Its half-life is about 1–2 minutes, because DPP-4 cuts it and the kidneys clear it.<Cite id="muller-2019-glp1-review" /> Thirty minutes after an injection under the skin in eight people with type 2 diabetes, most of the GLP-1-related material in their blood was already the cut-down fragment.<Cite id="deacon-1995-glp1-degradation" /> Drug designers therefore had to keep the receptor activity and remove the short life. An earlier analogue, liraglutide, already used a fatty-acid side chain to bind albumin and was given once a day. The aim with semaglutide was a once-weekly analogue with stronger albumin binding and full stability against breakdown.<Cite id="lau-2015-semaglutide-discovery" />

### Injection and tablet

Peptides are normally digested in the gut, so most are injected. Semaglutide tablets contain an absorption enhancer, SNAC (salcaprozate sodium). In studies in dogs and people, absorption happened in the stomach, right next to the dissolving tablet, with SNAC protecting the peptide locally.<Cite id="buckley-2018-oral-semaglutide" /> Even so, only about 1–2% of the dose is absorbed and the amount varies a lot between people.<Cite id="ema-epar-rybelsus" /> That is why tablet strengths are much higher than injection strengths, and why different products are not interchangeable milligram for milligram.<Cite id="fda-label-rybelsus" />

### Why "semaglutide" is not one thing

The evidence on this page belongs to manufactured products: a known substance made under controlled conditions, in a pen or tablet that delivers a fixed amount, with a label that lists the risks and is updated as new risks are found. A vial sold online as "research semaglutide" shares the name and nothing else that matters for safety. Regulators in the US, UK and the Netherlands have all warned about such products (see [If someone is going to do this anyway](#harm-reduction)).

Lumping authorised GLP-1 medicines together with grey-market peptides misleads in two directions:

- **It flatters the experimental peptides.** "Ozempic works, it is a peptide, so the other peptides probably work too." Semaglutide's evidence comes from its own trials in tens of thousands of people, not from being a peptide. Most peptides sold alongside it have no comparable human trial at all.
- **It undersells the authorised medicine, and oversells the vial.** "It is all grey-market peptide stuff" can put people off a medicine that, in specific groups, lowered the risk of heart attack, stroke and kidney failure in large randomised trials. And it invites people to treat an online vial as if it carried the authorised product's safety record. It does not.

<Section id="mechanism">

Semaglutide does one thing at the molecular level: it switches on the GLP-1 receptor, and keeps doing so for days. Almost everything else, wanted or unwanted, follows from where that receptor is found: in the pancreas, the brain and the gut.

<Mechanism />

### Step by step, and what each step rests on

**Staying in the blood.** The half-life of about one week is measured in people, not inferred: the EU label states an elimination half-life of about one week and more than 99% binding to plasma albumin.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> The causal chain is mechanical. Albumin is too big to be filtered by the kidneys, and a drug that spends most of its time stuck to albumin is filtered only in the small free fraction. Aib at position 8 stops DPP-4 from cutting the peptide. Together these turn a hormone that lasts minutes into one that lasts days. One practical consequence: semaglutide is still in the circulation for about five to seven weeks after the last dose, so side effects do not stop the day you stop.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy,ema-epar-rybelsus" />

**Receptor and second messenger.** The GLP-1 receptor sits on the cell surface. When it is activated it switches on adenylate cyclase, so the messenger molecule cAMP rises inside the cell. In beta cells, cAMP acts through two routes (PKA and Epac2) to increase insulin release.<Cite id="muller-2019-glp1-review" /> <Claim level="in-vitro">This signalling chain is established in cell lines and animal islets</Claim>; it has not been traced step by step in human pancreas, but the human outcome it predicts is well documented.

**Why the insulin effect depends on glucose.** cAMP does not start insulin release by itself. It amplifies a release that glucose has already triggered. So when blood glucose is high, insulin rises a lot; when it is normal, little extra insulin is released. <Claim level="human-controlled">In a 12-week randomised trial in 75 people with type 2 diabetes, semaglutide increased first- and second-phase insulin secretion and lowered glucagon after meals</Claim>.<Cite id="kapitza-2017-beta-cell" /> The EU label adds that during hypoglycaemia semaglutide reduces insulin secretion and does not impair glucagon release.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> This is the reason semaglutide on its own rarely causes low blood sugar, and also why that protection disappears when it is combined with medicines that force insulin release regardless of glucose (see [Risks](#risks)).

**Appetite.** <Claim level="animal">In rats and mice, labelled semaglutide reached the brainstem, hypothalamus and septal area through regions where the blood–brain barrier is open, and activated brain areas thought to be involved in ending a meal</Claim>.<Cite id="gabery-2020-semaglutide-brain" /> <Claim level="human-controlled">In 72 adults with obesity, 20 weeks of semaglutide cut energy intake at a free-choice lunch by 35% compared with placebo, with less hunger and fewer cravings</Claim>.<Cite id="friedrichsen-2021-appetite" /> So the effect on eating is measured in people, but the brain sites that produce it are inferred from animals.

**The stomach.** GLP-1 slows the emptying of the stomach after a meal.<Cite id="muller-2019-glp1-review" /> The EU label describes a minor delay in gastric emptying in the early post-meal phase, and notes that no clinically relevant effect on the rate of gastric emptying was seen at the <Q>2.4 mg</Q> strength, probably because of tolerance.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> This fits the appetite trial, which found no clear delay at week 20.<Cite id="friedrichsen-2021-appetite" /> The slowing is largest at the start and after a dose increase, which is also when nausea is most common.

### What the mechanism does not explain

The heart and kidney benefits in outcome trials are real in the populations studied, but their mechanism is not settled. The EU label says the cardiovascular benefit is likely multifactorial (weight, blood pressure, lipids, glucose and inflammation markers all improve) and that "the exact mechanism of cardiovascular risk reduction has not been established".<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> GLP-1 receptors are also found in blood vessels, the heart, the kidneys and immune cells, but which of these matter in people is unknown.

</Section>

<Section id="evidence">

<StudyTable />

Semaglutide has a large body of randomised evidence behind it. That does not make every claim made about it true. Below is what each key study tested, in whom, and what it could not show.

### Where the idea came from

The incretin idea came from academic physiology. <Claim level="human-limited">Kreymann and colleagues (London, 1987) found GLP-1 in the human gut, showed that blood levels rose after a meal, and showed that infusing it into seven volunteers raised insulin and lowered glucose and glucagon</Claim>.<Cite id="kreymann-1987-glp1" /> <Claim level="human-limited">Nauck and colleagues (1993) showed in 10 people with type 2 diabetes that the effect switched off once glucose reached normal</Claim>.<Cite id="nauck-1993-glp1-type2" /> These small studies explain the mechanism; they are not evidence that the medicine works.

### Type 2 diabetes: glucose first, then outcomes

<Claim level="established">Semaglutide lowers HbA1c in type 2 diabetes</Claim>; this is the basis of the Ozempic and Rybelsus authorisations. As one example, PIONEER 1 randomised 703 adults on diet and exercise alone to daily oral semaglutide or placebo for 26 weeks; all three tablet strengths lowered HbA1c, and body weight fell at the highest strength.<Cite id="aroda-2019-pioneer-1" />

Lowering glucose is not the same as preventing heart attacks, so diabetes medicines must show they do not increase cardiovascular risk. SUSTAIN-6 randomised 3,297 people with type 2 diabetes at high cardiovascular risk to weekly semaglutide (<Q>0.5 mg</Q> or <Q>1.0 mg</Q>) or placebo for 104 weeks. The combined outcome of cardiovascular death, non-fatal heart attack or non-fatal stroke occurred in 6.6% versus 8.9% (hazard ratio 0.74, 95% CI 0.58–0.95).<Cite id="marso-2016-sustain-6" /> The trial was designed to rule out harm, not to prove benefit, so its favourable result was encouraging rather than conclusive. The same trial found more diabetic retinopathy complications (see [Risks](#risks)).

PIONEER 6 did the same safety check for the tablet in 3,183 people, followed for a median of 15.9 months: 3.8% versus 4.8% had a major cardiovascular event (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.57–1.11). This ruled out a large excess risk but could not show a benefit.<Cite id="husain-2019-pioneer-6" /> The larger and longer SOUL trial then tested benefit directly: in 9,650 people with type 2 diabetes and atherosclerotic disease, chronic kidney disease or both, followed for a mean of 47.5 months, <Claim level="human-controlled">major cardiovascular events occurred in 12.0% with oral semaglutide and 13.8% with placebo (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.77–0.96)</Claim>. Its confirmatory kidney outcomes did not differ significantly.<Cite id="mcguire-2025-soul" />

### Kidneys: FLOW

FLOW randomised 3,533 people with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease to weekly semaglutide (<Q>1.0 mg</Q>) or placebo. <Claim level="human-controlled">The risk of kidney failure, a large fall in kidney function, or death from kidney or cardiovascular causes was 24% lower with semaglutide (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.66–0.88), and cardiovascular death was lower too (HR 0.71)</Claim>.<Cite id="perkovic-2024-flow" /> The trial was stopped early on the recommendation of a prespecified interim analysis. Trials stopped early tend to overstate how large an effect is.

### Weight management: STEP 1, STEP 4 and what happens after stopping

STEP 1 randomised 1,961 adults with obesity (or overweight with a weight-related condition) but no diabetes to weekly semaglutide (<Q>2.4 mg</Q>) or placebo for 68 weeks, both with lifestyle support. <Claim level="established">Mean body weight fell by 14.9% versus 2.4%</Claim>, and half of the semaglutide group lost 15% or more.<Cite id="wilding-2021-step-1" /> These numbers are averages: some people lost much more, and some very little.

<Claim level="human-controlled">The weight loss lasts only as long as treatment does</Claim>. In STEP 4, 803 people who had already lost weight during a 20-week run-in were randomised to continue or switch to placebo: over the next 48 weeks the first group lost a further 7.9% and the second regained 6.9%.<Cite id="rubino-2021-step-4" /> In a STEP 1 extension, 327 participants were followed for a year after stopping both the drug and the lifestyle programme. Those who had taken semaglutide regained about two-thirds of the weight they had lost, and blood pressure, lipids and glucose moved back towards baseline.<Cite id="wilding-2022-step-1-extension" />

A daily tablet for weight management was tested in OASIS 4: 307 adults with overweight or obesity, 64 weeks, mean weight change −13.6% versus −2.2% with placebo. Gastrointestinal adverse events occurred in 74.0% versus 42.2%.<Cite id="wharton-2025-oasis-4" /> The EMA describes the same trial (307 adults, 64 weeks) as the basis for its recommendation on Wegovy tablets, authorised in July 2026.<Cite id="ema-2026-wegovy-tablets,ema-wegovy-procedural-steps" />

### Heart outcomes without diabetes: SELECT

SELECT asked whether semaglutide prevents cardiovascular events in people with overweight or obesity who already have cardiovascular disease but not diabetes. It randomised 17,604 adults aged 45 or older and followed them for a mean of 39.8 months. <Claim level="human-controlled">Cardiovascular death, non-fatal heart attack or non-fatal stroke occurred in 6.5% with semaglutide and 8.0% with placebo (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.72–0.90)</Claim>. Adverse events led 16.6% of the semaglutide group to stop permanently, against 8.2% on placebo.<Cite id="lincoff-2023-select" /> The result applies to people with established heart or vessel disease; it does not show that semaglutide prevents a first heart attack in people without it.

### Newer uses, and a clear negative result

In the US, Wegovy received accelerated approval in 2025 for a liver disease, MASH, with moderate to advanced scarring, based on liver biopsy results.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy" /> In the interim analysis of the ESSENCE trial (800 people, 72 weeks), steatohepatitis resolved without worsening of fibrosis in 62.9% with semaglutide versus 34.3% with placebo.<Cite id="sanyal-2025-essence" /> Whether this prevents cirrhosis or liver deaths is still being tested, and the indication is not part of the EU Wegovy label as of 20 October 2026.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

Observational data had suggested that people taking GLP-1 medicines develop dementia less often. The randomised evoke and evoke+ trials tested this in 3,808 people with early Alzheimer's disease. <Claim level="human-controlled">Oral semaglutide did not slow clinical decline compared with placebo</Claim>.<Cite id="cummings-2026-evoke" /> This is a useful reminder: associations in health records are not the same as effects in a trial, even for a medicine that works well elsewhere.

### Who paid for the evidence

Every pivotal semaglutide trial listed here was funded by the manufacturer, and many of the authors were company employees. That is normal for medicines and does not make the results false, but it means the questions asked were mostly the manufacturer's questions. Many studies without company authors have since been published: a crude PubMed count on 20 October 2026 found 118 randomised-trial records on semaglutide with no Novo Nordisk-affiliated author. Independent academic groups and regulators, not the manufacturer, tested the most important safety questions of recent years: NAION, thyroid cancer and suicidal thoughts.<Cite id="hathaway-2024-naion,bezin-2023-thyroid-cancer,pasternak-2024-thyroid-cancer,ema-prac-2024-04-suicidal-thoughts" />

</Section>

<Section id="unknowns">

- **Use beyond a few years.** The longest outcome trials followed people for about four years on average. Obesity treatment is meant to be long-term, but Zorginstituut Nederland noted in 2024 that there were no weight-management data beyond two years.<Cite id="zin-2024-wegovy-pharmacotherapeutic-report,mcguire-2025-soul" />
- **How to stop.** Weight returns after stopping, and no trial has tested a way of tapering off while keeping the benefit.<Cite id="zin-2024-wegovy-pharmacotherapeutic-report,wilding-2022-step-1-extension" />
- **NAION.** The size of the risk outside type 2 diabetes, who is most at risk, and whether it depends on dose or speed of weight loss.<Cite id="ema-prac-2025-06-naion" />
- **Thyroid cancer.** Large registry studies disagree, and follow-up is short for a slow cancer. The rodent finding has not been shown to apply to people, nor fully ruled out.<Cite id="bezin-2023-thyroid-cancer,pasternak-2024-thyroid-cancer" />
- **Muscle and function.** How much of the lean mass lost is muscle, and whether it matters for strength and falls, especially in older people.<Cite id="neeland-2024-lean-mass" />
- **Pregnancy.** Animal studies showed harm to the embryo; human data are limited.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **Higher strengths.** The newer <Q>7.2 mg</Q> Wegovy pen showed more skin-sensation side effects (dysaesthesia) than the lower strengths; longer experience is still building up.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **Who benefits most.** Zorginstituut could not determine which patients gain the most health from treatment for obesity, which drives its reimbursement decisions.<Cite id="zin-2024-wegovy-not-reimbursed" />
- **Online products.** By definition, nothing is known about the content, purity or sterility of semaglutide sold outside the legal supply chain.

### European Union

Ozempic, Rybelsus and Wegovy are authorised through the EU centralised procedure, which makes them valid in every member state.<Cite id="ema-epar-ozempic,ema-epar-rybelsus,ema-epar-wegovy" /> In July 2026 the European Commission added Wegovy tablets and a higher-strength Wegovy pen.<Cite id="ema-wegovy-procedural-steps" /> The EMA safety committee (PRAC) has reviewed three signals in recent years:

- **Suicidal thoughts (April 2024):** the evidence does not support a causal link; no change to the product information was needed.<Cite id="ema-prac-2024-04-suicidal-thoughts,cbg-ozempic-shortage" />
- **Aspiration during anaesthesia (July 2024):** no causal link could be established, but a warning about residual stomach content was added because of the known slowing of gastric emptying.<Cite id="ema-prac-2024-07-aspiration" />
- **NAION (June 2025):** a very rare side effect of semaglutide, now in the label.<Cite id="ema-prac-2025-06-naion" />

### Netherlands

The EU authorisations apply directly. Semaglutide is prescription-only. Reimbursement is a separate decision, and here the picture is uneven as of 20 October 2026:<Cite id="fk-semaglutide,fk-regeling-zorgverzekering-bijlage-2" />

- **Ozempic and Rybelsus** are reimbursed for type 2 diabetes only, under the conditions of Bijlage 2 of the Regeling zorgverzekering (for example a BMI of at least 30 when other medicines are not enough, or a very high cardiovascular risk).
- **Wegovy** is not in the reimbursement system (GVS). In July 2024 Zorginstituut Nederland advised against reimbursement. It called the medicine effective for weight loss but said the health gain, the long-term effect and the right way to stop were unclear, and estimated costs of up to €1.3 billion a year.<Cite id="zin-2024-wegovy-not-reimbursed" /> A reassessment under a "staged approach" (first for people with a BMI of 30 or more with related disease, or a BMI of 35 or more) had its first scientific discussion on 5 October 2026; consultation is expected in early November 2026 and the later steps are not yet scheduled.<Cite id="zin-wegovy-reassessment,zin-2025-obesity-staged-approach" />

There is a real cost to this gap, and it runs both ways. An authorised medicine with outcome-trial evidence has been available since 2022, but people who cannot pay for it themselves do not get it, while unregulated online products are cheap and easy to find. At the same time, the Zorginstituut's concerns about long-term evidence, stopping and a budget impact that could displace other care are legitimate. Neither side of that trade-off is free.

The Netherlands also lived through a shortage. In March 2023 the CBG warned of a year-long global Ozempic shortage and against off-label use for weight loss without medical supervision; in its updates it noted that it was unclear whether off-label use had caused the shortage. The shortage was declared resolved on 2 December 2024.<Cite id="cbg-ozempic-shortage" /> In June 2024, reporting the recommendations of the EMA's shortage steering group, the CBG wrote that off-label slimming use by people without obesity had made the shortages much worse.<Cite id="cbg-2024-glp1-shortage-recommendations" /> The CBG's current position is that these medicines should be used only for their authorised purposes and under a doctor's care.<Cite id="cbg-glp1-position" /> The Health and Youth Care Inspectorate (IGJ) acts against illegal online sellers. In August 2026 it said it had received reports of harm to consumers from retatrutide and from falsified tirzepatide and semaglutide, and ordered a webshop without the required licences to stop trading in medicines containing these substances.<Cite id="igj-2026-webshop-order-glp1" />

### Germany

Authorised through the EU procedure.<Cite id="ema-epar-ozempic" /> During the shortage, the BfArM's advisory board on supply shortages recommended avoiding off-label use; it lifted its recommendation for Ozempic on 18 March 2026 because supply had stabilised.<Cite id="bfarm-glp1-shortage-archive" /> In 2023 falsified German-labelled Ozempic pens were found at wholesale level; laboratory analysis showed they contained insulin, not semaglutide.<Cite id="bfarm-ozempic-falsification" />

### Belgium

Authorised through the EU procedure and prescription-only.<Cite id="ema-epar-ozempic" /> Any additional Belgian prescribing or reimbursement rules are not covered here, because we could not verify them on the Belgian regulator's website on 20 October 2026.

### United Kingdom

The MHRA lists Ozempic, Rybelsus and Wegovy as licensed GLP-1 medicines, available only on prescription. Its public guidance says that products supplied as a powder in vials, to be mixed with liquid before injection, are not authorised and pose significant health risks.<Cite id="mhra-glp1-what-you-need-to-know" /> In 2023 the MHRA reported seizing 369 potentially fake Ozempic pens and hospital admissions with very low blood sugar, suggesting that some pens contained insulin.<Cite id="mhra-2023-fake-weight-loss-pens" />

### United States

The FDA approved Ozempic on 5 December 2017, Rybelsus on 20 September 2019, Wegovy injection on 4 June 2021 and Wegovy tablets on 22 December 2025. Later approvals added cardiovascular risk reduction for Wegovy (March 2024) and a kidney indication for Ozempic (January 2025).<Cite id="fda-drugsatfda-ozempic,fda-drugsatfda-rybelsus,fda-drugsatfda-wegovy,fda-drugsatfda-wegovy-tablets" />

The US labels differ from the EU labels in ways worth knowing. They carry a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumours in rodents, and they contraindicate use in people with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2; the EU labels list only hypersensitivity as a contraindication.<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic,ema-epar-wegovy" /> In early 2026 the warning on suicidal behaviour and ideation was removed from the US Wegovy label.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy" /> The US labels we checked (Ozempic, revised May 2026; Wegovy, revised June 2026) do not list NAION.<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic,fda-label-wegovy" />

From 2022 semaglutide injections were on the FDA shortage list, which allowed pharmacies to make ("compound") copies. The FDA declared the shortage resolved on 21 February 2025. The grace period for state-licensed pharmacies ended after a court ruling on 24 April 2025, and for outsourcing facilities on 22 May 2025.<Cite id="fda-glp1-compounding-policy" /> In April 2026 the FDA proposed that semaglutide should not be added to the list of substances outsourcing facilities may compound from bulk powder; the decision is not final.<Cite id="fda-2026-503b-glp1-exclusion-proposal" /> By 31 May 2026 the FDA had received 990 adverse-event reports linked to compounded semaglutide, and it notes that such events are likely under-reported.<Cite id="fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" />

### Sport (WADA)

<Section id="risks">

The risks below come from the EU product information and the trials, unless stated otherwise. Frequencies are from the trials named.

### Stomach and gut

Gastrointestinal effects are the most common side effects. In the four main Wegovy weight trials (STEP 1–4, 68 weeks), nausea occurred in 43.9% with semaglutide versus 16.1% with placebo, diarrhoea in 29.7% versus 15.9%, vomiting in 24.5% versus 6.3% and constipation in 24.2% versus 11.1%. Most episodes were mild to moderate and occurred while the dose was being increased; 4.3% stopped treatment because of them.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> Vomiting and diarrhoea can cause dehydration, which in rare cases has worsened kidney function.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> Intestinal obstruction has been reported after authorisation.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

### Gallbladder

Gallstones are more common with GLP-1 medicines. In STEP 1–4, gallstones were reported in 1.6% with semaglutide and 1.1% with placebo, and gallbladder inflammation in 0.6% versus 0.3%.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> <Claim level="human-controlled">A meta-analysis of 76 randomised trials of GLP-1 receptor agonists found a higher risk of gallbladder and bile-duct disease (relative risk 1.37), larger in weight-loss trials, at higher doses and with longer use</Claim>.<Cite id="he-2022-gallbladder" />

### Pancreas

Acute pancreatitis has been seen with GLP-1 receptor agonists. In the Wegovy weight trials, adjudicated acute pancreatitis occurred in 0.2% with semaglutide and less than 0.1% with placebo; in SELECT the figures were 0.2% and 0.3%. The label says to stop semaglutide if pancreatitis is suspected and not to restart it if confirmed.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

### Eyes: diabetic retinopathy

In SUSTAIN-6, diabetic retinopathy complications (bleeding in the eye, blindness, or the need for laser or eye injections) were more common with semaglutide (3.0% versus 1.8%; HR 1.76).<Cite id="marso-2016-sustain-6,ema-epar-wegovy" /> The excess was seen in insulin-treated people who already had retinopathy. <Claim level="human-limited">A post hoc analysis attributed most of it to the size and speed of the fall in HbA1c in the first 16 weeks</Claim>, a known "early worsening" effect also seen with insulin.<Cite id="vilsboll-2018-retinopathy" /> The leading explanation is therefore the rapid fall in glucose rather than a direct effect on the eye, but the label notes that other mechanisms cannot be excluded.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

### Eyes: NAION

NAION is a sudden loss of blood flow to the front of the optic nerve, causing painless vision loss in one eye. In June 2025 the PRAC concluded that it is a very rare side effect of semaglutide (up to 1 in 10,000 people). <Claim level="human-limited">Large epidemiological studies in type 2 diabetes suggest roughly a doubling of risk, about one extra case per 10,000 person-years of treatment</Claim>.<Cite id="ema-prac-2025-06-naion" /> An earlier single-centre study had found much larger hazard ratios (4.28 and 7.64), but it drew on patients referred to a specialist eye service, a group in which the risk can look larger than in the general population.<Cite id="hathaway-2024-naion" /> The EMA's conclusion does not identify a mechanism.

### Low blood sugar

On its own, semaglutide rarely causes hypoglycaemia, because its insulin effect depends on glucose (see [Mechanism](#mechanism)). Insulin and sulfonylureas do not depend on glucose: they push insulin up, or add it, regardless. Combined with them, semaglutide's extra glucose lowering can tip blood sugar too low, and the label advises considering a lower dose of the insulin or sulfonylurea.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

### Thyroid C-cell tumours in rodents, and why that may not carry over

In two-year studies in rats and mice, semaglutide caused thyroid C-cell tumours at exposures similar to those in patients.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy,fda-label-ozempic" /> The proposed mechanism is specific to rodents. <Claim level="animal">Rat and mouse C-cells carry GLP-1 receptors; GLP-1 agonists stimulated calcitonin release and, over time, C-cell hyperplasia in rats and, to a lesser extent, mice</Claim>. <Claim level="animal">Human and monkey C-cells had low GLP-1 receptor levels, GLP-1 agonists did not switch on adenylate cyclase or calcitonin release in primates, and liraglutide given to monkeys for 20 months did not cause C-cell hyperplasia</Claim>.<Cite id="knudsen-2010-thyroid-c-cells" /> The EU label therefore rates the human relevance as low but not completely excluded.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

Human data are reassuring but not final. A French case-control study found more thyroid cancer, including medullary cancer, after one to three years of GLP-1 drugs; a larger Scandinavian cohort found no clear increase over a mean of 3.9 years, though it could not rule out a modest one.<Cite id="bezin-2023-thyroid-cancer,pasternak-2024-thyroid-cancer" /> Neither looked at semaglutide alone. <Claim level="hypothesis">People on these medicines may also have their thyroid examined more often, which would find more small cancers and inflate an observed risk</Claim>. This is why the US label contraindicates semaglutide in people with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2, whose baseline risk is already high.<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic" />

### Anaesthesia and aspiration

Because semaglutide can slow stomach emptying, food may still be in the stomach during general anaesthesia or deep sedation, and could be breathed into the lungs. Cases of pulmonary aspiration have been reported. The PRAC could not establish a causal link but added a warning, and asked patients to tell the doctor about their GLP-1 medicine before a planned procedure.<Cite id="ema-prac-2024-07-aspiration,ema-epar-wegovy" />

### Muscle and lean mass

Any large weight loss includes some lean mass. <Claim level="human-limited">Across trials of GLP-1-based medicines, lean mass has made up anywhere from about 15% to 40–60% of the weight lost</Claim>. Lean mass is not the same as muscle: it includes organs, bone and water. MRI studies suggest the muscle changes may be roughly what is expected for the weight lost, with better muscle quality, but older people and those at risk of frailty may lose function.<Cite id="neeland-2024-lean-mass" /> This is a narrative review, not a trial, and the question is still open.

### Weight regain after stopping

The STEP 1 investigators concluded that their findings confirm obesity is a chronic condition. After stopping semaglutide, most of the lost weight returns within a year, and the improvements in blood pressure, lipids and glucose fade with it.<Cite id="wilding-2022-step-1-extension,rubino-2021-step-4" /> This is not a side effect of the medicine, but it matters for anyone deciding whether to start.

### Mood and suicidal thoughts

Reports of suicidal thoughts led to an EU review. In April 2024 the PRAC concluded that the evidence does not support a causal link.<Cite id="ema-prac-2024-04-suicidal-thoughts" /> If your mood changes on any medicine, tell your doctor.

### Pregnancy and breastfeeding

In rats, semaglutide harmed embryos, partly through effects on the yolk sac that may not apply to humans; in rabbits and monkeys there was more pregnancy loss, together with marked weight loss in the mothers. Human data are limited. The EU label says not to use semaglutide in pregnancy or while breastfeeding, and to stop at least two months before a planned pregnancy because of its long half-life.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> Weight loss can also improve fertility, which raises the chance of an unplanned pregnancy.<Cite id="fk-semaglutide" />

### Other label-listed effects

Other effects listed in the EU label include a small rise in heart rate (on average about 3 beats per minute), hair loss (2.5% versus 1.0%), headache, dizziness, tiredness, rare allergic reactions including anaphylaxis and angioedema, and, mainly at the <Q>7.2 mg</Q> strength, altered skin sensation (21.6% versus 0.3% with placebo in STEP UP).<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

<Section id="harm-reduction">

For an authorised medicine, the legal route is the one with the fewest unknowns. This section explains that route, why the online alternatives are a different product, and what to watch for. It gives no doses: your prescriber decides the dose.

### The legal route in the Netherlands

Semaglutide needs a prescription from a doctor, usually your GP (huisarts) or a medical specialist, and is dispensed by a pharmacy. For diabetes, Ozempic or Rybelsus may be reimbursed under conditions. For weight management, Wegovy is authorised but, as of 20 October 2026, not reimbursed: you pay for it yourself.<Cite id="fk-semaglutide,zin-wegovy-reassessment" /> Dutch prescribing advice places obesity medicines on top of a combined lifestyle programme (GLI), not instead of it.<Cite id="fk-semaglutide" /> See [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/) for how to prepare for that conversation.

A legal prescription brings things an online vial cannot: a check that the medicine suits you, a pharmacist who checks interactions, and follow-up if side effects appear.

### Why an online "research" vial is a different product

The FDA has warned companies selling semaglutide falsely labelled "for research purposes" or "not for human consumption" directly to consumers, with dosing instructions, and calls these products "of unknown quality".<Cite id="fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" /> Some products are salt forms (semaglutide sodium or acetate), which are different active ingredients from the one in the authorised medicines.<Cite id="fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" /> The MHRA states that powder-in-vial GLP-1 products are not authorised.<Cite id="mhra-glp1-what-you-need-to-know" /> The CBG's warning is simple: you do not know what is in it, how to use it, or what it will do to your health.<Cite id="cbg-glp1-position" /> Read more about [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/).

### Dosing errors with vials

Authorised pens deliver a fixed amount. Vials do not. The FDA has received reports of people taking five to 20 times the intended amount from multi-dose vials of compounded semaglutide, often because of confusion between milligrams, millilitres and syringe "units", or because the syringe was far larger than the volume needed. Some needed hospital care; reported effects included vomiting, fainting, dehydration, pancreatitis and gallstones. Because semaglutide has a half-life of about a week, the FDA notes that a prolonged period of observation and treatment may be needed after an overdose.<Cite id="fda-2024-compounded-semaglutide-dosing-errors" />

If you use a vial anyway, the FDA's general advice for multi-dose vials applies: a new sterile needle and syringe every time, and do not keep using a vial beyond 28 days after first opening it; do not use a product that arrived warm.<Cite id="fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" /> See [sterile technique](/en/safety/sterile-technique/).

### Fake pens

Falsified Ozempic pens have been found in the EU, the UK and elsewhere since 2023. Some contained insulin instead of semaglutide, which can cause dangerously low blood sugar.<Cite id="ema-2023-falsified-ozempic,bfarm-ozempic-falsification,mhra-2023-fake-weight-loss-pens,who-2024-falsified-semaglutide" /> In the Netherlands, IGJ advised getting prescription medicines only through your doctor and pharmacy, and contacting your pharmacist if you doubt a pen.<Cite id="igj-2023-falsified-ozempic" />

<Checklist>

- Get pens and tablets only from a pharmacy, on a prescription.
- Compare your pen with the pictures and description in the package leaflet.
- Do not use a pen that looks different, has spelling errors, or came without a leaflet. Take it back to the pharmacy.
- Report a suspected fake or a side effect to Lareb.

</Checklist>

### Who should not use semaglutide

- **Pregnancy, trying to conceive, breastfeeding:** animal studies showed harm to the embryo, and the long half-life means stopping at least two months before a planned pregnancy.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma, or MEN 2:** contraindicated in the US label because of the rodent C-cell finding; not a formal contraindication in the EU, so discuss it with your doctor.<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic,ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **Previous pancreatitis:** the EU label advises caution.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **Severe gastroparesis (a very slow stomach):** not recommended.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **Unstable diabetic retinopathy:** Wegovy is not recommended; if you have retinopathy you need close eye follow-up.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **Type 1 diabetes:** Wegovy has not been studied in type 1 diabetes and is not recommended; semaglutide is never a substitute for insulin.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

### Interactions

- **Insulin or sulfonylureas (such as gliclazide):** higher risk of low blood sugar.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **Other GLP-1 medicines:** do not combine; overdose effects are likely.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **Tablets that need fast or precise absorption:** slower stomach emptying can change how quickly they are absorbed. With semaglutide tablets, exposure to levothyroxine rose by 33%, so thyroid tests may need checking.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy,ema-epar-rybelsus" />
- **Warfarin or acenocoumarol:** lower INR has been reported with acenocoumarol; the label recommends frequent INR checks when starting.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **Surgery or sedation:** tell the doctor or anaesthetist that you use semaglutide.<Cite id="ema-prac-2024-07-aspiration" />

### Checks that make sense, and why

- **Eye examination if you have diabetic retinopathy:** a fast fall in blood glucose can temporarily worsen it.<Cite id="vilsboll-2018-retinopathy" />
- **Blood glucose if you also use insulin or a sulfonylurea:** see the interaction above.
- **Kidney function if you have prolonged vomiting or diarrhoea:** dehydration can harm the kidneys.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />
- **INR if you use warfarin or acenocoumarol**, and thyroid tests if you use levothyroxine.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy,ema-epar-rybelsus" />

### When to stop and get help

<Callout type="stop" title="Get medical help now">
- **Severe, constant pain in the upper belly**, often spreading to the back, with or without vomiting: possible pancreatitis. Stop semaglutide and get urgent care.
- **Sudden loss or blurring of vision in one eye:** possible NAION. Contact a doctor without delay.
- **Shaking, sweating, confusion or drowsiness after injecting a pen or vial from a non-pharmacy source:** possible insulin in a fake product, which can cause dangerously low blood sugar. Take something sugary if you can swallow safely, and call 112 if symptoms are severe or the person becomes drowsy or confused.
- **Swelling of the face, lips or throat, or difficulty breathing:** possible allergic reaction. Call 112.
- **Vomiting so much that you cannot keep fluids down**, or passing very little urine: risk of dehydration and kidney injury. Get same-day care.
</Callout>
