<Section id="what-it-is">

<Lead question="What is thymosin alpha-1?">It is a natural immune peptide. Italy and some other countries use it as a medicine.</Lead>

<Compare title="Approved in some countries, but not in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia." detail="What is known about thymosin alpha-1, checked on 22 and 23 October 2026.">

| | Thymosin alpha-1 (thymalfasin) |
|---|---|
| Made by the body? | <Verdict is="yes" /> First found in calf thymus in 1977. The medicine is a lab-made copy.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-thymosin-alpha-1-briefing" /> |
| Approved as a medicine anywhere? | <Verdict is="yes" /> In Italy, India and Hong Kong. Its maker reports more approvals in Asia, Latin America, Eastern Europe and the Middle East.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-thymosin-alpha-1-briefing" /> {/* residue-ok: geography in the maker's list of approvals, not reader framing */} |
| Approved in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia? | <Verdict is="no" /> None of the four has approved it.<Cite id="openfda-drugsfda-search-2026-10-22,mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-d-2026-10-23" /> |
| The same as TB-500? | <Verdict is="no" /> TB-500 comes from thymosin beta-4, a different protein.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> |

</Compare>

In the US, pharmacy-made versions are marketed as injections and nose sprays for many uses, from hepatitis to chronic fatigue.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-thymosin-alpha-1-briefing" /> Those, and products sold online, are not the authorised medicine.

<InDetail title="The molecule, and where it is approved">

Thymosin alpha-1 is a chain of 28 amino acids with an acetyl group at the start, formula C129H215N33O55, mass <Q>3108.3 g/mol</Q>. Its official medicine name is thymalfasin.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-thymosin-alpha-1-briefing,pubchem-thymalfasin" />

In Italy it is authorised to strengthen the response to flu vaccine in people with weak immune systems. In India it is approved for hepatitis B. Its maker reports approvals in other countries, mainly for hepatitis B and as an add-on to vaccines; the FDA could not check these itself.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-thymosin-alpha-1-briefing" /> Each of these approvals is national: it applies only in that country. It does not make thymosin alpha-1 an approved medicine in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia.<Cite id="openfda-drugsfda-search-2026-10-22,mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-d-2026-10-23" />

</InDetail>

<Section id="evidence">

<Lead question="Does it work?">Not clear. The biggest trial found no gain. Hepatitis B results are mixed.</Lead>

<KeyNumber title="No fewer deaths in sepsis" value="23.4% vs 24.1%" label="died within 28 days, on the drug and on a dummy drug" who="TESTS: 1,106 adults with sepsis, 22 hospitals in China, double-blind phase 3 trial" source="wu-2025-tests-sepsis" level="human-controlled" />

<KeyNumber title="A US trial with a dummy drug found no clear gain" value="14% vs 4%" label="cleared the virus with the drug and with placebo; this gap could be chance" who="Mutchnick 1999: 97 people with chronic hepatitis B, six months, US double-blind phase 3 trial" source="mutchnick-1999-thymosin-hbv" level="human-controlled" />

An open Taiwanese trial without placebo did report more people clearing the virus.<Cite id="chien-1998-thymosin-hbv" /> The FDA found the evidence for hepatitis B and its other reviewed uses insufficient.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-thymosin-alpha-1-briefing" />

<InDetail title="Every study in one table">

<StudyTable />

A 2025 pooling of 11 sepsis trials found fewer deaths overall, but not in the high-quality or multicentre trials. Its authors see a possible benefit in some groups, such as people with cancer, and say more patients are needed.<Cite id="gu-2025-thymosin-sepsis-meta" /> In the large sepsis trial itself, the hints in subgroups pointed both ways: possibly better with diabetes, possibly worse under the age of 60. Only new trials can settle this.<Cite id="wu-2025-tests-sepsis" /> Hundreds of laboratory papers come from many groups, and the original US group and two Italian groups appear on fewer than one in five.<Cite id="pubmed-thymosin-alpha-1-count-2026-10-22" /> Many trials are still running, mostly in China and in cancer.<Cite id="ctgov-thymalfasin-search-2026-10-22" />

</InDetail>

In sport its status is unclear everywhere. It is not named on the list, and it is approved in some countries. Ask your anti-doping organisation.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en,wada-2027-list-en" />

<InDetail title="Status in each country, and in sport">

<RegulatoryTable />

On 4 December 2024 the FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted 4 for and 17 against adding thymosin alpha-1 to the 503A list, for both the free base and the acetate. Several members saw no convincing evidence of benefit and safety.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-12-minutes,fda-pcac-2024-12-meeting" /> The FDA lists it among substances once in category 2, for possible significant safety risks; the nomination was withdrawn.<Cite id="fda-category-2-safety-risks" />

In sport, it is not named on the WADA list. Section S0 bans substances that no government health authority has approved, and thymosin alpha-1 is approved in several countries.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en,wada-2027-list-en" /> We found no ruling from an anti-doping body. Do not mix it up with thymosin beta-4 (TB-500), which is banned.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="The UK, Canada and Australia">

- **United Kingdom.** The MHRA's database of licensed medicines has no thymalfasin or Zadaxin product.<Cite id="mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23" /> It is not a controlled drug under the Misuse of Drugs Act.<Cite id="uk-mda-1971-sch-2" /> The FDA's 2024 review notes wellness clinics in the UK that market it.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-thymosin-alpha-1-briefing" />
- **Canada.** No thymalfasin product has a Drug Identification Number (DIN), the number every authorised drug carries.<Cite id="ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-extract-search-merge-2026-10-24" /> Health Canada says injectable peptides are generally regulated as prescription drugs. In 2025 it named thymosin alpha-1 among unauthorised injectable peptides seized from a clinic and from sellers.<Cite id="ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides,ca-hc-recalls-search-status-d-2026-10-23" /> In 2014 Health Canada authorised one trial of the brand-name product in sepsis. The trial lists no site in Canada and is still marked "not yet recruiting".<Cite id="ca-hc-ctdb-search-status-d-2026-10-24" />
- **Australia.** Thymosin alpha-1 is not in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG), so it is not approved. Unlike thymosin beta-4, it is not named in the Poisons Standard.<Cite id="au-artg-search-status-d-2026-10-23,au-poisons-standard-2026-10" /> A product that is not in the ARTG still may not be imported or supplied unless an exemption applies.<Cite id="au-tg-act-s19b-19d-offences,au-tga-2026-04-peptide-responsibilities" /> In 2020 the regulator, the TGA, fined a company whose website advertised thymosin alpha-1 and thymosin beta-4 as a way to prevent COVID-19. The TGA said neither product was in the ARTG.<Cite id="au-tga-search-status-d-2026-10-23" />

More: the law in [the US](/en/legal/us/), [the UK](/en/legal/uk/), [Canada](/en/legal/ca/) and [Australia](/en/legal/au/).

<Section id="risks">

<Lead question="What are the risks?">In trials, side effects were mostly mild. It changes how the immune system works.</Lead>

<Compare title="Trials found few side effects. Some groups need extra care." detail="From the trials and the product labels quoted by the FDA, as of 22 October 2026.">

| Possible harm | Shown in people? |
|---|---|
| Pain or redness where it is injected | <Verdict is="yes" /> The most common side effect in trials.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-thymosin-alpha-1-briefing" /> |
| More side effects than placebo overall | <Verdict is="no" /> Not in the large sepsis trial.<Cite id="wu-2025-tests-sepsis" /> |
| A flare of liver inflammation in hepatitis B | <Verdict is="yes" /> A label warns of short rises in a liver enzyme.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-thymosin-alpha-1-briefing" /> |
| Problems in autoimmune disease or after a transplant | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Labels advise caution, because it boosts the immune system.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-thymosin-alpha-1-briefing" /> |
| An immune reaction to a pharmacy-made or online product | <Verdict is="unknown" /> The FDA names this risk.<Cite id="fda-category-2-safety-risks" /> |

</Compare>

<InDetail title="What the labels say, and why">

The FDA quotes two foreign labels. The Italian label says not to use it in children, to take care in people with allergies, and to judge autoimmune disease case by case.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-thymosin-alpha-1-briefing" /> The Indonesian label warns of short liver-enzyme flares in hepatitis B. It says not to use it in people whose immune system is deliberately suppressed, such as after a transplant, unless the benefit clearly outweighs the risk.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-thymosin-alpha-1-briefing" /> The reason is the same in each case: a drug that turns the immune system up can work against treatments that turn it down.

In the 1,106-person sepsis trial, serious adverse events occurred in 26.8% on the drug and 29.3% on placebo.<Cite id="wu-2025-tests-sepsis" /> Those numbers reflect how ill people with sepsis are, not harm from the drug.

</InDetail>

### When to stop and get help

<Callout type="stop" title="Stop and get help if" id="stop-now">
- you get hives, swelling of the face or throat, or difficulty breathing (an allergic reaction);
- the injection site becomes hot, red, swollen or painful (infection or contamination);
- you get a high fever with chills, or feel very ill, after an injection.

If it is severe, call your local emergency number.
</Callout>
