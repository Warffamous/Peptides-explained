<Lead question="What is tesamorelin?">A lab-made version of a brain hormone. In the US it is a prescription medicine called Egrifta.</Lead>

Tesamorelin is a medicine with real trials behind it, for one narrow use: deep belly fat in adults with HIV. It is on this site because vials sold online carry the same name.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label,fda-warning-letters-index" /> The authorised medicine itself is also a powder in a vial that is mixed before injection, so looking like Egrifta proves nothing.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

<InDetail title="The authorised products, and why this page looks different">

The FDA approved Egrifta on 10 November 2010 to reduce excess abdominal fat in adults with HIV and lipodystrophy, a change in body fat seen in some people on HIV treatment.<Cite id="fda-drugsatfda-egrifta,fda-2010-egrifta-approval-letter" /> The 2 mg vial was renamed Egrifta SV on 5 July 2019, and a newer formulation, Egrifta WR, was approved on 25 March 2025.<Cite id="fda-2019-egrifta-sv-name-letter,fda-2025-egrifta-wr-approval-letter" /> The licence holder is Theratechnologies.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-wr-label" />

<AuthorisedProducts />

The label limits the approved use in three ways. Long-term heart safety has not been established; it is not for weight loss, because it does not change body weight; and there are no data that it helps people take their HIV medicines.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

<Callout type="note" title="Why this page looks different">
Most compounds on this site are experimental. Tesamorelin is a real medicine, tested in large trials in people and approved in the United States. Canada approved it too, but the company stopped selling it there in 2022, and it is not approved in the UK or Australia.<Cite id="fda-drugsatfda-egrifta,ca-sbd-egrifta,mhra-products-search-tesamorelin,au-artg-search-status-b-2026-10-23" /> That makes it an unusual case: a medicine with known short-term risks for one use in the US, and a product that is not approved, or no longer sold, in the other countries this site covers.
</Callout>

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="The molecule, and where the hormone was found">

In 1982 a team led by Roger Guillemin isolated a 44-amino-acid peptide from a pancreatic tumour that had caused acromegaly, a disease of too much growth hormone. It turned out to be the hormone the brain uses to order growth-hormone release: growth hormone-releasing hormone, or GHRH.<Cite id="guillemin-1982-ghrh-tumour" />

Tesamorelin is that same chain of 44 amino acids, <Seq>Tyr-Ala-Asp-Ala-Ile-Phe-…-Arg-Ala-Arg-Leu-NH2</Seq>, with one change: a hexenoyl group, a chain of six carbon atoms with one double bond, attached to the first amino acid, tyrosine.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label,guillemin-1982-ghrh-tumour,chembl-tesamorelin" /> Its formula is C221H366N72O67S and its mass <Q>5135.9 Da</Q>; the medicine contains it as an acetate salt.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label,pubchem-tesamorelin" />

### Why the extra group, and what it does not change

Natural GHRH can be cut at its start by the enzyme DPP-4, which inactivates it. In the developer's laboratory tests, the hexenoyl group protected tesamorelin from DPP-4 and slowed its breakdown in rat, dog and human plasma.<Cite id="ferdinandi-2007-th9507-nonclinical" /> It is still short-lived: after an injection under the skin its half-life in healthy people is about 8 to 11 minutes, and less than 4% of the injected amount reaches the blood.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label,dailymed-egrifta-wr-label" /> It works as a short daily push on the body's own system.

### A peptide that US law counts as a protein

US law defines a protein as a chain of more than 40 amino acids.<Cite id="ecfr-21-600-3-protein" /> With 44, tesamorelin crossed that line, so on 23 March 2020 its approval was "deemed to be" a biologics licence.<Cite id="fda-drugsatfda-egrifta" /> That matters for compounding (see the regulatory section).

</InDetail>

<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label,stanley-2011-tesamorelin-gh-pulsatility,falutz-2007-nejm-tesamorelin,moller-2009-gh-metabolism,veldhuis-2001-gh-pulsatility,ema-egrifta-withdrawal" />

Tesamorelin is not known to act on fat directly. It turns up the body's own growth-hormone system. That is why its wanted and unwanted effects look like a milder version of growth hormone's: less deep belly fat, but also fluid retention and higher blood sugar.<Cite id="fda-2010-egrifta-summary-review" />

<InDetail title="Step by step: the chain of effects and its evidence">

<Mechanism />

### What each step rests on

**The receptor.** The US label states that, in laboratory tests, tesamorelin binds and stimulates human GHRH receptors with a potency similar to the body's own hormone.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> GHRH is made in the hypothalamus and acts on the growth-hormone cells (somatotrophs) of the pituitary.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label,veldhuis-2001-gh-pulsatility" />

**Growth hormone in bursts.** Growth hormone is normally released in pulses, driven by GHRH and held back by somatostatin.<Cite id="veldhuis-2001-gh-pulsatility" /> <Claim level="human-limited">In 13 healthy men given tesamorelin daily for two weeks, without a comparison group, overnight growth hormone rose, both in its peaks and between them, and IGF-1 rose by 181 µg/L</Claim>.<Cite id="stanley-2011-tesamorelin-gh-pulsatility" /> In the trials, other pituitary hormones (TSH, LH, ACTH, prolactin) did not change in a clinically significant way.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

**IGF-1.** <Claim level="established">In the first phase 3 trial, IGF-1 rose by 81.0% with tesamorelin and fell by 5.0% with placebo over 26 weeks</Claim>.<Cite id="falutz-2007-nejm-tesamorelin" /> After 26 weeks, 47% of people had IGF-1 above 2 standard deviation scores (SDS) and 36% above 3 SDS, a level well above the normal range for their age.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

**Fat breakdown and blood sugar.** <Claim level="established">The most prominent metabolic effect of growth hormone in people is a marked increase in the breakdown of stored fat (lipolysis), releasing free fatty acids</Claim>. The same fatty acids make liver and muscle respond less to insulin, so growth hormone works against insulin.<Cite id="moller-2009-gh-metabolism" /> That one mechanism explains both the main benefit and the main metabolic risk.

**Deep belly fat, not body weight.** <Claim level="established">On CT scans, visceral fat fell while belly fat under the skin and body weight barely changed</Claim>.<Cite id="falutz-2010-tesamorelin-pooled,dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> Why visceral fat responds more than fat under the skin is not established. The authors of the liver trial note that the tissue-specific mechanisms "require further study".<Cite id="stanley-2019-tesamorelin-nafld" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Why a hormone releaser and not growth hormone itself">

Growth hormone and IGF-1 act back on the brain and pituitary. A burst of growth hormone switches on somatostatin and damps GHRH, and IGF-1 adds a slower brake.<Cite id="veldhuis-2001-gh-pulsatility,desousa-2025-gh-feedback" /> Tesamorelin works through this system rather than around it, and growth hormone stays pulsatile on it.<Cite id="stanley-2011-tesamorelin-gh-pulsatility" />

<Claim level="hypothesis">Researchers have argued that keeping this feedback in place limits side effects compared with giving growth hormone itself</Claim>.<Cite id="stanley-2019-tesamorelin-nafld" /> We found no published trial that tested this directly. And the brake does not stop IGF-1 from rising far above normal in more than a third of people, as the label shows.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="The two phase 3 trials, and what happened after stopping">

### Study 1 and Study 2

The approval rests on two randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials paid for by the developer. People were 18 to 65 years old, had HIV, were on stable HIV treatment and had a large waist; people treated for diabetes were not included.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

- **Study 1** randomised 412 adults (86% men) for 26 weeks. <Claim level="established">Visceral fat fell by 15.2% with tesamorelin and rose by 5.0% with placebo; triglycerides fell by 50 mg/dL against a rise of 9</Claim>. More people on tesamorelin stopped because of an adverse event.<Cite id="falutz-2007-nejm-tesamorelin" />
- **Study 2** randomised 404 adults. <Claim level="established">Visceral fat fell by 10.9% against 0.6% at six months, and distress about belly appearance improved</Claim>.<Cite id="falutz-2010-tesamorelin-jaids" />

Pooled, in 806 people, the treatment effect on visceral fat was −15.4%, with no significant change in fat under the skin and no clinically meaningful change in glucose measures at 26 or 52 weeks.<Cite id="falutz-2010-tesamorelin-pooled" />

### Stopping

After 26 weeks, people on tesamorelin were randomised again, blind, to continue or to switch to placebo. <Claim level="human-controlled">Those who continued kept a reduction of about 18% at 52 weeks; in those who switched, visceral fat came back</Claim>.<Cite id="falutz-2008-tesamorelin-extension,falutz-2010-tesamorelin-jaids" /> The label gives the size: visceral fat grew by 22% and 16% in the 26 weeks after switching to placebo in the two trials.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> The effect lasts only while the medicine is used.

### What the FDA itself noted

The FDA's division director wrote that the clear falls in visceral fat "were not accompanied by robust or consistent changes" in people's body image or in blood fats. She added that data were absent on long-term heart benefit and on the long-term risks of high IGF-1.<Cite id="fda-2010-egrifta-summary-review" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Fatty liver: the most promising newer evidence">

Fat in the liver is common in people with HIV and can lead to scarring (fibrosis). One academic group tested whether tesamorelin helps.<Cite id="stanley-2019-tesamorelin-nafld" />

- In a 6-month trial in 50 adults with HIV and extra belly fat, <Claim level="human-controlled">liver fat fell compared with placebo (a net 2.9 percentage points)</Claim>; fasting glucose rose a little in the first two weeks, but not over six months.<Cite id="stanley-2014-tesamorelin-liver-fat" />
- In a 12-month trial in 61 adults with HIV and fatty liver, <Claim level="human-controlled">liver fat fell by 4.1 percentage points more than with placebo (37% relative), and 35% against 4% reached normal liver fat</Claim>. <Claim level="human-controlled">Liver scarring got worse in 2 of 19 people on tesamorelin against 9 of 24 on placebo</Claim>, but existing scarring did not improve, and two people on tesamorelin stopped because of high blood sugar.<Cite id="stanley-2019-tesamorelin-nafld" />

This is promising and from a well-designed trial with liver biopsies. It is also small, a year long and from one group, and it measured liver fat and scarring, not liver failure or survival. Fatty liver is not an approved use. The authors themselves call for longer studies.<Cite id="stanley-2019-tesamorelin-nafld" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Outside HIV: obesity, diabetes and the ageing brain">

- **Obesity without HIV.** In 60 adults with abdominal obesity and low growth hormone, <Claim level="human-controlled">12 months of tesamorelin lowered visceral fat, triglycerides and CRP, and slightly reduced the thickness of the neck-artery wall, without changing glucose</Claim>.<Cite id="makimura-2012-tesamorelin-obesity" /> Artery thickness is a marker, not a heart attack.
- **Type 2 diabetes.** In a developer-run trial in 53 adults, <Claim level="human-controlled">12 weeks of tesamorelin did not change insulin response, fasting glucose or HbA1c compared with placebo</Claim>.<Cite id="clemmons-2017-tesamorelin-type-2-diabetes" />
- **Thinking in older adults.** In 152 adults aged 55 to 87, 66 of them with mild memory problems, <Claim level="human-controlled">20 weeks of tesamorelin improved a combined thinking score against placebo, mainly on tests of executive function (planning, switching between tasks and self-control)</Claim>. Gains on single tests were small, about a quarter of a standard deviation. Mild side effects were reported by 68% against 36%.<Cite id="baker-2012-ghrh-cognition" />
- **Later thinking trials.** In 73 people with HIV and thinking problems, <Claim level="human-controlled">6 months of tesamorelin did not improve thinking more than standard care</Claim>.<Cite id="ellis-2025-tesamorelin-cognition" /> A 22-person pilot with a low dose found no significant changes.<Cite id="stewart-2026-ghrh-cognition-pilot" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="'Anti-ageing' and body-building claims: outside the evidence">

Growth hormone, IGF-1 and GHRH all fall with age.<Cite id="baker-2012-ghrh-cognition" /> In 2004, tesamorelin was being developed for "age-associated conditions resulting from diminished growth hormone (GH) secretion".<Cite id="jansen-2004-th9507-dog" /> Already in 2003, a review by researchers who ran such trials concluded that the results so far "do not yet support the routine use" of GHRH in normal ageing.<Cite id="merriam-2003-ghrh-aging" /> What the record shows since then:

- **No trial measured ageing.** The trials measured belly fat, liver fat and test scores over months. None measured how long people live, or ageing itself.<Cite id="badran-2026-tesamorelin-meta-analysis,baker-2012-ghrh-cognition" />
- **No information in older people.** The label states that there is no information on its use in people over 65.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />
- **No strength benefit was shown.** In the trials, body weight did not change. Lean mass (everything except fat, including water) rose a little, about 1.4 kg in a pooled analysis, and fell again in people who stopped. No published trial has tested strength or sport performance as its main outcome; one trial of physical function with exercise, in people with HIV, is still running.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label,badran-2026-tesamorelin-meta-analysis,ctgov-nct06554717" />
- **Higher is not shown to be better.** Lower growth hormone in older age is common, and no trial has shown that raising it improves health. Raising IGF-1 far above the normal range for years has unknown effects, as the label says.<Cite id="baker-2012-ghrh-cognition,dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

A test of an idea is not the same as an answer. A benefit on thinking tests in one 20-week trial, not confirmed by two later, smaller trials, is a reason for better trials. It does not show that daily injections help healthy people.<Cite id="baker-2012-ghrh-cognition,ellis-2025-tesamorelin-cognition,stewart-2026-ghrh-cognition-pilot" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Who paid for the evidence">

The two approval trials and the diabetes trial were paid for and run by the developer, Theratechnologies.<Cite id="falutz-2007-nejm-tesamorelin,falutz-2010-tesamorelin-jaids,clemmons-2017-tesamorelin-type-2-diabetes" /> Most later trials come from one group at Massachusetts General Hospital, funded by the US National Institutes of Health. That group is academic, but it is not fully separate from the developer. Its senior author was also the senior author of the phase 3 trial reports, and has been a consultant to the developer and a member of its scientific advisory board.<Cite id="falutz-2007-nejm-tesamorelin,stanley-2019-tesamorelin-nafld" /> The developer gave the drug for these trials, paid part of the costs of the obesity trial, and in the 2019 liver trial supplied the drug at reduced cost and received reports of serious adverse events.<Cite id="stanley-2011-tesamorelin-gh-pulsatility,stanley-2014-tesamorelin-liver-fat,makimura-2012-tesamorelin-obesity,stanley-2019-tesamorelin-nafld" /> The thinking trials come from three other universities; for two of them, the developer supplied the drug free.<Cite id="baker-2012-ghrh-cognition,ellis-2025-tesamorelin-cognition,stewart-2026-ghrh-cognition-pilot" />

The main result has been seen in every trial that measured it. A 2026 meta-analysis of five randomised trials found the same fall in visceral fat.<Cite id="badran-2026-tesamorelin-meta-analysis" /> The main open questions are not whether it shrinks visceral fat, but whether that improves health, and what it costs in safety over years.

</InDetail>

</Section>

<InDetail title="Three regulators, the same trials, different answers">

In May 2010 all 16 voting members of the FDA's advisory committee backed approval. Several cited powerful testimonies from patients and the lack of any approved treatment. The FDA approved it, but required long-term studies of cancer, diabetic eye disease, diabetes, allergic reactions and heart events.<Cite id="fda-2010-egrifta-summary-review,fda-2010-egrifta-approval-letter" />

{/* residue-ok: EMA evidence (the committee's assessment of the same two trials; not a legal frame for the reader) */}
The medicines committee of the European Medicines Agency came to the opposite provisional view in 2012, on the same two main trials. It doubted that a smaller belly-fat area brings real health benefit. It also found the trial patients unlike the European patients with HIV it was judging for, who have a lower BMI and less belly fat. It called the rise in IGF-1 a "major safety concern", because high IGF-1 may be linked to cancer and to worse diabetic eye disease. And it noted that no long-term safety data were provided. The applicant then withdrew.<Cite id="ema-egrifta-withdrawal" />

Health Canada struggled with the same questions. Its first decision was negative. In March 2013, after the company's reply, it still found that the file did not meet the requirements: three of its four major objections remained. They concerned the balance of benefit and risk, the use it would be approved for and the raised IGF-1. At the company's request, a panel of outside Canadian experts looked at the file again in August 2013. Health Canada then approved it on 29 April 2014, with stricter conditions than in the US: treatment stops if IGF-1 is above 2 standard deviation scores after 26 weeks (the US label uses 3).<Cite id="ca-sbd-egrifta,ca-pm-egrifta-2015" />

{/* residue-ok: EMA evidence (the committee's open questions) */}
Each answer has a cost. People in the UK and Australia with this kind of fat gain cannot get tesamorelin as an approved medicine, and in Canada it is no longer sold; whether that pushes some towards unchecked products has not been measured.<Cite id="mhra-products-search-tesamorelin,au-artg-search-status-b-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-egrifta-din-02438712" /> Equally, the long-term studies the FDA asked for were stopped in 2018 without posted results, so the European committee's questions are still open.<Cite id="ema-egrifta-withdrawal,ctgov-nct01579695,ctgov-nct01591902" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="The United States">

<Section id="risks">

<Lead question="What are the risks?">Blood sugar can rise. Joint pain, swelling and sore injection spots are more common. Long-term effects are unknown.</Lead>

<IconArray title="Reactions at the injection spot, muscle pain and swelling were more common than with placebo." detail="People in 100 with each problem in the first 26 weeks of the two phase 3 trials (543 on tesamorelin, 263 on placebo), from the US label." a="Tesamorelin" b="Placebo" source="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" level="human-controlled" rows={[

Unless stated otherwise, risks and frequencies on this page come from the US label, based on 740 people with HIV treated in the trials.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

<InDetail title="Blood sugar and diabetes">

Growth hormone works against insulin, so more of it can raise blood sugar.<Cite id="moller-2009-gh-metabolism" /> In the trials, average HbA1c started at 5.3%. <Claim level="established">HbA1c reached the diabetes range (6.5% or more) in 5% of people on tesamorelin against 1% on placebo (hazard ratio 3.3)</Claim>. The label asks for a glucose check before starting and regular checks during use.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

In some studies the rise came early. In one trial, fasting glucose rose a little after two weeks but not over six months; the same group describes a modest early worsening of insulin resistance that returned to baseline with longer use.<Cite id="stanley-2014-tesamorelin-liver-fat,stanley-2019-tesamorelin-nafld" /> In the 2019 liver trial, two people on tesamorelin, one with diabetes at the start, had to stop because of high blood sugar.<Cite id="stanley-2019-tesamorelin-nafld" /> People treated for diabetes were excluded from the approval trials.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="IGF-1, growth and cancer: why the label is cautious">

Tesamorelin raises growth hormone, which the label calls "a known growth factor", and IGF-1, which helps cells grow and survive.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label,renehan-2004-igf1-cancer" /> That is the reason for the caution:

1. **IGF-1 goes high.** After 26 weeks, 36% of people had IGF-1 more than 3 SDS above normal. The label advises checking IGF-1 and considering stopping if it stays high.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />
2. **High IGF-1 is linked to some cancers.** <Claim level="human-limited">In observational studies, people with naturally higher IGF-1 had modestly more prostate and premenopausal breast cancer</Claim>. These studies show a link, not cause.<Cite id="renehan-2004-igf1-cancer" />
3. **Growth signals could feed an existing tumour.** <Claim level="hypothesis">The worry is not that tesamorelin starts a cancer, but that a growth signal could help an existing, perhaps undiagnosed, one grow</Claim>. The label therefore forbids use with active cancer and says to stop at any sign of cancer coming back.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />
4. **The long-term answer is missing.** No lifetime cancer studies were done in rodents, and the 10-year human safety study was stopped.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-wr-label,ctgov-nct01579695" />

The label adds that people with HIV already have a higher background risk of cancer.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> Standard laboratory tests for damage to genes were negative.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-wr-label" /> <Claim level="hypothesis">Such tests look for damage to genes; they are not designed to show whether a growth signal helps an existing tumour grow</Claim>.

{/* residue-ok: EMA evidence (the committee's IGF-1 and eye-disease concern) */}
**Eyes.** High IGF-1 may worsen diabetic eye disease (retinopathy). The label asks for regular eye checks in people with diabetes.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label,ema-egrifta-withdrawal" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Fluid, joints and nerves">

Growth hormone makes the body hold on to fluid. The label describes firmer, fuller tissues and aching muscles and joints. This shows up as swelling (oedema), joint pain and carpal tunnel syndrome: numbness and tingling in the hand when a nerve at the wrist is squeezed. These were either short-lived or went away after stopping.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> Tingling or numb skin (paraesthesia) was reported by 5% against 2% on placebo.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> The FDA's reviewer noted that these are the same kinds of side effects as growth hormone's, only milder.<Cite id="fda-2010-egrifta-summary-review" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Allergic reactions and antibodies">

Allergic (hypersensitivity) reactions, such as itching, redness, flushing, hives and rash, occurred in 4% of people on tesamorelin. The label advises stopping and getting prompt medical help if one is suspected.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

Tesamorelin is a large peptide, and the immune system notices it. <Claim level="established">Antibodies against tesamorelin were found in 50% of people after 26 weeks; in about 60% of them, the antibodies also recognised the body's own GHRH</Claim>. Antibodies that blocked tesamorelin in a laboratory test were found in 10% at 52 weeks. Of people who had antibodies and then stopped, 18% still had them six months later. People with and without antibodies lost similar amounts of visceral fat.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

The label's warnings section gives a wider figure for injection-site reactions (redness, itching, pain, irritation or bruising): 25% against 14% in the first 26 weeks. It advises changing the injection spot to lower the risk.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Pregnancy, children, older people and serious illness">

- **Pregnancy.** Forbidden by the label: less belly fat brings no benefit in pregnancy, and <Claim level="animal">in rats, the offspring developed fluid on the brain (hydrocephaly) at about two to four times the human exposure, and skull bones formed late at lower amounts</Claim>.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />
- **Children and teenagers.** Not for use. In children whose bones are still growing it may speed up growth too much.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />
- **Over 65.** There is no information.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />
- **Serious illness.** In critically ill people (after open-heart or belly surgery or several injuries, or with sudden breathing failure), more died when they were given growth hormone in medical amounts. The label advises considering stopping tesamorelin in critically ill people.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />
- **Pituitary problems.** Forbidden after pituitary surgery, a pituitary tumour, radiation to the head or a head injury that affected the pituitary, because the system tesamorelin acts on is damaged.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Interactions">

- **Steroid replacement.** Growth hormone lowers the conversion of cortisone into active cortisol. People who take steroid replacement because their adrenal glands do not work may need a higher dose; their doctor decides. Cortisone and prednisone are affected most.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />
- **Medicines broken down by liver enzymes (cytochrome P450).** Growth hormone may change how fast some of these are cleared, such as steroids, sex hormones, epilepsy medicines and ciclosporin. In tests in healthy people, tesamorelin lowered the levels of simvastatin and ritonavir only slightly (by 15% or less).<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />
- **Diabetes medicines.** Blood sugar can rise, so diabetes treatment may need adjusting.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

</InDetail>

### When to stop and get help

<Callout type="stop" title="Stop and get help" id="stop-now">
- **Swelling of the face, lips or throat, or trouble breathing:** possible severe allergic reaction. Call your local emergency number.
- **Hives, an itchy rash or flushing after an injection:** possible allergic reaction. Stop and get medical help the same day.
- **An injection spot that becomes hot, red, swollen and more painful, or a fever:** possible infection. Get same-day care.
- **Great thirst, peeing a lot, feeling weak, or blurred vision:** possible high blood sugar. Stop and have your blood sugar checked soon. If you also vomit, breathe fast, feel very sleepy or confused, get urgent help.
- **Worse eyesight, if you have diabetes:** contact a doctor without delay.
- **A new lump, or signs that a past cancer is back:** stop and see a doctor.
</Callout>

### The legal route

In the US, Egrifta is a medicine you get from a pharmacy with a [prescription](/en/legal-routes/#prescription), for one approved use.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> A doctor may also prescribe an approved medicine for another use ([off-label use](/en/legal-routes/#off-label)), but then the FDA has not judged it safe and effective for that use.<Cite id="fda-off-label-use" /> US pharmacies may not make their own tesamorelin under the [compounding rules](/en/legal-routes/#us-compounding), because it is licensed as a biological product.<Cite id="fda-2020-notice-compounders-biologics" />

In the UK and Australia no tesamorelin product is approved, and in Canada none is sold now.<Cite id="mhra-products-search-tesamorelin,ca-dpd-extract-search-status-b-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-b-2026-10-23" /> Our page on [treatment routes](/en/legal-routes/) explains the other legal routes in each country and their limits: [UK "specials"](/en/legal-routes/#uk-specials), [Canada's Special Access Program](/en/legal-routes/#canada-special-access), [Australia's Special Access Scheme](/en/legal-routes/#australia-sas), [importing for yourself](/en/legal-routes/#personal-import) and [clinical trials](/en/legal-routes/#trials). Each has strict conditions, and none turns an unapproved product into an approved one. ClinicalTrials.gov lists no open tesamorelin trial with a site in the UK, Canada or Australia.<Cite id="ctgov-search-status-b-2026-10-23,au-anzctr-search-status-b-2026-10-23" />

### Why a "research" vial is a different product

The evidence on this page belongs to Egrifta: a known substance, made under licence, with a label that is kept up to date.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> A vial sold online as "tesamorelin" shares the name but none of those guarantees. The FDA has told sellers of such products that they are unapproved new drugs, whatever the "research use only" label says.<Cite id="fda-warning-letters-index" /> See [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/).

Even the authorised powder must be mixed before injection, which is a moment when germs can get in. At approval, the FDA named "microbial contamination possibly resulting in soft tissue infections" during mixing as a possible serious risk, and required work on a simpler single-vial product.<Cite id="fda-2010-egrifta-approval-letter" /> The label says to use the liquid only if it is clear, colourless and free of particles, and never to share a syringe, even with a new needle.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> See [sterile technique](/en/safety/sterile-technique/).

### Who should not use it, and why

- **Anyone pregnant or trying to conceive**, because of the rat findings and because it brings no benefit in pregnancy.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />
- **Anyone with active cancer**, or a past cancer that is not inactive and fully treated, because growth hormone and IGF-1 are growth signals.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />
- **Anyone with pituitary disease**, pituitary surgery, head radiation or a head injury that affected the pituitary.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />
- **Children and teenagers**, whose bones are still growing.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />
- **Athletes subject to anti-doping rules**, because it is banned at all times.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en" />

### Checks that make sense, and why

A doctor can help you choose which of these fit you:

<Checklist>

- **Blood sugar or HbA1c before starting and during use**, because growth hormone works against insulin and diabetes became more common in the trials.
- **IGF-1 during use**, because it went far above normal in more than a third of people, and the label advises considering stopping if it stays high.
- **An eye check if you have diabetes**, because high IGF-1 may worsen diabetic eye disease.
- **The cancer screening due for your age, and any unexplained lump checked first**, because of the growth-signal effect.
- **A pregnancy test if you could be pregnant**, because of the rat findings.

</Checklist>
