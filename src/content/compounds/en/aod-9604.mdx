<Section id="what-it-is">

<Lead question="What is AOD-9604?">A lab-made piece of growth hormone, first developed as a diet pill. It is not an approved medicine.</Lead>

<Compare title="AOD-9604 comes from growth hormone, but does not act like it, and is not approved." detail="What is known about the substance sold under this name, from the FDA's 2024 review, checked on 23 October 2026.">

| | AOD-9604 |
|---|---|
| Made in a lab? | <Verdict is="yes" /> A chain of 16 amino acids: growth hormone's last 15, with a tyrosine added in front.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" /> |
| Acts like growth hormone? | <Verdict is="no" /> It did not bind growth hormone receptors in cells, and did not raise IGF-1 in trials.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing,stier-2013-aod9604-safety" /> |
| Approved as a medicine? | <Verdict is="no" /> Not part of any FDA-approved drug, and not approved in the UK, Canada or Australia.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing,mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-d-2026-10-23" /> |
| Tested as an injection in people? | <Verdict is="no" /> The FDA found no such study.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" /> |

</Compare>

The Australian company Metabolic Pharmaceuticals developed it in the late 1990s as an anti-obesity drug, and stopped in 2007.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" /> US clinics now market it, mostly as injections or pills, for weight loss, joints and anti-ageing.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" />

<InDetail title="The molecule, and the 'GRAS' claim">

AOD-9604 is <Seq>Tyr-Leu-Arg-Ile-Val-Gln-Cys-Arg-Ser-Val-Glu-Gly-Ser-Cys-Gly-Phe</Seq>, with a bridge between the two cysteines. Formula C78H123N23O23S2, mass <Q>1815.1 g/mol</Q>.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing,pubchem-aod-9604" /> It is sold as a free base or an acetate salt, and the FDA found it not well characterised: no public tests for impurities, clumps, germs or bacterial toxins.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" /> PubChem lists "Tyr-somatostatin (177-191)" as a synonym. That is wrong: it is based on growth hormone (somatotropin), not somatostatin.<Cite id="pubchem-aod-9604" />

"GRAS" is a US food term: generally recognised as safe. If a product description calls AOD-9604 GRAS, note that the FDA's public list of GRAS notices has no entry for it.<Cite id="fda-gras-inventory-search-2026-10-23" /> GRAS is about food, not medicines.

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="What each step rests on">

The animal results come almost entirely from the group that created it, and the FDA notes that none of these studies tested different doses or corrected its statistics for many comparisons.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing,heffernan-2001-aod9604-mice,ng-2000-aod9604-rats" /> In obese mice, the effect needed the beta-3 receptor on fat cells, but the authors concluded that AOD-9604 does not act on it directly.<Cite id="heffernan-2001-aod9604-mice" />

<Section id="evidence">

<Lead question="Does it work?">No. In the largest trial, people did not lose clearly more weight than on a dummy pill.</Lead>

<KeyNumber title="In 502 adults, weight loss was no better than placebo" value="502" label="adults with obesity took part; at 12 weeks they had not lost clearly more weight" who="OPTIONS trial: daily pill for 24 weeks with diet and exercise (company report, quoted by the FDA)" source="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" level="human-controlled" />

<KeyNumber title="The maker gave up on it as a diet drug" value="2007" label="the year the maker said the results did not back it as a diet drug" counted="Company announcement of 21 February 2007, quoted in the FDA's 2024 review" source="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" />

The FDA concluded that the evidence does not support it for obesity by any route.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" /> Claims about joints, bones or anti-ageing rest on, at most, animal studies.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing,kwon-2015-aod9604-rabbit-oa" />

<InDetail title="Every study in one table">

<StudyTable />

Three smaller trials, in 23, 16 and 36 people with obesity, found no significant weight loss against placebo; a 12-week trial in 300 people is known only from a meeting abstract that reported small differences.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" /> All human trials were run and paid for by the developer, and the authors of the safety summary worked for it or advised it.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing,stier-2013-aod9604-safety" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="The UK, Canada and Australia">

- **United Kingdom.** The MHRA's database of licensed medicines has no AOD-9604 product.<Cite id="mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23" /> It is not a controlled drug under the Misuse of Drugs Act. Home Office testing found it once among Border Force samples in 2017 to 2018, and listed it as not controlled.<Cite id="uk-mda-1971-sch-2,uk-home-office-fews-2017-18" />
- **Canada.** No AOD-9604 product has a Drug Identification Number (DIN), the number every authorised drug carries.<Cite id="ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-extract-search-merge-2026-10-24" /> Health Canada says injectable peptides are generally regulated as prescription drugs. In 2025 it named AOD-9604 among unauthorised injectable peptides seized from sellers.<Cite id="ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides,ca-hc-recalls-search-status-d-2026-10-23" />

### Australia, where it began

AOD-9604 was developed in Australia. Its developer, Metabolic Pharmaceuticals, ran its large weight-loss trial at sites across the country; the trial was registered in 2005.<Cite id="anzctr-actrn12605000067673,fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" /> In February 2007 the company said the results did not support the drug's commercial viability as an obesity treatment, and stopped that work.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" />

In February 2013 the Australian Crime Commission reported that professional athletes were using AOD-9604. It described the substance as not approved for human use, not scheduled and not then banned in sport.<Cite id="au-acc-2013-organised-crime-drugs-sport" /> Two months later, WADA said that AOD-9604, approved by no government health authority, fell under S0, the class of unapproved substances banned at all times.<Cite id="wada-2013-04-aod9604-statement" />

The Poisons Standard first listed AOD-9604 as a prescription-only medicine (Schedule 4) in the edition in force from 1 June 2015. It was proposed in 2014 together with other performance and image enhancing drugs.<Cite id="au-poisons-standard-2015-06,au-poisons-standard-2015-02,au-tga-2014-09-scheduling-consultation" /> It is still there in the October 2026 edition, and also in Appendix D: medicines that must not be possessed without authority, such as a prescription.<Cite id="au-poisons-standard-2026-10" /> State and territory law gives this force. In Victoria, for example, having any Schedule 4 medicine without authority is an offence, and New South Wales lists AOD-9604 among its stricter "S4D" medicines.<Cite id="au-vic-dpcs-act-1981,au-nsw-health-s4d,au-nsw-health-s4d-2026" /> We did not check the other states and territories.

Being scheduled does not make it approved: it has never been in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG).<Cite id="au-artg-search-status-d-2026-10-23,au-tga-search-status-d-2026-10-23" /> A product that is not in the ARTG still may not be imported or supplied unless an exemption applies.<Cite id="au-tg-act-s19b-19d-offences,au-tga-2026-04-peptide-responsibilities" /> Since June 2026 the regulator, the TGA, has made unapproved peptides a priority for its checks.<Cite id="au-tga-2026-06-peptide-compliance-priority" />

More: the law in [the US](/en/legal/us/), [the UK](/en/legal/uk/), [Canada](/en/legal/ca/) and [Australia](/en/legal/au/).

<Section id="risks">

<Lead question="What are the risks?">Its long-term safety was never studied. Products sold online may hold something else.</Lead>

<Compare title="Some warning signs appeared in small studies. None was followed up." detail="What the FDA found in the published summaries and animal studies, as of 23 October 2026.">

| Possible harm | Shown in people? |
|---|---|
| Chest tightness | <Verdict is="yes" /> Once, severe, in a drip study; judged possibly related.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" /> |
| Cancer | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Four cancers in one 12-week trial were judged unrelated; the FDA found too little information to judge.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" /> |
| Effects on bone or liver | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Changes in bone markers in rats and liver cells in monkeys.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" /> |
| An immune reaction to an injection | <Verdict is="unknown" /> The FDA's concern for injected peptides; not studied.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" /> |

</Compare>

<InDetail title="What was reported, and why the gaps matter">

In the company trials, the most common complaints were headache, tiredness and dizziness with drips, and headache, diarrhoea and wind with pills; mild or moderate euphoria was reported 5 times on the drug and never on placebo.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" /> In a 26-week rat study, a bone marker, osteocalcin, changed with the dose; in a 9-month monkey study, small changes appeared in liver cells. The FDA calls both possible safety signals.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" /> Tests for damage to genes gave unclear results.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2024-aod9604-briefing" />

All of this concerns the developer's product. AOD-9604 has been found in seized products tested by a national public-health laboratory, and in confiscated vials in the US.<Cite id="vanhee-2014-aod9604-seized,cox-2015-aod9604-detection" /> See [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/).

</InDetail>

### When to stop and get help

<Callout type="stop" title="Stop and get help if" id="stop-now">
- you get chest pain or tightness, or trouble breathing;
- the injection site becomes hot, red, swollen or painful, or you get a fever (possible infection);
- you get hives, swelling of the face or throat, or difficulty breathing (an allergic reaction).

If it is severe, call your local emergency number.
</Callout>
