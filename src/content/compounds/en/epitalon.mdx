<Section id="what-it-is">

<Lead question="What is epitalon?">A lab-made chain of four amino acids, sold for anti-ageing. No country is known to have approved it.</Lead>

<Compare title="Epitalon is lab-made, comes from one research group, and no country is known to have approved it." detail="What is known about the substance sold under this name, checked on 22 October 2026.">

| | Epitalon |
|---|---|
| Made in a lab? | <Verdict is="yes" /> A chain of four amino acids: alanine, glutamic acid, aspartic acid and glycine.<Cite id="pubchem-epitalon,fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" /> |
| Where does it come from? | <Verdict is="unknown" /> It was designed in St Petersburg as the possible active part of an extract of cattle pineal glands.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" /> |
| Approved as a medicine anywhere? | <Verdict is="no" /> Not FDA-approved, and the FDA found none in ten other countries, including Canada, Australia and the UK.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" /> |
| Tested for safety in people? | <Verdict is="no" /> The FDA found no clinical safety studies.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" /> |

</Compare>

It is promoted online as an anti-ageing substance, as injections, capsules and an oral spray, some labelled for research use only.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" /> A national public-health laboratory has found it in illegal pharmaceutical products.<Cite id="vanhee-2015-epitalon-illegal" />

<InDetail title="The molecule, and where it came from">

Epitalon is <Seq>Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly</Seq>, formula C14H22N4O9, mass <Q>390.35 g/mol</Q>.<Cite id="pubchem-epitalon,fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" /> Researchers led by Vladimir Khavinson in St Petersburg first made it in the late 1980s. It was meant to copy what they took to be the active part of epithalamin, an extract of cattle pineal glands.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" /> PubChem lists "epithalamin" as another name for epitalon. That is wrong: the extract and the tetrapeptide are different products.<Cite id="pubchem-epitalon,fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" />

</InDetail>

</Section>

<Section id="mechanism">

<Lead question="How does it work?">Nobody knows. In cells it switches on telomerase, which rebuilds the ends of chromosomes.</Lead>

<Mechanism />

<InDetail title="What each step rests on">

The telomerase finding first came from the originating group, in cells in a dish.<Cite id="khavinson-2003-epitalon-telomerase" /> In 2025 an independent laboratory in London repeated it in normal human cells.<Cite id="al-dulaimi-2025-epitalon-telomeres" /> The melatonin finding comes from old monkeys in the originating network. In rat pineal glands kept in a dish, epitalon did not raise melatonin release, so how it would act on the gland is unclear.<Cite id="goncharova-2001-epitalon-monkeys,fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" />

Two used an under-the-tongue spray in night-shift workers; one used injections next to the eye in people with retinal disease.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing,khavinson-2002-epitalon-retinitis" /> In the randomised study, a melatonin breakdown product in urine rose with epitalon. <RegulatoryTable />

All statuses were checked on 22 and 23 October 2026. The FDA found no approved epitalon product in Canada, Australia, the UK or seven other countries.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" /> The FDA had put epitalon in category 2, for substances that may present significant safety risks; the nominations were later withdrawn.<Cite id="fda-category-2-safety-risks" />

- **United Kingdom.** The MHRA's database of licensed medicines has no epitalon product, and the FDA found none in the UK.<Cite id="mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23,fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" /> It is not a controlled drug under the Misuse of Drugs Act.<Cite id="uk-mda-1971-sch-2" />
- **Canada.** No epitalon product has a Drug Identification Number (DIN), the number every authorised drug carries.<Cite id="ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-extract-search-merge-2026-10-24" /> Health Canada says injectable peptides are generally regulated as prescription drugs. <InDetail title="The July 2026 US vote, point by point">

- On 23 July 2026 the FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) voted 8–6 with one abstention to recommend BPC-157 for the 503A bulk drug substances list (count as reported from the public meeting).<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-07-meeting,fda-pcac-2026-questions" />
- The committee considered seven peptides, including epitalon, which the FDA reviewed for insomnia.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-07-meeting,fr-2026-07361-pcac-notice" /> As reported from the meeting, it recommended six: BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Semax and epitalon; emideltide (DSIP) was not recommended. The FDA asked for separate votes on the free base and the acetate salt of each.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-questions" /> The FDA had posted no minutes by 23 October 2026, so the counts and outcome above are as reported from the public meeting.
- The vote is advisory and non-binding, and it went against FDA staff's own recommendation not to list epitalon.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-briefing-intro" />
- Legal compounding remains prohibited until the FDA formally adopts the recommendation and completes rulemaking.<Cite id="fda-503a-bulks-page" />
- None of the six it recommended is an approved medicine in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia (Semax is registered as a medicine in Russia). A 503A listing would allow pharmacy compounding on prescription in the US. It is not a marketing authorisation, says nothing about efficacy, and changes nothing outside the US.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-presentations-day1,fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing,mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23,uk-mhra-substance-index-status-c-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-c-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-d-2026-10-23" />

<Section id="risks">

<Lead question="What are the risks?">Unknown in people. The FDA flags a possible cancer risk, and products are unchecked.</Lead>

<Compare title="Several harms are possible. None has been studied properly in people." detail="What the FDA review and the cell and animal studies suggest, as of 22 October 2026.">

| Possible harm | Shown in people? |
|---|---|
| Helping cancer cells keep dividing | <Verdict is="unknown" /> A worry based on how telomerase works. The animal tumour studies were too short to answer it.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" /> |
| An immune reaction to the peptide or its impurities | <Verdict is="unknown" /> The FDA cannot rule it out for injections.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" /> |
| Infection from germs in the product | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Illegal products have been found; their quality is unknown.<Cite id="vanhee-2015-epitalon-illegal" /> |
| Side effects in people | <Verdict is="unknown" /> No study reported safety data.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" /> |

</Compare>

<InDetail title="Why a cancer effect is theorised, and the evidence on both sides">

Most body cells keep telomerase switched off, so their telomeres shorten until the cell stops dividing. That limit is one of the body's brakes on cancer. The FDA writes that, because epitalon switches on telomerase and lengthens telomeres, continuous use "could enable cells to evade senescence and become cancerous". It adds that long telomeres are linked to a higher risk of some cancers.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" /> In 2025 an independent laboratory found that epitalon also lengthened telomeres in two breast-cancer cell lines.<Cite id="al-dulaimi-2025-epitalon-telomeres" />

On the other side, the originating group reports fewer leukaemias in treated mice, with no change in total tumours.<Cite id="anisimov-2003-epitalon-shr-mice" /> The FDA notes that these studies used one dose, female mice only, and short exposures, so they cannot settle the question. No standard two-year cancer study exists.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" />

</InDetail>

</Section>

### When to stop and get help

<Callout type="stop" title="Stop and get help if" id="stop-now">
- the injection site becomes hot, red, swollen or painful (infection or contamination);
- you get a fever or chills, or feel very ill, after an injection;
- you get hives, swelling of the face or throat, or difficulty breathing (an allergic reaction).

If it is severe, call your local emergency number.
</Callout>

<Cite id="nhs-cellulitis,nhs-sepsis,nhs-anaphylaxis,fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" />

<WarningSigns only="general,injection,allergic" />

<InDetail title="Checks that make sense, and who should not use it">

A doctor can help you decide which checks fit you (see [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/)):

- **Cancer screening due for your age, and any new lump, bleeding or weight loss checked**, because of the telomerase concern above.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" />
- **Anti-doping rules**, if you compete: epitalon falls under S0 and is banned at all times.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en" />

People with cancer or a history of cancer have the most to lose from the telomerase concern. There are no data on pregnancy or breastfeeding.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-epitalon-briefing" /> See [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/) and [sterile technique](/en/safety/sterile-technique/).

</InDetail>
