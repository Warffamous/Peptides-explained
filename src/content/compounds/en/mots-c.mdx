<Section id="what-it-is">

<Lead question="What is MOTS-c?">A small peptide made inside our cells. It is not an approved medicine.</Lead>

<Compare title="MOTS-c is made by the body, sold as a lab-made powder, and approved nowhere." detail="What is known about the substance sold under this name, checked on 22 October 2026.">

| | MOTS-c |
|---|---|
| Made by the body? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Its recipe sits in the DNA of mitochondria, the energy parts of cells.<Cite id="lee-2015-mots-c" /> |
| Lab-made for sale? | <Verdict is="yes" /> As a free base or an acetate salt. The FDA calls neither well characterised.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing" /> |
| Approved as a medicine anywhere? | <Verdict is="no" /> Not in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia, and we found no approval elsewhere.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing,mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-d-2026-10-23" /> |
| Given to people in a published study? | <Verdict is="no" /> The FDA found no human data.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing" /> |

</Compare>

A group at the University of Southern California described MOTS-c in 2015.<Cite id="lee-2015-mots-c" /> It is promoted online as an injection for weight loss, blood sugar and exercise.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing" /> None of these uses has been tested in people.

<InDetail title="The molecule, and the company version">

MOTS-c is a chain of 16 amino acids, <Seq>Met-Arg-Trp-Gln-Glu-Met-Gly-Tyr-Ile-Phe-Tyr-Pro-Arg-Lys-Leu-Arg</Seq>, with formula C101H152N28O22S2 and mass <Q>2174.6 g/mol</Q>.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing,pubchem-mots-c" /> Its recipe is a short stretch inside a mitochondrial gene, which is why it is called a mitochondrial-derived peptide.<Cite id="lee-2015-mots-c" />

A company, CohBar, developed an altered version, CB4211, and tested it in a phase 1a/1b study from 2018 to 2021.<Cite id="ctgov-nct03998514,sec-cohbar-2021-cb4211-topline" /> In 2023 it said the formulation was not suitable for further development. It announced that it would liquidate and dissolve the company.<Cite id="sec-cohbar-2023-s4,sec-cohbar-2023-11-8k" />

</InDetail>

</Section>

<Section id="mechanism">

<Lead question="How does it work?">In cells and mice it switches on an energy sensor. Its direct target is unknown.</Lead>

<Mechanism />

<InDetail title="What each step rests on">

The energy-sensor link comes from the discovering group's cell work. MOTS-c slowed two linked processes in cells. AICAR, a natural switch for AMPK, then built up.<Cite id="lee-2015-mots-c" /> The FDA notes that all studies were in rodents or cells. No dose–response was tested in living animals, and the molecular target is unknown.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing" /> An independent group in China reported less weight gain in mice whose ovaries had been removed, and AMPK again seemed involved.<Cite id="lu-2019-mots-c-ovariectomy" />

</InDetail>

</Section>

<Section id="evidence">

<Lead question="Does it work?">Unknown in people. In mice it lowered blood sugar and weight gain.</Lead>

<KeyNumber title="Mice on a high-fat diet gained less weight" value="about 20%" label="lower body weight than untreated mice after 8 weeks" who="Lee 2015: male mice on a high-fat diet, daily injections into the belly (FDA summary)" source="fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing,lee-2015-mots-c" level="animal" />

<KeyNumber title="The first trial of MOTS-c in people is still running" value="120" label="adults with slightly high blood sugar, half on a dummy drug; results after early 2027" who="MOTS-MET, phase 2a, one site in China, recruiting since February 2026" source="ctgov-nct07505745" />

The animal work is large and comes from many laboratories.<Cite id="pubmed-mots-c-count-2026-10-22" /> In people, studies so far only measure the body's own MOTS-c. A blood test widely used for that may not be reliable.<Cite id="knoop-2019-mots-c-detection" />

<InDetail title="Every study in one table">

<StudyTable />

In mice, MOTS-c improved insulin sensitivity and running performance, and when started late in life it improved physical capacity.<Cite id="lee-2015-mots-c,reynolds-2021-mots-c-exercise" /> In 10 healthy young men, a session on an exercise bike raised their own MOTS-c; nobody was given the peptide.<Cite id="reynolds-2021-mots-c-exercise" /> The two lead authors of the discovery work were consultants for CohBar.<Cite id="lee-2015-mots-c,reynolds-2021-mots-c-exercise" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="The company version, CB4211: what was reported">

The company reported its results in a press release and slides, not in a journal. In 65 healthy adults and then 23 people with fatty liver, it reported no serious adverse events. Liver fat fell by about the same amount with CB4211 and with placebo. Some liver enzymes and fasting glucose fell more with the drug.<Cite id="sec-cohbar-2021-cb4211-topline" /> The study was paused to change the formulation, because drug stayed at the injection site.<Cite id="sec-cohbar-2021-cb4211-slides" /> No results are posted on the trial registry.<Cite id="ctgov-nct03998514" />

</InDetail>

</Section>

<InDetail title="The UK, Canada and Australia">

- **United Kingdom.** The MHRA's database of licensed medicines has no MOTS-c product.<Cite id="mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23" /> It is not a controlled drug under the Misuse of Drugs Act.<Cite id="uk-mda-1971-sch-2" />
- **Canada.** No MOTS-c product has a Drug Identification Number (DIN), the number every authorised drug carries.<Cite id="ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-extract-search-merge-2026-10-24" /> Health Canada says injectable peptides are generally regulated as prescription drugs. It seized MOTS-c from a clinic and from sellers in 2025, and named it in its April 2026 warning about peptides bought online.<Cite id="ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides,ca-hc-recalls-search-status-d-2026-10-23" />
- **Australia.** MOTS-c is not in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG), so it is not approved. The Poisons Standard does not name it, so it has no schedule.<Cite id="au-artg-search-status-d-2026-10-23,au-poisons-standard-2026-10" /> A product that is not in the ARTG still may not be imported or supplied unless an exemption applies.<Cite id="au-tg-act-s19b-19d-offences,au-tga-2026-04-peptide-responsibilities" /> Since June 2026 the regulator, the TGA, has made unapproved peptides a priority for its checks.<Cite id="au-tga-2026-06-peptide-compliance-priority" /> Sport Integrity Australia names MOTS-c among the peptides banned in sport.<Cite id="au-sia-peptides" />

More: the law in [the US](/en/legal/us/), [the UK](/en/legal/uk/), [Canada](/en/legal/ca/) and [Australia](/en/legal/au/).

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="The July 2026 US vote, point by point">

- On 23 July 2026 the FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) voted 8–6 with one abstention to recommend BPC-157 for the 503A bulk drug substances list (count as reported from the public meeting).<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-07-meeting,fda-pcac-2026-questions" />
- The committee considered seven peptides, including MOTS-c, which the FDA reviewed for obesity and osteoporosis.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-07-meeting,fr-2026-07361-pcac-notice" /> As reported from the meeting, it recommended six: BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Semax and epitalon; emideltide (DSIP) was not recommended. The FDA asked for separate votes on the free base and the acetate salt of each.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-questions" /> The FDA had posted no minutes by 23 October 2026, so the counts and outcome above are as reported from the public meeting.
- The vote is advisory and non-binding, and it went against FDA staff's own recommendation not to list MOTS-c.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-briefing-intro" />
- Legal compounding remains prohibited until the FDA formally adopts the recommendation and completes rulemaking.<Cite id="fda-503a-bulks-page" />
- None of the six it recommended is an approved medicine in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia (Semax is registered as a medicine in Russia). A 503A listing would allow pharmacy compounding on prescription in the US. It is not a marketing authorisation, says nothing about efficacy, and changes nothing outside the US.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-presentations-day1,fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing,mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23,uk-mhra-substance-index-status-c-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-c-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-d-2026-10-23" />

<Section id="risks">

<Lead question="What are the risks?">Its effects in people are unknown. The product itself may be the biggest risk.</Lead>

<Compare title="Several harms are possible. None has been studied properly in people." detail="What the FDA review and the company's own trial summary suggest, as of 22 October 2026.">

| Possible harm | Shown in people? |
|---|---|
| Infection from germs or bacterial toxins in the product | <Verdict is="unknown" /> The FDA found no published tests for them.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing" /> |
| An immune reaction to the peptide or its impurities | <Verdict is="unknown" /> The FDA cannot rule it out for injections.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing" /> |
| Reactions where it is injected | <Verdict is="yes" /> Common with the company version, CB4211.<Cite id="sec-cohbar-2021-cb4211-slides" /> |
| Blood sugar falling too low | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Blood sugar fell by about half in obese mice.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing" /> |
| Harm from long-term use | <Verdict is="unknown" /> No toxicity studies of any kind exist.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing" /> |

</Compare>

<InDetail title="What has been reported, and why the gaps matter">

The FDA's adverse-event database held no reports for MOTS-c up to March 2025. US compounding pharmacies generally do not report adverse events to the FDA, so this says little.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing" /> In the company's CB4211 study, the company reported injection-site reactions in 79% of people on the drug and 33% on placebo.<Cite id="sec-cohbar-2021-cb4211-slides" /> Because the FDA does not know what MOTS-c binds, it cannot predict which organs it affects. No studies have looked at genetic damage, cancer or effects on pregnancy.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing" />

</InDetail>

<Section id="harm-reduction">

<Lead question="How can someone lower the risk?">Check the vial first, bring a doctor in, and know when to stop.</Lead>

<ProductCheck extra="Check which form the label names. MOTS-c is sold as a free base or an acetate salt. The FDA treats them as different substances." extraSources="fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing" />

### When to stop and get help

<Callout type="stop" title="Stop and get help if" id="stop-now">
- the injection site becomes hot, red, swollen or painful, or you get a fever (infection or contamination);
- you feel shaky, sweaty, confused or very hungry, especially if you also use insulin or diabetes tablets (blood sugar may be too low; take something sugary if you can swallow safely);
- you get hives, swelling of the face or throat, or difficulty breathing (an allergic reaction).

If it is severe, call your local emergency number.
</Callout>

<Cite id="nhs-cellulitis,nhs-low-blood-sugar,nhs-anaphylaxis,fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing" />

<WarningSigns only="general,injection,allergic,metabolic" />

<InDetail title="Checks that make sense, and who should not use it">

A doctor can help with these checks (see [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/)):

- **Blood sugar**, if you use insulin or diabetes tablets, because MOTS-c lowered blood sugar in mice.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing" />
- **Anti-doping rules**, if you compete: MOTS-c is banned at all times, and an anti-doping laboratory has a test for it.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en,knoop-2019-mots-c-detection" />

Pregnancy and breastfeeding are reasons not to use it: there are no studies on effects in pregnancy.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-mots-c-briefing" /> See [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/) and [sterile technique](/en/safety/sterile-technique/).

</InDetail>

You can report a side effect yourself: to [FDA MedWatch](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medwatch-fda-safety-information-and-adverse-event-reporting-program) in the US, the [Yellow Card scheme](https://yellowcard.mhra.gov.uk/) in the UK, Health Canada's [Canada Vigilance Program](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medeffect-canada/adverse-reaction-reporting.html) or the [TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/safety/report-problem/report-adverse-event-or-safety-problem) in Australia.<Cite id="fda-medwatch,mhra-yellow-card,ca-hc-medeffect-reporting,ca-hc-canada-vigilance,au-tga-report-adverse-event" />
