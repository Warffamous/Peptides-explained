<Section id="what-it-is">

<Lead question="What is SS-31?">A tiny lab-made peptide aimed at mitochondria. In the US it is a medicine for one rare disease.</Lead>

<Compare title="The US medicine and an online 'SS-31' vial share a name, not a guarantee." detail="What is known about each, from the FDA label, regulators' warnings and medicine registers, checked on 23 October 2026.">

</Compare>

SS stands for Szeto-Schiller, after the two researchers who designed this family of peptides.<Cite id="pubchem-elamipretide,zhao-2004-ss31-mitochondria" /> Barth syndrome is a rare inherited disease, mostly of boys and men, that can weaken the heart and muscles.<Cite id="fda-2025-forzinity-approval,reid-thompson-2021-tazpower" />

<InDetail title="The molecule, and how it was approved">

<AuthorisedProducts />

SS-31 is <Seq>D-Arg-Dmt-Lys-Phe-NH2</Seq>: four building blocks, two of them changed in the lab. The first is the mirror-image form of arginine; the second, Dmt, is tyrosine with two extra methyl groups. The formula of the free peptide is C32H49N9O5, mass <Q>639.8 g/mol</Q>.<Cite id="fda-label-forzinity-2025,pubchem-elamipretide" /> The design alternates ring-shaped and positively charged building blocks, which lets it slip into cells.<Cite id="zhao-2004-ss31-mitochondria" />

The developer, Stealth BioTherapeutics, applied to the FDA in January 2024. On 10 October 2024 an FDA advisory committee voted 10 to 6 that it is effective for Barth syndrome, while noting "significant uncertainty".<Cite id="fda-crdac-2024-10-elamipretide-minutes" /> The FDA did not approve it at first: on 15 May 2025 it sent a letter that did not approve it. The company replied in full in August, and the FDA granted accelerated approval on 19 September 2025.<Cite id="fda-2025-forzinity-approval-letter" /> Accelerated approval is based on a measure "reasonably likely to predict" benefit, here the strength of the muscle that straightens the knee, not on proof that patients feel or function better.<Cite id="fda-2025-forzinity-approval" />

</InDetail>

</Section>

<Section id="mechanism">

<Lead question="How does it work?">It sticks to cardiolipin, a fat in the inner wall of mitochondria. How this helps patients is unclear.</Lead>

<Mechanism />

<InDetail title="What each step rests on">

The first two steps come from cell, isolated-organ and rat studies, mostly by the inventors.<Cite id="zhao-2004-ss31-mitochondria,birk-2013-ss31-cardiolipin" /> In Barth syndrome, a faulty gene changes cardiolipin itself, which is why this disease was a logical target.<Cite id="reid-thompson-2021-tazpower" /> That a molecule binds the right fat does not show that patients get stronger: the trials below are the test of that.

</InDetail>

</Section>

<Lead question="Does it work?">The proof is weak and comes from a few patients. Bigger trials in other diseases showed no benefit.</Lead>

The FDA used its accelerated route, meant for serious diseases without good treatment, and accepted a measure that may predict benefit. A new randomised trial in 48 patients started in 2026.<Cite id="fda-2025-forzinity-approval,ctgov-nct07531251" />

<InDetail title="Every study in one table">

<StudyTable />

Trials in other conditions were negative: one hour of the drug during a stent procedure for a heart attack did not reduce heart damage, and 4 weeks in 71 people with heart failure did not improve the heart's pumping.<Cite id="gibson-2016-embrace-stemi,butler-2020-progress-hf" /> A phase 3 trial in dry age-related macular degeneration, an eye disease, is still running.<Cite id="ctgov-nct06373731" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Who paid for the evidence">

Every clinical trial we found was funded by Stealth BioTherapeutics; in the Barth syndrome trial the company helped design it and analyse the data.<Cite id="reid-thompson-2021-tazpower,karaa-2023-mmpower-3,pubmed-elamipretide-count-2026-10-23" /> The animal research is broader: most of it comes from groups other than the inventors.<Cite id="pubmed-elamipretide-count-2026-10-23" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="What the US label warns about">

- **Allergic reactions.** Serious reactions needing emergency treatment have been reported, with rash, bumps, eczema-like skin or cough, from minutes to months after starting. After a serious reaction it must not be used again.<Cite id="fda-label-forzinity-2025" />
- **White blood cells.** Eosinophils, white cells involved in allergy, often rose, peaking around 90 days, without symptoms.<Cite id="fda-label-forzinity-2025" />
- **Kidneys.** The body clears the drug through the urine, so blood levels are higher when the kidneys work poorly. The label lowers the dose for severe kidney disease.<Cite id="fda-label-forzinity-2025" />
- **Newborn babies.** The preservative, benzyl alcohol, can harm newborns; the medicine is not approved for them.<Cite id="fda-label-forzinity-2025" />
- **Unknowns.** No cancer studies in animals had been done when it was approved; the FDA requires them now.<Cite id="fda-label-forzinity-2025,fda-2025-forzinity-approval-letter" /> There are no data in pregnancy, and the longest use in the trials was 192 weeks, in 3 patients.<Cite id="fda-label-forzinity-2025" />

A product bought as a research chemical adds risks the label does not cover: unknown content, strength and sterility.<Cite id="fda-warning-letters-index" /> See [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/).

</InDetail>

<Section id="harm-reduction">

<Lead question="How can someone lower the risk?">The US medicine has the fewest unknowns. Check any other product first, and know when to stop.</Lead>

<ProductCheck extra="A vial sold as 'SS-31' for research is not the US medicine. The FDA has warned sellers of such vials." extraSources="fda-warning-letters-index,fda-label-forzinity-2025" />

### When to stop and get help

<Callout type="stop" title="Stop and get help if" id="stop-now">
- you get hives, swelling of the face or throat, or difficulty breathing (a serious allergic reaction);
- the injection site becomes hot, red, swollen or painful (possible infection);
- you get a fever or chills, or feel very ill, after an injection.

If it is severe, call your local emergency number.
</Callout>

<Cite id="fda-label-forzinity-2025,nhs-anaphylaxis,nhs-cellulitis,nhs-sepsis" />

<WarningSigns only="general,injection,allergic" />

<InDetail title="Checks that make sense, and who should be careful">

A doctor can help you decide which checks fit you (see [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/)):

- **Kidney function**, because the drug leaves the body through the kidneys.<Cite id="fda-label-forzinity-2025" />
- **A blood count**, because eosinophils often rose on treatment.<Cite id="fda-label-forzinity-2025" />
- **Any rash that spreads or does not settle**, because allergic reactions can start months later.<Cite id="fda-label-forzinity-2025" />

There are no data in pregnancy or breastfeeding.<Cite id="fda-label-forzinity-2025" /> For injecting, see [sterile technique](/en/safety/sterile-technique/).

</InDetail>
