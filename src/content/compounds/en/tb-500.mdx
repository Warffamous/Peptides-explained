TB-500 is promoted online for tendon, muscle, ligament and bone injuries, wounds, inflammation, hair growth, heart health and much else.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> That reputation borrows almost entirely from research on the whole protein. It is also sold in blends with BPC-157.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> See the [BPC-157 page](/en/compounds/bpc-157/).

<InDetail title="The molecule, and two molecules that share one name">

TB-500 is a synthetic peptide of seven amino acids, <Seq>Ac-Leu-Lys-Lys-Thr-Glu-Thr-Gln-OH</Seq>, with an acetyl group on its first amino acid. Its formula is C38H68N10O14 and its mass is <Q>889.0 Da</Q>.<Cite id="pubchem-tb-500,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> It is a copy of positions 17 to 23 of thymosin beta-4, a natural protein of 43 amino acids.<Cite id="esposito-2012-tb500-identity,ho-2012-equine-tb500,chembl-timbetasin" />

### Two molecules, one name

The confusion around TB-500 starts with its name. Several websites, the FDA notes, describe TB-500 as a synthetic version of thymosin beta-4 and use the two names as if they were the same; a 2026 journal article calls it "synthetic thymosin beta-4" too.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,bicer-2026-achilles" /> The FDA's 2026 review says plainly that thymosin beta-4 and TB-500 "are not the same substance".<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> The difference is large: one is a whole protein, the other a lab-made copy of a short piece from its middle.

<InDetail title="Where it came from">

Thymosin beta-4 was first sequenced in 1981 from calf thymus, which is where the name comes from.<Cite id="low-1981-sequence" /> It was first thought to be a thymus hormone, but it is present in large amounts inside many kinds of cells, where it holds single units of actin.<Cite id="safer-1991-actin-sequestering,mannherz-2009-beta-thymosins" /> In 2003 an NIH group reported that a peptide copying positions 17 to 23 could, on its own, help wounds close in old mice.<Cite id="philp-2003-lkktetq-wounds" /> According to the FDA, TB-500 appears to have been made first in that work. A veterinary preparation called TB-500 appeared in about 2011, aimed at racehorses and racing greyhounds.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,ho-2012-equine-tb500" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="How it is made, and why the label means little">

TB-500 can be made by solid-phase peptide synthesis, the usual method for short peptides: amino acids are joined one by one on resin beads, the last one is acetylated, and the chain is cut free.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,esposito-2012-tb500-identity" /> Each step can leave shortened or wrongly built chains behind. It is sold as a free base or as an acetate salt, which the FDA treats as two different substances.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" />

The bigger problem is identity. The FDA found "multiple salts, and derivatives, including different active moieties" sold under the same common name.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> A French horse-racing laboratory reported that products sold as TB500 or TB1000 did not consistently contain what their descriptions said, and the same laboratory noted in 2025 that many products online claim to contain either the acetylated fragment "or TB4 itself".<Cite id="delcourt-2023-misbranded,delcourt-2025-equine-tb4" /> The anti-doping laboratory in Cologne found thymosin beta-4 among confiscated black-market products.<Cite id="krug-2014-black-market-germany" /> So a vial labelled TB-500 may contain the 7-amino-acid fragment, the whole 43-amino-acid protein, something else, or a mixture.

</InDetail>

### What the whole protein does in a cell

Inside cells, actin exists as single units and as long threads built from them. Cells grow and shrink these threads to change shape and to crawl. <Claim level="in-vitro">Thymosin beta-4 binds single actin units one-to-one and stops them joining threads</Claim>. In resting human platelets at least half of the actin is free of threads, and most of that is held by thymosin beta-4.<Cite id="safer-1991-actin-sequestering" /> The protein acts as a stock of building blocks that the cell can release when it needs to move.<Cite id="mannherz-2009-beta-thymosins" />

The body already has a great deal of it. In human blood it sits almost entirely inside white cells and platelets; plasma holds less than 1% of the amount in whole blood. When blood is left to clot in a tube, cells release it and the level in the serum rises about fifty-fold within a day.<Cite id="hannappel-1987-blood-levels" /> The originating research group proposed that platelets release it at a wound, where it helps start repair.<Cite id="treadwell-2012-dermal-review,goldstein-2005-moonlights" /> Outside cells, <Claim level="in-vitro">the whole protein made human blood-vessel cells move faster through a pathway involving ATP synthase on the cell surface</Claim>.<Cite id="freeman-2011-atp-synthase" />

### Step 1: does TB-500 grip actin?

TB-500 contains the stretch LKKTET, the part of the protein that sits on actin.<Cite id="sosne-2010-active-sites,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> That makes it plausible that the fragment binds actin too. But the FDA's 2026 review calls this a hypothesis, and says it "remains to be determined" whether actin binding explains any effect of the fragment.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> The added acetyl group matters as well. Acetylation changes a peptide's charge, shape and lifespan, so, the FDA warns, results with the unacetylated LKKTETQ "cannot be directly extrapolated" to TB-500.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" />

### Step 2: it is broken down quickly

Doping laboratories have studied what happens to TB-500 because they need to detect it. <Claim level="animal">After an injection under the skin in horses, blood levels peaked within 1 to 2 hours and could not be measured after 6 to 10 hours</Claim>.<Cite id="ho-2012-equine-tb500,kwok-2013-horse-peptides,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> <Claim level="animal">In horses and rats, and in human serum, liver and kidney samples, enzymes cut it into shorter pieces, mostly by trimming its end: Ac-LKKTE, Ac-LKK, Ac-LK and others</Claim>.<Cite id="ho-2012-equine-tb500,rahaman-2024-tb500-metabolites,zvereva-2016-peptide-metabolism,esposito-2015-in-vitro-metabolism" /> No study has measured TB-500 in human blood.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" />

### Steps 3 and 4: cells and tissue

<Claim level="in-vitro">The whole protein made skin cells crawl faster in a lab chamber</Claim>.<Cite id="malinda-1999-wound-healing" /> For TB-500 itself the picture is mixed. <Claim level="in-vitro">It did not close scratch "wounds" in a layer of fibroblasts, but its breakdown product Ac-LKKTE closed them slightly</Claim>, so any healing effect may come from that product.<Cite id="rahaman-2024-tb500-metabolites" /> <Claim level="in-vitro">The LKKTETQ peptide reduced growth-factor-driven activation and movement of scar-forming liver cells</Claim>, the opposite direction to the wound story,<Cite id="shah-2018-stellate-cells" /> and <Claim level="in-vitro">made mast cells release their contents</Claim>.<Cite id="wyczolkowska-2007-mast-cells" /> The few animal results, including mouse skin wounds and rat tendons, are discussed under the evidence below.<Cite id="philp-2003-lkktetq-wounds,bicer-2026-achilles" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="What TB-500 cannot do, and the protein's disputed natural role">

Some of thymosin beta-4's reported actions depend on a part that TB-500 does not have.<Cite id="sosne-2010-active-sites" /> The body cuts the protein's first four amino acids off as the peptide Ac-SDKP. The enzyme prolyl oligopeptidase does this, and the enzyme ACE breaks Ac-SDKP down again.<Cite id="grillon-1990-acsdkp,cavasin-2004-pop-acsdkp" /> <Claim level="animal">Ac-SDKP reduced scarring of the heart and kidneys in rats with high blood pressure</Claim>.<Cite id="cavasin-2004-pop-acsdkp" /> TB-500 starts at position 17, so it cannot produce Ac-SDKP, and any benefit that depends on Ac-SDKP does not apply to it. The heart-cell survival signal described below was found with the whole protein; whether the fragment can trigger it is unknown.<Cite id="bock-marquette-2004-cardiac" />

The whole protein's natural role is also disputed. A 2007 study reported severe heart-vessel defects in mice with reduced thymosin beta-4,<Cite id="smart-2007-epicardial,banerjee-2012-dispensable" /> but <Claim level="animal">mice bred with none at all were born with normal hearts and blood vessels</Claim>.<Cite id="banerjee-2012-dispensable,banerjee-2013-vascular" /> What extra protein does in an experiment may not reflect what the body's own protein is for.

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Cell and animal studies, one by one">

### TB-500 and its core in cells and animals

The FDA found no animal study of TB-500's effect on wounds in 2026, and only one cell study: the negative scratch test above.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,rahaman-2024-tb500-metabolites" /> The founding study, from the NIH group that also developed the whole protein, used the core without its acetyl group. <Claim level="animal">In 26-month-old mice, LKKTETQ on small skin wounds increased closure and collagen at day 7, much like the whole protein</Claim>.<Cite id="philp-2003-lkktetq-wounds" /> The FDA notes that only one strength was tested and that the result cannot simply be carried over to TB-500.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" />

In 2026 a Turkish team tested a product called TB-500 on an injury in rats. They cut and repaired the Achilles tendons of 32 rats and gave BPC-157, TB-500, both or an inactive injection for four weeks. <Claim level="animal">The TB-500 group had a higher load to failure than controls (median 37.4 against 26.9 newtons) and better tendon-structure scores</Claim>; adding BPC-157 gave nothing extra. The pathologist did not know the groups, but only four tendons per group went to each test, and the product came from a research-chemical seller with no reported sequence or purity.<Cite id="bicer-2026-achilles" /> A Chinese group reported that, in a mouse model of Alzheimer's disease, <Claim level="animal">peptides it called TB500 and Ac-SDKP reduced brain inflammation and improved memory tests</Claim> (mouse numbers not in the abstract).<Cite id="ou-2026-alzheimer-mice" /> None of these results has been repeated.

### The whole protein in animals

The originating group reported in 1999 that <Claim level="animal">thymosin beta-4, put on skin wounds or injected into the abdomen of rats, increased new skin cover by 42% at day 4 and up to 61% at day 7</Claim>.<Cite id="malinda-1999-wound-healing" /> An independent laboratory in Texas reported in 2004 that, after tying off a heart artery in mice, <Claim level="animal">thymosin beta-4 switched on the cell-survival signals integrin-linked kinase and Akt, more heart-muscle cells survived early, and heart function was better</Claim>.<Cite id="bock-marquette-2004-cardiac" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="The whole protein in people: injection, skin gel and eye drops">

The US company RegeneRx developed thymosin beta-4 in three forms: a skin gel (RGN-137), eye drops (RGN-259, whose later trials were sponsored by the company ReGenTree) and an injection (RGN-352).<Cite id="chembl-timbetasin,crockford-2007-regenerx,ctgov-nct01311518,ctgov-nct02974907" /> A Chinese company later made a genetically engineered version, NL005.<Cite id="wang-2021-rhtb4-phase1" />

**Injection.** <Claim level="human-controlled">In 40 healthy volunteers given intravenous thymosin beta-4 or placebo for up to 14 days, side effects were infrequent and mild or moderate, with no serious adverse events</Claim>.<Cite id="ruff-2010-phase1-iv" /> The planned phase 2 heart-attack trial was withdrawn before it enrolled anyone.<Cite id="ctgov-nct01311518" /> <Claim level="human-controlled">In 84 healthy volunteers given NL005 or placebo, there were no serious adverse events</Claim>.<Cite id="wang-2021-rhtb4-phase1" /> In a randomised, placebo-controlled trial in 96 people after a major heart attack, <Claim level="human-controlled">the amount of dead heart muscle did not differ overall from placebo</Claim>. It was smaller in a subgroup of 43 who received the first dose within 8 hours, but a positive subgroup in a trial that missed its main result can be chance.<Cite id="zhang-2025-stemi" /> Two other phase 2 heart-attack trials of the company's recombinant protein, with 62 and 90 people, finished in 2021 and 2023 and have posted no results on the register.<Cite id="ctgov-nct05485818,ctgov-nct05984134" />

**Skin gel.** In two phase 2 trials, <Claim level="human-controlled">8 of 54 people with pressure ulcers healed on the gel against 3 of 18 on placebo, and 12 of 55 with venous leg ulcers against 4 of 17</Claim>: no better than placebo.<Cite id="ctgov-nct00382174,ctgov-nct00832091,guarnera-2010-venous-ulcers" /> The developers' review says the gel sped healing "by almost a month in those patients that did heal", a comparison limited to the people who healed.<Cite id="treadwell-2012-dermal-review" /> In 30 people with the fragile-skin disease epidermolysis bullosa, <Claim level="human-controlled">8 of 22 healed on the gel and 5 of 8 on placebo</Claim>; that trial stopped early, and a later one stopped after 4 patients.<Cite id="ctgov-nct00311766,ctgov-nct03578029" />

**Eye drops.** Nine people with eye wounds that would not heal were treated on compassionate grounds, and the larger wounds healed in 6.<Cite id="dunn-2010-neurotrophic-cases" /> In the controlled trials:

- <Claim level="human-controlled">In 72 people with dry eye, neither main outcome differed from placebo</Claim>, although some secondary measures did.<Cite id="sosne-2015-cae-dry-eye" />
- <Claim level="human-controlled">In 9 people with severe dry eye, symptom and staining scores were lower than with placebo at several time points</Claim>, in a trial whose main goal was safety.<Cite id="sosne-2015-severe-dry-eye" />
- In ARISE-2, with 601 people, <Claim level="human-controlled">neither main outcome favoured the drops</Claim>.<Cite id="ctgov-nct02974907" />
- In ARISE-3, with 700 people, <Claim level="human-controlled">neither main outcome was better than with placebo</Claim>. Its main results were collected by November 2020 but posted only on 28 August 2026.<Cite id="ctgov-nct03937882" /> ARISE-1, with 317 people, ended in 2016 and has posted none.<Cite id="ctgov-nct02597803" />
- <Claim level="human-controlled">In 12 people with diabetes whose corneal surface was removed during eye surgery, all wounds healed by day 14 with drops or with placebo</Claim>; the trial stopped early for slow recruitment.<Cite id="ctgov-nct00598871" />
- In SEER-1, in the rare eye disease neurotrophic keratopathy, <Claim level="human-controlled">the wound healed completely in 6 of 10 people on the drops and 1 of 8 on placebo</Claim>. The difference was not statistically significant, and the trial stopped early for a "business decision".<Cite id="sosne-2022-seer1,ctgov-nct02600429" /> A 2025 Cochrane review rated this evidence low-certainty.<Cite id="kruoch-2025-cochrane-nk" />

We found no journal paper for any of the three ARISE trials; the register records are the only public account of their results.

</InDetail>

### Human reports involving products called TB-500

In a Florida knee-pain phone survey, 4 of 16 people had received BPC-157 with an undefined product called "TB4", and 3 of those 4 reported relief.<Cite id="lee-2021-knee" /> In 2026, a US dental team described three patients with numbness after a jaw-nerve injury who had weekly gum injections of five drugs, including TB-500. <InDetail title="The United States, and the July 2026 vote point by point">

TB-500 is not FDA-approved, is part of no approved drug and has no official US monograph, so a pharmacy could legally compound with it under section 503A only if it were placed on the 503A bulks list through rulemaking.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-503a-bulks-page" /> The FDA had earlier listed "thymosin beta-4, fragment (LKKTETQ), also known as TB-500" among substances that may present significant safety risks (Category 2), citing immunogenicity, impurities and the absence of human data. It now appears among substances whose nominations were withdrawn.<Cite id="fda-category-2-safety-risks,fda-503a-categories-2026-05" />

The FDA evaluated TB-500 anyway, for wound healing. Its staff proposed that neither the free base nor the acetate be listed: the substance is not well characterised, there is no evidence of effectiveness, and its safety in people is unknown.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-briefing-intro" /> What happened next is widely misreported:

- On 23 July 2026 the FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) voted 8–6 with one abstention to recommend BPC-157 for the 503A bulk drug substances list (as reported from the public meeting). TB-500 was discussed in the afternoon of the same day.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-07-meeting,fda-pcac-2026-briefing-intro" /> As reported from the meeting, the TB-500 vote had the same 8–6 split with one abstention.
- The committee considered seven peptides over two days.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-07-meeting,fda-pcac-2026-briefing-intro" /> As reported from the meeting, it recommended six: BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Semax and epitalon. Emideltide (DSIP) was not recommended.
- The vote is advisory and non-binding, and it went against the FDA staff's own recommendation.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-briefing-intro" />
- Legal compounding remains prohibited until the FDA formally adopts the recommendation and completes rulemaking.<Cite id="fda-503a-bulks-page" />
- None of these is an approved medicine in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia (Semax is registered as a medicine in Russia). A 503A listing, if it ever happens, would allow pharmacy compounding on prescription in the US. It is not a marketing authorisation, says nothing about efficacy, and changes nothing outside the US.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-presentations-day1,fda-pcac-2026-briefing-intro,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing,fda-503a-bulks-page,uk-mhra-substance-index-status-c-2026-10-23,mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-c-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-d-2026-10-23" />

The voting questions asked about TB-500 free base and TB-500 acetate separately.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-questions" /> The FDA had not posted minutes by 20 October 2026, so the counts above are as reported from the public meeting. By the same date it had not proposed a rule to add TB-500 to the list.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-07-meeting,federalregister-503a-search-2026-10-20" /> Even a listing would not make compounded TB-500 an FDA-checked product: compounded drugs are not FDA-approved, and adverse events need not be reported to the FDA.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-presentations-day1" /> We found no FDA approval or licence for the whole protein, thymosin beta-4, either.<Cite id="us-fda-drugsfda-search-compounds-c-2026-10-24,us-fda-purple-book-search-compounds-c-2026-10-24" />

### United Kingdom

No medicine containing TB-500 or thymosin beta-4 has a UK licence. The MHRA, the UK medicines regulator, lists neither in its index of licensed medicines (checked 23 October 2026), and the FDA also found no approved TB-500 product in the UK.<Cite id="uk-mhra-substance-index-status-c-2026-10-23,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> Selling or supplying a medicine that has no UK licence is against the law (Human Medicines Regulations 2012, regulation 46).<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-46" /> For what a "research use only" label does and does not change, see [research use only](/en/legal/research-use-only/).

We found no newer official statement.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-17,mhra-foi-22-431-personal-import-seizures" />

### Canada

Health Canada has not authorised TB-500 or thymosin beta-4. It says that unauthorised drugs like these "have not been assessed for safety, efficacy, and quality".<Cite id="ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides" /> No product containing either appears in Health Canada's drug databases (checked 23 October 2026), and the FDA also found no approved TB-500 product in Canada.<Cite id="ca-dpd-search-status-c-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,ca-noc-search-status-c-2026-10-23,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> In Canadian law, no one may sell or advertise a new drug until Health Canada has issued a "notice of compliance", and a manufacturer may not sell a drug in dosage form without a Drug Identification Number (DIN), the eight-digit number on an authorised product's label.<Cite id="ca-food-and-drug-regulations,ca-fdr-c08-001-new-drug,ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides" /> Health Canada also says that "peptides are generally regulated as prescription drugs" in Canada, and that a "research use only" label does not make these products legal.<Cite id="ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides" />

Health Canada names TB-500 among unauthorised injectable peptides it has seized, in an April 2026 public warning and in seizure notices from 2025.<Cite id="ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides,ca-hc-recalls-search-status-c-2026-10-23" /> Some of those notices write "Thymosin-β4 (TB4 or TB-500)", using TB-500 as another name for the whole protein.<Cite id="ca-hc-recalls-search-status-c-2026-10-23" /> Border officers can hold a parcel and pass it to Health Canada, which can refuse it entry or seize it. Health Canada says it works with the Canada Border Services Agency to stop shipments of unauthorised peptides.<Cite id="ca-cbsa-d19-9-1,ca-hc-gui-0116-personal-import,ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides" />

### Australia

Neither TB-500 nor thymosin beta-4 is on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG), the public list of therapeutic goods that may be supplied in Australia (checked 23 October 2026).<Cite id="au-artg-search-status-c-2026-10-23,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> The TGA, Australia's medicines regulator, names TB-500 as an example of an unapproved peptide product.<Cite id="au-tga-2026-04-peptide-responsibilities" /> A peptide product regulated as a therapeutic good must be on the ARTG before it can be imported, made, supplied or advertised, with limited exceptions.<Cite id="au-tga-how-we-regulate-peptides" />

Since 1 June 2016 TB-500 and thymosin beta-4 have both been named in Schedule 4 of the Poisons Standard, the national list that sorts medicines by how tightly they are controlled. Schedule 4 means "prescription only medicine". Both are also on the Poisons Standard's list of substances that must not be possessed without authority, such as a prescription (Appendix D).<Cite id="au-poisons-standard-2026-10,au-poisons-standard-2016-06,au-poisons-standard-2016-03" /> The Poisons Standard does not say which molecule "TB-500" means, but the whole protein has its own, identical entry. The TGA proposed these entries in 2015 "to control the manner in which they are advertised, sold and accessed without legitimate purpose".<Cite id="au-poisons-standard-2026-10,au-tga-2015-10-tb500-scheduling-consultation" />

These rules take effect through state and territory law, which differs. In Victoria, having any Schedule 4 medicine without authority is an offence. In New South Wales, TB-500 and thymosin beta-4 are on the state's own list of prescription medicines that people may not have without authority, such as a prescription.<Cite id="au-poisons-standard-2026-10,au-vic-dpcs-act-1981,au-nsw-health-s4d,au-nsw-health-s4d-2026,au-nsw-ptg-act-1966" /> We have not checked the other states and territories.

Bringing in a Schedule 4 medicine for your own use needs, among other conditions, an Australian doctor's prescription or written authority, and packaging that shows what it is. The TGA does not release vials that are unlabelled or marked only with codes, and the Australian Border Force seizes and destroys them.<Cite id="au-tg-regulations-1990-sch-5-item-1,au-tga-personal-importation-scheme,au-tga-2026-04-peptide-responsibilities,au-tga-2026-05-peptide-import-warning" /> In August 2026 the TGA and NSW Police seized products that included a combination product of BPC-157 and TB-500.<Cite id="au-tga-2026-08-peptide-seizure" /> The TGA has received reports of harm, such as severe allergic reactions, linked to unapproved peptide products as a group. The reports are not broken down by compound.<Cite id="au-tga-2026-04-peptide-responsibilities,au-tga-2026-06-cmo-peptide-statement" />

TB-500 and thymosin beta-4 are prohibited in sport at all times, and methods to detect TB-500 and its breakdown products have been published.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en,ho-2012-equine-tb500,rahaman-2024-tb500-metabolites" />

<InDetail title="What has been reported in people">

No harm from TB-500 alone has been published. The FDA's adverse-event database held no TB-500 reports up to 26 March 2025, and its food and supplement complaint system held two reports on blended TB-500 and BPC-157 products, neither with a safety assessment.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> That silence does not mean TB-500 is harmless: reporting is voluntary, and the FDA does not receive all reports.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" />

One report, found in the FDA's separate review of BPC-157, shows what can happen with blends. A 40-year-old woman injected a product containing BPC-157 and TB-500, labelled "for research purposes only". Within a week her skin darkened all over and her gums turned dark. The reaction came back when she started again, and stopped when she gave up all peptides. Because the product contained two peptides, the FDA could not say which one caused it.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" />

In trials of the whole protein, side effects were usually similar to placebo.<Cite id="ruff-2010-phase1-iv,ctgov-nct02974907,wang-2021-rhtb4-phase1" /> In ARISE-3, 47 of 350 people on the drops reported other adverse events against 31 of 349 on placebo, and in the Chinese volunteers antibodies against the protein appeared briefly in 2 of the 82 people tested.<Cite id="ctgov-nct03937882,wang-2021-rhtb4-phase1" /> These data come from a different molecule, mostly put on the eye or skin, for days or weeks.

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Why an effect on cancer is theorised">

The short animation shows why the vessel growth that may help healing is also the reason for the cancer worry.

<Explainer id="angiogenesis-double-edge" />

The worry follows from what thymosin beta-4 does, in four steps:

1. **Tumours spread by crawling and by growing vessels.** Invasion, spread and the recruitment of new blood vessels are recognised hallmarks of cancer.<Cite id="hanahan-2011-hallmarks" />
2. **The protein helps cells move and vessels grow.** <Claim level="animal">It made skin and blood-vessel cells move faster in the lab and increased new vessels in rat wounds</Claim>, the same actions that make it interesting for wounds.<Cite id="malinda-1999-wound-healing,freeman-2011-atp-synthase" />
3. **In animals, tumour cells with more of it spread more.** <Claim level="animal">Mouse melanoma cells engineered to make more thymosin beta-4 formed larger tumours and about four times as many lung metastases, with 4.4 times more tumour vessels</Claim>.<Cite id="cha-2003-metastasis" /> <Claim level="animal">Mouse fibrosarcoma cells with more of it formed more tumours and metastases, and fewer when it was reduced</Claim>.<Cite id="kobayashi-2002-fibrosarcoma" /> <Claim level="in-vitro">In human colon cancer cells it raised levels of the signalling protein integrin-linked kinase and made the cells loosen and invade</Claim>, and patients' liver metastases contained more of its message than their original bowel tumours.<Cite id="wang-2004-colorectal,huang-2007-emt" /> Independent reviewers describe β-thymosins as involved in tumour spread.<Cite id="sribenja-2013-beta-thymosins-cancer" />
4. **Fragments can do it too.** <Claim level="in-vitro">In ovarian cancer cells, several thymosin beta-4 fragments, including one containing positions 17–23 and some without the actin-binding site, increased movement and invasion</Claim>.<Cite id="yoon-2021-ovarian-cancer" />

So the concern is not that TB-500 starts a cancer. It is that something which helps cells crawl and vessels grow could help an existing, perhaps undiagnosed, tumour grow or spread. The argument has limits. In the animal studies the tumour cells made extra protein themselves; nobody injected TB-500. In the Chinese volunteers, small temporary rises in a tumour marker occurred with both drug and placebo.<Cite id="wang-2021-rhtb4-phase1" /> No carcinogenicity study of TB-500 exists.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> The risk is plausible but untested.

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Immune reactions, the product itself, and pregnancy">

### Immune and allergic reactions

The FDA concluded that, injected under the skin or into muscle, TB-500 "may pose a significant risk for immunogenicity", possibly made worse by clumps of peptide and impurities from its manufacture. No study has tested this.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> An immune response can range from harmless antibodies to severe reactions. The FDA notes that antibodies against a peptide can, in general, also neutralise the body's own version of it, and TB-500's sequence is part of the body's own thymosin beta-4.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,chembl-timbetasin" />

A second route is direct. <Claim level="in-vitro">The 17–23 peptide made mast cells release their contents</Claim>.<Cite id="wyczolkowska-2007-mast-cells" /> Mast cells release histamine, which causes itching, redness and swelling. Whether this happens in skin after an injection is unknown.

### The product itself

The most immediate risk may be what is in the vial. The certificate of analysis the FDA found for TB-500 tested identity and purity, but not the amount of peptide, impurities, clumps, germs or bacterial toxins (endotoxin). The FDA calls the last two tests critical for injections.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> Even the paperwork sent to the FDA by the US pharmacy that nominated TB-500 was inconsistent: it named the free base, attached a certificate for the acetate, and gave a formula that matched neither.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> See [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/).

### Talk to a doctor first

A doctor can check for the conditions where the theoretical risks matter most: cancer, pregnancy and allergies. A doctor can also make sure the injury is properly diagnosed. A torn tendon or ligament may need a scan, or surgery within weeks. Using a peptide instead of a diagnosis can delay care that works. See [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/).

<Callout type="stop" title="Stop and get help if" id="stop-now">
- the injection site becomes hot, red, swollen or painful, or you get a fever (infection);
- you get hives, swelling of the face or throat, or difficulty breathing (allergic reaction);
- your skin or gums darken, as in one reported case with a TB-500 blend;
- you notice a new lump, a changing mole, or unexplained weight loss.<Cite id="nhs-cancer-symptoms,nhs-moles" />

If it is severe, call your local emergency number.

<InDetail title="What you cannot know about the product">

A vial labelled TB-500 may hold a different peptide, a different salt, or more or less than the label says. It may also hold near-copies of the chain, solvents or bacterial toxins. No regulator has checked it (see [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/)). If you inject anything, follow [sterile technique](/en/safety/sterile-technique/) and [injection safety](/en/safety/injection-safety/). A dirty product or needle can cause an infection.

<InDetail title="Checks that make sense, and why">

There is no blood test that shows whether TB-500 is working or harming you. No organ is known to be at special risk, because no toxicology studies exist. A doctor can help you choose which of these fit you:

<Checklist>

- **Cancer screening that is due for your age**, because of the theoretical effect on tumour growth and spread.
- **Any lump, unexplained weight loss or bleeding checked first.**
- **Any mole that changes checked**, because one animal study of cancer spread used melanoma cells.
- **A note of exactly what you used and when**, so a doctor can link any reaction to it.

</Checklist>

<InDetail title="Interactions, and who should not use it at all">

### Plausible interactions (none has been studied)

- **Other peptides in blends**, such as BPC-157. They add a second unknown, make any reaction impossible to trace, and gave no extra benefit in rats.<Cite id="bicer-2026-achilles,fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" />
- **Cancer medicines that block blood-vessel growth**, because the whole protein pushes vessel growth the other way.<Cite id="cha-2003-metastasis" />
- **ACE inhibitors (a type of blood pressure medicine)**, but only if the vial contains the whole protein. ACE breaks down Ac-SDKP, and one dose of an ACE inhibitor raised blood Ac-SDKP about 5.5-fold in 8 healthy people.<Cite id="azizi-1996-ace-acsdkp,cavasin-2004-pop-acsdkp" /> What that means for someone injecting the protein is unknown. TB-500 itself cannot form Ac-SDKP.

### Who should not use it at all

- **Anyone pregnant, breastfeeding or trying to conceive**: there are no reproductive studies, and cell movement and vessel growth are central to a developing baby.
- **Anyone with cancer, a history of cancer or an unexplained lump**, because of the effects on cell movement, spread and vessels described above.
- **Children and teenagers**, whose bodies are still growing and for whom there are no data.
- **People who have had severe allergic reactions, or who have a mast-cell disorder**, because the fragment triggered mast cells in the lab.
- **Athletes subject to anti-doping rules**, because it is prohibited at all times.

</InDetail>
