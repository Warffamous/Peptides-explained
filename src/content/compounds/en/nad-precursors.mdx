<Section id="what-it-is">

<Lead question="What are NAD+ precursors?">Forms of vitamin B3 that the body turns into NAD+. They are not peptides.</Lead>

<Compare title="Three close relatives. None is an approved medicine in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia." detail="What each is and how it is treated, checked on 23 October 2026.">

|---|---|---|---|

</Compare>

They are on this site because they are sold beside peptides: in the US, NAD+ was put forward for pharmacy compounding and sits on the same FDA list as GHK-Cu, and clinics offer NAD+ drips as "longevity" treatments.<Cite id="fda-503a-categories-2026-05,godic-2026-iv-longevity-review" />

<InDetail title="Why the category confusion matters">

A peptide is a chain of amino acids. NR is a vitamin B3 ring joined to a sugar, NMN is NR with a phosphate group, and NAD+ joins NMN to a second unit that carries adenine; none contains an amino acid.<Cite id="pubchem-nad" />

The category decides which checks a product has had.
{/* residue-ok: EFSA evidence (a food-safety assessment), not the reader's legal frame */}
EFSA, the European Food Safety Authority, assessed NR as a food to be swallowed, within limits, by adults.<Cite id="efsa-2019-nr-novel-food" /> Great Britain allows it in food supplements for adults.<Cite id="uk-fsa-gb-novel-96-nr-chloride" /> Supplement rules do not cover injections or drips. In the US, a dietary supplement must be meant to be swallowed, and in Canada nothing injected can be a natural health product.<Cite id="usc-21-321,ca-nhpr-schedule-2" /> In Australia, NR and NMN are permitted in listed medicines only by mouth.<Cite id="au-permissible-ingredients-determination-2026-3" /> A supplement assessment therefore says nothing about whether an injection is sterile or safe. In sport, a drip of more than 100 mL in 12 hours is banned even if its contents are allowed.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en,usada-iv-infusions" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="The molecules">

NAD+ (nadide) is C21H27N7O14P2, mass <Q>663.4 g/mol</Q>; nicotinamide riboside is C11H15N2O5+, <Q>255.25 g/mol</Q>; NMN is C11H15N2O8P, <Q>334.22 g/mol</Q>.<Cite id="pubchem-nad" /> EFSA calls NR and NMN sources of nicotinamide, a form of niacin (vitamin B3).<Cite id="efsa-2019-nr-novel-food,efsa-2026-nmn-novel-food" />

</InDetail>

<Lead question="How do they work?">The body turns them into NAD+, a helper for energy and repair. Pills raise NAD+ in blood.</Lead>

<Mechanism />

<InDetail title="What each step rests on">

The rise in blood NAD+ is shown in people.<Cite id="martens-2018-nr-older-adults" /> The idea that more NAD+ means healthier ageing comes from rodents, in which obesity and ageing lower NAD+ production.<Cite id="yoshino-2021-nmn-prediabetic-women" /> NAD+ given by drip is removed from the blood very quickly, and which tissues take it up was not measured.<Cite id="grant-2019-iv-nad-pilot" />

<InDetail title="Status in each country, and in sport">

<RegulatoryTable />

In the US, the FDA had said in October 2022 that NMN could not be a supplement, because it had been studied as a drug. On 29 September 2025 it reversed this: NMN was sold as a supplement from 2017, before the drug studies. It remains a new dietary ingredient that needs notification before sale.<Cite id="fda-2025-nmn-petition-response" /> NAD+ is in category 1 for pharmacy compounding: while the FDA evaluates it, the agency does not intend to act against pharmacies that compound it under set conditions.<Cite id="fda-503a-categories-2026-05,fda-503a-bulks-page" /> Category 1 is not an approval, says nothing about whether NAD+ works, and changes nothing outside the US.

In sport, none of the three is named on the 2026 or 2027 List. Drips or injections of more than 100 mL in 12 hours are banned unless received in hospital care or with an exemption.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en,wada-2027-list-en,usada-iv-infusions" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="The UK, Canada and Australia">

- **United Kingdom.** No UK-licensed medicine contains NR, NMN or NAD+.<Cite id="mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23" /> NR chloride is an authorised novel food (a food not widely eaten before) in England, Scotland and Wales, for food supplements for adults. NMN and NAD+ are not on the novel-food register.<Cite id="uk-fsa-gb-novel-96-nr-chloride,uk-fsa-novel-food-register-search-status-d-2026-10-23" /> We found no MHRA decision on whether NMN or NAD+ products count as medicines.<Cite id="uk-govuk-search-status-d-2026-10-23" /> None of them is a controlled drug.<Cite id="uk-mda-1971-sch-2" />
- **Canada.** No NR, NMN or NAD+ product has a Drug Identification Number (DIN), the number every authorised drug carries.<Cite id="ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-extract-search-merge-2026-10-24" /> Vitamin products taken by mouth can instead be natural health products, which need a Health Canada product licence.<Cite id="ca-nhpr-definition,ca-nhpr-schedule-1" /> On 26 October 2026 Health Canada's database of licensed natural health products listed 173 active licences for products with NMN and 31 for products with NR, all taken by mouth.<Cite id="ca-lnhpd-api-nr-nmn-2026-10-26" /> In 2024 Health Canada published a monograph, a licensing standard, for oral NMN for adults; we could not confirm that it is still current.<Cite id="ca-nhpid-nmn-monograph" /> Anything injected cannot be a natural health product, so an NAD+ drip or injection is a drug and needs a DIN.<Cite id="ca-nhpr-schedule-2,ca-food-and-drug-regulations" /> In September 2025 Health Canada published a recall of products that a compounding laboratory had sold without DINs, including an NAD+ drip. In April 2026 it listed NAD+ among unauthorised injectables it had seized.<Cite id="ca-hc-recalls-search-status-d-2026-10-23,ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides" />
- **Australia.** No registered medicine (one with an AUST R number) contains NR, NMN or NAD+.<Cite id="au-artg-search-status-d-2026-10-23" /> Oral NR, and oral NMN since 10 December 2025, may be ingredients of "listed" medicines, which carry an AUST L number. The regulator, the TGA, does not evaluate these products before they are listed: the sponsor, the company responsible, certifies that they meet the rules. Their ingredients must come from a list the TGA has assessed as low risk, and there are limits on the amount, how long they are used and who may use them.<Cite id="au-permissible-ingredients-determination-2026-3,au-tga-2026-08-nad-nmn-representations-guidance,au-tga-2025-04-nad-nmn-safety-alert" /> NAD+ itself is not a permitted ingredient; the NAD+ products on the register are for export only. No listed medicine may claim to raise NAD+ levels.<Cite id="au-tga-2025-04-nad-nmn-safety-alert,au-artg-search-status-d-2026-10-23,au-tga-2026-08-nad-nmn-representations-guidance" /> The TGA says that supplying or advertising a product that refers to NAD+ or NMN is likely to break the law if the product is not in its register and not exempt. None of the three is in the Poisons Standard.<Cite id="au-tga-2025-04-nad-nmn-safety-alert,au-poisons-standard-2026-10" /> We found no TGA statement about NAD+ drips given in clinics.<Cite id="au-tga-search-status-d-2026-10-23" />

More: the law in [the US](/en/legal/us/), [the UK](/en/legal/uk/), [Canada](/en/legal/ca/) and [Australia](/en/legal/au/).

<Section id="risks">

<Lead question="What are the risks?">Pills seem well tolerated in short trials. Drips and injections add infection and other risks.</Lead>

<Compare title="Pills caused few problems in short trials. Some long-term questions are open." detail="What trials, EFSA and animal studies show, as of 23 October 2026.">

| Possible harm | Shown in people? |
|---|---|
| Serious side effects from pills in trials | <Verdict is="no" /> Not more common than on placebo in the largest trial.<Cite id="brakedal-2026-nopark" /> |
| Harm from high intakes over years | <Verdict is="unknown" /> EFSA noted possible ways high intakes could harm, and said the safe upper limit may need a new review.<Cite id="efsa-2021-nr-extension" /> |
| Helping an existing cancer grow | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Seen in mice and cancer cells.<Cite id="maric-2023-nr-metastasis-mice,nakazzi-2026-b3-pancreatic-cancer" /> |
| Infection or reactions from drips and injections | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Procedural risks; not studied for NAD+.<Cite id="godic-2026-iv-longevity-review,nhs-cellulitis" /> |

</Compare>

<InDetail title="Why these risks are theorised">

Cancer cells also need NAD+. In mice with a breast cancer, NR led to more cancer spread to the brain; in pancreatic cancer cells and mice, NMN helped tumours resist chemotherapy.<Cite id="maric-2023-nr-metastasis-mice,nakazzi-2026-b3-pancreatic-cancer" /> These are animal findings, not proof in people. EFSA's safety conclusion for NMN does not cover pregnant or breastfeeding women; for NR, EFSA accepted only a lower intake for them. A 90-day rat study of NMN showed effects on reproductive organs.<Cite id="efsa-2019-nr-novel-food,efsa-2026-nmn-novel-food" /> In Parkinson disease, a year of NR was slightly worse than placebo.<Cite id="brakedal-2026-nopark" /> See [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/).

</InDetail>

</Section>

<Section id="harm-reduction">

<Lead question="How can someone lower the risk?">Know which kind of product you have. Treat injections like any injection, and know when to stop.</Lead>

<ProductCheck extra="Check whether it is meant to be swallowed or injected. Supplement rules do not cover injections or drips." extraSources="usc-21-321,ca-nhpr-schedule-2" />

### When to stop and get help

<Callout type="stop" title="Stop and get help if" id="stop-now">
- the skin where a needle or drip went in becomes hot, red, swollen or painful (possible infection);
- you get a fever or chills, or feel very ill, after a drip or injection;
- you get hives, swelling of the face or throat, or difficulty breathing (an allergic reaction).

If it is severe, call your local emergency number.
</Callout>

<Cite id="nhs-cellulitis,nhs-sepsis,nhs-anaphylaxis,godic-2026-iv-longevity-review" />

<WarningSigns only="general,injection,allergic" />

<InDetail title="Checks that make sense, and who should be careful">

A doctor can help you decide what fits you (see [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/)):

- **Cancer treatment**: tell your cancer team, because in mice and cancer cells these compounds helped tumours.<Cite id="nakazzi-2026-b3-pancreatic-cancer,maric-2023-nr-metastasis-mice" />
- **Pregnancy or breastfeeding**: EFSA's safety conclusion for NMN does not cover you, and for NR it accepted only a lower intake.<Cite id="efsa-2026-nmn-novel-food,efsa-2019-nr-novel-food" /> In Australia, listed medicines with NMN may not be used in pregnancy or breastfeeding.<Cite id="au-permissible-ingredients-determination-2026-3" />
- **Parkinson's disease**: in the largest trial, NR did slightly worse than placebo.<Cite id="brakedal-2026-nopark" />
- **Sport**: a drip of more than 100 mL in 12 hours breaks anti-doping rules.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en,usada-iv-infusions" />

For injections, see [sterile technique](/en/safety/sterile-technique/).

</InDetail>

You can report a side effect yourself: to [FDA MedWatch](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medwatch-fda-safety-information-and-adverse-event-reporting-program) in the US, the [Yellow Card scheme](https://yellowcard.mhra.gov.uk/) in the UK, Health Canada's [Canada Vigilance Program](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medeffect-canada/adverse-reaction-reporting.html) or the [TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/safety/report-problem/report-adverse-event-or-safety-problem) in Australia.<Cite id="fda-medwatch,mhra-yellow-card,ca-hc-medeffect-reporting,ca-hc-canada-vigilance,au-tga-report-adverse-event" />
