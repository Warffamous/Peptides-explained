"Sharps" are needles and anything else that can cut or prick the skin. A used needle can carry blood. Blood can carry HIV, hepatitis B and hepatitis C, even when you cannot see it.<Cite id="who-2010-injection-toolkit" />

Your own used needle is mostly a danger to other people. A loose needle in a bin or a bag can prick a child, a partner, a cleaner or a waste worker.<Cite id="drugsinfo-injecteren,frid-2016-fitter-recommendations" /> Safe disposal takes a few seconds. It protects everyone around you.

## Never in a bin, the recycling or the toilet

- **Not in household waste.** Someone who empties or sorts the bin can prick themselves on a loose needle.<Cite id="drugsinfo-injecteren,fda-sharps-dos-donts" />
- **Not in the plastic recycling (PMD).** Needles cannot be recycled.<Cite id="fda-sharps-dos-donts" />
- **Not down the toilet or the sink.**<Cite id="fda-sharps-dos-donts" />

Medicines and their leftovers also do not belong in household waste, the sink or the toilet.<Cite id="apotheek-medicijnafval,ema-epar-wegovy" />

## In the Netherlands

### Get a needle container

In the Netherlands, used needles always go into a needle container (naaldencontainer). You can get one at your pharmacy (apotheek).<Cite id="apotheek-medicijnafval" /> It is a box of hard plastic that needles cannot easily pierce, with a lid that closes tightly.<Cite id="fda-sharps-containers,rivm-hygiene-piercing" />

No container yet? Then use a strong, hard plastic or metal container with a tight lid for now, such as an empty detergent bottle. Write "used needles" on it. Swap it for a proper container as soon as you can.<Cite id="drugsinfo-injecteren,fda-sharps-containers" />

### Use it safely

<Checklist>

- Keep the container within reach every time you inject.
- Put the needle and syringe in straight after use. Do not put the cap back on first.
- Do not push the contents down to make room. Do not try to take anything out.
- Close it for good when it is three-quarters full, or at the fill line.
- Keep it upright, closed and out of reach of children and pets.

</Checklist>

<Cite id="who-2010-injection-toolkit,fda-sharps-disposal-steps,fda-sharps-dos-donts" />

### Return it when it is full

- **Your pharmacy.** You can usually return a full needle container to your pharmacy. Ask yours first.<Cite id="apotheek-nadroparine,apotheek-medicijnafval" />
- **Your municipality's collection point for small chemical waste** (klein chemisch afval, kca). This can be a recycling centre (milieustraat or milieuplein), a collection van (chemokar) or a depot. Each municipality decides how it collects, so check your municipality's website or call them.<Cite id="rijksoverheid-kca,apotheek-medicijnafval" />
- **Other places.** Used needles can also be handed in at some hospitals, GGDs and addiction services.<Cite id="drugsinfo-injecteren" />

Dutch pharmacists count used needles in a container as medicine waste. Medicine waste goes back to the pharmacy or to a chemical waste point, not into the household waste.<Cite id="apotheek-medicijnafval" />

## After a needle-stick injury

A needle-stick injury is a prick from a needle that someone has already used. The risk depends on whose needle it was and whether there was blood on it.<Cite id="thuisarts-hiv-pep,who-2010-injection-toolkit" />

<Callout type="stop" title="Pricked by a needle someone else used?">

1. **Let the wound bleed.** Do not suck it.
2. **Wash it** with mild soap under warm running water. Do not scrub it with a brush.
3. **Cover it** with a waterproof plaster.
4. **Call straight away**: the GGD, your GP (huisarts), or outside office hours the out-of-hours GP service (huisartsenpost) or a hospital emergency department. Do not wait until the next day.

Treatment should ideally start within 1 hour, and no later than 72 hours.

</Callout>

<Cite id="rivm-hygiene-piercing,who-2010-injection-toolkit,thuisarts-hiv-pep" />

### What the doctor will do

The doctor first asks questions. Whose needle was it? Was there blood on it? When did it happen? Are you vaccinated against hepatitis B? Then the doctor decides what you need.<Cite id="thuisarts-hiv-pep,who-2010-injection-toolkit" />

- **HIV:** you may get PEP. PEP is a month of daily pills that makes an HIV infection much less likely. It works only if you start very quickly, preferably within 2 hours. After 72 hours it no longer works. Most GGDs can be reached 24 hours a day for PEP. When the GGD is closed, call a hospital emergency department.<Cite id="thuisarts-hiv-pep,who-2010-injection-toolkit" />
- **Hepatitis B:** if you are not vaccinated, you may get a vaccine and sometimes antibodies, as soon as possible. If you were vaccinated before, you probably do not need this.<Cite id="thuisarts-hiv-pep,who-2010-injection-toolkit" />
- **Hepatitis C:** there is no preventive treatment. Instead, you get blood tests in the following weeks and months, so that an infection can be found and treated early.<Cite id="who-2010-injection-toolkit" />

If the person whose needle it was is known, they can be asked to get a quick HIV test. This helps the doctor decide whether you need PEP.<Cite id="thuisarts-hiv-pep" />

If only you used the needle, you cannot catch a virus from your own blood. Clean the wound and watch for signs of infection, such as spreading redness or pus.<Cite id="nhs-cellulitis" /> See the [warning signs](/en/safety/warning-signs/).

## Other countries

The rules for household sharps differ between countries, and often between towns. Your pharmacist can usually tell you what to do locally.

### Germany

How medicines are disposed of differs by region in Germany. In most regions they go into the residual waste (Restmüll); in others, to mobile collection or recycling centres.<Cite id="gesund-bund-arzneimittel-entsorgen" /> We found no federal guidance on household needles. Use a closed, puncture-proof container and ask your pharmacy or your local waste advice service (Abfallberatung).

<JurisdictionCard code="DE" />

### Belgium

We found no official Belgian guidance for households on our list of trusted sources. Use a closed, puncture-proof container and ask your pharmacist or your municipality.

<JurisdictionCard code="BE" />

### United Kingdom

In England and Wales, some councils collect used syringes and other clinical waste from homes. There may be a charge. You can find your council's service on GOV.UK.<Cite id="govuk-clinical-waste-collection" /> Ask your GP or pharmacist about a sharps bin.

<JurisdictionCard code="UK" />

### United States

The FDA advises two steps. First, put needles in a sharps container straight after use. Second, when it is three-quarters full, follow your local rules. Options include drop-off sites such as pharmacies, hospitals and health departments, household hazardous waste sites, mail-back programmes and special pick-up services.<Cite id="fda-sharps-disposal-steps" /> If you have no approved container, a heavy-duty plastic household container with a tight, puncture-resistant lid can be used.<Cite id="fda-sharps-containers" />
