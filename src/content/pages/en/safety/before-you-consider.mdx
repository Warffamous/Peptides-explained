An authorised medicine comes with a list of people who must not use it. Doctors call these contraindications. The list exists because the medicine was tested and the risks were written down.

Most peptides sold online have no such list. They were never tested well enough in people.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing" /> So we work from what each substance does in the body. If a substance pushes growth, it is a worry for someone with cancer. If it lowers blood sugar, it is a worry for someone on insulin. This page explains these red lines one by one.

This is not a full list for any one product. It does not replace a doctor who knows you. See [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/).

<Diagram id="red-lines-overview" caption="Who should not use which kind of product, and the main reason why">

| Situation | Which products | Main reason |
|---|---|---|
| Pregnant, breastfeeding or trying to conceive | All | Harm to an unborn baby seen in animals for GLP-1 medicines; no human data for the others |
| Under 18 | All | The body is still growing; long-term effects in young people are unknown |
| Cancer now or in the past | Healing and growth peptides (BPC-157, TB-500, growth hormone boosters) | They push blood vessel growth, cell movement or growth hormone, which could help a tumour grow |
| Insulin or sulfonylurea tablets | GLP-1 type products (semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide) | Together they can push blood sugar dangerously low |
| Heart disease or high blood pressure | Melanocortin peptides (melanotan II, PT-141) | PT-141 briefly raises blood pressure after each dose; one case report of a racing heart after melanotan II |
| Eating disorder, now or in the past | Appetite-lowering products (GLP-1 type) | Less hunger can make restriction worse |
| Kidney disease | All, especially GLP-1 type | Kidneys clear many peptides; vomiting and diarrhoea can harm the kidneys |
| Pancreatitis in the past | GLP-1 type | They are linked to an inflamed pancreas |
| Medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2 in you or your family | GLP-1 type | Thyroid tumours in rodents; US labels forbid use |
| Weak immune system | All injections | Higher risk of infection and sepsis |
| Blood thinners | All injections, and some products directly | More bleeding and bruising; some products change clotting |
| Competitive sport | Most unapproved peptides | Banned at all times; you are responsible for what is in your body |

</Diagram>

## Pregnancy and breastfeeding

An unborn baby grows very fast. A substance that changes growth, blood vessels, hormones or appetite could disturb that growth.

For GLP-1 medicines, animal studies showed harm to the unborn young. The EU label for Wegovy says not to use it in pregnancy or while breastfeeding. It says to stop at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy. The reason is that the medicine stays in the body for a long time. For Mounjaro the label says to stop at least 1 month before.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy,ema-epar-mounjaro" />

For experimental peptides there are no human pregnancy data at all. BPC-157 has one study in pregnant rats. It found no birth defects in the young rats. But it did not test fertility, or how the young grew up after birth. TB-500 and KPV have no such studies.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing" /> "No data" does not mean "safe". It means nobody knows.

## Under 18

Teenagers are still growing. The teenage years are the key time for building strong bones.<Cite id="haider-2026-glp1-adolescents" /> Some peptides raise growth hormone, which is a growth signal.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> What extra growth signals do to a body that is still growing has not been studied.

Some authorised medicines are allowed for teenagers. Wegovy, for example, is authorised from age 12, on prescription.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> Even for these, the long-term effects on bones, fertility and mental health in teenagers are still uncertain.<Cite id="haider-2026-glp1-adolescents" /> For unapproved peptides, there is nothing to go on. We advise against them for anyone under 18.

## Cancer, now or in the past

A tumour needs a blood supply to grow. Making new blood vessels is one of the basic tools of cancer.<Cite id="hanahan-2011-hallmarks" />

Several "healing" peptides act on exactly this. In studies in cells, chick eggs and rats, <Claim level="animal">BPC-157 increased blood vessel growth</Claim>.<Cite id="hsieh-2017-vegfr2" /> In a study in mice, <Claim level="animal">melanoma cells that made extra thymosin beta-4 spread further and grew more blood vessels</Claim>.<Cite id="cha-2003-metastasis" /> Thymosin beta-4 is the natural protein that TB-500 is based on. TB-500 itself was not tested in that study.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> Growth hormone boosters raise growth hormone and IGF-1, which are growth factors. The label of tesamorelin, an authorised growth hormone booster, forbids its use in active cancer.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

<Claim level="hypothesis">Whether these peptides could feed a hidden or treated tumour in people has never been studied.</Claim> Nobody can tell you the risk is low. If you have had cancer, or have an unexplained lump, do not use them. See [BPC-157](/en/compounds/bpc-157/), [TB-500](/en/compounds/tb-500/), [CJC-1295](/en/compounds/cjc-1295/), [ipamorelin](/en/compounds/ipamorelin/) and [tesamorelin](/en/compounds/tesamorelin/).

Tanning peptides are a separate worry for anyone who has had melanoma. In one case report, a man with past melanoma injected an alpha-MSH peptide. New, abnormal-looking moles appeared, and old moles grew darker.<Cite id="cardones-2009-eruptive-nevi" /> See [melanotan II](/en/compounds/melanotan-ii/).

## Diabetes treated with insulin or sulfonylurea tablets

GLP-1 type products (semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide) make the pancreas release more insulin, but mainly when blood sugar is high. On their own, they rarely push blood sugar too low.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

Insulin and sulfonylurea tablets (such as gliclazide) work differently. They lower blood sugar whatever its level. Together with a GLP-1 type product, blood sugar can fall dangerously low. The EU labels warn about this, and a doctor may need to lower the insulin or tablet dose.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy,ema-epar-mounjaro,sola-2015-sulfonylureas" />

The short animation shows why the combination can push blood sugar too low.

The Wegovy label says semaglutide is not a substitute for insulin, and that it was not studied in type 1 diabetes.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> Never use a GLP-1 type product instead of insulin. See [semaglutide](/en/compounds/semaglutide/), [tirzepatide](/en/compounds/tirzepatide/) and [retatrutide](/en/compounds/retatrutide/).

## Heart disease or high blood pressure

Bremelanotide (PT-141) is a melanocortin peptide. It is authorised in the US as Vyleesi. Its label says it raises blood pressure for some hours after each dose. It must not be used with uncontrolled high blood pressure or known heart disease.<Cite id="dailymed-vyleesi-label" />

Melanotan II is a close relative of bremelanotide.<Cite id="nelson-2012-melanotan-rhabdomyolysis" /> It is not an authorised medicine in the EU.<Cite id="ema-medicines-search" /> In one case report, a man had a racing heart, muscle breakdown and kidney problems after injecting it.<Cite id="nelson-2012-melanotan-rhabdomyolysis" /> See [PT-141](/en/compounds/pt-141/) and [melanotan II](/en/compounds/melanotan-ii/).

## Eating disorders

GLP-1 type products make you less hungry and full sooner. A 2025 review looked at 25 studies in people with obesity or type 2 diabetes. Eating behaviour mostly improved or stayed the same. The data were limited.<Cite id="jebeile-2025-glp1-eating-behaviour" />

For someone who restricts food, less hunger can make the restriction deeper. Experts are concerned about misuse by people with eating disorders.<Cite id="schaefer-2025-glp1-eating-disorders" /> One case report describes misuse of semaglutide by a person with atypical anorexia.<Cite id="guerdjikova-2024-semaglutide-anorexia" /> If you have, or had, an eating disorder, do not use appetite-lowering products without your treatment team.

## Kidney disease

The kidneys filter many peptides out of the blood and break them down.<Cite id="diao-2013-peptide-pharmacokinetics" /> If your kidneys work less well, a product may stay in your body longer than expected. This is not true for every peptide. For semaglutide, kidney disease did not change the amount in the blood in a way that matters.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> For unapproved peptides, nobody has measured it.

GLP-1 type products often cause vomiting and diarrhoea at first. Losing a lot of fluid can, in rare cases, damage the kidneys.<Cite id="ema-epar-mounjaro,ema-epar-wegovy" /> People with moderate kidney disease may get more stomach and gut side effects on semaglutide. It is not recommended with severe kidney disease.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

## Pancreatitis, or thyroid cancer in the family

- **Pancreatitis in the past:** GLP-1 medicines are linked to acute pancreatitis. The labels advise caution if you have had it.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy,ema-epar-mounjaro" />
- **Medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2, in you or your family:** GLP-1 medicines caused thyroid tumours in rodents. Whether this happens in people is unknown. The US label forbids use in this group. In the EU it is not a formal contraindication, so discuss it with a doctor.<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic,ema-epar-wegovy" />

## A weak immune system

Some illnesses and medicines weaken the immune system. Examples are HIV, chemotherapy, steroids and other medicines that suppress the immune system. A weak immune system makes skin infections and sepsis more likely.<Cite id="nhs-cellulitis,nhs-sepsis,nhs-boils" /> Every injection is a small wound, so the risk of each injection is higher. Talk to the doctor who treats your condition first.

## Blood thinners

Blood thinners (anticoagulants) make it take longer for blood to clot. You bruise and bleed more easily.<Cite id="nhs-anticoagulant-side-effects" /> So injections can bruise more.

Some products may also act on clotting directly. In 28-day studies in rats and dogs, BPC-157 changed a blood clotting test.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> The INR is the blood test for warfarin-type blood thinners. A lower INR has been reported in people who took semaglutide with acenocoumarol, a blood thinner of this type. So the label advises frequent INR checks when someone on these blood thinners starts semaglutide.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" />

## Competitive sport

Most unapproved peptides are banned in sport at all times. The World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) bans any substance with no approval for human use, and names BPC-157. It also bans growth hormone boosters such as CJC-1295, ipamorelin and tesamorelin.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en,wada-2027-list-en" />

Anti-doping rules use strict liability. If a banned substance is found in your body, that is a rule violation, even if you did not mean to take it.<Cite id="wada-code-2021,wada-code-2027" /> A product with the wrong contents can end a career. See [sport and doping](/en/legal/sport-and-doping/).

## Rules for everyone

### Never share needles, syringes, vials or pens

A used needle can carry a tiny amount of blood into a vial or a pen. The next person can then get HIV or hepatitis B or C, even with a new needle. Blood does not need to be visible to pass on these viruses.<Cite id="who-2010-injection-toolkit,fda-label-ozempic" /> Never share a pen, even if you change the needle. Never share a vial, water or other equipment.<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic,ema-epar-wegovy,mainline-steroids-safe-injecting,drugsinfo-injecteren" />

### Never use a product that looks wrong

A liquid that should be clear but is cloudy, coloured or has bits in it may be spoiled or contaminated. So may a vial with a broken seal. Do not use it.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy,dailymed-egrifta-sv-label,dailymed-sterile-water-label" /> See [sterile technique](/en/safety/sterile-technique/) and [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/).

### Never stop a medicine your doctor prescribed

Do not stop or change a prescribed medicine on your own.<Cite id="thuisarts-dikke-ogen-lippen" /> The condition it treats can come back or get worse. If you think a peptide could replace a medicine, discuss it with your doctor first.

### Tell your doctor

A doctor can check for the risks on this page. Many need only a conversation or a simple test. In the Netherlands, your doctor must keep what you say private.<Cite id="knmg-beroepsgeheim" />

<Checklist>

- Are you pregnant, breastfeeding or trying to conceive?
- Are you under 18?
- Have you ever had cancer, or do you have an unexplained lump?
- Do you use insulin or diabetes tablets?
- Do you have heart disease or high blood pressure?
- Have you ever had an eating disorder?
- Do you have kidney disease, or have you had pancreatitis?
- Has anyone in your family had medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2?
- Do you have a weak immune system, or take blood thinners?
- Do you compete in a sport with doping tests?

</Checklist>

If you answer yes to any of these, talk to a doctor before you do anything. Then read the [warning signs](/en/safety/warning-signs/), so you know when to get help.
