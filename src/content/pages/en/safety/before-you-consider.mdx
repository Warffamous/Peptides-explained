<Lead question="Who should not use these products?">People who are pregnant, breastfeeding or under 18. Cancer and some other illnesses rule out some products.</Lead>

<Diagram id="red-lines-overview" caption="Who should not use which kind of product, and the main reason why">

| Situation | Which products | Main reason |
|---|---|---|
| Pregnant, breastfeeding or trying to conceive | All | Harm to an unborn baby seen in animals for GLP-1 medicines; no human data for the others |
| Under 18 | All | The body is still growing; long-term effects in young people are unknown |
| Cancer now or in the past | Healing and growth peptides (BPC-157, TB-500, growth hormone boosters) | They push blood vessel growth, cell movement or growth hormone, which could help a tumour grow |
| Insulin or sulfonylurea tablets | GLP-1 type products (semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide) | Together they can push blood sugar dangerously low |
| Heart disease or high blood pressure | Melanocortin peptides (melanotan II, PT-141) | PT-141 briefly raises blood pressure after each dose; one case report of a racing heart after melanotan II |
| Eating disorder, now or in the past | Appetite-lowering products (GLP-1 type) | Less hunger can make restriction worse |
| Kidney disease | All, especially GLP-1 type | Kidneys clear many peptides; vomiting and diarrhoea can harm the kidneys |
| Pancreatitis in the past | GLP-1 type | They are linked to an inflamed pancreas |
| Medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2 in you or your family | GLP-1 type | Thyroid tumours in rodents; US and Canadian labels forbid use |
| Weak immune system | All injections | Higher risk of infection and sepsis |
| Blood thinners | All injections, and some products directly | More bleeding and bruising; some products change clotting |
| Competitive sport | Most unapproved peptides | Banned at all times; you are responsible for what is in your body |

</Diagram>

This is not a full list for any one product. A doctor who knows you can check what applies to you. See [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/).

<InDetail title="Why we made this list ourselves">

An authorised medicine comes with a list of people who must not use it. Doctors call these contraindications. The list exists because the medicine was tested and the risks were written down.

Most peptides sold online have no such list. They were never tested well enough in people.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing" /> So we work from what each substance does in the body. If a substance pushes growth, it is a worry for someone with cancer. If it lowers blood sugar, it is a worry for someone on insulin. This page explains these situations one by one.

</InDetail>

## Pregnancy and breastfeeding

<Lead question="Can I use them if I am pregnant or breastfeeding?">No. Animal tests showed harm to the unborn young with GLP-1 medicines. For others, nobody knows.</Lead>

<TimeStrip title="Labels say to stop one or two months before a planned pregnancy. Other peptides have no human data." detail="How long before a planned pregnancy the US and Canadian labels say to stop. The US label for Mounjaro gives no time." min={0} max={3} step={1} axis="Months before a planned pregnancy" source="fda-label-wegovy,ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02,ca-pm-mounjaro-2026-06-23,fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing" rows={[
  { label: "Wegovy (semaglutide), US and Canadian labels", spans: [{ from: 0, to: 2, text: "stop at least 2 months before" }] },
  { label: "Mounjaro (tirzepatide), Canadian label", spans: [{ from: 0, to: 1, text: "stop at least 1 month before" }] },
  { label: "Experimental peptides (BPC-157, TB-500, KPV)", spans: [{ from: 0, to: 3, text: "no human pregnancy data at all", unknown: true }] },
]} />

An unborn baby grows very fast. A substance that changes growth, blood vessels, hormones or appetite could disturb that growth. "No data" does not mean "safe". It means nobody knows.

<InDetail title="What the labels and the animal studies say">

For GLP-1 medicines, animal studies showed harm to the unborn young.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy,fda-label-mounjaro" /> The US label for Wegovy says to stop it when a pregnancy is found, if it is used for weight loss. It says to stop at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy. The reason is that the medicine stays in the body for a long time.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy" /> The Canadian label says not to use Wegovy in pregnancy or while breastfeeding, and gives the same 2 months.<Cite id="ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02" /> For Mounjaro, the Canadian label says not to use it in pregnancy, and to stop at least 1 month before a planned pregnancy. The US label gives no time.<Cite id="ca-pm-mounjaro-2026-06-23,fda-label-mounjaro" />

For experimental peptides there are no human pregnancy data at all. BPC-157 has one study in pregnant rats. It found no birth defects in the young rats. But it did not test fertility, or how the young grew up after birth. TB-500 and KPV have no such studies.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing" />

</InDetail>

## Under 18

<Lead question="Can teenagers use them?">We advise against unapproved peptides under 18. Nobody has studied what they do to a growing body.</Lead>

<Compare title="Even a teen medicine has open questions. For unapproved peptides there is nothing to go on.">

| | Authorised medicine with a use in teenagers (for example Wegovy) | Unapproved peptide |
|---|---|---|
| Authorised for teenagers? | <Verdict is="yes" /> In the US, Wegovy from age 12, on prescription<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> Not an approved medicine |
| Long-term effects in teenagers known? | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Effects on bones, fertility and mental health are still uncertain<Cite id="haider-2026-glp1-adolescents" /> | <Verdict is="unknown" /> There is nothing to go on |

</Compare>

Teenagers are still growing. The teenage years are the key time for building strong bones.<Cite id="haider-2026-glp1-adolescents" />

<InDetail title="Growth signals, and what is known about authorised medicines">

Some peptides raise growth hormone, which is a growth signal.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> What extra growth signals do to a body that is still growing has not been studied.

Some authorised medicines are allowed for teenagers. Wegovy, for example, is approved in the US from age 12 for teenagers with obesity, on prescription. In Canada, those teenagers must also weigh more than 60 kg.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy,ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02" /> Even for these, the long-term effects on bones, fertility and mental health in teenagers are still uncertain.<Cite id="haider-2026-glp1-adolescents" /> For unapproved peptides, there is nothing to go on. We advise against them for anyone under 18.

</InDetail>

## Cancer, now or in the past

<Lead question="Can I use them if I have, or had, cancer?">Not healing or growth peptides. They push growth signals that could help a tumour grow.</Lead>

<Compare title="Some push blood vessel growth or growth signals. What they do to a tumour in people is unknown.">

| | BPC-157 | TB-500 | Growth hormone boosters (CJC-1295, ipamorelin, tesamorelin) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pushes blood vessel growth or growth signals? | <Verdict is="yes" /> More blood vessel growth in cells, chick eggs and rats<Cite id="hsieh-2017-vegfr2" /> | <Verdict is="unknown" /> TB-500 itself was not tested. In mice, melanoma cells with extra thymosin beta-4, the protein it is based on, spread further<Cite id="cha-2003-metastasis,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> Tesamorelin raises growth hormone and IGF-1, which are growth factors<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />. Anti-doping rules class CJC-1295 and ipamorelin as growth hormone releasing factors<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en" /> |
| Could it feed a tumour in people? | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Never studied | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Never studied | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Not known. A 10-year US safety study of tesamorelin, meant to look at cancer, was stopped in 2018 with no results posted<Cite id="ctgov-nct01579695,fda-2010-egrifta-approval-letter" />. The others were never studied. The tesamorelin label forbids its use in active cancer<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> |

</Compare>

A tumour needs a blood supply to grow. Making new blood vessels is one of the basic tools of cancer.<Cite id="hanahan-2011-hallmarks" />

If you have had cancer, or have an unexplained lump, do not use them. See [BPC-157](/en/compounds/bpc-157/), [TB-500](/en/compounds/tb-500/), [CJC-1295](/en/compounds/cjc-1295/), [ipamorelin](/en/compounds/ipamorelin/) and [tesamorelin](/en/compounds/tesamorelin/).

<InDetail title="What the lab and animal studies found, and what nobody knows">

Several "healing" peptides act on exactly this. In studies in cells, chick eggs and rats, <Claim level="animal">BPC-157 increased blood vessel growth</Claim>.<Cite id="hsieh-2017-vegfr2" /> In a study in mice, <Claim level="animal">melanoma cells that made extra thymosin beta-4 spread further and grew more blood vessels</Claim>.<Cite id="cha-2003-metastasis" /> Thymosin beta-4 is the natural protein that TB-500 is based on. TB-500 itself was not tested in that study.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> Tesamorelin, a growth hormone booster approved in the US, raises growth hormone and IGF-1, which are growth factors. Its label forbids its use in active cancer.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> Anti-doping rules class CJC-1295 and ipamorelin as growth hormone releasing factors too.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en" />

<Claim level="hypothesis">These peptides could, in theory, help a hidden or treated tumour grow.</Claim> For BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295 and ipamorelin, this has never been studied in people. For tesamorelin, the FDA required a safety study of at least 10 years when it approved the medicine in 2010.<Cite id="fda-2010-egrifta-approval-letter" /> Its main measure was how soon cancers appeared in people with HIV using tesamorelin, compared with similar people who did not use it. It enrolled 391 people and was stopped in 2018, with no reason given and no results posted.<Cite id="ctgov-nct01579695" /> So nobody can tell you the risk is low.

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Tanning peptides after melanoma">

Tanning peptides are a separate worry for anyone who has had melanoma. In one case report, a man with past melanoma injected an alpha-MSH peptide. New, abnormal-looking moles appeared, and old moles grew darker.<Cite id="cardones-2009-eruptive-nevi" /> See [melanotan II](/en/compounds/melanotan-ii/).

</InDetail>

## Diabetes treated with insulin or sulfonylurea tablets

<Lead question="Can I use them with insulin or diabetes tablets?">Not without your doctor. Together with a GLP-1 type product, blood sugar can fall dangerously low.</Lead>

The short animation shows why the combination can push blood sugar too low.

<Explainer id="glp1-class-hypo" />

Never use a GLP-1 type product instead of insulin. See [semaglutide](/en/compounds/semaglutide/), [tirzepatide](/en/compounds/tirzepatide/) and [retatrutide](/en/compounds/retatrutide/).

<InDetail title="Why the combination is the problem, and what the labels say">

GLP-1 type products (semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide) make the pancreas release more insulin, but mainly when blood sugar is high.<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic" /> On their own, they rarely push blood sugar too low. In a 40-week placebo-controlled trial of tirzepatide alone, in 475 adults with type 2 diabetes, no one on tirzepatide had blood sugar below the label's cut-off for low blood sugar.<Cite id="fda-label-mounjaro" />

Insulin and sulfonylurea tablets (such as gliclazide) work differently. They lower blood sugar whatever its level.<Cite id="sola-2015-sulfonylureas" /> Together with a GLP-1 type product, blood sugar can fall dangerously low. The US labels warn about this, and a doctor may need to lower the insulin or tablet dose.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy,fda-label-mounjaro" />

The Canadian label says Wegovy is not a substitute for insulin, and is not for type 1 diabetes. The US label says its use in type 1 diabetes, or together with insulin, has not been studied.<Cite id="ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02,fda-label-wegovy" />

</InDetail>

## Heart disease or high blood pressure

<Lead question="Can I use them with heart disease or high blood pressure?">Do not use PT-141 or melanotan II. PT-141 raises blood pressure for some hours after each dose.</Lead>

<Compare title="PT-141 raises blood pressure. For melanotan II, there is one case report of a racing heart.">

| | Bremelanotide (PT-141) | Melanotan II |
|---|---|---|
| An authorised medicine? | <Verdict is="yes" /> In the US, as Vyleesi<Cite id="dailymed-vyleesi-label" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> Not in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia<Cite id="fr-2016-melanotan-debarment,mhra-2024-foi-melanotan-ii,ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides,au-tga-2026-08-melanotan-ii-safety-alert" /> |
| Raises blood pressure or heart rate? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Blood pressure rises for some hours after each dose<Cite id="dailymed-vyleesi-label" /> | <Verdict is="unknown" /> One case report of a racing heart<Cite id="nelson-2012-melanotan-rhabdomyolysis" /> |
| Label warning about heart disease? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Not with uncontrolled high blood pressure or known heart disease<Cite id="dailymed-vyleesi-label" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> No official label, because no regulator in these countries approved it<Cite id="fr-2016-melanotan-debarment,mhra-2024-foi-melanotan-ii,ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides,au-tga-2026-08-melanotan-ii-safety-alert" /> |

</Compare>

See [PT-141](/en/compounds/pt-141/) and [melanotan II](/en/compounds/melanotan-ii/).

<InDetail title="What the label and the case report say">

Bremelanotide (PT-141) is a melanocortin peptide. It is authorised in the US as Vyleesi. Its label says it raises blood pressure for some hours after each dose. It must not be used with uncontrolled high blood pressure or known heart disease.<Cite id="dailymed-vyleesi-label" />

Melanotan II is a close relative of bremelanotide.<Cite id="nelson-2012-melanotan-rhabdomyolysis" /> It is not an approved medicine in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia.<Cite id="fr-2016-melanotan-debarment,mhra-2024-foi-melanotan-ii,ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides,au-tga-2026-08-melanotan-ii-safety-alert" /> In one case report, a man had a racing heart, muscle breakdown and kidney problems after injecting it.<Cite id="nelson-2012-melanotan-rhabdomyolysis" />

</InDetail>

## Eating disorders

<Lead question="Can I use them if I have had an eating disorder?">Not products that lower hunger, unless your treatment team agrees. Less hunger can make restriction worse.</Lead>

<Diagram id="appetite-and-restriction" caption="Why less hunger can be a danger for someone who restricts food">

1. GLP-1 type products make you less hungry and full sooner.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy,haider-2026-glp1-adolescents" />
2. For someone who restricts food, less hunger can make the restriction deeper.
3. Experts are concerned about misuse by people with eating disorders.<Cite id="schaefer-2025-glp1-eating-disorders" />

</Diagram>

If you have, or had, an eating disorder, do not use appetite-lowering products without your treatment team.

<InDetail title="What the studies show, and their limits">

A 2025 review looked at 25 studies in people with obesity or type 2 diabetes. Eating behaviour mostly improved or stayed the same. The data were limited.<Cite id="jebeile-2025-glp1-eating-behaviour" />

One case report describes misuse of semaglutide by a person with atypical anorexia.<Cite id="guerdjikova-2024-semaglutide-anorexia" />

</InDetail>

## Kidney disease

<Lead question="Can I use them with kidney disease?">Ask a doctor first. A product may stay in you longer, and fluid loss can harm the kidneys.</Lead>

<Compare title="For semaglutide, the effect of weak kidneys was measured. For unapproved peptides, it was not.">

| | Semaglutide (Wegovy) | Unapproved peptides |
|---|---|---|
| Measured in people with kidney disease? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Kidney disease did not change the amount in the blood in a way that matters<Cite id="ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02,fda-label-wegovy" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> Nobody has measured it |
| More side effects with kidney disease? | <Verdict is="yes" /> In trials, kidney side effects were more common in people who already had kidney disease<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy" /> | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Not studied |
| Advised with kidney failure? | <Verdict is="no" /> Experience with severe kidney disease is limited. The Canadian label does not recommend it with kidney failure<Cite id="ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02" /> | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Not studied |

</Compare>

The kidneys filter many peptides out of the blood and break them down.<Cite id="diao-2013-peptide-pharmacokinetics" /> If your kidneys work less well, a product may stay in your body longer than expected.

<InDetail title="Fluid loss, and what the semaglutide label says">

This is not true for every peptide. For semaglutide, kidney disease did not change the amount in the blood in a way that matters.<Cite id="ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02,fda-label-wegovy" /> The Canadian label says this was tested with a single dose in people with mild to severe kidney disease and on dialysis.<Cite id="ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02" /> For unapproved peptides, nobody has measured it.

GLP-1 type products often cause vomiting and diarrhoea at first. Losing a lot of fluid can, in rare cases, damage the kidneys.<Cite id="fda-label-mounjaro,fda-label-wegovy" /> In Wegovy trials, kidney side effects were more common in people who already had kidney disease.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy" /> Experience with severe kidney disease is limited, and the Canadian label does not recommend Wegovy for people with kidney failure (end-stage kidney disease).<Cite id="ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02" />

</InDetail>

## Pancreatitis, or thyroid cancer in the family

<Lead question="What if I had pancreatitis, or thyroid cancer runs in my family?">Talk to a doctor first. Labels warn about pancreatitis. Some also warn about a rare thyroid cancer.</Lead>

<Compare title="In the US and Canada, the label says no if a rare thyroid cancer runs in your family." detail="Medullary thyroid cancer (MTC) or MEN 2, in you or your family.">

| | US labels (Ozempic, Wegovy) | Canadian label (Wegovy) |
|---|---|---|
| Thyroid tumours in rodents? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Semaglutide caused thyroid tumours in rodents<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic,fda-label-wegovy" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> The same finding<Cite id="ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02" /> |
| Shown to happen in people? | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Unknown<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic,fda-label-wegovy" /> | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Unknown<Cite id="ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02" /> |
| Use forbidden with MTC or MEN 2 in you or your family? | <Verdict is="yes" /> The US labels forbid use in this group<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic,fda-label-wegovy" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> The Canadian label forbids it too<Cite id="ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02" /> |

</Compare>

- **Pancreatitis in the past:** GLP-1 medicines are linked to acute pancreatitis.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy" /> Wegovy and Mounjaro were not studied in people who had pancreatitis before, so the Canadian labels advise caution.<Cite id="ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02,ca-pm-mounjaro-2026-06-23" />

<InDetail title="The thyroid warning in full">

- **Medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2, in you or your family:** GLP-1 medicines caused thyroid tumours in rodents. Whether this happens in people is unknown. The US and Canadian labels forbid use in this group.<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic,fda-label-wegovy,ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02" /> We did not check how the UK and Australian labels treat it. Discuss it with a doctor.

</InDetail>

## A weak immune system

<Lead question="What if my immune system is weak?">Each injection is more likely to cause an infection. Talk to your own doctor first.</Lead>

<Diagram id="weak-immune-system" caption="Why a weak immune system makes each injection riskier">

1. Some illnesses and medicines weaken the immune system. Examples are HIV, chemotherapy, steroids and other medicines that suppress the immune system.
2. A weak immune system makes skin infections and sepsis more likely.<Cite id="nhs-cellulitis,nhs-sepsis,nhs-boils" />
3. Every injection is a small wound, so the risk of each injection is higher.

</Diagram>

Talk to the doctor who treats your condition first.

## Blood thinners

<Lead question="Can I use them with blood thinners?">Talk to your doctor first. Injections bruise more, and some products may change clotting.</Lead>

<Diagram id="blood-thinners" caption="Two ways a product can add to the effect of a blood thinner">

1. **Through the injection.** Blood thinners (anticoagulants) make it take longer for blood to clot. You bruise and bleed more easily.<Cite id="nhs-anticoagulant-side-effects" /> So injections can bruise more.
2. **Through the product itself.** Some products may also act on clotting directly. In 28-day studies in rats and dogs, BPC-157 changed a blood clotting test.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" />

</Diagram>

<InDetail title="The INR test, and semaglutide with blood thinners">

The INR is the blood test for warfarin-type blood thinners. A lower INR has been reported in people who took semaglutide with acenocoumarol, a blood thinner of this type. So the Canadian label advises frequent INR checks when someone on these blood thinners starts semaglutide.<Cite id="ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02" />

</InDetail>

## Competitive sport

<Lead question="Can I use them if I compete in sport?">Most are banned at all times. A positive test counts, even if you did not mean it.</Lead>

<Compare title="Most unapproved peptides and growth hormone boosters are banned in sport at all times.">

| | Unapproved peptides (for example BPC-157) | Growth hormone boosters (CJC-1295, ipamorelin, tesamorelin) |
|---|---|---|
| Banned in sport? | <Verdict is="yes" /> At all times. WADA bans any substance with no approval for human use, and names BPC-157<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en,wada-2027-list-en" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> At all times<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en,wada-2027-list-en" /> |
| A violation even if you did not mean to take it? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Strict liability: what is found in your body counts<Cite id="wada-code-2021,wada-code-2027" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> The same rule applies<Cite id="wada-code-2021,wada-code-2027" /> |

</Compare>

A product with the wrong contents can end a career. See [sport and doping](/en/legal/sport-and-doping/).

<InDetail title="What the anti-doping rules say">

Most unapproved peptides are banned in sport at all times. The World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) bans any substance with no approval for human use, and names BPC-157. It also bans growth hormone boosters such as CJC-1295, ipamorelin and tesamorelin.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en,wada-2027-list-en" />

Anti-doping rules use strict liability. If a banned substance is found in your body, that is a rule violation. This is true even if you did not mean to take it.<Cite id="wada-code-2021,wada-code-2027" />

</InDetail>

## Rules for everyone

<Lead question="What applies to everyone?">Never share equipment. Never use a product that looks wrong. Never stop a prescribed medicine.</Lead>

<Diagram id="rules-for-everyone" caption="Three rules for everyone, and the main reason for each">

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Never share needles, syringes, vials or pens | Blood on them can pass on HIV and hepatitis B or C. A shared vial or pen can do this even with a new needle.<Cite id="who-2010-injection-toolkit,fda-label-ozempic" /> |
| Never use a product that looks wrong<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy,dailymed-egrifta-sv-label,dailymed-sterile-water-label" /> | It may be spoiled or contaminated. |
| Never stop a medicine your doctor prescribed<Cite id="nhs-herbal-medicines-complementary" /> | The condition it treats can come back or get worse. |

</Diagram>

<InDetail title="Why sharing is dangerous, even with a new needle">

A used needle can carry a tiny amount of blood into a vial or a pen. The next person can then get HIV or hepatitis B or C, even with a new needle. Blood does not need to be visible to pass on these viruses.<Cite id="who-2010-injection-toolkit,fda-label-ozempic" /> Never share a pen, even if you change the needle. Never share a vial, water or other equipment.<Cite id="fda-label-ozempic,fda-label-mounjaro,who-2010-injection-toolkit" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="What a product that looks wrong can mean">

A liquid that should be clear but is cloudy, coloured or has bits in it may be spoiled or contaminated. So may a vial with a broken seal. Do not use it.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy,dailymed-egrifta-sv-label,dailymed-sterile-water-label" /> See [sterile technique](/en/safety/sterile-technique/) and [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/).

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Why not to stop a prescribed medicine">

Do not stop or change a prescribed medicine on your own. Talk to your doctor first.<Cite id="nhs-herbal-medicines-complementary" /> The condition it treats can come back or get worse. If you think a peptide could replace a medicine, discuss it with your doctor first.

</InDetail>

## Tell your doctor

<Lead question="Should I tell my doctor?">Yes. A doctor can check these risks with you. What you say stays private, with few exceptions.</Lead>

A doctor can check for the risks on this page. Many need only a conversation or a simple test. In the US, the UK, Canada and Australia, privacy rules protect what you tell your doctor, with a few exceptions set by law.<Cite id="hhs-hipaa-your-rights,gmc-confidentiality-duties,gmc-confidentiality-framework,opc-privacy-laws-summary,oaic-health-information" /> See [your rights](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/#your-rights-privacy-and-consent).

<Checklist>

- Are you pregnant, breastfeeding or trying to conceive?
- Are you under 18?
- Have you ever had cancer, or do you have an unexplained lump?
- Do you use insulin or diabetes tablets?
- Do you have heart disease or high blood pressure?
- Have you ever had an eating disorder?
- Do you have kidney disease, or have you had pancreatitis?
- Has anyone in your family had medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2?
- Do you have a weak immune system, or take blood thinners?
- Do you compete in a sport with doping tests?

</Checklist>

If you answer yes to any of these, talk to a doctor before you start. Then read the [warning signs](/en/safety/warning-signs/), so you know when to get help.
