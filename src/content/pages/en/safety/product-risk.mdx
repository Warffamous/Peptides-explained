This page is for anyone who has, or is thinking about, a peptide product that did not come from a pharmacy. It names no sellers and gives no amounts.

## What can be wrong inside a vial

<Lead question="What can be wrong with a vial?">Six things: the substance, the amount, extra chemicals, germs, toxins from germs, and clumps.</Lead>

A medicine from a pharmacy has been checked. Its maker must test every batch against set limits before it is released.<Cite id="ecfr-21-211-165,ca-fdr-c02-018" /> A product from outside that system has not been checked by your country's medicines regulator for safety, quality or how it was made.<Cite id="mhra-2026-01-seizures-2025,ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides" />

<Infographic id="online-vial-risks" />

<InDetail title="How each problem happens">

### The wrong substance

One name can cover a different salt form, or even a different active substance.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" /> A salt form is the same molecule paired with a small partner molecule. Regulators treat it as a different active ingredient.<Cite id="fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" /> A national public-health lab found a different growth factor (EGF) in a product thought to hold IGF-1.<Cite id="vanhee-2017-egf-not-igf1" /> An Australian test of one product sold as retatrutide found semaglutide and no retatrutide.<Cite id="rubin-2026-jama-news" />

### The wrong amount

How much peptide goes into each vial depends on how well the maker weighs and fills. Public-health labs that tested ten fake peptide products found that the amount of drug per vial varied a lot.<Cite id="janvier-2018-falsified-peptides-belgium" />

### Extra chemicals from making the peptide

Peptides are built one amino acid at a time. If one step does not finish, the chain misses a piece. These are called deletion sequences. Other steps can leave chains that are too short, have an extra piece, or have a wrong building block.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline,dhondt-2014-peptide-impurities" /> Solvents and other chemicals from the process can also stay behind.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" /> A proper factory removes most of them by purifying the product, then tests what is left.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline,ashraf-2024-online-semaglutide" /> A review of peptide impurities also found products polluted with other, unrelated peptides, a sign of poor manufacturing.<Cite id="dhondt-2014-peptide-impurities" /> {/* residue-ok: EMA evidence (synthetic-peptide guideline, cited for the chemistry, not as the reader's rules) */}

### Metals

Making a peptide can involve metals that speed up the chemical reactions (catalysts). So guidance for makers of peptide medicines lists metals among the impurities to test for.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" /> One public-health lab tested ten peptide products from suspected illegal online pharmacies. Several held arsenic at up to ten times the limit for injected medicines. One held lead.<Cite id="janvier-2018-falsified-peptides-belgium" /> {/* residue-ok: EMA evidence (synthetic-peptide guideline, cited for the chemistry, not as the reader's rules) */}

### Germs and toxins from germs

A vial can hold living germs. A public-health lab found a bacterium called Bacillus cereus in two illegal peptide products meant for injection.<Cite id="janvier-2018-bacillus-cereus" /> Toxins from germs are a separate problem. Endotoxin is part of the outer wall of some bacteria. It is set free when the bacteria die. It is a major cause of fever reactions to medicines with germs in them.<Cite id="schneier-2020-endotoxin-review" /> So a vial can have no living germs and still hold endotoxin.<Cite id="ashraf-2024-semaglutide-test-buys" />

### Breakdown and clumps

Peptides break down over time, faster with heat. Parts of the chain can react with air or water.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline,manning-2010-protein-stability" /> Peptide chains can also stick together into clumps. <Claim level="in-vitro">Clumped peptide can lose its activity</Claim>, and <Claim level="human-limited">clumps in protein medicines have been linked to immune reactions</Claim>.<Cite id="zapadka-2017-peptide-aggregation,rosenberg-2006-aggregates-immunology" /> How a product was stored and shipped is usually unknown. See [sterile technique](/en/safety/sterile-technique/#storage-and-the-cold-chain). {/* residue-ok: EMA evidence (synthetic-peptide guideline, cited for the chemistry, not as the reader's rules) */}

</InDetail>

## What labs found in real products

<Lead question="Have real products been tested?">Yes. Public and university labs found problems in many of the products they tested.</Lead>

<KeyNumber title="Most of the powder was not semaglutide" value="7.7–14.37%" label="of the powder was semaglutide. The seller's papers said it was at least 99% pure." counted="3 vials sold as semaglutide without a prescription, bought from illegal online pharmacies and tested in Hungary" source="ashraf-2024-online-semaglutide" level="in-vitro" />

<KeyNumber title="The amount did not match the label" value="51.3–190%" label="of the amount on the label, in 3 products sold as retatrutide" counted="3 products labelled as retatrutide, submitted anonymously and tested in Australia in 2026" source="piatkowski-2026-retatrutide-product-testing" level="in-vitro" />

<KeyNumber title="Some held a toxic metal" value="up to 10×" label="the arsenic limit for drugs you inject, in some samples" counted="The 10 fake peptide products found most often on one national market, bought from 3 suspected illegal online pharmacies" source="janvier-2018-falsified-peptides-belgium" level="in-vitro" />

Most of these studies tested only a few products. They do not tell you how often a given product is bad. They show that problems are common enough to find.

<InDetail title="All the studies we found, and what each one tested">

| Who tested | What | What they found |
|---|---|---|
| University of Pécs, Hungary (2024) | 3 vials sold as semaglutide, from illegal online pharmacies | More semaglutide than the label said (29–39% more), yet it made up only 8–14% of the powder. No living germs. A raised level of toxins from germs in one. These studies tested seized products or a few test buys, not a random sample. So they cannot say how often a product on the market is bad. They tested products sold to the public, not the authorised medicines with the same names.

## Fake pens that held insulin

<Lead question="What about fake weight-loss pens?">Some fake pens and vials held insulin instead. Insulin can make your blood sugar drop far too low.</Lead>

<Diagram id="insulin-in-fake-pen" caption="How insulin in a fake pen leads to dangerously low blood sugar">

1. **The pen or vial looks like semaglutide**, but holds insulin instead.<Cite id="antonacci-2026-falsified-semaglutide-coma,who-2024-falsified-semaglutide" />
2. **Insulin lowers blood sugar**, even when it is already normal. Semaglutide alone does not cause a serious drop in healthy people.<Cite id="stammers-2026-insulin-falsified-semaglutide,nhs-low-blood-sugar" />
3. **Blood sugar drops.** You may shake, sweat and feel hungry, then become confused or drowsy.<Cite id="nhs-low-blood-sugar" />
4. **Without sugar, this can end in a coma.** Two people in one case report needed sugar through a drip for up to two days.<Cite id="stammers-2026-insulin-falsified-semaglutide" />

</Diagram>

<Callout type="stop" title="Shaky, sweaty or confused after an injection?" id="stop-now">

- If you can swallow safely, take something sugary now, such as fruit juice or glucose tablets.
- If you become drowsy or confused, someone must call your local emergency number. Do not give food or drink to someone who cannot swallow safely.
- Tell the doctor what you injected, and keep the pen or vial.

<Cite id="nhs-low-blood-sugar,mhra-2023-fake-weight-loss-pens,stammers-2026-insulin-falsified-semaglutide" />

</Callout>

<InDetail title="Where fake pens with insulin were found">

- **United Kingdom, 2023.** The medicines regulator seized fake pens. People were treated in hospital for very low blood sugar, which suggested the pens held insulin.<Cite id="mhra-2023-fake-weight-loss-pens" />
- **A 2026 case report.** Hospital doctors described two people in a coma from insulin. Both had injected semaglutide obtained without a prescription.<Cite id="stammers-2026-insulin-falsified-semaglutide" />
- **Italy, 2026.** A woman went into a coma from low blood sugar. Lab tests showed her vial, bought online as semaglutide, held insulin.<Cite id="antonacci-2026-falsified-semaglutide-coma" />
- **A large database of side-effect reports.** A study looked at Europe's side-effect database from 2018 to the end of 2025. It found 234 reports about possible fake semaglutide. Low blood sugar was reported more often than with the real medicine.<Cite id="zinzi-2026-counterfeit-semaglutide-eudravigilance" /> {/* residue-ok: evidence (a study of the EudraVigilance side-effect database) */}

The World Health Organization also warned that a fake pen may hold another substance, such as insulin.<Cite id="who-2024-falsified-semaglutide" />

</InDetail>

## What a lab report can and cannot tell you

<Lead question="We have a lab report, so we know what is in the vial and that it is clean?">A report shows what was in the sample tested. "Clean" needs germ and toxin tests for that batch.</Lead>

<Diagram id="lab-report-ladder" caption="What a lab report can and cannot tell you, from the most useful evidence to the least">

1. **Most informative: a report from an independent lab accredited to ISO/IEC 17025, for the exact batch on your label.** The batch number on the report matches the one on your vial. It tests identity (mass spectrometry), the amount per vial (an assay), purity (HPLC), and ideally endotoxin and sterility.<Cite id="iso-iec-17025-2017,nist-nvlap,fda-2016-ich-q7-gmp-api" />
   - *It can show:* what was in the sample that this lab tested.
   - *It leaves unknown:* whether your vial is sterile (most reports do not test sterility; some test endotoxin); the real amount in your own vial; whether your vial really came from the tested batch; whether the report is genuine; how the vial was stored and shipped after the test.
2. **Less informative: the seller's own report, or a report from a lab you cannot identify, check or verify.**
   - *It can show:* what someone says a lab found.
   - *It leaves unknown:* everything in step 1, and also whether the test was done at all, on what, and by whom. A report with an empty batch field cannot be linked to any vial.
3. **No report.**
   - *It leaves unknown:* what is in the vial, how much, how pure, whether it holds germs or toxins from germs, and how it was stored and shipped.
4. **Not evidence at all: "the seller uses it himself", reviews, or "third-party tested" without a report you can read.**
   - *It shows:* nothing about what is in your vial.
   - *It leaves unknown:* what is in the vial, how much, how pure, whether it holds germs or toxins from germs, and how it was stored and shipped. It is no better than no report.

Reports from accredited labs must say that their results relate only to the items tested.<Cite id="nist-2018-iso17025-crosswalk-reporting" /> So a report covers one sample, not your vial. "Clean" would need a sterility test and an endotoxin test for that batch. Neither covers what happens after: storage, shipping, or a needle going into the vial.<Cite id="ecfr-21-211-167,fda-2004-aseptic-processing-guidance,fda-2026-pyrogen-endotoxins-qa" />

Only the pharmacy supply chain has these checks built in. The maker follows good manufacturing practice (GMP), a responsible person approves every tested batch before release, and the regulator inspects. No report replaces that.<Cite id="ecfr-21-211-22,ecfr-21-211-165,uk-hmr-2012-sch-7-qualified-person,ca-fdr-c02-014,fda-cgmp-facts,mhra-gmp-gdp-guidance,au-tga-manufacturing" />

</Diagram>

<InDetail title="What each step leaves unknown, and why">

- **Your vial or the sample?** A report describes the sample the lab received. Products differ from vial to vial, so it cannot speak for yours.<Cite id="janvier-2018-falsified-peptides-belgium,piatkowski-2026-retatrutide-product-testing" />
- **Which batch?** On a medicine, the batch (lot) number on the label traces how that package was made.<Cite id="ecfr-21-201-100" /> A proper certificate names the batch it covers.<Cite id="fda-2016-ich-q7-gmp-api" /> Without a matching batch number, nothing links the report to your vial.
- **Is it sterile?** The FDA described example reports for BPC-157, KPV and TB-500. None showed a test for germs.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> Even a real sterility test checks only a few vials. FDA guidance gives an example: test 20 vials from a batch of 10,000 in which 1 in 1,000 has germs, and the batch passes 98 times in 100.<Cite id="fda-2004-aseptic-processing-guidance" /> That is why medicine makers do not rely on testing alone.<Cite id="fda-cgmp-facts" />
- **Is the report genuine?** A proper certificate is dated and signed, and names the lab that did the tests.<Cite id="fda-2016-ich-q7-gmp-api,nist-2018-iso17025-crosswalk-reporting" /> If you cannot check it with that lab, you cannot know it is genuine.
- **What happened after the test?** A report says nothing about how the vial was stored and shipped. The FDA has had complaints about compounded GLP-1 products that arrived warm.<Cite id="fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Why '99% pure' can mislead">

Most reports show a purity figure from HPLC. HPLC pushes a dissolved sample through a column. Different molecules come out at different times, and a detector draws a peak for each. Purity is the size of the main peak compared with all the peaks the detector can see. So the result depends on what the detector can see.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,choules-2020-nmr-mannitol,ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" /> {/* residue-ok: EMA evidence (synthetic-peptide guideline, cited for the chemistry, not as the reader's rules) */}

That leaves gaps:

- **It does not say what the main peak is.** A wrong molecule can give a clean peak. Identity needs another method, such as mass spectrometry, which weighs the molecules. Guidance for makers of synthetic peptides advises at least two different methods.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" /> {/* residue-ok: EMA evidence (synthetic-peptide guideline, cited for the chemistry, not as the reader's rules) */}
- **It misses what the detector cannot see.** A US study tested two peptides bought for research. By weight, 20% and 43% of them was a sugar alcohol (mannitol). The usual test cannot see it.<Cite id="choules-2020-nmr-mannitol" />
- **It is not the amount of peptide in the powder.** In the Hungarian study, the test showed one clean semaglutide peak. Yet semaglutide was only 8–14% of the powder, while the papers claimed at least 99% purity.<Cite id="ashraf-2024-online-semaglutide" />
- **Two molecules can hide in one peak.** Guidance for makers warns about impurities that come out at the same time as the peptide.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" /> {/* residue-ok: EMA evidence (synthetic-peptide guideline, cited for the chemistry, not as the reader's rules) */}
- **It tests no germs, no toxins from germs and no metals.** Those need separate tests. In the US, the methods for sterility and endotoxin are set out in the US Pharmacopeia (chapters 71 and 85).<Cite id="fda-2004-aseptic-processing-guidance,fda-2026-pyrogen-endotoxins-qa" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Who made the report, and why that matters">

- **Which lab?** ISO/IEC 17025 is the international standard for testing labs. It sets requirements for their competence, impartiality and consistent work.<Cite id="iso-iec-17025-2017" /> When a lab is accredited to it, an outside body checks how the lab works. In the US, one such body is NVLAP, run by NIST.<Cite id="nist-nvlap" /> A report from a lab you cannot find or check is weak evidence.
- **Who paid?** A report the seller chooses to show you comes from someone who gains when it looks good. The example reports the FDA found came from sellers' own websites.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" />

</InDetail>

## What a medicine maker must test

<Lead question="What does a medicine maker have to test?">Every batch: what it is, how much, how pure, and for injections, germs and toxins.</Lead>

<Compare title="A typical seller's lab report leaves out most of the tests a medicine maker must do." detail="Left: what the FDA found in certificates for BPC-157, KPV and TB-500 in 2026. Right: what US rules and international guidance ask of a medicine maker, for every batch.">

| | A typical seller's lab report | What a medicine maker must test |
|---|---|---|
| Shows it is the right substance? | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Some list an identity test. Many show only a purity figure.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> Every batch is tested for identity before release, with a test specific to the molecule.<Cite id="ecfr-21-211-165,fda-1999-ich-q6b" /> |
| Says how much peptide is in the vial? | <Verdict is="no" /> Most give only a purity figure.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> The strength of every batch is measured.<Cite id="ecfr-21-211-165,fda-1999-ich-q6b" /> |
| Names the impurities? | <Verdict is="no" /> Impurities were not named.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> Purity must meet set limits, and impurities are measured.<Cite id="ecfr-21-211-160,fda-1999-ich-q6b" /> |
| Tests for germs and toxins from germs? | <Verdict is="no" /> None of the example reports the FDA described showed germ tests.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> Every batch sold as sterile is tested for germs and for endotoxin, by set methods.<Cite id="ecfr-21-211-167,fda-2004-aseptic-processing-guidance,fda-2026-pyrogen-endotoxins-qa" /> |

</Compare>

<InDetail title="How the pharmacy supply chain checks every batch, by country">

Testing alone is not enough. Usually only a small sample of a batch is tested, so the whole process must be controlled. This is good manufacturing practice (GMP).<Cite id="fda-cgmp-facts" />

- **United States.** A quality control unit at the maker approves or rejects every batch.<Cite id="ecfr-21-211-22" /> Each batch is tested for identity and strength before release. A sterile product is tested for germs and for toxins from germs.<Cite id="ecfr-21-211-165,ecfr-21-211-167" /> The FDA inspects factories worldwide.<Cite id="fda-cgmp-facts" />
- **United Kingdom.** A qualified person must make sure that each batch was made and checked as the law and the licence require.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-sch-7-qualified-person" /> The MHRA inspects makers.<Cite id="mhra-gmp-gdp-guidance" />
- **Canada.** Each batch is tested against its specifications before sale. No batch may be sold until the person in charge of quality control approves it.<Cite id="ca-fdr-c02-018,ca-fdr-c02-014" /> A sterile medicine must be made by a method that has been shown to make it sterile.<Cite id="ca-fdr-c02-029" />
- **Australia.** Makers need a licence and must follow GMP. The TGA inspects them regularly.<Cite id="au-tga-manufacturing" />

The international guidance on these tests (ICH Q6B, which the FDA also publishes) covers peptide and protein medicines made in living cells. It lists tests for identity, purity and impurities, and quantity, and, for injections, sterility and endotoxin. It does not cover peptides made by chemical synthesis; those have their own guidance.<Cite id="fda-1999-ich-q6b,ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" /> {/* residue-ok: EMA evidence (synthetic-peptide guideline, cited for the chemistry, not as the reader's rules) */}

</InDetail>

## Mistakes with units and syringes

<Lead question="Can I get the amount wrong even if the product is fine?">Yes. People have mixed up the units on a syringe and injected far too much.</Lead>

<KeyNumber title="Some people injected many times the amount they meant to" value="5 to 20×" label="the amount they meant to inject, in reports to the US regulator" who="People in the US using compounded semaglutide from multi-dose vials; reports to the FDA, up to July 2024" source="fda-2024-compounded-semaglutide-dosing-errors" level="human-limited" />

This site gives no amounts and no mixing sums. A doctor or pharmacist can check your understanding of a prescribed medicine.

<InDetail title="Why these mistakes happen">

A pen from a pharmacy gives a fixed dose with each click. A vial does not. You draw up the liquid yourself, so every step can go wrong.<Cite id="lambson-2023-semaglutide-administration-errors" />

- **Three units mean different things.** Milligrams (mg) measure the amount of substance. Millilitres (mL) measure the amount of liquid. The "units" on an insulin syringe are marks for volume, made for insulin. They say nothing about how much peptide is in that liquid.<Cite id="fda-2024-compounded-semaglutide-dosing-errors,lambson-2023-semaglutide-administration-errors" />
- **The strength of the liquid differs.** Products come in different strengths. So the same mark on a syringe can hold a very different amount of peptide.<Cite id="fda-2024-compounded-semaglutide-dosing-errors" />
- **What happened.** Some people needed hospital care, and some health workers also got the conversion wrong.<Cite id="fda-2024-compounded-semaglutide-dosing-errors" /> A US poison centre described two people who injected ten times the intended dose and were sick for days.<Cite id="lambson-2023-semaglutide-administration-errors" />

</InDetail>

## If you already have a product

<Lead question="I already have a product. What can I check?">The seal, the label, how it looks and how it was kept. Keep the vial. Tell a doctor.</Lead>

<ProductCheck />

A doctor can also check you for problems that a clean-looking vial can hide.<Cite id="vic-health-2026-retatrutide-liver" /> A clean vial is not proof of a clean product. Germs, toxins from germs and the wrong substance are invisible.<Cite id="ashraf-2024-online-semaglutide,smith-2013-fungal-methylprednisolone" /> Read [sterile technique](/en/safety/sterile-technique/), [monitoring](/en/safety/monitoring/) and [if something goes wrong](/en/safety/if-something-goes-wrong/).

<WarningSigns only="injection,allergic,metabolic" />
