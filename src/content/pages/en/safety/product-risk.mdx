This page is for anyone who has, or is thinking about, a peptide product that did not come from a pharmacy. It names no sellers and gives no amounts.

## What can be wrong inside a vial

<Lead question="What can be wrong with a vial?">Six things: the substance, the amount, extra chemicals, germs, toxins from germs, and clumps.</Lead>

A medicine from a pharmacy has been checked by a regulator. Its maker must test it against set limits.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" /> For a product from outside that system, nobody can show you that such checks were done.<Cite id="igj-online-medicijnen,igj-2025-illegal-online-medicines" />

<Infographic id="online-vial-risks" />

<InDetail title="How each problem happens">

### The wrong substance

One name can cover a different salt form, or even a different active substance.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" /> A salt form is the same molecule paired with a small partner molecule. Regulators treat it as a different active ingredient.<Cite id="fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" /> A Belgian lab found a different growth factor (EGF) in a product thought to hold IGF-1.<Cite id="vanhee-2017-egf-not-igf1" /> An Australian test of one product sold as retatrutide found semaglutide and no retatrutide.<Cite id="rubin-2026-jama-news" />

### The wrong amount

How much peptide goes into each vial depends on how well the maker weighs and fills. Belgian public-health labs found that the amount of drug per vial varied a lot.<Cite id="janvier-2018-falsified-peptides-belgium" />

### Extra chemicals from making the peptide

Peptides are built one amino acid at a time. If one step does not finish, the chain misses a piece. These are called deletion sequences. Other steps can leave chains that are too short, have an extra piece, or have a wrong building block.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline,dhondt-2014-peptide-impurities" /> Solvents and other chemicals from the process can also stay behind.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" /> A proper factory removes most of them by purifying the product, then tests what is left.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline,ashraf-2024-online-semaglutide" /> A review of peptide impurities also found products polluted with other, unrelated peptides, a sign of poor manufacturing.<Cite id="dhondt-2014-peptide-impurities" />

### Metals

Making a peptide can involve metals that speed up the chemical reactions (catalysts). So EU guidance asks makers to test for metals.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" /> Belgian labs tested ten peptide products from suspected illegal online pharmacies. Several held arsenic at up to ten times the limit for injected medicines. One held lead.<Cite id="janvier-2018-falsified-peptides-belgium" />

### Germs and toxins from germs

A vial can hold living germs. A Belgian lab found a bacterium called Bacillus cereus in two illegal peptide products meant for injection.<Cite id="janvier-2018-bacillus-cereus" /> Toxins from germs are a separate problem. Endotoxin is part of the outer wall of some bacteria. It is set free when the bacteria die. It is a major cause of fever reactions to medicines with germs in them.<Cite id="schneier-2020-endotoxin-review" /> So a vial can have no living germs and still hold endotoxin.<Cite id="ashraf-2024-semaglutide-test-buys" />

### Breakdown and clumps

Peptides break down over time, faster with heat. Parts of the chain can react with air or water.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline,manning-2010-protein-stability" /> Peptide chains can also stick together into clumps. <Claim level="in-vitro">Clumped peptide can lose its activity</Claim>, and <Claim level="human-limited">clumps in protein medicines have been linked to immune reactions</Claim>.<Cite id="zapadka-2017-peptide-aggregation,rosenberg-2006-aggregates-immunology" /> How a product was stored and shipped is usually unknown. See [sterile technique](/en/safety/sterile-technique/#storage-and-the-cold-chain).

</InDetail>

## What labs found in real products

<Lead question="Have real products been tested?">Yes. Public and university labs found problems in many of the products they tested.</Lead>

<KeyNumber title="Most of the powder was not semaglutide" value="7.7–14.37%" label="of the powder was semaglutide. The seller's papers said it was at least 99% pure." counted="3 vials sold as semaglutide without a prescription, bought from illegal online pharmacies and tested in Hungary" source="ashraf-2024-online-semaglutide" level="in-vitro" />

<KeyNumber title="The amount did not match the label" value="51.3–190%" label="of the amount on the label, in 3 products sold as retatrutide" counted="3 products labelled as retatrutide, submitted anonymously and tested in Australia in 2026" source="piatkowski-2026-retatrutide-product-testing" level="in-vitro" />

<KeyNumber title="Some held a toxic metal" value="up to 10×" label="the arsenic limit for drugs you inject, in some samples" counted="The 10 peptide products most often found on the Belgian market, from 3 suspected illegal online pharmacies" source="janvier-2018-falsified-peptides-belgium" level="in-vitro" />

Most of these studies tested only a few products. They do not tell you how often a given product is bad. They show that problems are common enough to find.

<InDetail title="All the studies we found, and what each one tested">

| Who tested | What | What they found |
|---|---|---|
| University of Pécs, Hungary (2024) | 3 vials sold as semaglutide, from illegal online pharmacies | More semaglutide than the label said (29–39% more), yet it made up only 8–14% of the powder. No living germs. A raised level of toxins from germs in one. The paper is unclear about the other two. 3 other orders never arrived.<Cite id="ashraf-2024-online-semaglutide,ashraf-2024-semaglutide-test-buys" /> |
| University of Queensland, Australia (2026) | 3 products sold as retatrutide | All held retatrutide, at 51%, 165% and 190% of the label. Lead was found, at a level the authors judged well below the limit. Germs and toxins were not tested.<Cite id="piatkowski-2026-retatrutide-product-testing" /> |
| Belgian public-health labs (2018) | The 10 peptide products most often found in Belgium | A large spread in amount per vial. Purity as low as 5% for some peptides. Arsenic up to ten times the limit; lead in one sample.<Cite id="janvier-2018-falsified-peptides-belgium" /> |
| Belgian public-health labs (2018) | 2 illegal peptide products for injection | The bacterium Bacillus cereus.<Cite id="janvier-2018-bacillus-cereus" /> |
| Anti-doping lab, Cologne (2014) | 337 black-market products from Germany, 2010–2013 | 12.8% held peptide hormones or growth factors. The authors call several substances fake or non-approved.<Cite id="krug-2014-black-market-germany" /> |
| Anti-Doping Switzerland and Cologne (2017) | 1,190 doping products seized at the Swiss border, most labelled as muscle-building (anabolic) drugs | Fewer than 20% held the substance on the label in the stated amount.<Cite id="weber-2017-swiss-border" /> |

These studies tested seized products or a few test buys, not a random sample. So they cannot say how often a product on the market is bad. They tested products sold to the public, not the authorised medicines with the same names.

</InDetail>

## Fake pens that held insulin

<Lead question="What about fake weight-loss pens?">Some fake pens and vials held insulin instead. Insulin can make your blood sugar drop far too low.</Lead>

<Diagram id="insulin-in-fake-pen" caption="How insulin in a fake pen leads to dangerously low blood sugar">

1. **The pen or vial looks like semaglutide**, but holds insulin instead.<Cite id="bfarm-ozempic-falsification,antonacci-2026-falsified-semaglutide-coma" />
2. **Insulin lowers blood sugar**, even when it is already normal. Semaglutide alone does not cause a serious drop in healthy people.<Cite id="stammers-2026-insulin-falsified-semaglutide,nhs-low-blood-sugar" />
3. **Blood sugar drops.** You may shake, sweat and feel hungry, then become confused or drowsy.<Cite id="nhs-low-blood-sugar" />
4. **Without sugar, this can end in a coma.** Two people treated in Dutch hospitals needed sugar through a drip for up to two days.<Cite id="stammers-2026-insulin-falsified-semaglutide" />

</Diagram>

<Callout type="stop" title="Shaky, sweaty or confused after an injection?" id="stop-now">

- If you can swallow safely, take something sugary now, such as fruit juice or glucose tablets.
- If you become drowsy or confused, someone must call your local emergency number. Do not give food or drink to someone who cannot swallow safely.
- Tell the doctor what you injected, and keep the pen or vial.

<Cite id="nhs-low-blood-sugar,mhra-2023-fake-weight-loss-pens,stammers-2026-insulin-falsified-semaglutide" />

</Callout>

<InDetail title="Where fake pens with insulin were found">

- **Germany and Austria, 2023.** Fake pens labelled as Ozempic were found at wholesalers. Tests found no semaglutide in them, and an official German lab found insulin. Some fakes had reached patients in Austria.<Cite id="bfarm-ozempic-falsification,ema-2023-falsified-ozempic" />
- **United Kingdom, 2023.** The medicines regulator seized fake pens. People were treated in hospital for very low blood sugar, which suggested the pens held insulin.<Cite id="mhra-2023-fake-weight-loss-pens" />
- **The Netherlands, 2026.** Dutch hospital doctors described two people in a coma from insulin. Both had injected semaglutide obtained without a prescription.<Cite id="stammers-2026-insulin-falsified-semaglutide" />
- **Italy, 2026.** A woman went into a coma from low blood sugar. Lab tests showed her vial, bought online as semaglutide, held insulin.<Cite id="antonacci-2026-falsified-semaglutide-coma" />
- **Across Europe.** A study looked at the EU side-effect database from 2018 to the end of 2025. It found 234 reports about possible fake semaglutide. Low blood sugar was reported more often than with the real medicine.<Cite id="zinzi-2026-counterfeit-semaglutide-eudravigilance" />
- **Now.** Insulin pens were stolen from a Dutch wholesaler. On 20 October 2026 the Dutch inspectorate warned that they may be sold online, possibly as an obesity medicine.<Cite id="igj-2026-10-stolen-medicines" />

The World Health Organization also warned that a fake pen may hold another substance, such as insulin.<Cite id="who-2024-falsified-semaglutide" />

</InDetail>

## What a lab certificate shows, and what it does not

<Lead question="My product came with a lab report. Is that enough?">No. It shows one test of one sample. It says nothing about germs or your own vial.</Lead>

A certificate of analysis is a lab report on a sample. It is only as good as the tests it includes, and the lab that did them.

<Compare title="A typical seller's lab report leaves out most of the tests a medicine needs." detail="Left: what the FDA found in certificates for BPC-157, KPV and TB-500 in 2026. Right: what EU guidance asks a maker of a peptide medicine to test.">

| | A typical seller's lab report | What EU guidance asks for a peptide medicine |
|---|---|---|
| Proves it is the right substance, with two different methods? | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Some list an identity test. Many show only a purity figure.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> At least two different methods are advised.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" /> |
| Says how much peptide is in the vial? | <Verdict is="no" /> Most give only a purity figure.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> The content is measured.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" /> |
| Names the impurities one by one? | <Verdict is="no" /> Impurities were not named.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> Each impurity is reported, down to small amounts.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" /> |
| Tests for germs and toxins from germs? | <Verdict is="no" /> None of the example reports the FDA described showed germ tests.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> Both are on the list.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" /> |

</Compare>

<InDetail title="Why '99% pure' can mislead">

Most reports show a purity figure from HPLC. HPLC pushes a dissolved sample through a column. Different molecules come out at different times, and a detector draws a peak for each. Purity is the size of the main peak compared with all the peaks the detector can see. So the result depends on what the detector can see.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,choules-2020-nmr-mannitol,ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" />

That leaves gaps:

- **It does not say what the main peak is.** A wrong molecule can give a clean peak. Identity needs another method, such as mass spectrometry, which weighs the molecules.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" />
- **It misses what the detector cannot see.** A US study tested two peptides bought for research. By weight, 20% and 43% of them was a sugar alcohol (mannitol). The usual test cannot see it.<Cite id="choules-2020-nmr-mannitol" />
- **It is not the amount of peptide in the powder.** In the Hungarian study, the test showed one clean semaglutide peak. Yet semaglutide was only 8–14% of the powder, while the papers claimed at least 99% purity.<Cite id="ashraf-2024-online-semaglutide" />
- **Two molecules can hide in one peak.** EU guidance warns about impurities that come out at the same time as the peptide.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" />
- **It tests no germs, no toxins from germs and no metals.** Those need separate tests.<Cite id="ema-2025-synthetic-peptides-guideline" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Who made the report, and why that matters">

- **Which sample?** A report describes the sample the lab received. Products differ from vial to vial, so it cannot speak for yours.<Cite id="janvier-2018-falsified-peptides-belgium,piatkowski-2026-retatrutide-product-testing" />
- **Which lab?** Labs can be accredited to an international standard, ISO/IEC 17025. An outside body then checks how the lab works.<Cite id="nist-nvlap" /> A report from a lab you cannot find or check is weak evidence.
- **Who paid?** A report the seller chooses to show you comes from someone who gains when it looks good. The example reports the FDA found came from sellers' own websites.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" />

</InDetail>

## Mistakes with units and syringes

<Lead question="Can I get the amount wrong even if the product is fine?">Yes. People have mixed up the units on a syringe and injected far too much.</Lead>

<KeyNumber title="Some people injected many times the amount they meant to" value="5 to 20×" label="the amount they meant to inject, in reports to the US regulator" who="People in the US using compounded semaglutide from multi-dose vials; reports to the FDA, up to July 2024" source="fda-2024-compounded-semaglutide-dosing-errors" level="human-limited" />

This site gives no amounts and no mixing sums. A doctor or pharmacist can check your understanding of a prescribed medicine.

<InDetail title="Why these mistakes happen">

A pen from a pharmacy gives a fixed dose with each click. A vial does not. You draw up the liquid yourself, so every step can go wrong.<Cite id="lambson-2023-semaglutide-administration-errors" />

- **Three units mean different things.** Milligrams (mg) measure the amount of substance. Millilitres (mL) measure the amount of liquid. The "units" on an insulin syringe are marks for volume, made for insulin. They say nothing about how much peptide is in that liquid.<Cite id="fda-2024-compounded-semaglutide-dosing-errors,lambson-2023-semaglutide-administration-errors" />
- **The strength of the liquid differs.** Products come in different strengths. So the same mark on a syringe can hold a very different amount of peptide.<Cite id="fda-2024-compounded-semaglutide-dosing-errors" />
- **What happened.** Some people needed hospital care, and some health workers also got the conversion wrong.<Cite id="fda-2024-compounded-semaglutide-dosing-errors" /> A US poison centre described two people who injected ten times the intended dose and were sick for days.<Cite id="lambson-2023-semaglutide-administration-errors" />

</InDetail>

## Warning signs in the product, the use and the seller

<Lead question="How can I spot a risky product or seller?">Look for warning signs. But a product with no warning signs can still be bad.</Lead>

<Diagram id="red-flags-product-use-seller" caption="Warning signs in three places: the product, how it is used, and who sells it">

| In the product | In how it is used | In the seller |
|---|---|---|
| No expiry date, or the date has passed<Cite id="fda-considering-online-pharmacy" /> | A vial is shared, or a used needle went into it<Cite id="who-2010-injection-toolkit" /> | Sells a prescription medicine without a prescription<Cite id="fda-considering-online-pharmacy,igj-online-medicijnen" /> |
| Broken seal, damaged packaging, or a label you cannot read<Cite id="fda-considering-online-pharmacy" /> | An opened GLP-1 type vial is used for more than 28 days<Cite id="fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" /> | Labels it "for research" or "not for human use", yet gives instructions for injecting<Cite id="fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" /> |
| It looks different from the medicine it claims to be<Cite id="fda-considering-online-pharmacy,ema-2023-falsified-ozempic" /> | A product that arrived warm is used anyway<Cite id="fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" /> | No licensed pharmacist you can ask questions<Cite id="fda-considering-online-pharmacy" /> |
| Cloudy liquid, bits in it, or powder that will not dissolve clearly<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy,dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> | Amounts are worked out with syringe units without help<Cite id="fda-2024-compounded-semaglutide-dosing-errors" /> | Asks for extra payment to "clear customs"<Cite id="ashraf-2024-semaglutide-test-buys" /> |
| Only the seller's own lab report, with purity and nothing else<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> | Injections given by someone with no medical training<Cite id="buzzi-2026-retatrutide-case-report" /> | No information about where the product was made<Cite id="igj-2025-illegal-online-medicines" /> |

</Diagram>

These signs help you spot danger. They cannot help you find a good product outside a pharmacy. Nothing you can see shows what is inside.<Cite id="igj-online-medicijnen" />

## If you already have a product

<Lead question="I already have a product. What can I check?">The seal, the label, how it looks and how it was kept. Keep the vial. Tell a doctor.</Lead>
