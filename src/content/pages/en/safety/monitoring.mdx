Monitoring means checking your body at set moments: blood tests, blood pressure, your skin and your weight. This page explains which checks fit which kind of product, what each one can find, and what it misses. It gives no schedule. How often to check is something to agree with a doctor who knows you.

## Why and when to check

<Lead question="Why check at all?">A test can show some harms before you feel them. A first test helps you compare later.</Lead>

<Diagram id="baseline-then-compare" caption="Check before you start, then again at moments agreed with your doctor, and compare">

1. **Before you start: a first test.** Doctors call it a baseline. Normal results differ from person to person, so your own first result is what to compare later results with.<Cite id="medlineplus-egfr,dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />
2. **While you use it: the same tests again**, at moments you agree with your doctor. Tesamorelin is a growth hormone booster authorised in the US. Its label asks for blood sugar checks before and during use. The label of bremelanotide asks the same for blood pressure.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label,dailymed-vyleesi-label" />
3. **Compare.** Your doctor compares new results with your own earlier ones, to see a change.<Cite id="medlineplus-egfr,medlineplus-liver-function-tests" />
4. **When you feel unwell: see a doctor then**, whatever the plan. Do not wait for the next check.<Cite id="vic-health-2026-retatrutide-liver" />

</Diagram>

Tell your doctor what you use, so the two of you can choose the right checks. See [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/).

## Blood tests: what each one shows

<Lead question="Which blood tests matter?">Kidney, blood sugar, IGF-1, liver, blood fat and blood count tests. Which ones depends on the product.</Lead>

<Diagram id="blood-tests-by-product" caption="Which blood test fits which kind of product, and what each one misses">

| Test | What it shows | Most useful for | What it misses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kidney function (creatinine, eGFR) | How well your kidneys filter the blood | GLP-1 type products (semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide) | Lots of muscle, a meal with meat or some medicines can change the result |
| Blood sugar (HbA1c, fasting glucose) | HbA1c: your average blood sugar over 2–3 months | Growth hormone boosters; GLP-1 type products used with insulin or diabetes tablets | A sudden low after one injection |
| IGF-1 | How active growth hormone is in your body | Growth hormone boosters (tesamorelin, CJC-1295) | What a high level means in the long run |
| Liver tests | Liver cells under stress, and how the liver works | Products from outside a pharmacy, such as those sold as retatrutide; GLP-1 type products | The cause: an abnormal result needs more tests |
| Blood fats (cholesterol) | Your risk of heart disease | Everyone, as a general heart check | Almost nothing about an experimental peptide |
| Full blood count | Red and white blood cells and platelets | Anyone who injects | A skin infection: watch for red, hot, swollen skin instead |

</Diagram>

<Cite id="medlineplus-egfr,medlineplus-hba1c,medlineplus-igf1,medlineplus-liver-function-tests,medlineplus-cholesterol,medlineplus-cbc,dailymed-egrifta-sv-label,fda-label-wegovy,fda-label-mounjaro,vic-health-2026-retatrutide-liver,nhs-cellulitis" />

<InDetail title="Kidneys, blood sugar and IGF-1: the reasons">

### Kidneys

GLP-1 type products often cause feeling sick, vomiting and diarrhoea, especially at first. Losing a lot of fluid dries you out. In rare cases this can harm the kidneys, even causing acute kidney failure.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy,fda-label-mounjaro" /> A man with type 1 diabetes had kidney injury and dangerous ketone levels after a product sold online as retatrutide. He also had a gut infection, so the cause is not certain.<Cite id="branine-2026-retatrutide-ketosis-case" />

The kidney test (eGFR) is worked out mainly from creatinine, a waste product from muscle. Body size, a lot of muscle, meat and some medicines can change it. So tell your doctor if you train hard or have gained muscle.<Cite id="medlineplus-egfr" />

### Blood sugar

<Claim level="human-controlled">Boosting growth hormone can raise blood sugar.</Claim> Tesamorelin was tested in two placebo-controlled trials in adults with HIV and extra belly fat. Within 26 weeks, 5% of people on tesamorelin reached a diabetes-level HbA1c (6.5% or more). On placebo it was 1%. The label asks for blood sugar checks before and during use.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" />

GLP-1 type products work the other way. On their own they rarely push blood sugar too low. Together with insulin or some diabetes tablets they can.<Cite id="fda-label-mounjaro,fda-label-wegovy" /> The short animation shows why.

<Explainer id="glp1-class-hypo" />

HbA1c shows your average over two to three months, so it cannot catch a sudden low. It is also less reliable if you have anaemia.<Cite id="medlineplus-hba1c" /> A sudden low after an injection is a warning sign, not something to wait for a test for. See [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/#fake-pens-that-held-insulin).

### IGF-1

IGF-1 carries out many effects of growth hormone. Growth hormone goes up and down during the day, but IGF-1 is steadier, so it is a dependable test.<Cite id="medlineplus-igf1" />

<Claim level="human-controlled">Tesamorelin raises IGF-1.</Claim> After 26 weeks in those trials, almost half of the people on tesamorelin (47%) had IGF-1 above the normal range. The label says the effects of high IGF-1 over a long time are unknown, and asks for IGF-1 checks.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> <Claim level="human-controlled">CJC-1295 also raised IGF-1.</Claim> Two placebo-controlled trials tested it in healthy adults. One injection raised IGF-1 to 1.5 to 3 times its usual level, for 9 to 11 days. The published summary does not say how many people took part. Three of the authors worked for the company that made it.<Cite id="teichman-2006-cjc1295" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Liver, blood fats and blood count: the reasons">

### Liver

Products from outside a pharmacy can hold impurities.<Cite id="janvier-2018-falsified-peptides-belgium" /> Some may harm organs such as the liver. In the Australian state of Victoria, six people had acute liver injury in 2026 after using a product sold as retatrutide. The health department thinks an impurity may be the cause. It asked people who keep using the product to see their doctor about liver tests.<Cite id="vic-health-2026-retatrutide-liver" /> Gallstones are also a known side effect of GLP-1 medicines.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy" />

Liver tests measure enzymes made mainly in the liver, a protein (albumin) and a waste product (bilirubin). An abnormal result does not always mean liver disease. More tests are needed to find the cause.<Cite id="medlineplus-liver-function-tests" />

### Blood fats

A cholesterol test helps judge your risk of heart disease.<Cite id="medlineplus-cholesterol" /> Semaglutide improved blood fats a little in trials.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy" /> For experimental peptides, we found no human data on blood fats. So this test is a general health check, not a check of the product.

### Full blood count

A full blood count looks at red and white blood cells and platelets. It can help find infections and anaemia.<Cite id="medlineplus-cbc" /> Every injection can let germs in. A skin infection usually shows first as redness, heat and swelling around the spot.<Cite id="nhs-cellulitis" /> See [warning signs](/en/safety/warning-signs/).

</InDetail>

## Checks without a needle

<Lead question="What else is worth checking?">Your blood pressure and pulse, your skin and moles, your weight and strength, and whether you are pregnant.</Lead>

<Diagram id="checks-without-a-needle" caption="Four checks you can do with a doctor, or partly at home">

| Check | Most useful for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Blood pressure and pulse | Tanning and sex-drive peptides (melanotan II, PT-141); GLP-1 type products | PT-141 raises blood pressure for some hours after each dose. GLP-1 medicines raise the resting pulse a little. |
| Skin and moles | Tanning peptides (melanotan I and II) | New or changing moles have been reported. |
| Weight and strength | GLP-1 type products | Part of the weight lost is not fat. |
| Pregnancy test | Anyone who could become pregnant | Several must not be used in pregnancy. For many, there are no data. |

</Diagram>

<Cite id="dailymed-vyleesi-label,fda-label-wegovy,fda-label-mounjaro,habbema-2017-melanotan-review,neeland-2024-lean-mass,dailymed-egrifta-sv-label,fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" />

<InDetail title="Blood pressure and pulse: the reasons">

Bremelanotide (PT-141) is authorised in the US as Vyleesi. After each dose, blood pressure goes up for a while. In studies, the average rise was up to 6 points in the top number and 3 in the bottom number. It peaked 2 to 4 hours after the dose and was usually gone within 12 hours. The label asks doctors to check that blood pressure is well controlled before and during use. It must not be used with uncontrolled high blood pressure or known heart disease.<Cite id="dailymed-vyleesi-label" /> So a reading taken just after a dose and one taken before it are not the same thing.

Melanotan II is a close relative. In one case report, a man had a racing heart, muscle breakdown and kidney problems after injecting it.<Cite id="nelson-2012-melanotan-rhabdomyolysis" />

GLP-1 medicines raise the resting pulse a little. In trials, the pulse rose on average by 1 to 4 beats per minute more with semaglutide (Wegovy) than with placebo. With tirzepatide (Mounjaro) it rose by 2 to 4, against 1 with placebo.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy,fda-label-mounjaro" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Skin, weight and pregnancy: the reasons">

### Skin and moles

Tanning peptides copy a hormone that makes skin cells produce pigment. Case reports describe new moles and changing moles in people using melanotan. Four describe melanoma growing from an existing mole during or shortly after use. These reports do not prove that melanotan causes melanoma.<Cite id="habbema-2017-melanotan-review,cardones-2009-eruptive-nevi" />

PT-141 can cause dark patches on the face, gums or breasts. In the main trials, 1% of people had them. In a smaller study where people used it every day for 8 days, 38% had them.<Cite id="dailymed-vyleesi-label" />

Photos of your skin before you start make changes easier to see. See a doctor about any mole that changes size, shape or colour, itches or bleeds.<Cite id="nhs-moles" />

### Weight and strength

When people lose weight on GLP-1 type medicines, part of the loss is lean mass. In trials this ranged from about 15% or less to 40–60% of the weight lost. Lean mass is not only muscle: it also includes organs, bone and water. The authors of a 2024 review think the loss of muscle is mostly what you would expect with that much weight loss.<Cite id="neeland-2024-lean-mass" /> In a body-scan study of 140 people within one semaglutide trial, more fat than lean mass was lost.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> The US label also says semaglutide lowers weight with more fat lost than lean mass.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy" /> Notice if everyday tasks, such as climbing stairs, get harder, and tell your doctor. {/* residue-ok: EMA evidence (EPAR body-scan substudy) */}

### Pregnancy

GLP-1 medicines harmed the unborn young in animal studies.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy,fda-label-mounjaro" /> The US and Canadian labels say to stop semaglutide (Wegovy) at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy. The Canadian label says to stop tirzepatide (Mounjaro) at least 1 month before.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy,ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02,ca-pm-mounjaro-2026-06-23" /> Tesamorelin must not be used in pregnancy.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> For BPC-157, TB-500 and KPV, the FDA found no studies in pregnant women. Animal data were missing, or limited to one rat study.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing" />

Tirzepatide may make the pill work less well. The US label advises another method than the pill, or adding a condom. This is for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose increase. The Canadian label gives the same advice.<Cite id="fda-label-mounjaro,ca-pm-mounjaro-2026-06-23" /> See [before you consider a peptide](/en/safety/before-you-consider/).

</InDetail>

## What checks cannot do

<Lead question="Can tests show that a product is harmless?">No. They can catch some harms early. They cannot show what is in a vial or predict cancer.</Lead>

<Compare title="Checks can catch some problems early. They cannot show that a product is harmless." detail="What blood tests and the other checks on this page can and cannot tell you.">

| | Blood tests and other checks |
|---|---|
| Can they show fluid loss is hurting your kidneys? | <Verdict is="yes" /> A kidney test can show a drop.<Cite id="medlineplus-egfr" /> |
| Can they show blood sugar creeping up? | <Verdict is="yes" /> HbA1c shows the average over months.<Cite id="medlineplus-hba1c" /> |
| Can they show what is in your vial? | <Verdict is="no" /> That needs a lab test of the product, which covers one sample.<Cite id="ashraf-2024-online-semaglutide,nist-2018-iso17025-crosswalk-reporting" /> |
| Can they warn you about germs before you inject? | <Verdict is="no" /> Germs and toxins in a vial are invisible.<Cite id="ashraf-2024-online-semaglutide,smith-2013-fungal-methylprednisolone" /> |
| Can they predict a cancer risk years from now? | <Verdict is="no" /> For BPC-157, for example, the FDA found no cancer studies at all.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> |
| Can they show long-term effects of a high IGF-1? | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Those effects are not known.<Cite id="dailymed-egrifta-sv-label" /> |

</Compare>

<InDetail title="Why normal results are not the all-clear">

- **Tests look back, not ahead.** They show what has already changed in your body, not what a product may do later.
- **They test you, not the next vial.** A normal result after one vial says nothing about the next one, which may differ.<Cite id="janvier-2018-falsified-peptides-belgium" />
- **They only find what they look for.** A liver test will not show a skin problem, and none of these tests measure cancer risk.<Cite id="medlineplus-liver-function-tests" />
- **Some risks are unknown.** For BPC-157, TB-500 and KPV, for example, the FDA found too little human data to judge long-term safety.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing" />

</InDetail>

## Getting checks where you live

<Lead question="How do I get these checks?">Ask your own doctor. Say what you use, and ask which checks fit you.</Lead>

<Diagram id="getting-checks" caption="How to ask your doctor for checks, in five steps">

1. **Book an appointment** with your doctor. Write your questions down and bring them.<Cite id="au-healthdirect-questions-to-ask-your-doctor" />
2. **Say what you use.** Bring the product or a photo of its label. Bring a list of everything else you take, including vitamins and supplements.<Cite id="au-healthdirect-questions-to-ask-your-doctor,vic-health-2026-retatrutide-liver" />
3. **Agree which checks fit you**, and when to repeat them. You can ask whether you really need each test.<Cite id="au-healthdirect-paying-for-diagnostic-testing" />
4. **Ask about the cost before the test.** Who pays depends on your country and your health cover. See the details below.
5. **Ask for your results.** You can ask to see your record and get a copy, with a few exceptions. In the US, the UK and Australia the law gives you this right. In Canada, the doctors' code of ethics asks them to give you a copy; we did not check each province's law.<Cite id="ecfr-45-164-524,uk-govuk-data-protection,nhs-constitution-england,oaic-access-health-information,cpsa-cma-code-of-ethics" />

</Diagram>

What you tell your doctor is private. In the US, the UK, Canada and Australia, privacy rules protect it, with a few exceptions set by law.<Cite id="hhs-hipaa-your-rights,gmc-confidentiality-duties,gmc-confidentiality-framework,opc-privacy-laws-summary,oaic-health-information" /> See [your rights](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/#your-rights-privacy-and-consent) and [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/).

<InDetail title="Who pays for the tests, by country">

- **United States.** What you pay depends on your health insurance. Ask your doctor's office or your insurer before the test. We did not check the rules of individual plans.
- **United Kingdom.** In England, NHS services are free, apart from a few exceptions set by Parliament.<Cite id="nhs-constitution-england" /> We did not check the rules in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.
- **Canada.** Each province and territory runs its own health insurance plan. The plans cover medically necessary physician and hospital services.<Cite id="ca-hc-health-care-system" /> Ask your doctor whether a test is covered where you live.
- **Australia.** Medicare covers most pathology tests, such as blood tests, when there is a medical reason for them. If the test is bulk billed, you pay nothing. Ask your doctor whether there will be a cost.<Cite id="au-healthdirect-paying-for-diagnostic-testing" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="If your doctor does not want to do the tests">

Your doctor does not have to do every test you ask for. In the UK, for example, a doctor who thinks the care you ask for would not serve you should not provide it. But the doctor should explain why, look at other options and mention your right to a second opinion.<Cite id="gmc-2020-consent-paras-48-49" /> We did not find a clinical guideline, in any of the four countries, on which checks fit people who use unapproved peptides (searched 24 October 2026). So doctors may differ in what they do.

What you can do:

- **Ask about your symptoms.** Yellow skin, dark urine or belly pain are reasons for a doctor to look. That is true whatever caused them.<Cite id="vic-health-2026-retatrutide-liver" />
- **Ask which warning signs to watch for**, so you know when to come back.
- **Ask for a second opinion.** In the UK, Canada and Australia, the rules for doctors ask them to respect your wish for one.<Cite id="gmc-2020-consent-paras-48-49,cpsa-cma-code-of-ethics,mba-good-medical-practice-2020" />
- **Tests you pay for yourself** exist. In the US, many tests sold straight to the public are not reviewed by the FDA before they are sold. The FDA advises against making health decisions on a result before you have discussed it with a doctor.<Cite id="fda-dtc-tests" /> Take any abnormal result to your doctor.

Health authorities do see a role for doctors here. People in Victoria, Australia, fell ill after a product sold as retatrutide. The health department asked those who kept using it to see their doctor about liver tests.<Cite id="vic-health-2026-retatrutide-liver" /> In 2026, Alberta's medical regulator asked doctors to ask patients about injections they were not prescribed, and to watch for complications.<Cite id="ca-cpsa-2026-04-injectable-peptides" />

</InDetail>

## Between checks: warning signs

<Lead question="What do I watch for between checks?">The warning signs below. They matter more than any planned test.</Lead>

Checks happen now and then. Your body can change in between. If you notice one of these signs, do not wait for your next test.
