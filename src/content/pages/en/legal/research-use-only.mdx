<Lead question="What does a 'research use only' label mean?">It is the seller's own statement that a product is for lab work. It is not a licence.</Lead>

<Diagram id="ruo-label-origin" caption="Where the words come from, and what they do and do not tell you.">

| | What we know |
|---|---|
| Where the wording comes from | In US rules, "For Research Use Only" is a statement for lab test kits that are still being developed. It is not a category of medicine.<Cite id="ecfr-21-809-10" /> |
| How it is used on peptide vials | Sellers put "research purposes only" or "not for human consumption" on vials of semaglutide, tirzepatide and other peptides that people inject.<Cite id="belcourt-2025-research-purposes,fda-warning-letters-index" /> |
| What it does not mean | It does not mean a regulator approved the product, checked its quality, or found it suitable to inject.<Cite id="eu-directive-2001-83" /> |

</Diagram>

A licensed medicine comes with a licence number, a leaflet and a maker who must answer for its quality. A "research" vial has none of these.<Cite id="eu-directive-2001-83,igj-qa-zonder-handelsvergunning" />

## Why the label does not decide what a product is

<Lead question="Does the label make the product legal in Europe?">No. EU law looks at how the product is sold as a whole, and at what it does.</Lead>

<Diagram id="ruo-eu-reasoning" caption="How EU law decides whether a labelled product is a medicine, step by step.">

1. **Two tests.** A product is a medicine if it is presented as treating or preventing disease, or if it can change body functions through a drug-like effect.<Cite id="eu-directive-2001-83" />
2. **Presentation is more than the label.** What counts is the impression an ordinary, well-informed person gets. Labels, leaflets and what is said all count, and so does what is only hinted at.<Cite id="cjeu-1983-van-bennekom" />
3. **The form is a clue too.** The EU court said the form of a product can be strong evidence of what the seller intends.<Cite id="cjeu-1983-van-bennekom" />
4. **Function depends on the substance.** If a substance can change body functions in a way that really matters, the label does not change that.<Cite id="cjeu-2009-hecht-pharma" />
5. **In doubt, medicines law wins.** A product that could be a medicine and also something else is treated as a medicine, once its medicine status is shown.<Cite id="eu-directive-2001-83,cjeu-2009-hecht-pharma" />

</Diagram>

Belgium's medicines agency says the same in plain words. If a product contains a pharmacologically active substance, or is presented as a medicine, it is a medicine. That it is sold elsewhere as something else "is not a valid argument".<Cite id="famhp-postal-seizure-qa" />

<InDetail title="Where the law is less clear">

We found no EU court ruling about "research use only" labels as such. The rulings above set general tests, and authorities apply them case by case.<Cite id="cjeu-2014-d-and-g" />

The "function" test has a limit. A product is a medicine by function only if science shows it can change body functions in a way that really matters.<Cite id="cjeu-2009-hecht-pharma" /> The EU court has also said this test covers substances that can benefit health, not substances taken only to get high.<Cite id="cjeu-2014-d-and-g" /> For a peptide tested only in animals, this can be argued about.

That is why the "presentation" test often decides such cases. A website that promises healing, fat loss or muscle can be enough. A vial made for injecting is a strong clue, but not proof on its own.<Cite id="cjeu-1983-van-bennekom" /> This is our reading of the rulings, not a court decision about any one product. Our legal reviewer will check it.

</InDetail>

## The United States: what the seller intends

<Lead question="Does the label protect sellers in the US?">No. The FDA looks at what the seller really intends, shown by all their words and actions.</Lead>

<Diagram id="ruo-us-intended-use" caption="How the US medicines agency decides what a product is for.">

1. **Intended use decides.** In US rules, the use a product is meant for is judged from the seller's real, "objective" intent.<Cite id="ecfr-21-201-128" />
2. **Everything counts as evidence.** Labels, adverts, what the seller says, and how the product is sold. Even a use the seller knows about but does not advertise can count.<Cite id="ecfr-21-201-128" />
3. **The FDA has applied this to peptide sellers.** It sent them warning letters from 2024 to 2026. A "research use only" statement does not stop a product being a drug, it said. What counts is that the seller's website shows it is meant for people.<Cite id="fda-warning-letters-index" />
4. **The result.** The FDA calls such products unapproved new drugs. Selling them across state lines breaks federal law.<Cite id="fda-warning-letters-index" />

</Diagram>

More on US law: [United States](/en/legal/us/).

<InDetail title="What the FDA's letters to peptide sellers say">

In its warning letters, the FDA quotes the sellers' own "research use only" and "not for human consumption" statements. It then sets them against the health claims on the sellers' own websites. Its conclusion: the website shows the products are meant as drugs for people, so they are unapproved new drugs.<Cite id="fda-warning-letters-index" /> In one letter from August 2026, the FDA said that bacteriostatic water sold to prepare such products is itself a drug.<Cite id="fda-warning-letters-index" />

</InDetail>

## What the label changes in each country

<Lead question="Is it legal for me to buy a research-labelled peptide?">The label changes nothing. What happens to a buyer differs by country.</Lead>

| | Netherlands | Belgium | United States |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does the label make it legal to sell? | <Verdict is="no" /> Selling a medicine without a licence is forbidden.<Cite id="nl-gmw-art-40-handelsvergunning" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> A licence is needed.<Cite id="be-medicines-law-1964" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> The FDA treats such products as unapproved drugs.<Cite id="fda-warning-letters-index" /> |

## What the label means for you

<Lead question="What does it mean for me if my product has this label?">Nobody had to check what is in it, how clean it is, or how it was made.</Lead>

<Compare title="A licensed medicine comes with checks. A research-labelled vial comes with none that anyone must do." detail="What EU law requires of a licensed medicine, against what is known about products sold as 'research only'.">

| | Licensed medicine from a pharmacy | Product labelled "research use only" |
|---|---|---|
| Made under medicine quality rules (GMP)? | <Verdict is="yes" /> The maker must follow GMP and use active substances made under GMP.<Cite id="eu-directive-2001-83" /> | <Verdict is="unknown" /> No one has to. You cannot tell from the vial.<Cite id="igj-online-medicijnen" /> |
| Made under the rules for sterile products? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Injections must follow the EU rules for sterile production.<Cite id="eudralex-volume-4-gmp" /> | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Unknown. In one lab test of three vials, sterility was not tested.<Cite id="piatkowski-2026-retatrutide-product-testing" /> |
| Does the amount match the label? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Checked as part of the licence and batch release.<Cite id="eu-directive-2001-83" /> | <Verdict is="unknown" /> In that test, from about half to almost double the label.<Cite id="piatkowski-2026-retatrutide-product-testing" /> |
| Must someone collect and report side effects? | <Verdict is="yes" /> The licence holder must run a system for this.<Cite id="eu-directive-2001-83" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> No one has that duty. You can still report to Lareb or the FAMHP.<Cite id="lareb-bijwerkingen-melden,famhp-adverse-reaction-qa" /> |
| Comes with a leaflet about warnings and interactions? | <Verdict is="yes" /> An official leaflet and product information.<Cite id="eu-directive-2001-83" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> There is no official leaflet.<Cite id="eu-directive-2001-83" /> |

</Compare>

<KeyNumber title="The amount in a vial may not match the label" value="51% to 190%" label="of the amount on the label, in three vials sold as retatrutide" counted="Three products sold as retatrutide in Australia, tested in an accredited lab (Piatkowski 2026); sterility and endotoxin were not tested" source="piatkowski-2026-retatrutide-product-testing" />

If your product did not come from a pharmacy, these are practical next steps:

- Keep the vial, the label and any paper with it. Write down the batch number. This helps if you need to report a side effect.
- A certificate of analysis covers one batch, and only the tests it lists. If it lists no sterility or endotoxin test, those were not shown.
- Read [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/) and [sterile technique](/en/safety/sterile-technique/) before you inject anything.
- Tell your doctor what you use. See [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/).

<Cite id="piatkowski-2026-retatrutide-product-testing,igj-online-medicijnen,famhp-adverse-reaction-qa" />

<InDetail title="The lab test of three vials, in more detail">

In 2026, researchers at the University of Queensland reported on three products sold as retatrutide in Australia. They were sent in anonymously to a drug-checking service and tested in an accredited lab. All three contained retatrutide. But the amount ranged from 51% to 190% of what the label said. Lead and other metals were found only at low levels. Sterility and endotoxin, a toxic part of some bacteria, were not tested. Three samples cannot show how common these problems are.<Cite id="piatkowski-2026-retatrutide-product-testing" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Why sellers use the label, and what it costs you">

Three pharmacists wrote an editorial about online sales of semaglutide and tirzepatide "for research purposes". They describe what the label does. It lets buyers skip the prescriber and the pharmacist. That makes it hard to spot risky drug combinations, or to check on the person afterwards. Quality standards may not be met.<Cite id="belcourt-2025-research-purposes" />

A doctor can still help you with those checks. See [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/).

</InDetail>
