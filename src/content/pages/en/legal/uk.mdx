The Human Medicines Regulations 2012 are the main UK law on medicines. This page explains what they say about products such as BPC-157, TB-500 or retatrutide. It does not tell you how to get round the law. The licence that counts is a UK licence.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-46" />

## What counts as a medicine

<Lead question="Is a peptide a medicinal product under UK law?">Often, yes. The law looks at how it is offered and at what it does in the body.</Lead>

<Diagram id="uk-medicinal-product-two-tests" caption="UK law has two tests. A product that meets either one is a medicinal product.">

| Test | The question the law asks |
|---|---|
| Presentation | Is the substance presented as preventing or treating disease in people? |
| Function | Can it be used in people to restore, correct or change a body function, through a drug-like effect? Or to make a diagnosis? |

</Diagram>

These are the words of regulation 2.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-2" /> They are the same two tests as in EU law.<Cite id="eu-directive-2001-83" /> See [research use only](/en/legal/research-use-only/) for why a "research" label does not change what a product is.

## The core rule: no licence, no sale

<Lead question="What exactly does the law forbid?">Selling, giving or offering a medicine with no UK licence. Keeping it to pass on is banned too.</Lead>

<Diagram id="uk-regulation-46" caption="What the law forbids for a medicine with no UK licence.">

1. **No sale or supply.** Nobody may sell or supply it, or offer to.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-46" />
2. **No stock for others.** Nobody may have it if they know, or have reason to believe, it will be sold or supplied to someone else.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-46" />
3. **No making it for that.** Nobody may make it, or arrange its sale, supply or making, if they know it is meant to be sold against the rule.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-46" />
4. **The penalty.** On conviction on indictment, the more serious kind of trial, up to two years in prison, a fine, or both.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-47" />

</Diagram>

The MHRA, the UK medicines regulator, applies this to peptides sold online. It says retatrutide has not been authorised in the UK, so "anyone selling this product is doing so illegally".<Cite id="mhra-2026-07-no-summer-shortcut" />

<InDetail title="Prescription-only medicines, and passing on to friends">

A prescription-only medicine may only be sold or supplied on a prescription from a doctor or another listed prescriber.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-214" /> Having one with the intent to supply it without a prescription is an offence. So is breaking the prescription rule. Each carries up to two years in prison on conviction on indictment.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-255" />

The law treats supply very differently from use. The ban covers selling and supplying, and arranging a sale or supply for someone else.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-46" /> In our reading, passing a product on to a friend, selling some on, or ordering for a group can all count as supply.

</InDetail>

## Importing for your own use

<Lead question="Can I import a peptide for my own use?">You need no import licence for a medicine just for you. Selling it on is still a crime.</Lead>

<Diagram id="uk-personal-import" caption="How the import rules work when you buy a medicine for yourself.">

1. **The usual rule.** Importing a medicine into Great Britain from most countries needs a manufacturer's licence.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-17" />
2. **The personal exception.** That rule does not apply to someone who imports a medicine to use on themselves or someone in their household.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-17" />
3. **The seller is not covered.** Selling or supplying an unlicensed medicinal product in the UK is still an offence.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-46" />
4. **Nobody has checked it.** An unauthorised medicine has not been assessed by the MHRA for safety, effectiveness or quality.<Cite id="mhra-2026-01-seizures-2025" />

</Diagram>

If your product did not come from a pharmacy, no regulator has checked what is in it. See [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/) for what you can and cannot check yourself.

<Callout type="uncertain" title="What we could not find">
We found no MHRA guidance for the public on importing an unlicensed medicine for yourself. The sources we checked do not say whether such a parcel can be stopped at the border. Our legal reviewer will check this.
</Callout>

## Specials: the legal route for an unlicensed medicine

<Lead question="Is there a legal way to get an unlicensed medicine in the UK?">Yes, but only in a narrow way. A doctor can order a "special" for one patient's needs.</Lead>

<Diagram id="uk-specials-conditions" caption="When a doctor may order a 'special': a medicine with no licence, made for one patient.">

1. **A prescriber asks for it.** The order comes from a doctor or another listed prescriber, unprompted by the maker.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-167" />
2. **For one patient's needs.** It is for a patient in their direct care. The MHRA says the needs must be clinical, and the prescriber decides them.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-167,mhra-specials-guidance" />
3. **No licensed medicine fits.** Specials are for when no licensed medicine can meet the patient's needs.<Cite id="mhra-specials-guidance" />
4. **No advertising.** Nobody may advertise the product.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-167" />
5. **Made or imported under licence.** A licensed maker or importer supplies it. Importers must tell the MHRA before they import.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-167,mhra-specials-guidance" />

</Diagram>

The MHRA notes that unlicensed medicines may not have been assessed by it.<Cite id="mhra-specials-guidance" /> For a peptide with no published human trials, a prescriber would have to judge that a clinical need exists. That is our reading, not an MHRA statement. See [legal routes](/en/legal-routes/) and [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/).

## Controlled drugs

<Lead question="Are any of these peptides controlled drugs in the UK?">No. Growth hormone and a few other hormones are Class C drugs. No peptide on this site is.</Lead>

<Diagram id="uk-class-c-hormones" caption="Which hormones the drugs law lists as Class C, and where our peptides stand.">

| Group | Under the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 |
|---|---|
| Somatropin, somatrem and somatotropin (growth hormone), chorionic gonadotrophin (HCG), non-human chorionic gonadotrophin, clenbuterol, zeranol, zilpaterol | Class C controlled drugs<Cite id="uk-mda-1971-sch-2" /> |
| BPC-157, TB-500, KPV, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, tesamorelin, hexarelin, AOD-9604, melanotan II, PT-141, MOTS-c, epitalon, Semax, Selank, thymosin alpha-1, GHK-Cu, SS-31, semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide | Not listed (we searched the schedule for each name on 22 October 2026)<Cite id="uk-mda-1971-sch-2" /> |

</Diagram>

So the drugs law does not apply to these peptides. The medicines law on this page does.

## Advertising

<Lead question="Can anyone advertise these products in the UK?">No. Only a medicine with a UK licence may be put in an advert.</Lead>

<KeyNumber title="The MHRA acted on more than 1,500 websites and accounts in 2025" value="1,500+" label="web shops and social media accounts that sold medicines against the law. The MHRA acted in 2025." counted="MHRA Criminal Enforcement Unit, working with internet service providers, January to December 2025; all medical products, not peptides only" source="mhra-2026-01-seizures-2025" />

Nobody may publish an advertisement for a medicinal product unless a UK licence or registration is in force for it.<Cite id="uk-hmr-2012-reg-279" /> The MHRA warned in July 2026 that prescription weight-loss medicines are being advertised illegally on social media.<Cite id="mhra-2026-07-no-summer-shortcut" /> This site sells nothing and names no seller.

## How the MHRA enforces the law

<Lead question="Does the MHRA act against sellers?">Yes. In 2025 and 2026 it raided sites that made unlicensed weight-loss injections.</Lead>

<KeyNumber title="Over 12,000 fake weight-loss pens were seized in one raid" value="12,000+" label="fake weight-loss pens found in one raid in Birmingham, 14 October 2026" counted="One MHRA and West Midlands Police operation; packaging for about 30,000 more products was also found" source="mhra-2026-10-counterfeit-pens" />

<KeyNumber title="In 2025, the MHRA and police seized almost 20 million doses of medicines sold against the law." value="almost 20 million" label="doses seized in 2025. They include 5,680 GLP-1 pens." counted="All illegally traded medicines seized by the MHRA and its partners in 2025, up from 17 million in 2024" source="mhra-2026-01-seizures-2025" />

The MHRA has its own criminal enforcement unit. In 2026 it took apart sites that made or packed unlicensed retatrutide and tirzepatide. One of them was also believed to make "peptide products".<Cite id="mhra-2026-05-largest-seizure,mhra-2026-10-counterfeit-pens" />

<InDetail title="The 2025 and 2026 operations, and what the MHRA says about the products">

- **October 2025:** the first illicit weight-loss medicine factory found in the UK; 5,000 unauthorised retatrutide and tirzepatide pens seized.<Cite id="mhra-2026-10-counterfeit-pens" />
- **February 2026:** about 2,000 doses awaiting dispatch seized, with equipment and suspected ingredients.<Cite id="mhra-2026-10-counterfeit-pens" />
- **May 2026:** two men arrested on suspicion of offences under the Human Medicines Regulations; about 12,000 doses recovered.<Cite id="mhra-2026-05-largest-seizure" />
- **October 2026:** over 12,000 fake retatrutide and tirzepatide pens seized from a warehouse in Birmingham.<Cite id="mhra-2026-10-counterfeit-pens" />

The MHRA says these products "have not been assessed for safety, quality or effectiveness". There is "no guarantee of what they contain". It also says products sold as retatrutide outside clinical trials are supplied illegally.<Cite id="mhra-2026-10-counterfeit-pens" />

For GLP-1 medicines, the MHRA says licensed products come as pre-filled pens or tablets. Products sold as a powder in a vial, to be mixed before injection, are not authorised.<Cite id="mhra-glp1-what-you-need-to-know" />

</InDetail>

## Sport

<Lead question="What if I compete in the UK?">UK Anti-Doping uses the world rules. You answer for anything found in your body.</Lead>

UK Anti-Doping (UKAD) explains strict liability like this: you are "solely responsible for any banned substance" in your system, "regardless of how it got there".<Cite id="ukad-athlete-responsibilities" /> Many peptides are banned at all times. UKAD says not every banned experimental peptide is named on the list.<Cite id="ukad-2027-list-summary" /> See [sport and doping](/en/legal/sport-and-doping/).

## Where to report

<Lead question="Where can I report a side effect in the UK?">To the Yellow Card scheme, run by the MHRA. Anyone can report.</Lead>

<JurisdictionCard code="UK" />

**Side effects.** Anyone can report a problem with a medicine to the Yellow Card scheme. The MHRA cannot give medical advice. If you are worried about your health, speak to a doctor or pharmacist, or call NHS 111.<Cite id="mhra-yellow-card" /> In its October 2026 warning about fake pens, the MHRA asked anyone who suspects a side effect to report it.<Cite id="mhra-2026-10-counterfeit-pens" />

**Before you report.** Keep the product, its label and any papers. Write down the batch number. Tell your doctor what you use: see [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/).
