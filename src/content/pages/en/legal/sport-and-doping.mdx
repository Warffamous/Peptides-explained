<Lead question="What do sport rules say about peptides?">Many peptides are banned at all times. If one is found in your sample, you are responsible.</Lead>

<Compare title="In sport, the rules are stricter than medicines law. Intent does not decide whether a test counts." detail="World Anti-Doping Code (2021, and 2027 from 1 January 2027) and the 2027 Prohibited List, checked on 22 October 2026.">

| | Under the anti-doping rules |
|---|---|
| Are many peptides banned at all times, in and out of competition? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Sections S0, S2 and S4 cover them, also outside competitions.<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" /> |
| Can a peptide be banned without being named on the list? | <Verdict is="yes" /> WADA says a peptide may still be banned even if the list does not name it.<Cite id="wada-2027-list-explanatory-note" /> |
| Does a positive test count if you did not know? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Intent or fault does not have to be shown. This is called strict liability.<Cite id="wada-code-2021,wada-code-2027" /> |
| Does a wrongly labelled product clear you? | <Verdict is="no" /> The Code says a mislabelled or contaminated supplement does not count as "no fault". A lower ban is rare.<Cite id="wada-code-2027" /> |
| Are semaglutide and tirzepatide banned? | <Verdict is="no" /> They are in WADA's monitoring programme, not on the list.<Cite id="wada-2027-monitoring-program" /> |

</Compare>

The World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) writes the World Anti-Doping Code and the yearly Prohibited List. Sports federations and national anti-doping organisations apply them. This page explains how those rules treat peptides. Criminal law on doping is a separate matter and differs by country: see [the Netherlands](/en/legal/nl/), [Germany](/en/legal/de/) and [the UK](/en/legal/uk/).

## Who the rules cover

<Lead question="Do these rules apply to me?">If you belong to a sports federation, you can be tested. Dutch gyms have hardly any tests.</Lead>

<Diagram id="who-is-tested-nl-be" caption="Who is tested for doping in the Netherlands and Belgium.">

| Who | How likely a test is |
|---|---|
| Top athletes in the Netherlands | Tested the most<Cite id="dopingautoriteit-controles-belgie" /> |
| Any member of a Dutch sports federation | Can in principle be tested. Some random tests happen at lower levels too<Cite id="dopingautoriteit-controles-belgie" /> |
| People who train in a Dutch gym | Hardly any tests, if any<Cite id="dopingautoriteit-eigen-kracht" /> |
| People who compete in Belgium | Far more tests at lower levels, even surprise tests in gyms<Cite id="dopingautoriteit-controles-belgie" /> |

</Diagram>

Refusing a test usually brings a two-year ban, and possibly four years. A ban from Belgium also applies in the Netherlands.<Cite id="dopingautoriteit-controles-belgie" /> In Germany, the criminal law on self-doping covers only elite athletes in a testing pool and people who earn a lot from sport.<Cite id="de-antidopg-4" />

<InDetail title="Recreational athletes under the 2027 Code">

From 1 January 2027, the 2027 World Anti-Doping Code applies.<Cite id="wada-code-2027" /> Each national anti-doping organisation decides who counts as a **recreational athlete**. Some people never count, if it happened in the same sport in the last five years. That covers anyone who competed at international or national level, took part professionally, or was in a testing pool.<Cite id="wada-code-2027" />

A recreational athlete who shows "no significant fault" can get anything from a reprimand to a two-year ban. That is for violations that do not involve a "substance of abuse".<Cite id="wada-code-2027" /> The violation itself still counts.

</InDetail>

## Strict liability: a test result counts on its own

<Lead question="What if I did not know a substance was in my body?">It still counts. You are responsible for what is found in your sample.</Lead>

<Diagram id="strict-liability-chain" caption="How a positive test leads to a ban.">

1. **A banned substance is found in your sample.** That alone is an anti-doping rule violation.<Cite id="wada-code-2027" />
2. **Nobody has to prove intent.** It is "not necessary that intent, Fault, Negligence or knowing Use" be shown.<Cite id="wada-code-2027" />
3. **The ban depends on the substance and on your fault.** The Code sets a standard length, which can go up or down.<Cite id="wada-code-2027" />
4. **Lower bans are hard to get.** You usually have to show how the substance got into your body.<Cite id="wada-code-2027" />

</Diagram>

UK Anti-Doping puts it plainly: you are "solely responsible for any banned substance" in your system, "regardless of how it got there".<Cite id="ukad-athlete-responsibilities" />

<KeyNumber title="The standard ban for a substance in section S2 is four years" value="4 years" label="the usual ban for an S2 substance; less if you show how it got in and no intent" counted="World Anti-Doping Code 2027, article 10.2.1; the 2021 Code, in force until 31 December 2026, also sets four years" source="wada-code-2027,wada-code-2021" />

<InDetail title="How long a ban can be">

The Code divides banned substances into **specified** and **non-specified** ones. Specified substances are those more likely to be taken for reasons other than sport. WADA stresses that they are not less dangerous. All substances in S0 are specified. All substances in S2, and those in S4.3 and S4.4, are non-specified.<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" />

| Kind of substance | Standard ban under the 2027 Code |
|---|---|
| Non-specified (for example S2, or MOTS-c in S4.4) | Four years. Two years if you show how it entered your body and that it was not on purpose. Three years if you show how it entered your body and that the use had nothing to do with sport, but not that it was not on purpose<Cite id="wada-code-2027" /> |
| Specified (for example BPC-157 in S0) | Two years. Four years if the anti-doping organisation shows it was on purpose; three if the use had nothing to do with sport<Cite id="wada-code-2027" /> |

Until 31 December 2026 the 2021 Code applies. It also sets four years for a non-specified substance unless you show the violation was not intentional.<Cite id="wada-code-2021" /> In the Netherlands, a first violation can bring anything from a warning to a four-year ban, depending on the substance and the degree of fault.<Cite id="dopingautoriteit-positieve-test" />

</InDetail>

## What the Prohibited List says about peptides

<Lead question="Which peptides are banned?">Many are. Some are named, and a catch-all rule covers peptides that no health authority has approved.</Lead>

<Compare title="The 2027 list names eight peptides on this site. Others can still be banned." detail="WADA Prohibited List 2027 and 2027 Monitoring Program, in effect from 1 January 2027. The 2026 list names the same peptides.">

| | Named on the 2027 list? | Banned in sport? |
|---|---|---|
| BPC-157 | <Verdict is="yes" /> In S0, non-approved substances<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> At all times<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" /> |
| TB-500 | <Verdict is="yes" /> In S2.3, as a derivative of thymosin beta-4<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> At all times<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" /> |
| CJC-1295, tesamorelin, ipamorelin, hexarelin | <Verdict is="yes" /> In S2.2.4, growth hormone releasing factors<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> At all times<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" /> |
| AOD-9604 | <Verdict is="yes" /> In S2.2.3, as a growth hormone fragment<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> At all times<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" /> |
| MOTS-c | <Verdict is="yes" /> In S4.4, metabolic modulators<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> At all times<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" /> |
| Retatrutide | <Verdict is="no" /> Not by name<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> S0 covers drugs still in clinical trials<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en,ukad-2027-list-summary" /> |
| Other peptides that no health authority has approved | <Verdict is="no" /> Not by name | <Verdict is="yes" /> Under S0, unless another section already covers them<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en,wada-2027-list-explanatory-note" /> |
| Semax | <Verdict is="no" /> Not by name<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" /> | <Verdict is="unknown" /> It is registered as a medicine in Russia, so S0 may not apply. Ask your anti-doping organisation<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" /> |
| Semaglutide, tirzepatide | <Verdict is="no" /> Not on the list<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> Only monitored<Cite id="wada-2027-monitoring-program" /> |

</Compare>

Section S0 bans any drug that no government health authority has approved for human use, if no other section covers it. Its examples include drugs still in development and, from 2027, "peptides".<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" /> WADA added the word for clarity. It says many unapproved peptides fall under S0 or other sections, and a peptide not named "may still be prohibited".<Cite id="wada-2027-list-explanatory-note" />

<InDetail title="How S0 and S2 are worded">

**S0, non-approved substances.** S0 covers "any pharmacological substance" that no later section covers and that has no current approval by any government health authority for human use. It names BPC-157 as an example. It has done so since 2022.<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en,wada-2022-list-modifications" /> The 2026 list had the same rule but did not yet mention peptides as an example.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en" />

**S2, peptide hormones, growth factors and related substances.** S2 bans the substances it names "and other substances with similar chemical structure or similar biological effect(s)". It names growth hormone fragments such as AOD-9604, and growth hormone releasing hormone and its analogues, such as CJC-1295 and tesamorelin. It also names growth hormone secretagogues such as ipamorelin, and releasing peptides such as examorelin (hexarelin). Under growth factors it names thymosin beta-4 and its derivatives, such as TB-500.<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" />

**S4.4, metabolic modulators.** S4.4 names MOTS-c among the activators of an energy-sensing enzyme called AMPK.<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="What changed for 2027">

WADA published the 2027 list on 21 September 2026. It takes effect on 1 January 2027.<Cite id="ukad-2027-list-summary" /> For peptides, the change is in the wording of S0, which now names peptides as an example.<Cite id="wada-2027-list-explanatory-note" /> UK Anti-Doping explains that peptides under clinical development are banned if they meet the Code's criteria. It adds that not every banned experimental peptide is listed by name.<Cite id="ukad-2027-list-summary" />

The Dutch Doping Authority says the changes are mostly clarifications and a few new examples. Its full Dutch translation of the 2027 list will be online from 1 January 2027.<Cite id="dopingautoriteit-2027-list-news" />

Semaglutide and tirzepatide stay in the 2027 Monitoring Program, in and out of competition. Substances in that programme are not banned.<Cite id="wada-2027-monitoring-program" />

</InDetail>

## Medical exemptions (TUEs)

<Lead question="Can a doctor's note make a banned peptide allowed?">Only a medical exemption can. For an unapproved peptide, its tests are very hard to meet.</Lead>

<Diagram id="tue-four-tests" caption="The four tests for a medical exemption, and how they fit an unapproved peptide.">

| The test (2027 standard) | For a peptide that no authority has approved (our reading) |
|---|---|
| You have a diagnosed condition, backed by clinical evidence | Possible, if you have such a condition |
| The substance is an indicated treatment for it, and there is no reasonable allowed alternative | Hard to show: there is no approved use, and no treatment guideline to point to |
| It will not boost performance beyond a return to your normal health | Not known: the effects in people have hardly been studied |
| The need is not caused by earlier banned use without an exemption | Depends on your history |

</Diagram>

<Cite id="wada-istue-2027" />

A Therapeutic Use Exemption (TUE) lets an athlete use a banned substance for a medical reason. All four tests must be met.<Cite id="wada-istue-2027" /> The same four tests apply under the 2023 standard, which is in force until the end of 2026.<Cite id="wada-istue-2023" /> A TUE decision does not consider whether the use is "legal in all jurisdictions".<Cite id="wada-istue-2027" />

<Callout type="uncertain" title="Our reading, not WADA's words">
WADA's standard for exemptions does not mention S0 or unapproved peptides. The right-hand column is our reading of the four tests. Only the anti-doping organisation that receives an application can decide it.
</Callout>

## Wrong contents and strict liability

<Lead question="What if the vial held something else than its label says?">It is still your positive test. A wrongly labelled product is rarely an excuse.</Lead>

<KeyNumber title="Fewer than one in five seized doping products held what the label said" value="less than 20%" label="products that held the substance on the label, in the stated amount" counted="1,190 doping products seized by Swiss customs and tested in a laboratory; most were labelled as anabolic agents, 146 as peptide hormones or growth factors (analysis study, published 2017)" source="weber-2017-swiss-border" />

Lab tests of seized products often find the wrong substance or the wrong amount.<Cite id="weber-2017-swiss-border,krug-2014-black-market-germany" /> Under the Code, a positive test from a mislabelled or contaminated supplement does not count as "no fault". A lower ban for a contaminated source has "rarely been applied" unless the athlete took great care.<Cite id="wada-code-2027" />

<InDetail title="Why a label rarely helps you">

The Code's rule for a **contaminated source** covers a banned substance that the product label does not mention.<Cite id="wada-code-2027" /> A vial labelled with the name of a banned peptide discloses that substance. In our reading, the rule cannot help you then. If the vial held a different banned substance, the athlete still has to show both where it came from and "no significant fault".<Cite id="wada-code-2027" />

The Dutch Doping Authority gives one practical step. If you think a contaminated supplement caused a positive test, report it to them at once and keep the product.<Cite id="dopingautoriteit-positieve-test" />

During a ban, you may not compete, train or take part in activities of your federation. You may not coach either. Other federations adopt the ban.<Cite id="dopingautoriteit-positieve-test" />

</InDetail>

If your product did not come from a pharmacy, no regulator has checked what is in it. See [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/) for what you can and cannot check yourself.

## Where to ask

<Lead question="Who can tell me if a substance is banned?">The doping body in your country can tell you. In the Netherlands, that is the Dopingautoriteit.</Lead>

<JurisdictionCard code="NL" />

In the Netherlands, the Dopingautoriteit answers questions about the rules, and its Dopingwaaier app will carry the Dutch 2027 list.<Cite id="dopingautoriteit-2027-list-news" /> In the UK, UK Anti-Doping asks athletes who are unsure about a non-approved peptide to contact it.<Cite id="ukad-2027-list-summary" /> A doctor can help you check your medicines and plan an exemption where one is possible: see [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/).
