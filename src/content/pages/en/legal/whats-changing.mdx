<Lead question="What is this page?">A list of rule changes we follow. Each one has its status, a date and a source.</Lead>

The tracker lists the rule changes that matter for the peptides on this site, in the US, the UK, Canada, Australia and sport. "Last update" is the date of the latest official step. We checked every item against its sources on 23 and 24 October 2026.

<ChangeTracker />

This page reports what regulators and lawmakers have published. It does not predict their decisions, or tell anyone how to use or get a product. For the law today, see [the US](/en/legal/us/), [the UK](/en/legal/uk/), [Canada](/en/legal/ca/), [Australia](/en/legal/au/) and the notes on [Ireland and New Zealand](/en/legal/ireland-and-new-zealand/).

## How to read the statuses

<Lead question="What do the labels on each item mean?">Each label says how far a change has got, from a first idea to a rule in force.</Lead>

<Diagram id="tracker-status-ladder" caption="The steps a rule change can go through, from first idea to rule in force.">

| Label | What it means |
|---|---|
| Proposed | An authority has put forward a change, but it is not yet decided |
| Public consultation | Anyone can send comments on a draft before a deadline |
| Advisory vote | Experts have given advice. The authority still has to decide |
| Rulemaking | The formal steps to make or change a law or rule are under way |
| Adopted | The change has been decided, but has not started yet |
| In force | The rule applies now |
| Stalled | Nothing has moved for a long time |
| Watching | Not a change in law, but something to follow, such as enforcement |

</Diagram>

An advisory vote and a proposal can both come to nothing. Only "adopted" and "in force" mean that a rule has really changed. When an item moves to a new status, we change its date and record the change in our [corrections log](/en/about/corrections/).

## The United States

<Lead question="What is happening in the US?">The FDA must decide what to do with its advisers' vote. The law has not changed yet.</Lead>

<Diagram id="us-503a-steps" caption="How a new ingredient gets on the US pharmacy list, and where things stand.">

1. **Nomination.** Someone asks the FDA to add an ingredient to the 503A list.<Cite id="fda-503a-bulks-page" />
2. **FDA review and advisory meeting.** FDA staff write a review, and an advisory committee votes. Seven peptides reached this step in July 2026. Five more substances are on the agenda of a meeting planned before the end of February 2027.<Cite id="fr-2026-07361-pcac-notice,fda-pcac-2027-early-announcement" />
3. **FDA decision.** The FDA says it will not decide until it has considered the committee's advice and finished its reviews. This is where the seven peptides stand.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-briefing-intro" />
4. **Proposed rule and comments.** The FDA publishes a proposed rule and takes public comments.<Cite id="fda-503a-bulks-page" />
5. **Final rule.** Only a final rule adds an ingredient to the list.<Cite id="fda-503a-bulks-page,usc-21-353a" />

</Diagram>

On 23 July 2026, the FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted 8–6, with one abstention, to recommend BPC-157 for the 503A list. It considered seven peptides and recommended six. These results are as reported from the public meeting; the FDA has posted no minutes.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-07-meeting,fda-pcac-2026-questions" /> The vote is advisory and non-binding, and went against FDA staff's own proposal.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-briefing-intro" />

<InDetail title="The July 2026 vote: the five points that matter">

1. **The vote.** On 23 July 2026 the committee voted 8–6, with one abstention, to recommend BPC-157 for the 503A bulks list. The count is as reported from the public meeting.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-07-meeting,fda-pcac-2026-questions" />
2. **Seven considered, six recommended.** The agenda had BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, emideltide (also called DSIP), Semax and epitalon. Each was voted on as a free base and as an acetate salt.<Cite id="fr-2026-07361-pcac-notice,fda-pcac-2026-questions" /> As reported from the meeting, all were recommended except emideltide.
3. **Advice only, against FDA staff.** FDA staff had proposed that none of the 14 forms be added.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-briefing-intro" /> Advisory committees make "non-binding recommendations".<Cite id="fda-pcac-2027-early-announcement" />
4. **Still prohibited for now.** Legal compounding stays prohibited until the FDA formally adopts the advice and completes rulemaking.<Cite id="fda-503a-bulks-page,usc-21-353a" /> On 22 and 23 October 2026 the Federal Register held no proposed rule.<Cite id="federalregister-fda-search-2026-10-22,federalregister-fda-search-2026-10-23" />
5. **Not an approval.** None of these is an approved medicine in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia (Semax is registered as a medicine in Russia).<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing,uk-mhra-substance-index-status-c-2026-10-23,mhra-products-search-status-d-2026-10-23,ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides,ca-dpd-search-canada-legal-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-c-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-d-2026-10-23" /> A listing would let US pharmacies compound them on prescription. It is not a marketing authorisation, says nothing about whether they work, and changes nothing outside the US.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-presentations-day1" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="The other US items, in short">

- **Interim categories.** By 22 April 2026, the FDA listed BPC-157, TB-500, KPV and other peptides as "previously in category 2". Their nominations were withdrawn. On the FDA's list of 14 May 2026, none of them is in category 1.<Cite id="fda-category-2-safety-risks,fda-503a-categories-2026-05" /> Only category 1 ingredients fall under the FDA's interim policy of not taking action against pharmacies.<Cite id="fda-503a-bulks-page" />
- **Semaglutide and tirzepatide.** On 30 April 2026 the FDA proposed to keep them off the list for outsourcing facilities. Comments closed on 30 July 2026. On 22 and 23 October 2026 no final decision had been published.<Cite id="fda-2026-503b-glp1-exclusion-proposal,fr-2026-08552-503b-glp1,fr-2026-12937-503b-comment-extension,federalregister-fda-search-2026-10-22,federalregister-fda-search-2026-10-23" />
- **Warning letters.** The FDA keeps writing to online sellers of "research use only" peptides. It calls their products unapproved new drugs.<Cite id="fda-warning-letters-index" />

More on each point: [US law](/en/legal/us/).

</InDetail>

<Callout type="note" title="What we do not track">
Announcements in the press, on social media or by sellers are not items on this page until the regulator publishes an official document. A headline that says a peptide is now "legal" or "approved" in the US is not the same as an FDA rule.
</Callout>

## The UK, Canada and Australia

<Lead question="What is changing in the UK, Canada and Australia?">Australia and New South Wales have new rules. England has a plan. All three act against sellers.</Lead>

<Diagram id="uk-ca-au-watch" caption="What we follow in each country, with the latest step we could check.">

| Country | What we follow | Latest step we could check |
|---|---|---|
| United Kingdom | MHRA raids on sites that make unlicensed weight-loss pens | Over 12,000 fake pens seized in Birmingham on 14 October 2026<Cite id="mhra-2026-10-counterfeit-pens" /> |
| United Kingdom (England) | A plan to let only doctors, dentists and some prescribing nurses, midwives and pharmacists give "wellbeing" drips into a vein | Open for comments from 21 October 2026 to 13 January 2027<Cite id="uk-dhsc-2026-high-risk-cosmetic-wellbeing-consultation" /> |
| Canada | Health Canada's action against injectable peptides that it has not authorised | In May 2026, BPC-157 and GHK-Cu powder was recalled from pharmacies<Cite id="ca-hc-recalls-database" /> |
| Australia | New rules for medicines you import for yourself | In force since 8 September 2026<Cite id="au-tg-amendment-2026-measures-1,au-tga-2026-09-personal-import-packaging" /> |
| Australia | Unapproved peptides as a TGA enforcement priority | Fines announced on 6 October 2026, including for people who imported prescription-only medicines<Cite id="au-tga-2026-06-peptide-compliance-priority,au-tga-2026-10-prescription-import-infringements" /> |
| Australia (New South Wales) | A new medicines and poisons law, which replaces the law of 1966 | Starts on 5 November 2026<Cite id="au-nsw-health-s4d-2026" /> |
| Australia | A review of the rules for advertising medicines | Comments closed on 3 September 2026. No decision yet<Cite id="au-tga-2026-07-advertising-code-consultation" /> |

</Diagram>

Three of these are rule changes: Australia's import rules, now in force; the new law in New South Wales, which starts on 5 November 2026; and England's plan, still open for comments. Australia's review of its advertising code could lead to a fourth, but no decision has been published. The rest is enforcement of laws that have not changed. See the pages on [the UK](/en/legal/uk/), [Canada](/en/legal/ca/) and [Australia](/en/legal/au/).

<InDetail title="More detail on the three countries">

**United Kingdom.** Since October 2025, the MHRA has raided several sites that made unlicensed retatrutide and tirzepatide pens. In May 2026, two men were arrested on suspicion of offences under the Human Medicines Regulations.<Cite id="mhra-2026-10-counterfeit-pens,uk-mhra-2026-02-second-facility,mhra-2026-05-largest-seizure" /> In 2025 the MHRA and its partners seized almost 20 million doses of illegally traded medicines.<Cite id="mhra-2026-01-seizures-2025" />

The plan in England covers substances put into a vein for a cosmetic or "wellbeing" reason, among other procedures. It does not name peptides or NAD+. Providers would have to register with the Care Quality Commission, which checks health and care services in England. Breaking the rules would be a crime.<Cite id="uk-dhsc-2026-high-risk-cosmetic-wellbeing-consultation" />

**Canada.** In April 2026, Health Canada warned the public about injectable peptides sold online. It said a "research use only" label does not make them legal, and that it works with the border agency to stop such parcels.<Cite id="ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides" /> Alberta's regulator of doctors warned its members the same month.<Cite id="ca-cpsa-2026-04-injectable-peptides" /> In May 2026 Health Canada recalled BPC-157 and GHK-Cu powder that had been sold to pharmacies as unauthorised ingredients.<Cite id="ca-hc-recalls-database" /> We found no 2026 change to Canada's list of prescription drugs that concerns these peptides.<Cite id="ca-hc-pdl-notices-2026" />

**Australia.** The rules of 8 September 2026 say that a medicine you import for yourself must be in packaging that identifies it. Travellers who bring in a prescription-only medicine now need a doctor's written authority too. The same rules make clear that medicines made up by a pharmacy may not be advertised to the public.<Cite id="au-tg-amendment-2026-measures-1,au-tga-2026-09-personal-import-packaging,au-tga-compounded-medicines-guidance" />

Since June 2026, unapproved peptides have been a TGA enforcement priority.<Cite id="au-tga-2026-06-peptide-compliance-priority,au-tga-2026-06-cmo-peptide-statement" /> On 28 September 2026 the TGA started a Federal Court case against a former company and its directors over advertising peptides such as BPC-157 and CJC-1295.<Cite id="au-tga-media-releases-peptide-log-2026-10-24" /> No peptide on this site was on the 2026 agendas for changing the Poisons Standard, the list of medicine schedules.<Cite id="au-tga-2026-scheduling-consultations-no-peptides" />

In New South Wales, a new medicines and poisons law replaces the law of 1966 on 5 November 2026. NSW Health's list of Schedule 4D medicines, the prescription medicines with stricter rules on who may have them, names TB-500, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, hexarelin and AOD-9604 under the new law too, but not BPC-157 or melanotan II. A prescription for one will be valid for 12 months instead of 6.<Cite id="au-nsw-health-s4d-2026,au-nsw-mptg-act-2022" /> We have not read the new Regulation, or the Act as it starts.

We do not name sellers on this site, even when a regulator does.

</InDetail>

## Sport

<Lead question="What changes in sport on 1 January 2027?">A new list and a new Code start. The list words its catch-all rule for peptides more clearly.</Lead>

<Compare title="From 1 January 2027, S0 names peptides as an example. The basic rules stay the same." detail="WADA Prohibited Lists 2026 and 2027, Monitoring Programs 2026 and 2027, World Anti-Doping Code 2021 and 2027.">

| | 2026 | 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Does S0 name "peptides" as an example? | <Verdict is="no" /> Not as a word<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> Added for clarity<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en,wada-2027-list-explanatory-note" /> |
| Is BPC-157 named in S0? | <Verdict is="yes" /> As an example<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> As an example<Cite id="wada-2027-list-en" /> |
| Are semaglutide and tirzepatide banned? | <Verdict is="no" /> Only monitored<Cite id="wada-2026-monitoring-program" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> Only monitored<Cite id="wada-2027-monitoring-program" /> |
| Does a positive test count without proof of intent? | <Verdict is="yes" /> 2021 Code<Cite id="wada-code-2021" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> 2027 Code<Cite id="wada-code-2027" /> |

</Compare>

WADA published the 2027 list on 21 September 2026.<Cite id="ukad-2027-list-summary" /> The 2027 Code and its standards were approved on 5 December 2025, and also start on 1 January 2027.<Cite id="wada-2027-code-standards-page" /> What this means for athletes: [sport and doping](/en/legal/sport-and-doping/).

<InDetail title="What the 2027 changes mean in practice">

WADA says many unapproved peptides already fell under S0 or other sections. The new word "peptides" in S0 makes that clearer. A peptide that the list does not name "may still be prohibited".<Cite id="wada-2027-list-explanatory-note" />

The new standard for medical exemptions keeps four tests that must all be met. One is that the substance is an indicated treatment, with no reasonable allowed alternative.<Cite id="wada-istue-2027" />

UK Anti-Doping says not every banned experimental peptide is named on the list, and asks athletes in doubt to contact it.<Cite id="ukad-2027-list-summary" /> Canada's 2027 Anti-Doping Program also starts on 1 January 2027.<Cite id="ca-sic-2027-cadp" />

</InDetail>
