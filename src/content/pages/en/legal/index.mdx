The four countries we cover follow the same basic pattern. A product offered to treat a disease, or one that can change how the body works, counts as a medicine. The law calls it a "drug" in the US and Canada, a "medicinal product" in the UK and a "therapeutic good" in Australia.<Cite id="usc-21-321,uk-hmr-2012-reg-2,ca-fda-s2-definitions,au-tg-act-s3-definitions" /> Selling it without the regulator's approval is against the law.<Cite id="usc-21-355,uk-hmr-2012-reg-46,ca-food-and-drug-regulations,au-tg-act-s19b-19d-offences" /> Penalties, import rules and enforcement differ by country.

We describe the law from the laws themselves and from official guidance, with the date we checked. Laws change, so each page says when we last checked it. We follow coming changes on [what's changing](/en/legal/whats-changing/).

This section does not tell anyone how to get round the law, where to find a product, or how to use one. For safety information, see [safety](/en/safety/).
