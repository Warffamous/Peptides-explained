<Lead question="Why short notes for these two countries?">Our main pages cover four countries. Here are the key facts for Ireland and New Zealand.</Lead>

<Diagram id="ie-nz-at-a-glance" caption="Ireland and New Zealand side by side: who controls medicines, and where to report.">

| | Ireland | New Zealand |
|---|---|---|
| Who controls medicines | The Health Products Regulatory Authority (HPRA) | Medsafe, part of the Ministry of Health |
| The main law | The Irish Medicines Board Acts 1995 and 2006, and the rules made under them | The Medicines Act 1981 |
| Injected peptides | Medicines for injection are, as a rule, prescription-only | BPC-157 and many other peptides are prescription medicines |
| Prescription medicines by post | The traveller's personal-use exemption does not cover them. Customs can stop them | Only with an original prescription or letter from a New Zealand prescriber |
| Reporting side effects | To the HPRA, online | To Medsafe, with a CARM report |
| Anti-doping | Sport Ireland | Sport Integrity Commission Te Kahu Raunui |

</Diagram>

This page explains the law. It does not tell you how to get round it. If your product did not come from a pharmacy, no regulator has checked what is in it. See [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/).

## Ireland

<Lead question="Who controls medicines in Ireland?">The HPRA, a national body for health products.</Lead>

<Photo id="leinster-house-dublin" />

The Health Products Regulatory Authority (HPRA) licenses medicines, inspects the companies that make or distribute them, and checks their safety.<Cite id="ie-hpra-what-we-regulate" />
{/* residue-ok: Ireland EU note (content brief, "What may stay" 4) */}
A medicine approved for the whole EU is approved in Ireland too.<Cite id="ie-hpra-licensing-procedures" /> In Ireland, medicines for injection are, as a rule, prescription-only.<Cite id="ie-si-2003-540-prescription-supply" /> It is illegal to sell prescription medicines online in Ireland.<Cite id="ie-hpra-buying-prescription-medicines-online" />

<InDetail title="The law in Ireland">

The Irish Medicines Board Act 1995 is the main law on medicines. The HPRA used to be called the Irish Medicines Board.<Cite id="ie-imb-act-1995" />

- **A licence first.** Under the Medicinal Products (Control of Placing on the Market) Regulations 2007, a medicine needs a licence before it is put on the market. A business may not sell, supply or keep a medicine unless it has reason to believe that it is licensed.<Cite id="ie-si-2007-540-placing-on-market" />
- **Prescriptions.** Under the Medicinal Products (Prescription and Control of Supply) Regulations 2003, medicines for injection are, as a rule, prescription-only.<Cite id="ie-si-2003-540-prescription-supply" />
- **Advertising.** Advertising a medicine that has no licence is banned.<Cite id="ie-si-2007-541-advertising" />
- **Penalties.** Breaking these rules is a crime under the Irish Medicines Board Acts 1995 and 2006. The most serious cases can lead to up to 10 years in prison.<Cite id="ie-imb-act-1995,ie-imb-misc-provisions-act-2006" />

We read the 2003 and 2007 Regulations as they were made. Later changes to them were checked only in part, so our legal reviewer will check them.

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Bringing medicines into Ireland">

Revenue, which runs Irish customs, says a traveller may carry a reasonable amount of a medicine for their own use. This does not apply to medicines sent by post or courier.<Cite id="ie-revenue-tdm-medicinal-products-2026" />

The HPRA says that illegal prescription medicines found coming into the country are detained by customs and may be destroyed. The HPRA then writes to the buyer. It cannot send the medicine on, or back to the seller.<Cite id="ie-hpra-buying-prescription-medicines-online" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="What the HPRA finds">

- **In 2025,** the HPRA detained 763,027 units of illegal medicines: single tablets, capsules, vials and the like. They included just under 14,000 parcels, each linked to a purchase by a member of the public. It closed or changed 4,762 websites, listings and social media pages.<Cite id="ie-hpra-2026-03-enforcement-2025" />
- **Weight-loss products.** Illegal GLP-1 products rose from 1,582 units in 2024 to 48,752 in 2025. Most were drops or patches, which are not authorised treatments.<Cite id="ie-hpra-2026-03-enforcement-2025" />
- **Fake pens.** In 2025, it found fake tirzepatide pens that contained insulin, which can cause severe low blood sugar.<Cite id="ie-hpra-2025-10-counterfeit-tirzepatide" />
- **Melanotan II.** Since 2022, it has detained about 1,000 units of melanotan II each year.<Cite id="ie-hpra-2024-03-enforcement-2023,ie-hpra-2025-04-enforcement-2024,ie-hpra-2026-03-enforcement-2025" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Side effects, sport and help in Ireland">

- **Side effects.** Anyone can report a side effect to the HPRA online, even if you are not sure that the product caused it. The HPRA cannot give medical advice, so talk to your doctor, pharmacist or nurse about your health.<Cite id="ie-hpra-report-side-effect,ie-hpra-reporting-as-patient" />
- **Illegal selling.** You can report it to the HPRA, in confidence.<Cite id="ie-hpra-report-illegal-activity" />
- **Sport.** Sport Ireland is the national anti-doping organisation.<Cite id="ie-sport-ireland-act-2015,ie-sport-ireland-anti-doping" /> The world anti-doping list applies, and BPC-157 is banned at all times.<Cite id="ie-sport-ireland-2026-list-advisory,wada-2026-list-en" /> Athletes can check medicines bought in Ireland on Medcheck Sport Ireland.<Cite id="ie-sport-ireland-medcheck" />
- **Help.** The HSE website shows where to get urgent care, such as out-of-hours doctor services, injury units and emergency departments.<Cite id="ie-hse-urgent-emergency-care" /> HSE needle exchange services give out sterile injecting equipment, free and in confidence.<Cite id="ie-hse-anabolic-steroids-reducing-harm" /> The National Poisons Information Centre runs a line for the public from 08:00 to 22:00.<Cite id="ie-npic-poisons" />

</InDetail>

## New Zealand

<Lead question="Who controls medicines in New Zealand?">Medsafe, part of the Ministry of Health. The Medicines Act 1981 is still the law.</Lead>

<Diagram id="nz-peptide-classification" caption="Which peptides on this site need a prescription in New Zealand, as of 23 October 2026.">

| Status | Peptides on this site |
|---|---|
| Prescription medicines, named alone or in a named group | BPC-157 and its analogues; ipamorelin, CJC-1295, tesamorelin, hexarelin and AOD-9604; SS-31 (elamipretide); semaglutide, tirzepatide and retatrutide; and, through peptide groups such as "thymic peptide hormones", peptides such as thymosin alpha-1, MOTS-c, epitalon, Semax and Selank |
| Listed as prescription in Medsafe's database | TB-500 |
| No entry of their own found | Melanotan II and PT-141 (a prescription group, "melanocyte stimulating compounds", exists, but nothing we read says whether it covers them); GHK-Cu and KPV |

</Diagram>

<Cite id="nz-gazette-2026-sl4278-classification,nz-medicines-regulations-1984,nz-medsafe-2025-06-peptides-mcc-submission,nz-medsafe-classification-database-2026-10-23" />

A new medicine needs the Minister's consent, given by Medsafe, before it can be sold or advertised.<Cite id="nz-medicines-act-1981,nz-medsafe-unapproved-medicines" /> It is against the law to import, get, keep or use a prescription medicine without a reasonable excuse, such as a prescription.<Cite id="nz-medicines-act-1981" /> Medsafe says a "research purposes" label "has no legal effect". Needing a prescription does not mean approved: Medsafe has not assessed these products.<Cite id="nz-medsafe-2026-05-peptide-advisory" />

<InDetail title="The law in New Zealand">

- **The Medicines Act 1981 still applies.** A new law, the Therapeutic Products Act 2023, was meant to replace it.<Cite id="nz-moh-2024-tpa-repeal-bill-disclosure" /> It was repealed on 18 December 2024, before most of it had come into force.<Cite id="nz-therapeutic-products-act-2023,nz-therapeutic-products-act-repeal-act-2024" /> The Ministry of Health is now developing a Medical Products Bill to replace the Medicines Act. No such bill had been put before Parliament by 24 October 2026.<Cite id="nz-moh-medical-products-bill,nz-legislation-bills-search-2026-10-23" />
- **How peptides became prescription medicines.** In December 2025, a notice added whole groups of peptides to the list of prescription medicines.<Cite id="nz-gazette-2025-go7397-classification,nz-medsafe-2025-06-peptides-mcc-submission" /> Such a notice lasts six months unless it is replaced sooner. The current notice, published on 3 August 2026, lists the same groups.<Cite id="nz-medicines-act-1981,nz-gazette-2026-sl4278-classification" />
- **Through a prescriber.** Under section 29 of the Medicines Act, an unapproved medicine can be supplied to a doctor, nurse practitioner or pharmacist prescriber to treat a patient in their care. Nurse practitioners and pharmacist prescribers can do this since 19 November 2025. The supplier reports the supply to Medsafe.<Cite id="nz-medicines-act-1981,nz-medsafe-unapproved-medicines,nz-medsafe-pu-2026-03-medicines-amendment-act" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Bringing medicines into New Zealand">

You may import a prescription medicine only with an original prescription or a letter from a New Zealand prescriber. The limit is three months' supply, for you or your close family only. Customs can hold the parcel until the prescription is checked.<Cite id="nz-medsafe-personal-importation,nz-medsafe-import-faqs" />

Until December 2025, Medsafe had to release peptides that were not on the prescription list to the buyer, with a warning letter, because the law gave it no grounds to seize them.<Cite id="nz-medsafe-2025-06-peptides-mcc-submission" /> Medsafe now says it continues to seize these products.<Cite id="nz-medsafe-2026-05-peptide-advisory" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Side effects, sport and help in New Zealand">

- **Side effects.** Patients and health professionals can report a side effect online, with a CARM report on Medsafe's website.<Cite id="nz-medsafe-report-a-problem,nz-otago-nzphvc-carm" />
- **Sport.** The Sport Integrity Commission Te Kahu Raunui runs anti-doping in New Zealand. It took over from Drug Free Sport New Zealand on 1 July 2024.<Cite id="nz-sportnz-integrity-path" /> The country's Sports Anti-Doping Rules follow the World Anti-Doping Code, and BPC-157 is banned at all times.<Cite id="nz-sic-sports-anti-doping-rules,wada-2026-list-en" /> The Commission says buying or having banned peptides can lead to a ban from all sport, and that parcels stopped by Customs "can and do lead to anti-doping sanctions".<Cite id="nz-sic-2026-06-peptides-trend" /> Athletes can check their medicines on Global DRO.<Cite id="nz-sic-checking-medications" />
- **Help.** Healthline gives free health advice by phone, 24 hours a day.<Cite id="nz-healthnz-healthline" /> The National Poisons Centre is free and open 24 hours a day.<Cite id="nz-national-poisons-centre" />
- **If you already have a product.** Medsafe says to get medical advice straight away if you feel unwell after injecting one. Leftover products can go to a community pharmacy, and used needles, in a sharps container, to a needle exchange or pharmacy.<Cite id="nz-medsafe-2026-05-peptide-advisory,nz-healthnz-safe-sharps-disposal" />

</InDetail>
