<Lead question="What does Belgian law say about peptides?">The same as EU law: a medicine needs a licence. Belgium is strict about parcels from abroad.</Lead>

<Diagram id="be-medicines-law-core" caption="The four rules of the Belgian medicines law that matter most here.">

| Rule | What it says |
|---|---|
| What a medicine is | Anything offered as treating or preventing disease in people, or anything that can change body functions through a drug-like effect, or make a diagnosis.<Cite id="be-medicines-law-1964-art-1" /> |
| The licence rule | A medicine may only be put on the market after a licence from the Belgian minister or the European Commission.<Cite id="be-medicines-law-1964" /> |
| Advertising | All advertising for a medicine without a licence is forbidden, and so is public advertising for prescription medicines.<Cite id="be-medicines-law-1964-art-9" /> |
| Buying and holding | Buying or holding medicines that do not follow the law is itself an offence that can bring a fine.<Cite id="be-medicines-law-1964-art-16" /> |

</Diagram>

The law is the Law of 25 March 1964 on medicines for human use. The FAMHP enforces it. In Dutch it is called the FAGG, in French the AFMPS.<Cite id="be-medicines-law-1964-art-1,famhp-postal-seizure-qa" />

<InDetail title="How the Belgian definition compares with the Dutch one">

Both come from the same EU directive, so they use the same two tests. One is how the product is presented. The other is what it can do in the body.<Cite id="be-medicines-law-1964-art-1,eu-directive-2001-83" /> The FAMHP puts it simply. A product is a medicine if it contains a pharmacologically active substance. It is also a medicine if it is presented as one, for example with a claim such as "prevents cancer". That it is sold elsewhere as a supplement or a cosmetic "is not a valid argument".<Cite id="famhp-postal-seizure-qa" />

One difference from Dutch law stands out. The Belgian law names buying and possessing such medicines as offences.<Cite id="be-medicines-law-1964-art-16" /> The Dutch law does not name buying.<Cite id="nl-gmw-art-40-handelsvergunning" /> We describe how the FAMHP handles this in practice below.

</InDetail>

## Parcels from abroad

<Lead question="Can I have a peptide sent to me in Belgium?">No. From outside the EEA, medicines may never be sent to you. From the EU, only approved ones.</Lead>

<Compare title="A peptide with no licence cannot legally be sent to you, from anywhere." detail="Belgian medicines law, article 12quater, and the FAMHP's answers on seized parcels (last updated 23 July 2024).">

| | From outside the EEA (the EU plus Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein) | From another EU country |
|---|---|---|
| Can a medicine be posted to you for your own use? | <Verdict is="no" /> "Never permitted", says the FAMHP.<Cite id="famhp-postal-seizure-qa" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> A reasonable amount, if it is approved there and was obtained legally.<Cite id="be-medicines-law-1964-art-12quater,famhp-postal-seizure-qa" /> |
| Can a peptide with no licence be posted to you? | <Verdict is="no" /> The FAMHP treats it as a medicine, and no medicine may be sent from there.<Cite id="famhp-postal-seizure-qa" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> Only medicines approved in an EU country may be sent.<Cite id="famhp-postal-seizure-qa" /> |

</Compare>

A medicine that is not on the Belgian market can only be imported for you by a Belgian pharmacist. You need your doctor's prescription and a statement from your doctor.<Cite id="famhp-postal-seizure-qa" />

<KeyNumber title="A second seized parcel can cost money" value="€400" label="the least you are asked to pay to settle the case after a second seizure" counted="The FAMHP's stated policy for seized postal parcels, page last updated 23 July 2024" source="famhp-postal-seizure-qa" />

<InDetail title="What happens when a parcel is seized">

The FAMHP describes its approach like this:<Cite id="famhp-postal-seizure-qa" />

- **First time:** you get a warning, and the case is closed.
- **Second time:** you are offered an out-of-court settlement of at least €400. In exceptional cases the file goes to the public prosecutor.
- **Antibiotics:** zero tolerance. A fine is possible the first time.
- **Your products:** you do not get them back. If you think the seizure was wrong, you can object by registered letter within 14 days.
- **A parcel you did not order:** contact the FAMHP; you can also report it to the police.

Each case is handled on its own, so the FAMHP says this may not apply to every product.<Cite id="famhp-postal-seizure-qa" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="Medicines online in Belgium">

In Belgium, prescription medicines may only be handed over in person, at a pharmacy, on a prescription. Only pharmacies may sell medicines online, and only medicines that need no prescription. Their websites must be notified to the FAMHP and the Order of Pharmacists.<Cite id="famhp-postal-seizure-qa" />

Legal online pharmacies in the EU show a common logo. Clicking it should lead to an official list of legal sellers. Many illegal websites look professional and claim their products come from Europe.<Cite id="famhp-postal-seizure-qa,eu-directive-2001-83" /> The FAMHP can only shut down ".be" websites itself.<Cite id="famhp-postal-seizure-qa" />

</InDetail>

## What this means for you in practice

<Lead question="What happens if a peptide is sent to me in Belgium?">A parcel that is found is seized. The first time, you get a warning. Later, you may pay.</Lead>

<Diagram id="be-in-practice" caption="What the Belgian rules mean for one person, step by step.">

1. **The parcel can be stopped.** The FAMHP treats a product with an active substance as a medicine, whatever the label says.<Cite id="famhp-postal-seizure-qa" />
2. **The first time, a warning.** The case is then closed.<Cite id="famhp-postal-seizure-qa" />
3. **The second time, a settlement.** You are offered a settlement of at least €400. In rare cases the file goes to the prosecutor.<Cite id="famhp-postal-seizure-qa" />
4. **You do not get the product back.** The FAMHP says the quality of such products cannot be guaranteed.<Cite id="famhp-postal-seizure-qa" />
5. **Reporting a side effect is a separate thing.** The FAMHP says it uses side-effect reports only for the scientific study of the medicine.<Cite id="famhp-adverse-reaction-qa" />

</Diagram>

Passing a product on or selling it is treated more strictly. If you know it does not meet the law, the penalty can include prison as well as a fine.<Cite id="be-medicines-law-1964-art-16" /> If you already use a product, see [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/) and [warning signs](/en/safety/warning-signs/).

## Legal ways to get a medicine that is not on the Belgian market

<Lead question="Is there a legal way to get it?">Only with a doctor and a pharmacist, and only if the drug is approved in another country.</Lead>

<Compare title="The Belgian routes work for medicines approved abroad. None fits a peptide approved nowhere." detail="Belgian medicines law, article 6quater, and the FAMHP's list of approved programmes (snapshot of 20 September 2026).">

| | A medicine approved in another country | A peptide approved nowhere (for example BPC-157) |
|---|---|---|
| Import by a pharmacist, with a prescription and a doctor's statement? | <Verdict is="yes" /> If no suitable medicine is on the Belgian market.<Cite id="be-medicines-law-1964-art-6quater" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> Only medicines approved in their country of origin.<Cite id="be-medicines-law-1964-art-6quater,famhp-postal-seizure-qa" /> |
| A compassionate use or medical need programme? | <Verdict is="yes" /> If the company runs one and it is approved.<Cite id="famhp-compassionate-use-medical-need" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> None is listed for any peptide on this site.<Cite id="famhp-compassionate-use-medical-need" /> |
| A clinical trial? | <Verdict is="yes" /> If a trial runs and you qualify.<Cite id="be-medicines-law-1964-art-6quater" /> | <Verdict is="unknown" /> Only if a trial exists. We found none running in Belgium.<Cite id="ctgov-nl-be-peptide-search-2026-10-22" /> |

</Compare>

<InDetail title="The routes in the law, one by one">

Article 6quater of the medicines law lists the exceptions to the licence rule:<Cite id="be-medicines-law-1964-art-6quater" />

1. **Special needs.** A medicine supplied on a genuine order from a prescriber. It is for patients who cannot be treated well with medicines approved and available in Belgium.
2. **Compassionate use.** For a group of patients with a long-term, seriously disabling or life-threatening disease. An EU licence application must be filed, or trials must be running.
3. **Medical need programmes.** For a medicine that is approved, but not yet for this use.
4. **Import by a pharmacist.** Only of a medicine approved in its country of origin. You need a prescription and a doctor's statement. The statement says you cannot be treated well with a medicine on the Belgian market.

The law also needs no licence for a medicine that a pharmacy makes for one patient on a prescription. The same goes for a medicine used in a clinical trial.<Cite id="be-medicines-law-1964-art-6quater" /> We have not checked the Belgian quality rules for pharmacy preparations in detail. In the Netherlands, the raw material must meet an official quality standard; see [legal routes](/en/legal-routes/).

</InDetail>

## Sport and doping in Belgium

<Lead question="Who makes the doping rules in Belgium?">The Communities, not the federal state. Testing also happens in gyms and at a low level.</Lead>

<Diagram id="be-doping-structure" caption="In Belgium, four bodies share the work against doping.">

1. **Four responsible bodies.** The Flemish, French and German-speaking Communities, and the Common Community Commission in Brussels, have a joint agreement on doping.<Cite id="dopinglijn-regelgeving" />
2. **Flemish rules cover hobby sport too.** In the Flemish decree, an athlete is anyone who plays sport in an organised setting, at any level, also for fun.<Cite id="be-flemish-antidoping-decree-2012" />
3. **Different rules for elite and other athletes.** Elite athletes are judged by their sports federation. Other athletes have their own disciplinary route.<Cite id="be-flemish-antidoping-decree-2012" />
4. **More testing than in the Netherlands.** Belgium tests far more at lower levels, with surprise tests in gyms. Refusing a test usually brings a ban of two or four years, which also applies in the Netherlands.<Cite id="dopingautoriteit-controles-belgie" />

</Diagram>

The World Anti-Doping Agency bans, at all times, any drug that no health authority has approved for human use. This applies when no other part of its list covers the drug already.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en" /> See [sport and doping](/en/legal/sport-and-doping/).

## Reporting a side effect

<Lead question="Where do I report a side effect in Belgium?">To the FAMHP, online. Anyone can report, also for someone else.</Lead>

<JurisdictionCard code="BE" />

You can report any side effect you suspect, even if you are not sure the product caused it. Family and friends can report for you, and so can your doctor or pharmacist.<Cite id="famhp-adverse-reaction-qa" /> The FAMHP needs to know who had the reaction, what happened, which product you suspect, and how it went on. If you know the batch number, add it.<Cite id="famhp-adverse-reaction-qa" />
