<Lead question="Why read about evidence?">Because many claims about peptides rest on small animal studies. These pages show how to check them.</Lead>

Many peptides sold outside pharmacies, such as BPC-157, TB-500 and KPV, have been studied almost only in cells and animals. Some have hardly been studied at all.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing" /> Animal studies are a real start. But in one large review, only 5 in 100 treatments tested in animals were later approved for use in people.<Cite id="ineichen-2024-animal-to-human" />

Who paid matters too. Studies paid for by a company more often report good results for its product.<Cite id="lundh-2017-industry-sponsorship" /> That does not make them wrong. It means someone independent should check.

These pages give no doses and no advice on what to use. They help you judge what you read. If you already use a product, see [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/) and the [warning signs](/en/safety/warning-signs/).
