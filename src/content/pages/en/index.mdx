## The most important distinction

<Lead question="Does the same name mean the same product?">No. The same name can mean a checked medicine or an unchecked vial.</Lead>

<Compare title="A pharmacy medicine and an online vial can share a name. They are not the same thing." detail="Semaglutide as an example, in the US, the UK, Canada and Australia. What regulators say about each. Checked on 24 October 2026.">

| | Semaglutide from a pharmacy | "Research semaglutide" sold online |
|---|---|---|
| An approved medicine? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Approved in the US, the UK, Canada and Australia; only on prescription<Cite id="fda-drugsatfda-wegovy,uk-mhra-substance-index-status-a-2026-10-24,ca-pm-wegovy-2026-10-02,au-artg-wegovy-356286,usc-21-353" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> It is not that medicine. The UK regulator says GLP-1 powder in vials is not authorised<Cite id="mhra-glp1-what-you-need-to-know" /> |
| Benefits and risks weighed by a regulator? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Before it was approved. Regulators ask the maker for evidence of its safety and effect, from trials in people<Cite id="ca-fdr-c08-004-notice-of-compliance,au-tga-prescription-medicines-overview,fda-label-wegovy" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> The US regulator warns that products sold "for research purposes" are of unknown quality<Cite id="fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" /> |
| Is what is inside checked? | <Verdict is="yes" /> In the US, every batch is tested before release, and injections are tested for germs<Cite id="ecfr-21-211-165,ecfr-21-211-167" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> No regulator has checked what is in it, or how it was made<Cite id="ca-hc-2026-04-think-twice-peptides,mhra-2026-01-seizures-2025" /> |

</Compare>

The same name can mean two very different things. **Semaglutide** from a pharmacy is an approved medicine: a regulator has checked its quality, its effects and its risks.<Cite id="au-tga-prescription-medicines-overview,ca-fdr-c08-004-notice-of-compliance,ecfr-21-211-165" /> A vial sold online as "research semaglutide" is not that medicine. No regulator has checked what is in it.<Cite id="mhra-glp1-what-you-need-to-know,fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" />

Most of the other peptides people talk about, such as [BPC-157](/en/compounds/bpc-157/), are not approved medicines in the US, the UK, Canada or Australia.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,uk-mhra-substance-index-status-c-2026-10-23,ca-dpd-search-status-c-2026-10-23,au-artg-search-status-c-2026-10-23" /> Their evidence comes mostly from animals.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> This site shows the shape of that evidence for every compound, in the same way, so you can compare honestly.
