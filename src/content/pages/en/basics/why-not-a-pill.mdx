Peptides are digested in the gut because the same enzymes that break down food protein cut peptide bonds. So most peptide medicines are injected. This page follows a swallowed peptide step by step, then looks at the exceptions: the semaglutide tablet, small-molecule pills, and the capsules and sprays sold online.

## The gut is built to take protein apart

<Lead question="Why can't you just swallow a peptide?">Your gut digests it like food protein, then blocks most of what is left.</Lead>

<Diagram id="journey-of-a-swallowed-peptide" caption="A swallowed peptide meets five barriers before it reaches the blood.">

1. **Stomach acid.** Acid can unfold a protein's shape. The enzyme pepsin, most active in strong acid, starts cutting the chain.
2. **Pancreatic enzymes.** In the small intestine, enzymes from the pancreas (trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase, carboxypeptidases) cut it into smaller pieces.
3. **The gut lining.** Enzymes on the surface of the gut cells cut the pieces down to single amino acids and pairs or triplets, which the body absorbs as food.
4. **The gut wall.** Any intact peptide left is too big and too water-loving to pass through the cells, and the seals between cells (tight junctions) keep large molecules out.
5. **The liver.** What does get through can be broken down in the gut wall or the liver before it reaches the rest of the body.

</Diagram>

<Cite id="zhu-2021-oral-delivery,santiago-2021-core-concepts,drucker-2020-oral-peptides" />

This is not a flaw in the peptide. It is the gut doing its job: turning protein into amino acids for food.<Cite id="zhu-2021-oral-delivery" /> Injecting under the skin skips all five barriers, which is why most peptide medicines are given that way.<Cite id="drucker-2020-oral-peptides,diao-2013-peptide-pharmacokinetics" />

<InDetail title="Each barrier in more detail">

### The stomach

Many proteins are stable only within a narrow range of acidity and can unfold in stomach fluid. Pepsin is most active at pH 2 to 3 and stops working above pH 5.<Cite id="zhu-2021-oral-delivery" /> <Claim level="in-vitro">In laboratory tests, no insulin could be detected after 30 minutes in simulated stomach fluid</Claim>.<Cite id="zhu-2021-oral-delivery" />

### The small intestine

The pancreas releases a set of protein-cutting enzymes into the upper small intestine, such as trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase and elastase. Enzymes fixed to the surface of the gut cells (the brush border) then finish the job, cutting what is left into single amino acids and pairs or triplets.<Cite id="zhu-2021-oral-delivery" />

### The gut wall

Cells of the gut lining form a continuous single layer, sealed to each other by tight junctions that make it a gatekeeper against large molecules. Large size and a strong attraction to water both make it hard for a peptide to pass through the cells' oily membranes.<Cite id="zhu-2021-oral-delivery" /> A widely used rule of thumb from drug chemistry says that poor absorption is more likely above a molecular weight of 500; many peptide medicines weigh several thousand (see [what is a peptide](/en/basics/what-is-a-peptide/)).<Cite id="lipinski-2001-rule-of-5" />

### The liver, and differences between people

Some of what is absorbed can be broken down in the gut wall or the liver before it reaches the rest of the body. This is called first-pass metabolism.<Cite id="santiago-2021-core-concepts" /> On top of all this, the gut differs a lot between people, which makes the share that gets through hard to predict.<Cite id="zhu-2021-oral-delivery" /> The semaglutide tablet shows this: the share absorbed varies widely from person to person (see below).<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy,ema-epar-rybelsus" />

</InDetail>

## How much gets through

<Lead question="How much of a swallowed peptide reaches the blood?">Very little. Even special tablets get only 1 or 2 in 100 parts through.</Lead>

<KeyNumber title="Injected: most of it reaches the blood" value="89%" label="of the dose got into the blood after a shot under the skin" who="Semaglutide; US label for Wegovy" source="fda-label-wegovy" level="established" />

<KeyNumber title="Swallowed: only a tiny share gets in" value="about 1–2%" label="of the dose got into the blood from a tablet, even with a helper" who="Semaglutide tablets (Wegovy tablets, Ozempic tablets); US labels" source="fda-label-wegovy,fda-label-rybelsus-ozempic-tablets" level="established" />

The same molecule, swallowed instead of injected, reaches the blood about 45 to 90 times less. For the older Rybelsus tablets, the US label gives an even lower share, about 0.4% to 1%.<Cite id="fda-label-rybelsus-ozempic-tablets" /> A review of oral peptide medicines that use absorption helpers puts typical oral bioavailability at about 1%, and variable.<Cite id="brayden-2020-oral-peptides" />

<InDetail title="Other approved oral peptides, and the natural exceptions">

### Desmopressin: an old oral peptide

Desmopressin is a synthetic, modified version of the hormone vasopressin, first approved in the US in 1978. Its tablets are an approved treatment, but the US label states that their bioavailability is about 0.16% of an injection into a vein, and about 5% of the nasal spray.<Cite id="fda-label-desmopressin-tablets" /> A tablet can still be useful when the molecule is potent enough that a tiny share is sufficient.<Cite id="brayden-2020-oral-peptides" />

### Peptides that do survive the gut

Some peptides are built differently. Cyclosporin is a ring-shaped peptide of molecular weight 1202.<Cite id="zhu-2021-oral-delivery" /> It has been approved in the US as a medicine taken by mouth since 1983, first as a drink and from 1990 also as capsules.<Cite id="fda-drugsatfda-sandimmune-oral" /> Alpha-amanitin, the ring-shaped toxin of the death cap mushroom, resists acid and enzymes, so it survives digestion and damages the liver within days of a meal.<Cite id="le-dare-2021-amanitins" /> Researchers now design small, compact ring-shaped peptides for this reason: they may be more orally available than ordinary linear chains.<Cite id="brayden-2020-oral-peptides" /> Being a peptide does not rule out oral absorption; it makes it the exception, which has to be shown for each molecule.

</InDetail>

## How the semaglutide tablet gets round the problem

<Lead question="How does the semaglutide tablet work, then?">A helper substance protects it in the stomach and lets a small share through.</Lead>

<Diagram id="how-snac-works" caption="The semaglutide tablet works in the stomach, right next to the tablet.">

1. **The tablet dissolves in the stomach.** It contains semaglutide and a helper substance, SNAC (salcaprozate sodium).
2. **A local shield.** Around the dissolving tablet, SNAC buffers the acid, which protects semaglutide from the stomach's enzymes.
3. **A short-lived door.** SNAC briefly helps semaglutide pass through the stomach-lining cells themselves, not between them.
4. **A small share gets in.** About 1–2% of the dose reaches the blood. It varies a lot from person to person.
5. **Food and water get in the way.** Absorption falls if the tablet is taken with food or a lot of water.

</Diagram>

<Cite id="buckley-2018-oral-semaglutide,fda-label-rybelsus-ozempic-tablets,fda-label-wegovy,ema-epar-rybelsus" />

This works because semaglutide is potent and has a half-life of about a week, so a small, variable daily share adds up to steady levels.<Cite id="fda-label-rybelsus-ozempic-tablets,brayden-2020-oral-peptides" /> The cost: so little gets in that the tablet has to be taken every day under strict rules, and how much reaches the blood varies a lot between people.<Cite id="fda-label-rybelsus-ozempic-tablets,fda-label-wegovy" />

<InDetail title="What the tablet costs: rules, variation and switching">

### The evidence for the mechanism

<Claim level="human-limited">In the developer's studies in dogs and people, semaglutide from the tablet was absorbed in the stomach, close to the tablet surface, and only when it was formulated with SNAC</Claim>. SNAC protected against enzyme breakdown by buffering the acid nearby and only briefly increased absorption. Absorption went through the cells, with no sign of an effect on the tight junctions between them.<Cite id="buckley-2018-oral-semaglutide" /> The US label states that SNAC helps semaglutide to be absorbed and that absorption happens mainly in the stomach.<Cite id="fda-label-rybelsus-ozempic-tablets" />
{/* residue-ok: EMA evidence */}
The European product information adds that the role of SNAC rests on laboratory data.<Cite id="ema-epar-rybelsus" />

### The rules on the label

Because food and water reduce absorption, the US label asks people to take the tablet on an empty stomach in the morning, with no more than 4 ounces (about 120 mL) of plain water, and to wait at least 30 minutes before eating, drinking or taking other tablets.<Cite id="fda-label-rybelsus-ozempic-tablets" /> In the studies behind the label, more semaglutide was absorbed with a small amount of water than with a full glass (50 mL against 240 mL), and with a longer wait before eating.<Cite id="fda-label-rybelsus-ozempic-tablets" /> Your prescriber explains how this fits your day.

### Variation between people

The share absorbed varies widely. The US label for Wegovy reports a much wider range of blood levels with the daily tablet than with the weekly injection.<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy" />
{/* residue-ok: EMA evidence */}
The European product information gives a between-person variation of about 100%, and states that 2 to 4 in 100 patients will not have any exposure at all.<Cite id="ema-epar-rybelsus" /> For the same reason, the effect of switching between the tablet and the injection cannot easily be predicted.<Cite id="ema-epar-rybelsus" /> In clinical trials the tablet still lowered blood sugar: in PIONEER 1, a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 703 adults with type 2 diabetes, with authors from the manufacturer, it lowered HbA1c (average blood sugar) compared with placebo at all three tablet strengths over 26 weeks.<Cite id="aroda-2019-pioneer-1" />

</InDetail>

## The other route: a small-molecule pill

<Lead question="Is there a pill that acts like a peptide?">Yes. Orforglipron is not a peptide, but it switches on the same GLP-1 receptor.</Lead>

| Approved in Australia? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Since 2022 (Rybelsus)<Cite id="au-artg-rybelsus-346198" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> Not on the Australian register on 24 October 2026<Cite id="au-artg-search-status-a-2026-10-24" /> |

</Compare>

Instead of protecting a large peptide, chemists found a small molecule that switches on the same receptor. At 883 daltons it is roughly a fifth the size of semaglutide.<Cite id="pubchem-orforglipron,pubchem-semaglutide" /> It is a partial agonist with its own profile, not a peptide in pill form (see [how signalling works](/en/basics/how-signalling-works/)).<Cite id="kawai-2020-orforglipron-structure" />

<InDetail title="Orforglipron: what is known, and what is different">

- **What it is.** <Claim level="in-vitro">In laboratory and structural studies by its developers, orforglipron (code LY3502970) bound its own pocket in the GLP-1 receptor, touching different parts of the receptor than GLP-1 does, acted as a partial agonist, and favoured the G-protein signal</Claim>.<Cite id="kawai-2020-orforglipron-structure,pubchem-orforglipron" />
- **Evidence in people.** <Claim level="human-controlled">In a manufacturer-funded, randomised, double-blind phase 2 trial in 272 adults with obesity, or with overweight and a weight-related condition, and without diabetes, mean weight change at 36 weeks ranged from −9.4% to −14.7% across dose groups, against −2.3% with placebo; stomach and gut side effects led 10 to 17 in 100 to stop</Claim>.<Cite id="wharton-2023-orforglipron-phase2" /> The European Medicines Agency's assessment, which led to a positive opinion in October 2026, rests on two weight-management studies in 4,740 adults and four diabetes studies in 3,765 adults.<Cite id="ema-2026-foundayo-chmp" /> {/* residue-ok: EMA evidence */}
- **Different trade-offs.** Orforglipron is broken down mainly by the liver enzyme CYP3A4, so the label adjusts its use with medicines that block or speed up that enzyme. Its half-life is about 29 to 49 hours.<Cite id="fda-label-foundayo" /> It does not act on the rat or mouse receptor, so the usual rodent tests could not show receptor-driven thyroid tumours; the US label still carries a boxed warning about thyroid tumours.<Cite id="fda-label-foundayo" />

</InDetail>

## Capsules, sprays and drops sold online

<Lead question="Do peptide capsules, sprays or drops work as well as injections?">There is no evidence that they do. No study in people has measured how much reaches the blood.</Lead>

<Compare title="For the tablet, we know how much gets in. For BPC-157 capsules and sprays, we do not." detail="Semaglutide tablets compared with BPC-157 sold as capsules, oral spray or nasal spray, from the US labels and the FDA's 2026 review.">

| | Semaglutide tablet (authorised) | BPC-157 capsules or sprays |
|---|---|---|
| Absorption measured in people? | <Verdict is="yes" /> About 1–2% reaches the blood<Cite id="fda-label-wegovy,fda-label-rybelsus-ozempic-tablets" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> No human data by mouth, nose or skin<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> |
| Built to get through the gut wall? | <Verdict is="yes" /> With SNAC, tested in dogs and people<Cite id="buckley-2018-oral-semaglutide" /> | <Verdict is="unknown" /> No published evidence<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> |
| Trial results in people published for this form? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Large controlled trials<Cite id="aroda-2019-pioneer-1" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> A phase 1 study of BPC-157 tablets was registered in 2015; no results were posted<Cite id="ctgov-nct02637284,fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> |

</Compare>

The FDA found BPC-157 sold as capsules, oral sprays and nasal sprays, but no human data on how much of it reaches the blood by these routes.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> Claims that such products work as well as an injection have no published support. If you already have one, the [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/) page explains what to check.

<InDetail title="What the evidence does and does not show">

### Taken by mouth

<Claim level="animal">Some rat studies have reported effects of BPC-157 given in drinking water</Claim>, and BPC-157 resisted gastric juice in a lab test.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> Neither shows that it reaches the blood in people. A rat study measures an effect, not absorption, and surviving stomach juice is only the first of the five barriers above. In two human studies of BPC-157 given as an enema, the authors reported that it was not detected in the blood.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" />

### Under the tongue and in the nose

Taking a peptide under the tongue or in the nose avoids the stomach, but the lining is still a barrier. Approved nasal peptides exist. The US label for desmopressin tablets puts their bioavailability at about 0.16% of an injection into a vein and about 5% of the nasal spray; together, these figures mean the nasal spray delivers only about 3% of what a vein injection does.<Cite id="fda-label-desmopressin-tablets" /> Semax is registered in Russia as nasal drops.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-semax-briefing" /> For TB-500 and KPV, the FDA found no human studies of absorption by any route.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing" />

### What this means

Without measurements, nobody can say whether a capsule or spray delivers nothing, a little, or an unpredictable amount, or whether that changes from one batch to the next. A product that delivers little is not automatically harmless either: what is in it, including impurities, is still unknown (see [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/)).

</InDetail>
