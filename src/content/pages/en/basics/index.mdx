<Lead question="Why read the basics first?">They explain why a peptide does what it does, and why so much is unknown.</Lead>

A peptide is a short chain of amino acids, the same building blocks as the protein in your food. Your body uses many peptides as messages. Some medicines copy them.<Cite id="nhgri-glossary-peptide,nobel-1984-merrifield-press,muttenthaler-2021-peptide-trends" />

Four questions explain most of what this site says about any peptide:

<Diagram id="basics-four-questions" caption="Four questions explain most of what you need to know about a peptide.">

| Question | Why it matters | Page |
|---|---|---|
| What is it made of? | Its size and shape decide where it can go and how long it lasts | [What is a peptide?](/en/basics/what-is-a-peptide/) |
| What does it switch on? | One switch can act in many organs, and the body can push back | [How signalling works](/en/basics/how-signalling-works/) |
| How long does it stay? | This decides how long effects and side effects last | [Pharmacokinetics](/en/basics/pharmacokinetics/) |
| Can it be swallowed? | Your gut digests most peptides like food | [Why not a pill](/en/basics/why-not-a-pill/) |

</Diagram>

<Cite id="di-2015-peptide-adme,santiago-2021-core-concepts,zhu-2021-oral-delivery" />

The pages use examples from medicines that a regulator has checked, such as semaglutide. For those, a lot is known. For many peptides sold outside pharmacies, such as BPC-157, TB-500 and KPV, the same basic facts were never measured in people.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing" />

These pages give no doses and no advice on what to use. If you already have a product, read [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/) and the [warning signs](/en/safety/warning-signs/).
