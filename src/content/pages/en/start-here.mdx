Go to the [warning signs](/en/safety/warning-signs/) now. If it is severe, call your local emergency number.

</Callout>

<Lead question="Who is this page for?">For anyone who is thinking about a peptide, or already uses one. We do not judge you.</Lead>

This page does not tell you what to do. It gives you six things to know, in the order you need them. It gives no doses and names no sellers. Every fact links to its source, so you can check it.

## 1. Most have never been tested properly in people

<Lead question="Have these peptides been tested in people?">Most have not. Only a few, such as semaglutide, went through large trials.</Lead>

<Compare title="One approved peptide was tested in thousands of people. Three others, not approved, were not." detail="Semaglutide compared with BPC-157, TB-500 and KPV. Checked on 22 October 2026.">

| | Semaglutide (an approved medicine) | BPC-157, TB-500 and KPV (experimental) |
|---|---|---|
| Tested in large, fair trials in people? | <Verdict is="yes" /> One weight trial alone had 1,961 adults.<Cite id="wilding-2021-step-1" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> No study of KPV, or of TB-500 alone, in people is published. BPC-157 had two small trials, known only from short conference abstracts.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> |
| Side effects counted, with how often they happen? | <Verdict is="yes" /> They are on the official label, with how often each one happens.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> No proper study has counted them.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing" /> |
| Safety followed over years? | <Verdict is="yes" /> One trial followed 17,604 adults for more than three years on average.<Cite id="lincoff-2023-select" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> The formal safety tests of BPC-157 with daily doses, in rats and dogs, lasted 28 days.<Cite id="xu-2020-preclinical-safety,fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> |
| Benefits and risks weighed by a regulator? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Before it was approved in the EU.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> None of them is an approved medicine.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing,ema-medicines-search" /> |

</Compare>

"Never tested properly" does not mean "certain to harm you". It means nobody knows. Nobody knows if it works in people. Nobody knows which side effects it causes, or how often. Nobody knows what months of use do. Most research on these three peptides was done in cells, rats and mice, not in people.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing" />

<InDetail title="Why animal results are not enough">

A result in rats is a reason to test something in people. It is not proof that it works in people. Lab results also often fail when other scientists repeat them. Scientists at one company could confirm only 6 of 53 key cancer studies.<Cite id="begley-2012-preclinical-standards,errington-2021-replicability" /> See [the translation gap](/en/evidence/the-translation-gap/).

Even after trials in people have started, most attempts to bring a medicine to patients fail. A study counted each medicine for one illness as one attempt. Only about 1 in 7 of these attempts ended in approval.<Cite id="wong-2019-clinical-trial-success" />

<KeyNumber title="Most attempts that reach trials in people never lead to an approved medicine" value="13.8%" label="of drug programmes that started human trials were approved in the end: about 1 in 7" counted="41,040 industry drug programmes (one drug for one illness) with trials from 2000 to 2015 (Wong 2019)" source="wong-2019-clinical-trial-success" />

For KPV, the FDA found no study in people at all. For TB-500 it found no human data. Two small reports gave people TB-500, or a related "TB4", only together with other substances.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,froum-2026-nerve-cases,lee-2021-knee" />

BPC-157 has been studied in people a little. In the 2000s, two small controlled trials tested it as an enema. One was in 32 healthy men, one in 53 people with bowel inflammation (ulcerative colitis). The results exist only as conference abstracts: short summaries for a meeting.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> Four small reports on 33 people in total were published later. None had a comparison group. In two of them, some or all people also got other substances.<Cite id="lee-2021-knee,lee-2024-interstitial-cystitis,lee-2025-intravenous,froum-2026-nerve-cases" /> See [BPC-157](/en/compounds/bpc-157/), [TB-500](/en/compounds/tb-500/) and [KPV](/en/compounds/kpv/).

</InDetail>

## 2. The vial is not the molecule

<Lead question="Is a vial from outside a pharmacy the same as the medicine?">No. No regulator has checked what is in it, how much, or whether it is free of germs.</Lead>

The picture shows what may be wrong with a vial that did not come from a pharmacy.

<Infographic id="online-vial-risks" />

This is true even when the name is right. Semaglutide is an approved medicine. But "research semaglutide" sold online is not that medicine: no regulator has checked it.<Cite id="cbg-glp1-position,fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" /> Clean technique cannot fix what is inside a vial. See [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/).

<InDetail title="What can be wrong, with real examples">

- **The wrong amount.** In 2026, researchers in Australia tested three products sold as retatrutide. They held from about half to almost twice the amount on the label.<Cite id="piatkowski-2026-retatrutide-product-testing" />
- **The wrong substance.** In 2023, fake pens sold as Ozempic in Germany held insulin, not semaglutide.<Cite id="bfarm-ozempic-falsification" /> Insulin can make blood sugar fall too low.<Cite id="nhs-low-blood-sugar" /> Someone who thinks they are injecting semaglutide does not expect that.
- **Germs before the vial is opened.** In the United States in 2012, injections made by one pharmacy were contaminated with a fungus. Fungus was found in vials that had never been opened. By July 2013, 749 people were ill and 61 had died.<Cite id="smith-2013-fungal-methylprednisolone" /> Careful technique by the people who gave the injections could not have prevented it.

The Dutch health inspectorate (IGJ) says that a medicine ordered online can hold too much active substance, too little, or none. If it is contaminated, it can damage your organs.<Cite id="igj-online-medicijnen" />

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="What you can check yourself, and what you cannot">

You can read the label and any paper that came with the product. You cannot see what is inside the vial, or how much of it. Without the checks that come with a doctor or pharmacist, you cannot know what you are getting.<Cite id="igj-online-medicijnen" />

<Checklist>

- **Is there a lab certificate?** It is often called a certificate of analysis. It lists the tests a lab did, and their results.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" />
- **Does it test for germs and bacterial toxins?** For anything injected, the FDA calls three tests key: no germs (sterility), no bacterial toxins (endotoxin) and no loose particles.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" />
- **Does it give more than purity?** Purity alone does not say which impurities are there, or how much peptide is in the vial.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" />
- **Does the name match exactly?** One name can cover different salt forms, or even a different substance.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" />

</Checklist>

The FDA looked at certificates for BPC-157, TB-500 and KPV. Most of the ones it found gave little more than a purity result. None showed a test for germs.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing" /> Our page on [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/) goes further.

</InDetail>

## 3. The law in plain words

<Lead question="Is it legal?">In the Netherlands, only medicines with a licence may be sold. BPC-157, TB-500 and KPV have none.</Lead>

<Compare title="An approved peptide comes on prescription from a pharmacy. An unapproved one has no normal legal route." detail="The rules in the Netherlands, checked on 22 October 2026. Other countries have their own rules.">

| | An approved peptide medicine (for example Wegovy) | An unapproved peptide (for example BPC-157) |
|---|---|---|
| Licensed to be sold as a medicine? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Approved in the EU since 2022.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> Not approved in the EU or anywhere else.<Cite id="ema-medicines-search,fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> |
| A legal way to get it? | <Verdict is="yes" /> With a doctor's prescription, from a pharmacy.<Cite id="nl-gmw-art-61-prescription,ema-epar-wegovy" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> Selling, supplying or importing it is against the law. The few exceptions, such as a clinical trial, are narrow.<Cite id="nl-gmw-art-40-handelsvergunning" /> |
| Allowed in competitive sport? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Semaglutide is not banned. The anti-doping agency keeps track of its use.<Cite id="wada-2026-monitoring-program" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> Banned at all times.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en" /> |

</Compare>

A licence to sell a medicine is called a marketing authorisation. Sellers often write "for research use only" on their products. That does not make a product a licensed medicine. It does not make it fit to inject, either.<Cite id="nl-gmw-art-40-handelsvergunning,fda-unapproved-glp1-concerns" /> The rules in other countries differ: see [Germany](/en/legal/de/), [Belgium](/en/legal/be/), [the UK](/en/legal/uk/), [the EU](/en/legal/eu/) and [the US](/en/legal/us/).

<InDetail title="What Dutch law says, in more detail">

The main law is the Medicines Act (Geneesmiddelenwet).

- **What counts as a medicine.** A substance meant or presented to treat or prevent illness is a medicine. So is a substance meant to change how the body works, by acting on its chemistry or immune system.<Cite id="nl-gmw-art-1-geneesmiddel" /> Whether a product sold "for research" counts is a legal question: see [research use only](/en/legal/research-use-only/).
- **No licence, no sale.** A medicine without a marketing authorisation may not be sold, offered, supplied, kept in stock or imported. The few exceptions include medicines for clinical trials and an exemption from the inspectorate for one patient.<Cite id="nl-gmw-art-40-handelsvergunning" />
- **Prescription medicines come from a pharmacy.** A pharmacist may not hand them over without a prescription.<Cite id="nl-gmw-art-61-prescription" />
- **No prescriptions from strangers online.** A doctor may not prescribe over the internet to someone they have never met, do not know, or whose medicine history they cannot see.<Cite id="nl-gmw-art-67-internet-prescribing" />

What this means for someone who orders a product for their own use is on our page on [Dutch law](/en/legal/nl/). We do not give legal advice. For the legal routes, see [legal routes to treatment](/en/legal-routes/).

</InDetail>

## 4. The real promise, and the real uncertainty

<Lead question="Is there real promise?">Yes, for some. A few peptide medicines work well. For many others, the promise is only in animals.</Lead>

<Diagram id="evidence-ladder-start-here" caption="How far six peptides have come, from the lab to an approved medicine. Checked on 22 October 2026.">

| Peptide | How far it has come | What that means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Semaglutide | Large trials in people, then approved in the EU<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy,wilding-2021-step-1" /> | Effects and side effects are well known. A doctor can prescribe it. |
| Tirzepatide | Large trials in people, then approved in the EU<Cite id="ema-epar-mounjaro" /> | Effects and side effects are well known. A doctor can prescribe it. |
| Retatrutide | Large trials in people, but not approved anywhere yet<Cite id="jastreboff-2026-triumph-1,ema-2026-10-under-evaluation,sec-lilly-2026-q2-results" /> | Weight loss in trials was large, but no regulator has weighed the results yet. In the Netherlands, in practice only available inside a trial.<Cite id="cbg-compassionate-use-list" /> |
| BPC-157 | Mostly rats; a few small reports in people<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> | Nobody knows if it works in people, or what harm it can do. |
| TB-500 | Lab and animal studies; no study of TB-500 alone in people<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing" /> | Nobody knows if it works in people, or what harm it can do. |
| KPV | Cells and mice only<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing" /> | Nobody knows if it works in people, or what harm it can do. |

</Diagram>

Well-tested peptide medicines can do a lot. In a fair trial of 1,961 adults with obesity or overweight, without diabetes, <Claim level="human-controlled">people on semaglutide lost about 15% of their weight on average after 68 weeks, against 2.4% with a dummy injection</Claim>.<Cite id="wilding-2021-step-1" /> For BPC-157, <Claim level="animal">many rat studies report faster healing</Claim>. But most of these studies come from one group in Zagreb, and BPC-157 has not been tested properly in people.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,pubmed-bpc157-count-2026-10-20" /> See [the Croatian lab](/en/history/the-croatian-lab/).

<InDetail title="Slow rules have real costs, and so do skipped trials">

Waiting for trials has real costs. Some people suffer while the evidence is collected. In the Netherlands, Wegovy is approved, but it is not paid for by basic health insurance for obesity. The Zorginstituut called it effective for weight loss, but advised against payment in July 2024. It pointed to unclear health gain and high costs.<Cite id="zin-2024-wegovy-not-reimbursed" /> So people who use it for weight loss usually pay for it themselves. A new assessment was under way in October 2026.<Cite id="zin-wegovy-reassessment" />

Skipping trials has costs too. Without a fair trial with a comparison group, nobody can tell which harms a product causes, or how often. Without a check on quality, nobody can be sure what is in the product. Our [position on reform](/en/the-case-for-reform/) says what we think should change. That page is an opinion, kept apart from the facts here.

Retatrutide shows both sides. In a fair trial of 2,339 adults with obesity, without diabetes, <Claim level="human-controlled">average weight loss after 80 weeks was 17.6% to 25.0%, depending on the dose, against 3.9% with a dummy injection</Claim>.<Cite id="jastreboff-2026-triumph-1" /> On 5 October 2026 it was not on the EU list of medicines under review.<Cite id="ema-2026-10-under-evaluation" /> Until a regulator approves it, a product sold as retatrutide is an unchecked product. See [retatrutide](/en/compounds/retatrutide/).

</InDetail>

## 5. Bring a doctor into it

<Lead question="Should I tell a doctor?">Yes. A doctor can check your risks, look for an approved option, and help if something goes wrong.</Lead>

<Diagram id="what-a-doctor-can-do" caption="Five things a doctor can do for you, even if they disagree with your plan">

| A doctor can | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Check whether you should not use these products at all | Some health problems and medicines raise the risk. For example, semaglutide together with insulin can make blood sugar fall too low.<Cite id="ema-epar-wegovy" /> See [before you consider a peptide](/en/safety/before-you-consider/). |
| Tell you about approved options for your goal | Dutch law says your doctor must explain the risks, and other possible treatments.<Cite id="nl-wgbo-art-448-information" /> |
| Do checks, such as blood tests | Checks show how your body is doing, not only how you feel. See [monitoring](/en/safety/monitoring/). |
| Treat side effects early | A skin infection called cellulitis needs antibiotics. Without them, it can spread.<Cite id="nhs-cellulitis" /> See the [warning signs](/en/safety/warning-signs/). |
| Report a side effect | Doctors and patients can report side effects to Lareb, also for products bought online.<Cite id="lareb-bijwerkingen-melden" /> |

</Diagram>

In the Netherlands, your doctor must keep what you say private.<Cite id="knmg-beroepsgeheim,nl-wgbo-art-457-confidentiality" /> The Dutch GPs' website for patients says you can come if you have thought of a way to treat yourself, but are not sure it is right.<Cite id="thuisarts-naar-de-huisarts" /> Our page on [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/) has words you can use.

## Questions people ask

<Lead question="What do people often ask?">Here are short, honest answers to common questions. Each one links to more.</Lead>

<Faq>

<FaqItem q="Is it legal to get peptides online in the Netherlands?">

Selling, supplying and importing a medicine without a licence is against Dutch law.<Cite id="nl-gmw-art-40-handelsvergunning" /> BPC-157, TB-500 and KPV have no licence.<Cite id="ema-medicines-search,fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> What this means for a person who orders for their own use is explained on our page on [Dutch law](/en/legal/nl/).

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem q="Did the FDA approve BPC-157 in 2026?">

No. On 23 July 2026, an advisory committee of the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) voted on BPC-157. As reported from the public meeting, it voted 8 to 6, with one abstention, to recommend BPC-157 for the "503A list". That list names substances that US pharmacies may use to make medicines on prescription. The committee looked at seven peptides. By the same reports, it recommended six: BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Semax and epitalon. The vote was only advice, and it went against the FDA staff's own advice.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-07-meeting,fda-pcac-2026-questions,fda-pcac-2026-briefing-intro" /> US pharmacies may not legally make these peptides until the FDA accepts the advice and makes a formal rule.<Cite id="fda-503a-bulks-page" /> None of them is an approved medicine, and a listing would not make them one. It says nothing about whether they work. It changes nothing in the EU or the Netherlands.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-presentations-day1,usc-21-353a,nl-geneesmiddelenwet" />

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem q="My product came with a lab certificate. Does that prove it is clean?">

No. A certificate shows only the tests that were done. The FDA looked at certificates for BPC-157, TB-500 and KPV. Most of the ones it found gave little more than a purity result, and none showed a test for germs.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-tb500-briefing,fda-pcac-2026-kpv-briefing" /> A product ordered online also gets no check like the one a doctor or pharmacist gives.<Cite id="igj-online-medicijnen" /> See [what you can check yourself](#2-the-vial-is-not-the-molecule) and [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/).

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem q="Will my doctor tell the police or my employer?">

No. Dutch doctors must keep what you tell them secret. They may also refuse to answer questions from the police.<Cite id="knmg-beroepsgeheim-doorbreken,nl-wgbo-art-457-confidentiality" /> The rare exceptions include serious danger to other people, child abuse or domestic violence, and some infectious diseases.<Cite id="knmg-beroepsgeheim-doorbreken" />

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem q="I compete in sport. Can I use peptides?">

Most unapproved peptides are banned at all times.<Cite id="wada-2026-list-en" /> If a banned substance is found in your body, that counts as a doping offence, even if you did not mean to take it.<Cite id="wada-code-2021,wada-code-2027" /> A product with the wrong contents can end a career. See [sport and doping](/en/legal/sport-and-doping/).

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem q="Why does this site not give doses?">

Because for unapproved peptides, nobody knows a safe or useful amount for people. For BPC-157, for example, the FDA called the few studies in people small and short, with amounts that were probably just a first try.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> Amounts used in rats cannot simply be scaled up to people. See [the translation gap](/en/evidence/the-translation-gap/) and our [editorial policy](/en/about/editorial-policy/).

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem q="Where can I report a side effect?">

In the Netherlands, anyone can report a side effect to Lareb, also for a product bought online. Lareb does not pass your report on to your doctor.<Cite id="lareb-bijwerkingen-melden" /> If you feel unwell, see a doctor as well.

</FaqItem>

</Faq>
