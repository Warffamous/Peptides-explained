## 3. Conflicts and how they were resolved

- **"Metabolite of melanotan II"** (brief): no primary source found that shows bremelanotide is formed from melanotan II in the body. The page uses the verified structural fact (free acid instead of amide; PubChem) and King 2007's wording "carboxylate derivative". Flagged for the reviewer.
- **Why the nasal spray was dropped** (brief: "stopped over blood-pressure effects"): the FDA review gives "variable systemic exposure via the intranasal route"; White 2017 (developer-funded) links the variable bioavailability to a heightened risk of adverse effects "including elevations in blood pressure"; Clayton 2016 says intensive BP monitoring addressed "US FDA concerns expressed after earlier studies of an intranasal BMT formulation". The page reports all three in that order and does not say the spray was stopped *because of* blood pressure alone.
- **Flushing on placebo**: label Table 1 0.3%; Clayton 2022 abstract 1.3%. Page uses the label ("under 1%"). Injection-site reactions: label 13.2% vs 8.4% (broad grouped term); Clayton 2022 5.4% vs 0.5% (narrower). Page uses the label figure in the IconArray.
- **Discontinuation**: label 40% vs 13% and 39% vs 25% (mITT, double-blind period); Kingsberg 41.9% vs 16.0% and 43.8% vs 28.4% (randomised). Page cites the label in the evidence fold and Kingsberg's figures in the study limitation, each attributed.
- **SBP > 170 mmHg episodes** (White 2017): the text says five episodes > 15 min after bremelanotide and none after placebo; Table 3 shows two placebo episodes < 15 min. Page says "for more than 15 minutes" to match the text exactly.
- **Effect size framing**: the trial authors call the result clinically meaningful (anchor responders 58% vs 36%); Spielmans (independent) and Mintzes et al. (independent) call it of little use (no more satisfying events; much more discontinuation). The page gives both, with the numbers, and leaves the judgement to the reader and her doctor.
- **Independence grade**: by count, ~17 of ~28 primary publications are developer-linked (~60%), below the ~70% threshold for single-group-dominant; but every efficacy trial used for approval is developer-run. Graded **few-groups**, with the reasoning in `grade_rationale`. Flagged for the reviewer.
- **Preclinical volume**: about nine primary animal/in-vitro publications name bremelanotide/PT-141 (Molinoff 2003, Pfaus 2004, Pfaus 2007, Aughton 2008 [rabbit tissue, not cited on the page], Sauter 2020 [dog PK, not cited], Zhang 2021, Suzuki 2024 [glioblastoma cells, not cited], Borland 2025, Krupke 2025 [dog buccal patch, not cited]); plus two analytical chemistry papers. < 10 → **minimal**. Borderline; melanotan II literature not counted.
- **Safety grade**: rubric "well-characterised = large trials plus post-marketing surveillance". There are large trials (1,247 in 24-week RCTs; ~3,500 in the programme) and marketing since 2019, but intermittent use (2–3 times a month), CVD excluded, no published post-marketing safety study, pregnancy studies to 2030. Graded **partial** (consistent with tesamorelin). Flagged.
- **WADA status**: no statement by WADA, USADA, UKAD or the Dopingautoriteit found; web search returned only commercial pages (not used). Recorded as `not-listed` because it is not named and S0 excludes approved substances. The at-a-glance tile will say "Not banned"; the page adds "check with your anti-doping organisation" and the strict-liability point.
- **"US only"**: the Korean bridging trial (Kwang Dong) suggests a possible Korean filing; approval status outside the US/EU/UK was not checked, so the page says "authorised in the US", never "only in the US".
- **Duplicate source id**: `greenfield-2009-mc4r-blood-pressure` was registered in parallel by the melanotan-ii group with identical metadata (same DOI). Resolved by 22 October 2026 (fact-check): the melanotan-ii copy is gone and the only entry is in `src/data/sources/pt-141.yaml`. **Keep ours**; melanotan-ii now relies on it.
