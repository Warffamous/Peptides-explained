## 3. Conflicts between sources, and how they were resolved

- **Wouters 2020 errata.** PubMed lists two JAMA errata (Sept 2022). The PMC version (which carries corrections) still
  gives the $985 million median in Key Points and Results, so the number was kept; the note records the errata.
- **Zinzi 2026: 89.3%.** The abstract gives 89.3% of *suspected ADRs* as serious, not of reports. The KeyNumber label
  says "Most side effects in them were serious: 89 in 100".
- **Janvier 2018 metals.** The abstract says one sample had lead and "multiple samples" were up to ten times the ICH limit
  (the subject is ambiguous, most likely arsenic). The page says only "arsenic and lead in some samples".
- **Davis 2017 is about all EMA cancer approvals**, not conditional ones; first draft implied it measured "faster
  routes". Reworded to "approved medicines can reach the market with uncertain benefit".
- **EMA repurposing pilot end date.** A search summary said it ran to December 2024; the registered source (2021 news)
  does not say so, so the page says only "In 2021 … started a pilot".
- **"Dutch law targets trade, not use."** Art. 40 lists importing among the forbidden acts, which can cover a person who
  orders from abroad. Pages say "Using a product is not itself named as an offence, although importing one is", matching
  `legal/nl`.
- **"Most peptides on this site have no licence anywhere"** (funding draft): not true for all 21 planned compounds
  (semaglutide, tirzepatide; tesamorelin, bremelanotide elsewhere; Semax in Russia). Replaced by "Many
  peptides on this site, such as BPC-157, have no licence as a medicine in the EU".
- **Hero for the reform page.** history/how-we-got-here already uses a photo of the same 1962 signing as its hero; the
  Kelsey photo moved into the reform body (`<Photo>`, the precautionary section) and the hero became the Tweede Kamer.
- **Brief's `<Lead question answer />` form.** The component takes the answer as children
  (`<Lead question="…">answer</Lead>`), as on every existing page and in the validator; the children form is used.

## 4. Choices that need the owner's sign-off (policy, not facts)

1. **Correction speed targets** on `about/corrections`: one working day for confirmed safety errors, seven days for
   other substantive errors. These are commitments the editor must be able to keep.
2. **Reader-donation and grant disclosure** (`about/funding`): "funder and amount on this page"; donations "only if we
   ever ask", "donors get nothing in return", totals on the page. Matches the brief; no frequency promised.
3. **Hosting and server logs** (`about/funding`, "The details"): the page says the server needs the reader's IP address
   to deliver pages and that we run no analytics. `config/site.json` points to a `pages.dev` host; whether the host
   keeps request logs, and for how long, is not stated on the site. Add a one-line processor disclosure before launch.
4. **Contact channel**: `config/site.json` has `contactEmail: null`; both `about/corrections` and `about/funding` say a
   contact will be listed before public launch.
5. **AI disclosure** (`about/editorial-policy`, "How we use AI"): describes AI-assisted drafting and an AI-assisted,
   editor-overseen checking step. If the owner's process differs (for example a human reads every fact), correct it.
6. **`reviewed_by` on the reform page** is `pending-medical-review` (it makes medical and legal factual claims) rather
   than `editorial-board`; the opinion is signed by the editorial board through `opinion.signed_by`.
7. **The six proposals** are the editorial board's opinion and should be read by the owner as the site's public
   position before launch.

### Errors found and fixed

1. **"Health claims" attributed to the FDA (reform page, position table and proposal 4).** The FDA page says products
   falsely labelled "for research purposes" were "sold directly to consumers for human use with dosing
   instructions". It says nothing about health claims. Both places now state only what the FDA says. The call to
   focus on sellers who make false health claims stays, worded as our opinion.
2. **MHRA KeyNumber inflated the peptide signal.** It led with "almost 20 million doses" in the section "People are
   already using these products". Most of those doses were sedatives, painkillers and erectile-dysfunction medicines
   (MHRA: 9.9 m, 4.1 m and 4.4 m). The key number is now the 5,680 GLP-1 pens, with the 20 million total in its label.
   The fold now says plainly that most of the seized doses were not peptides. "Illegal drugs" was corrected to
   illegal medicines (the MHRA's own word is "illegally traded medicines").
3. **EMA quote overstated.** EMA wrote that the lack of incentive "could be a wasted opportunity for public
   health", about old medicines no longer protected by a patent or data exclusivity. The page had dropped "could be"
   and the patent qualifier. Both are restored.
4. **BPC-157 framed one-sidedly.** The page cited the FDA briefing only for "never went beyond phase 2". The same
   briefing summarises that trial: 53 people with ulcerative colitis, no clear benefit, conference abstract only.
   The page now says so, which matches compounds/bpc-157.
5. **Ashraf 2024 incomplete.** The page reported only the low purity of the powder (7.7–14.4%). The abstract also
   says the semaglutide *content exceeded the label* by 28.56–38.69%, which is an overdose risk. Both facts now
   appear. "Sellers' papers" was changed to "labels" (abstract: "claimed on product labels by manufacturers"). The
   page no longer implies the purchases were made in Hungary; it says a Hungarian university made them.
6. **Wegovy reimbursement out of date.** The page gave the July 2024 advice as the current state. The Zorginstituut is
   reassessing Wegovy: dossier complete in July 2026, and its scientific advisory council first discussed it on
   5 October 2026 (work-agenda page re-read). One cited sentence was added (`zin-wegovy-reassessment`).
7. **Hidden use generalised from steroids.** The position table said "Use is hidden from doctors", citing only Pope
   2004 (steroid users). It now reads "often hidden …, as a study of steroid users found". The fold said "The
   mechanism is the same" without evidence; it now says "We expect the same pattern, but nobody has measured it". The
   chain table's "fear of judgement" was not in any source; it now gives the reason Pope 2004 measured (doubt about
   what doctors know).
8. **"No warning reaches regulators"** contradicted the page's own data (234 EudraVigilance reports). It now says
