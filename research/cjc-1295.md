## 1. The scoping decision: which molecule this page grades

There are two active molecules under one name (FDA briefing, 15 November 2024, `fda-pcac-2024-cjc1295-briefing`):

| | Sequence | Formula / mass | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CJC-1295 with DAC** (ConjuChem's CJC-1295; FDA: "CJC-1295 DAC") | Tyr-D-Ala-Asp-Ala-Ile-Phe-Thr-Gln-Ser-Tyr-Arg-Lys-Val-Leu-Ala-Gln-Leu-Ser-Ala-Arg-Lys-Leu-Leu-Gln-Asp-Ile-Leu-Ser-Arg-Lys(ε-maleimidopropionyl)-NH2, 30 residues | C165H269N47O46; 3647.2 (PubChem) / 3647.95 (FDA) | every human study, all developer pharmacology, two independent mouse studies |
| **"CJC-1295 without DAC"**, Mod GRF (1-29); FDA: "CJC-1295 (free base)" | Tyr-D-Ala-Asp-Ala-Ile-Phe-Thr-Gln-Ser-Tyr-Arg-Lys-Val-Leu-Ala-Gln-Leu-Ser-Ala-Arg-Lys-Leu-Leu-Gln-Asp-Ile-Leu-Ser-Arg-NH2, 29 residues | C152H252N44O42; 3367.9 (PubChem) / 3367.95 (FDA) | none: FDA found no pharmacology, PK, toxicology or human study |

Sequences verified three ways: (1) PubChem CID 91971820 isomeric SMILES read residue by residue (D-Ala2
from the chirality flag; Lys30 side-chain amide to the maleimidopropionyl; C-terminal amide) and its
systematic synonym; (2) GSRS UNII 62RC32V9N7, subunit `YaDAIFTQSYRKVLAQLSARKLLQDILSRK` with the
maleimidopropanamide modification at residue 30; (3) the FDA briefing, which spells out the 29-residue
sequence and the four substitutions (L-Ala2→D-Ala, Asn8→Gln, Gly15→Ala, Met27→Leu, from Jetté 2005).
The without-DAC sequence: PubChem CID 56841945 systematic name (ends in L-argininamide) and the FDA
briefing (Table 2, nominator's CoA).

**Decision.** Data file, sequence and the four grades describe the version with DAC, because the name
was coined for it (Jetté 2005), WADA and GSRS use it, and all human data are on it. `names.other` does
not list Mod GRF; the prose explains the difference in the first section and the harm-reduction
`<ProductCheck extra>`. The glossary has a `mod-grf` entry.

**Correction to the brief.** The brief described the version without DAC as "a different molecule with
a half-life of minutes". We found no source for any half-life of that molecule. The FDA states it found
no PK study of CJC-1295 (free base) and that direct evidence that the DAC (rather than the four
substitutions) extends the half-life "is lacking". Native GHRH(1-29) was detectable for only about an
hour in rats (Jetté 2005, via FDA). The page therefore says the half-life of the version without DAC is
not published, and never claims it is minutes.

**Naming confusion, documented (all cited on the page):** FDA "at least nine different names" for five
substances and calls it "a safety risk for patients"; both 503A nominations internally inconsistent;
GSRS UNII record inconsistent (FDA footnote); PubChem CID 56841945 (no-DAC structure) carries the
depositor synonyms "CJC 1295 with DAC", "DAC:GRF acetate" and "CJC-1295-no DAC acetate" at once
(our own check, 22 October 2026); Memdouh 2021 uses "CJC-1295" for the no-DAC peptide; Henninge 2010:
a 2009 Norwegian seizure contained the 29-residue amide; Gajda 2019: Danish seizures contained
glycine-extended modified GRF(1-29).

## 3. Key facts with source ids

- **Developer, discovery.** ConjuChem, Montreal; three maleimido GRF(1-29) derivatives; CJC-1295 4-fold GH
  AUC over 2 h vs hGRF(1-29) in rats; in plasma beyond 72 h; albumin band from 15 min to beyond 24 h
  (Western blot of one rat) (`jette-2005-cjc1295-albumin`). Albumin-bound CJC-1295 90.3% intact after
  DPP-IV vs no intact GHRH(1-29) (FDA account of Jetté).
- **DAC chemistry.** Maleimide on Lys30 bonds covalently to the free thiol of albumin Cys34 (Jetté);
  to "free thiols on the surface of plasma proteins" generally (`timms-2019-cjc1295-equine`); FDA:
  could bond to other proteins (off-target).
- **Human PK/PD.** Half-life 5.8–8.1 d; GH 2–10× for ≥6 d; IGF-1 1.5–3× for 9–11 d; IGF-1 above
  baseline up to 28 d after multiple doses; no serious adverse reactions (`teichman-2006-cjc1295`).
  FDA account: study 2 half-life 5.4–9.2 d; IGF-1 above the age/sex range only at the highest single dose.
  Pulsatility preserved, trough GH 7.5×, mean GH +46%, IGF-1 +45% (`ionescu-2006-cjc1295-pulsatility`);
  IGF-1 within normal range in all 12 (FDA).
- **Adverse events (FDA summary of the papers).** Study 1: AEs 33/35 vs 2/7; injection-site reactions
  ~70%; urticarial rash ~30%; headache 63% vs 14%; diarrhoea 43% (active only); vasodilatory reactions
  (flushing, warmth, transient hypotension) 30% (active only). Study 2: flushing 40–100%; one person
  transient leg contractions/incoordination; two transient dizziness/hypotension; nausea or abdominal
  pain 20%. Ionescu: dose-dependent heart-rate increase. FDA Category 2 page: "serious adverse events ...
  including increased heart rate and systemic vasodilatory reaction" (`fda-category-2-safety-risks`).
- **Development stopped.** Registry: NCT00267527 terminated, completion September 2006, no reason, no
  results (`ctgov-nct00267527`); EudraCT 2005-003797-25, Germany/BfArM, "Prematurely Ended", CJC-1295
  TFA salt (`euctr-2005-003797-25`). FDA briefing: "ConjuChem Biotechnology withdrew CJC-1295 DAC from
  clinical trials in 2006 after the death of a subject involved in a phase 2 trial"; details from
  "anecdotal reports" (aidsmap; NATAP via web.archive.org): 192 enrolled; chest discomfort 2 h after the
  11th weekly dose, ECG-confirmed acute MI, death about 1 h later; attending physician's most likely
  explanation asymptomatic coronary artery disease with plaque rupture and occlusion; data never published.
- **GH-excess class effects.** Tesamorelin (Egrifta SV) label, effective 29 July 2026
  (`dailymed-egrifta-sv-label`): fluid retention "thought to be related to the induction of GH secretion"
  (oedema, arthralgia, carpal tunnel); HbA1c ≥6.5% at 26 weeks 5% vs 1% (HR 3.3); IGF-1 >2 SDS in 47%
  at 26 weeks (36% >3 SDS); contraindicated in active malignancy and pregnancy (rat hydrocephaly);
  hypersensitivity 4%; injection-site reactions 25% vs 14% in section 5.6 but 17% vs 6% in Table 1
  (a narrower grouping), so the IconArray leaves injection-site reactions out and uses Table 1 rows
  (N = 543 vs 263, 26-week phase): peripheral oedema 6 vs 2, myalgia 6 vs 2, arthralgia 13 vs 11, plus
  the section 5.4 HbA1c figure (5 vs 1); GH may alter CYP450 clearance; glucocorticoid replacement may
  need increasing. Insulin resistance via FFA
  (`moller-2009-gh-metabolism`). rhGH label warnings list (FDA briefing).
- **Cancer/pituitary.** Renehan 2004 (IGF-1 association); Thorner 1982 (ectopic GHRH → somatotroph
  hyperplasia, acromegaly, reversed after tumour removal); Mayo 1988 (GHRH transgenic mice: "enormous
  pituitary glands"); Ben-Shlomo 2020 (31 mice, 8 weeks: pituitary +47%, IGF-1 up, DNA-damage markers up;

## 5. Conflicts between sources, and how they were resolved

1. **Number of people.** Teichman's abstract gives no n. The FDA summary gives 33/35 vs 2/7 in study 1
   (42) and 24 in study 2 (66 in total; 55 on CJC-1295), plus 12 in Ionescu, but also states a total of
   "63 healthy adults" exposed across the three studies. 55 + 12 = 67, not 63 (Sackmann-Sala's 11 are a
   subset of Ionescu's 12). We report the FDA's per-study figures for Teichman (n = 66 in the data file)
   and the FDA's "63" only as the FDA's own count. **Reviewer: check against the Teichman full text.**
2. **IGF-1 rise in Ionescu:** abstract 45%, FDA text 44% (165 → 240 ng/mL). We use the abstract.
3. **Half-life:** Teichman abstract 5.8–8.1 d; FDA account of study 2 5.4–9.2 d; Ionescu "8 d". We use
   the abstract figure and "about 6 to 8 days" in the fact sheet.
4. **Enrolment of the phase 2:** registry 120 (type not stated; record last updated October 2006);
   anecdotal reports cited by the FDA 192. The data file has `n: null`; the prose gives 192 as the FDA's
   account.
5. **Mass:** PubChem 3647.2 vs FDA 3647.95 g/mol (different atomic-weight conventions); PubChem used.
6. **"CJC-1295" meaning.** Developer and WADA: the DAC molecule. FDA 2024 and some doping laboratories:
   the no-DAC molecule ("CJC-1295 (free base)"), with the DAC molecule as "CJC-1295 DAC". The page states
   both conventions explicitly; the regulatory notes refer to the FDA's names only inside the vote counts.

## 6. The trial death: how it is handled

The brief asked not to repeat unverified claims. The primary sources (both registries) give only
"terminated" / "prematurely ended" with no reason. A primary regulatory source (the FDA briefing)
states that the developer withdrew CJC-1295 "after the death of a subject involved in a phase 2 trial"
and recounts the details from what it explicitly calls anecdotal internet reports. We report exactly
that, attributed to the FDA, including the attending physician's view that unrecognised coronary
disease was the most likely explanation, and say plainly that causality cannot be judged because the
data were never published. We did not cite aidsmap or NATAP (not on the allowlist; aidsmap blocked
automated access with a bot check, which we did not try to bypass). The summary and the glance strip do
not mention the death.
