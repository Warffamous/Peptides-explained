<Lead question="What does German law say about peptides?">Most peptides sold online likely count as medicines with no licence. Selling them is a crime.</Lead>

<Compare title="German law forbids selling a medicine with no licence. The medicines law does not name using one." detail="Arzneimittelgesetz (AMG), Heilmittelwerbegesetz (HWG) and Anti-Doping-Gesetz (AntiDopG), in the versions in force on 22 October 2026.">

| | Under German law |
|---|---|
| Is selling it, or offering it for sale, forbidden? | <Verdict is="yes" /> A ready-made medicine needs a licence before it is sold.<Cite id="de-amg-21,de-amg-4" /> Selling one without it is a crime.<Cite id="de-amg-96" /> |
| Is advertising it forbidden? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Ads for a medicine that needs a licence but has none are not allowed.<Cite id="de-hwg-3a" /> |
| Is bringing it into Germany forbidden? | <Verdict is="yes" /> As a rule, only licensed medicines may come in, and only to pharmacies and traders. The exceptions are narrow.<Cite id="de-amg-73" /> |
| Are fakes and dangerous medicines treated more strictly? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Trading in them can bring up to three years in prison.<Cite id="de-amg-8,de-amg-95" /> |
| Does the medicines law name using it yourself as a crime? | <Verdict is="no" /> It bans using a dangerous medicine on another person. It does not name using one on yourself.<Cite id="de-amg-5,de-amg-95" /> |
| Can owning it be a crime? | <Verdict is="yes" /> Under the anti-doping law, for some listed peptides: owning a large amount for doping in sport.<Cite id="de-antidopg-2,de-antidopg-anlage" /> |

</Compare>

The Arzneimittelgesetz (AMG) is the German Medicines Act. This page explains what it says about products such as BPC-157, TB-500 or "research" semaglutide. It also covers the anti-doping law, which reaches further in Germany than in the Netherlands.<Cite id="de-antidopg-2,dopingautoriteit-doping-en-de-wet" /> The page does not tell you how to get round the law. German medicines law follows EU law: see [the EU](/en/legal/eu/).

## What counts as a medicine

<Lead question="Is a peptide a medicine under German law?">Often, yes. The law looks at how a product is offered and at what it can do.</Lead>

<Diagram id="de-medicine-two-tests" caption="German law has two tests. A product that meets either one is a medicine.">

| Test | The question the law asks |
|---|---|
| Presentation | Is the product offered as something that heals, relieves or prevents disease in people? |
| Function | Can it be used in people to restore, correct or change how the body works, through a drug-like effect? |
| In case of doubt | Could it be a medicine and also a food or a cosmetic? Then it counts as a medicine. |

</Diagram>

These tests come from § 2 of the AMG.<Cite id="de-amg-2" /> They look at the whole product, not only its label. See [research use only](/en/legal/research-use-only/) for why a "research" label does not change this.

<InDetail title="The words of § 2, and two terms you will meet">

Under § 2, a medicine is a substance meant for use in or on the human body. It is presented as something that heals, relieves or prevents disease. A substance is also a medicine if it can be used in or on the body, or given to a person, to restore, correct or influence body functions. The effect must be pharmacological, immunological or metabolic.<Cite id="de-amg-2" />

Two more terms matter on this page:<Cite id="de-amg-4" />

- **Fertigarzneimittel** ("ready-made medicine"): a medicine made in advance and sold in a pack meant for the user. A vial sold online is a typical example, in our reading.
- **Inverkehrbringen** ("placing on the market"): keeping stock to sell or hand over, offering it, and handing it to others. All of these count, not only the sale itself.

</InDetail>

## The core rule: a licence before sale

<Lead question="What exactly does the law forbid?">Selling or handing over a ready-made medicine with no licence is banned. So are fakes.</Lead>

<Diagram id="de-licence-chain" caption="The rules that apply before a medicine may be sold in Germany.">

1. **A licence first.** A ready-made medicine may only be put on the market with a licence from the German federal authority or from the EU.<Cite id="de-amg-21" />
2. **"On the market" is broad.** Keeping stock to sell, offering it and handing it over all count.<Cite id="de-amg-4" />
3. **No dangerous medicines.** A medicine is banned if science gives good reason to suspect harm beyond what medicine finds acceptable.<Cite id="de-amg-5" />
4. **No fakes or poor quality.** Making or trading in falsified or poor-quality medicines is banned. So are misleading claims.<Cite id="de-amg-8" />
5. **Narrow exceptions.** Some medicines made in a pharmacy, medicines in clinical trials and compassionate use are the main ones.<Cite id="de-amg-21" /> See [legal routes](/en/legal-routes/).

</Diagram>

For BPC-157, the US medicines agency found no approved product in any country.<Cite id="fda-pcac-2026-bpc157-briefing" /> So no German licence exists for it. In our reading, none of the exceptions covers selling it as a ready-made product.

<InDetail title="How high the penalties can be">

| What | Where in the law | The most the law allows |
|---|---|---|
| Selling a ready-made medicine without a licence | AMG § 96 no. 5 | Up to one year in prison, or a fine<Cite id="de-amg-96" /> |
| Selling a dangerous medicine, or using it on someone else | AMG § 95 | Up to three years, or a fine. One to ten years in very serious cases, such as putting many people's health at risk<Cite id="de-amg-95" /> |
| Trading in fake or poor-quality medicines | AMG § 95 | Up to three years, or a fine<Cite id="de-amg-95" /> |
| Bringing a fake medicine into Germany | AMG § 96 no. 18e | Up to one year, or a fine<Cite id="de-amg-96" /> |
| Bringing in a medicine against the import rule | AMG § 97 | An administrative fine of up to €25,000<Cite id="de-amg-97" /> |
| Advertising a medicine with no licence | HWG § 15 | An administrative fine of up to €50,000<Cite id="de-hwg-15" /> |

An administrative fine (Ordnungswidrigkeit) is a fine from an authority. It is not a criminal conviction. These are maximums. They say what the law allows, not what usually happens.

</InDetail>

## Bringing medicines into Germany

<Lead question="Can a peptide be sent to me in Germany, or brought in?">As a rule, only licensed medicines may come in, to pharmacies and traders. The exceptions are narrow.</Lead>

<Diagram id="de-import-routes" caption="The ways a medicine can come into Germany, and what § 73 of the AMG says about each.">

| Way in | What § 73 says | For a peptide with no licence |
|---|---|---|
| A parcel from a shop to you | Only licensed medicines, and only from an EU or EEA pharmacy allowed to sell by mail | Not covered: the medicine itself has no licence |
| From another EU or EEA country, for your own use | Only if it may be sold in the country it comes from, in a normal personal amount, with no business acting in between | Hard to meet when the product is not licensed there either (our reading) |
| In your luggage when you enter Germany | Not covered by the import ban, in an amount that fits normal personal need | This exception does not mention a licence. The bans on dangerous and fake medicines, and the doping law, still apply |
| Through a German pharmacy, on your order | A pharmacy may import a medicine that is lawfully sold where it comes from, if Germany has no matching medicine | Only for medicines licensed somewhere |
| A fake medicine | Never allowed | Never allowed |

</Diagram>

Even where the import ban does not apply, other rules still do. Fake medicines may never be brought in. And the anti-doping law limits owning some peptides (see below).<Cite id="de-amg-73,de-antidopg-2" />

<InDetail title="The exact conditions in § 73">

- **The main rule (§ 73(1)).** A medicine that needs a licence may only be brought in if it is licensed for Germany. The person receiving it must be a drug company, a wholesaler or a pharmacy. Or, for parcels to a private person, it must come from an EU or EEA pharmacy that may sell by mail.<Cite id="de-amg-73" />
- **Fakes (§ 73(1b)).** Bringing falsified medicines or active substances into Germany is forbidden.<Cite id="de-amg-73" />
- **Research (§ 73(2) no. 2).** The main rule also does not apply to medicines for the own needs of research and science institutions, needed for scientific work. The text is about institutions, not private buyers.<Cite id="de-amg-73" />
- **Personal exceptions (§ 73(2) no. 6 and 6a).** The main rule does not apply to medicines brought in when you enter Germany, in an amount for normal personal need. It also does not apply to medicines from an EU or EEA country that may be sold there. They must be bought without a business in between, in such an amount.<Cite id="de-amg-73" />
- **What still applies (§ 73(4)).** Under these exceptions, most of the AMG does not apply. But the bans on dangerous medicines (§ 5) and on fakes (§ 8) still do.<Cite id="de-amg-73" />
- **Pharmacy import (§ 73(3)).** A pharmacy may bring in an unlicensed ready-made medicine in small amounts for one person's order. It must be lawfully sold in the country it comes from, and no matching medicine may be available in Germany. From outside the EU or EEA, a prescription is needed.<Cite id="de-amg-73" />
- **The fine.** Bringing in medicines against the main rule is an administrative offence, with a fine of up to €25,000.<Cite id="de-amg-97" />

</InDetail>

<Callout type="uncertain" title="What we could not find">
We found no official guidance on how customs and the courts apply the personal exceptions to peptides that have no licence anywhere. The table above gives our reading of the text. Our legal reviewer will check it.
</Callout>

## Advertising, and why this site sells nothing

<Lead question="Can anyone advertise these products in Germany?">No. Ads for a medicine that needs a licence but has none are not allowed.</Lead>

<KeyNumber title="Ads for a medicine with no licence can bring a big fine" value="€50,000" label="the most a fine can be for an ad for a medicine with no licence" counted="Heilmittelwerbegesetz § 15(3), for breaches of § 3a, in the version in force on 22 October 2026" source="de-hwg-15" />

The Heilmittelwerbegesetz (HWG) is the German law on advertising for medicines. It forbids ads for medicines that need a licence but have none.<Cite id="de-hwg-3a" /> Ads for prescription-only medicines may only be aimed at doctors, pharmacists and others who may trade in them.<Cite id="de-hwg-10" /> This site sells nothing and names no seller. The [Dutch page](/en/legal/nl/) explains why that matters under EU law.

## The anti-doping law

<Lead question="Does the German doping law matter if I am not an athlete?">Partly. Owning a large amount of some listed peptides for doping in sport is a crime for anyone.</Lead>

<Compare title="Six peptides on this site are named in the annex of the German doping law." detail="AntiDopG annex (last changed 10 March 2023) and the 2026 doping list as published in the German law gazette. Checked on 22 October 2026.">

| | Named in the annex (owning a large amount)? | On the doping list the law follows (selling, giving, self-doping)? |
|---|---|---|
| TB-500 | <Verdict is="yes" /> As thymosin beta-4 and its derivatives<Cite id="de-antidopg-anlage" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> Section S2<Cite id="de-bgbl-2025-ii-312" /> |
| CJC-1295, tesamorelin | <Verdict is="yes" /> As growth-hormone-releasing hormones<Cite id="de-antidopg-anlage" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> Section S2<Cite id="de-bgbl-2025-ii-312" /> |
| Ipamorelin, hexarelin | <Verdict is="yes" /> As growth-hormone secretagogues and releasing peptides<Cite id="de-antidopg-anlage" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> Section S2<Cite id="de-bgbl-2025-ii-312" /> |
| AOD-9604 | <Verdict is="yes" /> As a growth-hormone fragment<Cite id="de-antidopg-anlage" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> Section S2<Cite id="de-bgbl-2025-ii-312" /> |
| BPC-157 | <Verdict is="no" /> Not in the annex<Cite id="de-antidopg-anlage" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> Section S0<Cite id="de-bgbl-2025-ii-312" /> |
| MOTS-c | <Verdict is="no" /> Not in the annex<Cite id="de-antidopg-anlage" /> | <Verdict is="yes" /> Section S4<Cite id="de-bgbl-2025-ii-312" /> |
| Semaglutide, tirzepatide | <Verdict is="no" /> Not in the annex<Cite id="de-antidopg-anlage" /> | <Verdict is="no" /> Not banned; only monitored<Cite id="wada-2026-monitoring-program" /> |

</Compare>

The Anti-Doping-Gesetz (AntiDopG) has three main rules, and they cover different people. Only one of them, the rule on owning a large amount, depends on the annex. The other two follow the full doping list, which Germany publishes each year in its law gazette.<Cite id="de-antidopg-2,de-antidopg-3,de-bgbl-2025-ii-312" />

<InDetail title="The three rules, who they cover, and the penalties">

| Rule | Who it covers | What it bans | The most the law allows |
|---|---|---|---|
| § 2(1) | Anyone | Making, trading, selling, handing over or prescribing a substance on the doping list, for doping in sport | Up to three years, or a fine. One to ten years, for example for supply to someone under 18, or as a business or a gang<Cite id="de-antidopg-2,de-antidopg-4" /> |
| § 2(3) | Anyone | Buying, owning or bringing in a "nicht geringe Menge" (a large amount) of a substance in the annex, for doping in sport | Up to three years, or a fine. Up to one year if by negligence<Cite id="de-antidopg-2,de-antidopg-4" /> |
| § 3 | For the penalty: only elite athletes in a testing pool, or people who earn a lot from sport | Using a listed substance without medical need to gain an advantage in an organised competition. Buying or owning it for that | Up to three years for using it. Up to two years for buying or owning it. No penalty for owning it if you give it up before use<Cite id="de-antidopg-3,de-antidopg-4" /> |

The rule on owning a large amount does not say you must be an athlete or take part in competitions. Those limits appear only in the self-doping rule.<Cite id="de-antidopg-2,de-antidopg-4" /> It does require the purpose of doping in sport. How courts read "sport" for gym use is a question for our legal reviewer.

</InDetail>

<InDetail title="What a 'nicht geringe Menge' is, and why we do not print the amounts">

A separate regulation, the Dopingmittel-Mengen-Verordnung, sets the amount for each substance or group in the annex. It covers growth-hormone fragments, growth-hormone-releasing hormones, secretagogues, releasing peptides, and thymosin beta-4 and its derivatives.<Cite id="de-dmmv-2023" />

We do not print the amounts. This site gives no amounts of any kind. Below the amount, the rule on owning does not apply, but the other rules on this page still can.

The health ministry adds substances to the annex by regulation.<Cite id="de-antidopg-6" /> The annex was last changed in March 2023. New peptides on the yearly doping list are not in it unless a regulation adds them.<Cite id="de-antidopg-anlage,de-antidopg-6" />

</InDetail>

See [sport and doping](/en/legal/sport-and-doping/) for the sports rules themselves, such as testing and bans.

## Who enforces the law

<Lead question="Who enforces these rules?">The health authorities of the German states watch the trade. Police, customs and prosecutors pursue crimes.</Lead>

<Diagram id="de-enforcement-map" caption="Who does what when the law on medicines is broken in Germany.">

| Who | What they do |
|---|---|
| BfArM, the federal medicines institute | Informs the public about fakes found in the legal supply chain. Can order a recall in urgent cases<Cite id="bfarm-arzneimittelfaelschungen" /> |
| Health authorities of the states (Länder) | Supervise the trade in medicines. Seize and test products<Cite id="bfarm-arzneimittelfaelschungen" /> |
| Police, customs and public prosecutors | Investigate and prosecute crimes involving medicines<Cite id="bfarm-arzneimittelfaelschungen" /> |

</Diagram>

The BfArM keeps a register of pharmacies that may sell medicines by mail. They must show the EU safety logo.<Cite id="bfarm-arzneimittelfaelschungen" /> The BfArM deals only with fakes in the legal supply chain. It cannot seize or test products itself. That is the job of the state authorities, and crimes are a matter for police, customs and prosecutors.<Cite id="bfarm-arzneimittelfaelschungen" />

<InDetail title="What tests of seized products in Germany found">

From 2010 to 2013, the Cologne anti-doping laboratory tested 337 black-market products, working with German customs. Of what it found, 12.8% (about one in eight) was peptide hormones or growth factors, mostly growth hormone and growth-hormone-releasing peptides. Thymosin beta-4 was among the findings. The authors wrote that several substances were "of fake/non-approved nature".<Cite id="krug-2014-black-market-germany" />

If your product did not come from a pharmacy, no regulator has checked what is in it. See [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/) for what you can and cannot check yourself.

</InDetail>

## Where to report

<Lead question="Where can I report a side effect in Germany?">To the BfArM and the Paul-Ehrlich-Institut. Use their online portal or a paper form.</Lead>

<JurisdictionCard code="DE" />

**Side effects.** The BfArM and the Paul-Ehrlich-Institut run one portal for reports: nebenwirkungen.bund.de. Reports from patients and from doctors both help to show a medicine's risks. You can also send a paper form.<Cite id="bfarm-risiken-melden" /> The portal had a fault from 7 to 9 October 2026. If you reported online then, the institutes ask you to report again.<Cite id="bfarm-risiken-melden" />

**Before you report.** Keep the vial, its label and any papers. Write down the batch number. Tell your doctor what you use: see [talking to your doctor](/en/talking-to-your-doctor/).
