In 1922 doctors in Toronto first lowered blood sugar in people with diabetes with insulin.<Cite id="fda-history-100-years-insulin" /> Insulin is a peptide. Many peptide medicines followed, such as semaglutide.<Cite id="fda-drugsatfda-ozempic,ema-epar-ozempic" />

The rules changed too. In the late 1950s a sleeping pill called thalidomide harmed more than 10,000 babies.<Cite id="vargesson-2015-thalidomide" /> After that, the US and Europe began to require proof that a medicine works, and a licence before sale.<Cite id="us-pl-87-781-drug-amendments-1962,eu-directive-65-65" />

So an authorised medicine today has passed checks on its quality, its effect and its safety.<Cite id="eu-directive-2001-83-original" /> A product sold without a licence has not been checked in this way.<Cite id="mhra-2026-01-seizures-2025" /> That difference runs through this whole site.

This section describes the history. It gives no advice on what to use. If you already have a product, read [product risk](/en/safety/product-risk/) and the [warning signs](/en/safety/warning-signs/).
