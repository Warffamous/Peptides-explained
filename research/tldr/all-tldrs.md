## pages/en/basics/how-signalling-works.mdx (117 words)
- **How does a peptide work?** It fits a receptor on the cell surface and switches it on, like a key in a lock.
- **Why does it have side effects?** The same receptor sits in many organs. Wanted effects and side effects often come from the same receptor.
- **Can a precise peptide still harm?** Yes. It can act in the wrong organ, hit other receptors, especially at higher amounts, or push growth.
- **Can growth peptides cause cancer?** Unknown: long use of unapproved ones has not been studied in people. Cancers depend on growth signals too.
- **Does it keep working the same?** Often not. Cells turn down overused receptors, and hormone systems brake themselves. Effects can fade or reverse.

## pages/en/basics/what-is-a-peptide.mdx (110 words)
- **What is a peptide?** A short chain of building blocks called amino acids. Long, folded chains are called proteins.
- **Is a natural peptide harmless?** No. Insulin keeps you alive; too much is dangerous. The death cap mushroom's main poison is a peptide.
- **Peptide or protein: what's the difference?** Mainly size, and experts draw the line in different places. The word says nothing about risk.
- **Why are most peptides injected?** Gut enzymes cut them up, and their molecules are too big to pass the gut wall easily.
- **Is a peptide product always pure?** No. Making a peptide leaves some impurities. A product from outside the legal supply chain has no guarantee.

## pages/en/evidence/conflicts-of-interest.mdx (109 words)

## pages/en/evidence/how-to-read-a-study.mdx (120 words)
- **Do animal studies show it works?** Not in people. Only trials in people can show that.
- **Why does study size matter?** In small studies, chance alone can produce a big effect.
- **What makes a test fair?** A comparison group, chance deciding who gets what, nobody knowing who got what, and a plan published first.
- **What does ‘significant’ mean?** Only that the result would be surprising if the treatment did nothing. It says nothing about size.
- **What does ‘20% lower risk’ mean?** It depends on how common the problem was. In one big trial: 15 fewer people in 1,000.
- **Can I trust a headline?** Not on its own. First ask who was studied: a ‘heals tendons’ story may be about rats.
